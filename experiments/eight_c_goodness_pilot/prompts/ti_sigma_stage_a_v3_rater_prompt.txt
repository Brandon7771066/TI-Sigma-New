Definitions:

Goodness:
- Structural and evaluative adequacy expressed through the Eight Cs.
- Rate Goodness independently as a circularity safeguard. Do not derive it mechanically from the Eight C scores.

The Eight Cs:

1. Coherence
- Degree to which the parts and relations hang together as an intelligible whole.

2. Consistency
- Degree to which claims, rules, or actions avoid contradiction within the relevant frame.

3. Continuity
- Degree to which intelligible linkage is preserved across time, stages, or transitions.

4. Concreteness
- **Concreteness is the degree of tangibility or determinate intelligibility of the evaluated entity: how readily it can be understood, represented, or operationally grasped with minimal fuzziness, vagueness, or ambiguity.**
- Concreteness in GILE-G does not measure ontological instantiation. Ontological instantiation and concrete physical mechanisms belong to HEM.

5. Completion
- Degree to which the entity includes the parts or conditions needed for its stated function within scope.

6. Criticality
- Degree to which the evaluation appropriately tracks how much correctness, failure tolerance, and accuracy discipline matter for the case.

7. Closeness
- Degree to which the entity remains near the relevant target, need, or purpose rather than drifting into nearby but less relevant success.

8. Choice
- Degree to which the relevant bearer has meaningful, attributable, and normatively interpretable choice within the situation, including the alignment of that choice with the relevant target.

Category boundaries:
- Intuition concerns conscious certainty.
- Love concerns internal and external binding, extending across scale into relational positive affect and coordinated action.
- Elegance concerns beauty, simplicity, economy, ideal form, and related aesthetic or formal qualities.
- Goodness concerns structural and evaluative adequacy expressed through the Eight Cs.
- Cross-component interaction does not imply categorical identity.
