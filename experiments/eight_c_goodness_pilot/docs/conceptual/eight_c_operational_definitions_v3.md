# Eight-C Operational Definitions v3

Status Label: NEW_CANONICAL_DRAFT

These are working operational definitions for Stage A v3. The Eight Cs may couple, nest, scaffold, and modify one another while remaining separately rateable. Statistical independence is not assumed or required.

Goodness is evaluated independently as a scalar circularity safeguard rather than computed from the Eight Cs.

## Coherence

- Definition: degree to which parts, relations, and stated aims hang together as an intelligible whole.
- Operational question: do the components mutually support a readable, unified structure?
- Target of evaluation: internal organization of the evaluated entity relative to its own stated content.
- High-score anchor: elements clearly fit together into one intelligible pattern.
- Mid-score anchor: some major links are visible, but sections feel only partly integrated.
- Low-score anchor: pieces are scattered, disconnected, or jointly unintelligible.
- Do not confuse with: Consistency. A structure can cohere toward one aim while still containing contradictions.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: often scaffolds Completion and supports Goodness when other Cs are adequate.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may reinforce Intuition through readable structure and Elegance through clear form.
- Possible HEM boundary: HEM may constrain what structures are physically or ontologically realizable, but Coherence itself remains a Goodness rating.

## Consistency

- Definition: degree to which claims, constraints, and actions avoid contradiction across the relevant frame.
- Operational question: does the entity refrain from asserting or enacting mutually incompatible commitments?
- Target of evaluation: compatibility among claims, rules, assumptions, or outputs.
- High-score anchor: no material contradictions are evident within the evaluated scope.
- Mid-score anchor: minor tensions exist but may be reparable or context-bounded.
- Low-score anchor: direct incompatibilities undermine the entity on its own terms.
- Do not confuse with: Coherence. Something can be contradiction-free yet poorly integrated.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: supports Criticality and Completion, and can be stress-tested by contradiction ratings.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: strong Consistency may support Intuition if certainty is warranted, but does not itself create certainty.
- Possible HEM boundary: some apparent inconsistencies may be due to measurement uncertainty or competing domains rather than HEM failure.

## Continuity

- Definition: degree to which the evaluated entity preserves intelligible linkage across time, stages, or developmental transitions.
- Operational question: do phases connect in a trackable way rather than fragmenting across transitions?
- Target of evaluation: temporal, procedural, developmental, or stage-to-stage linkage.
- High-score anchor: transitions preserve recognizable direction and relation across phases.
- Mid-score anchor: some transitions hold, but important breaks or handoff gaps remain.
- Low-score anchor: sequence or development is discontinuous in a way that breaks intelligibility.
- Do not confuse with: Closeness. Staying near an immediate target is not the same as maintaining trajectory through time.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: may scaffold Completion and sustain Closeness over longer horizons.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: strong Continuity can support Love when long-range binding is maintained.
- Possible HEM boundary: HEM may govern physical persistence mechanisms, but Continuity rates the evaluative linkage, not the substrate alone.

## Concreteness

- Definition: degree of tangibility or determinate intelligibility of the evaluated entity: how readily it can be understood, represented, or operationally grasped with minimal fuzziness, vagueness, or ambiguity.
- Operational question: how graspable and determinate is the entity for the stated evaluative task?
- Target of evaluation: intelligibility and tangibility of the entity as presented or enacted.
- High-score anchor: the entity is specific, graspable, and minimally fuzzy for the context.
- Mid-score anchor: enough is graspable to proceed, but important ambiguity remains.
- Low-score anchor: the entity is so vague, indeterminate, or fuzzy that robust evaluation is impaired.
- Do not confuse with: ontological instantiation or concrete physical mechanism. Those belong to HEM rather than GILE-G.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: often supports Completion and Criticality by making evaluation more determinate.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may make Intuition more accountable by reducing fuzziness, and may support Elegance when clarity is economical rather than bloated.
- Possible HEM boundary: Concreteness in GILE-G does not measure ontological instantiation. Ontological instantiation and concrete physical mechanisms belong to HEM.

## Completion

- Definition: degree to which the evaluated entity includes the parts, stages, or conditions needed for its stated function within scope.
- Operational question: what important required elements are still missing relative to the relevant target?
- Target of evaluation: presence of necessary components for task-relevant adequacy.
- High-score anchor: the entity includes the needed elements for its intended use within scope.
- Mid-score anchor: broadly workable, but one or more nontrivial gaps remain.
- Low-score anchor: major omissions block adequate task performance.
- Do not confuse with: Concreteness. A fully specified fragment may still be incomplete.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: often depends on Coherence and Concreteness for interpretable adequacy.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may interact with Elegance when simpler structures still remain sufficiently complete.
- Possible HEM boundary: missing physical mechanisms may matter under HEM, but Completion rates adequacy for the stated evaluative target.

## Criticality

- Definition: degree to which the evaluation appropriately tracks how much correctness, failure tolerance, and accuracy discipline matter for the case.
- Operational question: does the entity meet the level of care and accuracy demanded by the stakes?
- Target of evaluation: fit between stakes and rigor, especially under high-cost failure conditions.
- High-score anchor: rigor and error control are commensurate with the consequences of failure.
- Mid-score anchor: acceptable under ordinary use, but underprepared for higher-stakes demands.
- Low-score anchor: error tolerance is dangerously misaligned with the stakes.
- Do not confuse with: mere harshness or perfectionism. Criticality is about task-relevant stakes, not maximal scoring severity.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: often sharpens Consistency, Completion, and contradiction handling.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may interact with Intuition when certainty claims outpace evidential care.
- Possible HEM boundary: some criticality failures are realized through HEM-level mechanisms, but the rating concerns evaluative adequacy to stakes.

## Closeness

- Definition: degree to which the evaluated entity remains near the relevant target, need, or purpose rather than drifting into adjacent but less relevant success.
- Operational question: how directly does the entity address the actual target that matters here?
- Target of evaluation: proximity to the relevant target or need under the stated purpose and scope.
- High-score anchor: the entity closely serves the relevant target with little irrelevant drift.
- Mid-score anchor: partly on target, but with some displacement or diffusion.
- Low-score anchor: success occurs mainly on a nearby but less relevant target.
- Do not confuse with: Continuity. A process can stay stable over time while remaining badly aimed.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: often modulates Goodness where local wins hide broader target failure.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may connect with Love when the relevant target concerns attunement to persons or relationships.
- Possible HEM boundary: HEM can shape what targets are physically reachable, but Closeness rates evaluative nearness to the relevant target.

## Choice

- Definition: degree to which the relevant bearer has meaningful, attributable, and normatively interpretable choice within the evaluated situation, including the alignment of that choice with the relevant target.
- Operational question: who can choose what, over which scope, and how aligned are those choices with the relevant target?
- Target of evaluation: presence, scope, and alignment of task-relevant agency.
- High-score anchor: the relevant bearer has meaningful choice and its scope aligns well with the relevant target.
- Mid-score anchor: some meaningful agency exists, but scope, clarity, or alignment is partial.
- Low-score anchor: meaningful agency is absent, merely formal, coerced, or misaligned with the relevant target.
- Do not confuse with: sheer number of options. More options can coexist with worse alignment.
- Expected interactions with other Cs: can modify Closeness, Criticality, and Goodness without collapsing into them.
- Possible I/L/E interaction: may interact with Love when agency is distributed relationally, and with Intuition when confidence claims depend on chooser perspective.
- Possible HEM boundary: HEM may constrain implementable action channels, but Choice evaluates the role and alignment of agency rather than mere mechanism.
