### Step 2: The Endorsement (Critical Blocker)

arXiv requires endorsement from an existing arXiv author for your FIRST submission in each category. Without this, your paper goes into a moderation queue and may not appear.

**The best endorsement paths — in priority order:**

#### Path A: Arturo Tozzi (fastest if he responds to outreach)
Tozzi publishes on arXiv (search "Tozzi arXiv" to find his profile). If he responds warmly to your outreach email, add this line to the follow-up:
> *"One practical thing — I'm preparing to submit the Collatz ν₂ paper to arXiv:math.NT and need an endorsement from an existing arXiv author. If you'd be willing, arXiv will send you an automated request. Happy to send the paper in advance."*

#### Path B: Email a Collatz researcher directly

After creating your account, arXiv will suggest potential endorsers based on your submission topic. You can email them directly through arXiv's interface.

### Step 3: arXiv Submission — Paper 1 (Ready Now)

**File:** `papers/COLLATZ_ARXIV_SUBMISSION.tex`  
**Category:** math.NT (primary), math.LO (secondary)  
**Title:** The ν₂ Countdown Theorem: A Formally Verified Bound on Consecutive Single-Halving Steps in the Collatz Sequence  
**MSC codes:** 11B37 (primary), 03B35 (secondary — for the Lean 4 formalization)

**arXiv Abstract (copy-paste):**
```
We prove and formally verify in Lean 4 a tight bound on the length of consecutive 
single-halving steps (k=1 runs) in the Collatz iteration. Specifically, we establish 
the ν₂ Countdown Theorem: for any odd n, the number of consecutive halvings in the 
orbit of (3n+1) is exactly ν₂(n+1)−1, where ν₂ denotes the 2-adic valuation. 
The bound is tight — achieved infinitely often — and the proof is fully formalized 
using Lean 4 and Mathlib, making it machine-checkable. We also derive corollaries 
bounding total halving steps in finite orbit segments and discuss implications for 
ternary representations of Collatz dynamics.
```

`10.5281/zenodo.19371947`
