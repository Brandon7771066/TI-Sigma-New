# Physicalism pragmatic-cost argument (Ch12 / V2)

The corpus argues physicalism carries a pragmatic cost (pathologizes dissent as
"ill"/"insane", dissolves objective value/GILE, denies free will → corrodes mental
outlook). Two non-obvious guards MUST stay attached or the argument breaks #69:

1. **Not an appeal-to-consequences.** A worldview being bleak/demoralizing is NOT
   by itself evidence it is false — treating "this is depressing" as "this is untrue"
   is the genuine *argumentum ad consequentiam* fallacy. Always state this explicitly.
   **Why:** without it the argument is a textbook fallacy and a hostile reader dismisses
   the whole chapter.
   **How to apply:** the pragmatic cost is admissible ONLY via the framework's twin-axis
   UOP (a worldview is judged on truth AND existence/HEM together), so the cost counts
   against physicalism's *imperial* whole-picture claim, never against its narrow
   physical claims.

2. **The free-will psychology is REAL BUT CONTESTED — never cite it as settled.**
   Vohs & Schooler (2008, *Psychological Science*, "The Value of Believing in Free
   Will") — weakening free-will belief increased cheating — is a real, iconic paper and
   safe to name. BUT the downstream free-will-disbelief→worse-behavior literature hit
   serious replication trouble (large multi-lab attempts recovered it weakly/null), so
   present it as contested-not-settled. The broader meaning/agency/mattering↔well-being
   pattern holds up better and is the safer claim. Causal story = preliminary.
   **Why:** overstating this research would be exactly the kind of overclaim the Part B
   fix was correcting; #69 forbids dressing a contested effect as established.
   **How to apply:** describe replications generically (don't invent author names/DOIs);
   keep the "(verified studies exist; replication contested; causal claim preliminary)"
   tag.
