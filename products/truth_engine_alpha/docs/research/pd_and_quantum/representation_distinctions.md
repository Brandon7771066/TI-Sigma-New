# Representation Distinctions

This document separates three layers that are often conflated in historical PD discussions.

## Layer 1: Semantics

- Objective: define what a PD score means conceptually.
- Domain: claim support pressure, contradiction pressure, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Output type: semantic labels (false, indeterminate, true) and rationale.

## Layer 2: Numerical Encoding

- Objective: assign stable numeric ranges, thresholds, and ratios.
- Domain: scalar intervals such as [-1, 1] or [-3, 2], and ratio constants such as 4/3 or 1/e.
- Output type: registry rows with status and provenance.

## Layer 3: Algebraic Container

- Objective: choose a mathematical structure to carry features (vector, quaternion, octonion, sedenion, qutrit state).
- Domain: representation mechanics only.
- Output type: transform functions and diagnostics.

## Non-equivalence Rules

- A new algebraic container does not validate a semantic claim.
- A ratio choice does not imply physical quantum behavior.
- A qutrit simulation is not a hardware claim.
- Historical proposals remain active as conflicts until explicitly superseded in the registry.

## Immediate Policy

- Keep Graph and Crystal production outputs unchanged.
- Keep PD and quantum work in research-only modules and result artifacts.
- Do not freeze one final representation while contradictory historical proposals are still being recovered.
