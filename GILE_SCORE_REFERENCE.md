**Author:** Brandon Charles Emerick

## In Plain Language

This guide explains what a GILE score means and how to read one. A GILE score is a single number, usually between 0 and roughly 0.93, that estimates how closely a theory, algorithm, or entity aligns with what the framework treats as the deepest available structure of truth. The higher the number, the stronger the alignment.

The guide groups scores into bands — from the highest "crown" level near 0.93, down through foundational and robust theories, to a critical floor near 0.42 below which something cannot sustain itself over time. It also explains why small differences (a few hundredths of a point) can be meaningful when ranking closely matched ideas, and why they should not be over-interpreted.

The single most important takeaway is that GILE scores are meant to work like a compass rather than a verdict: they help prioritize ideas, calibrate confidence, and decide how much weight to place on a given theory, especially for high-stakes decisions. The bands and example values below are calibration aids, several of them explicitly marked as estimates pending further validation.

## The Precision Question

GILE scores quoted to three decimals (0.920, 0.903) reflect:
1. **Careful calibration** — each theory evaluated against the GILE dimensions
2. **Relative ranking** — distinguishes theories of similar quality
3. **Confidence bounds** — do not over-interpret tiny differences (< 0.01)
4. **Reference precision** — the reference truth level itself sits at 0.93

**Rule of thumb:**
- Differences > 0.05: Clearly significant
- Differences 0.02–0.05: Meaningful in practice
- Differences < 0.02: Both theories are nearly equivalent

## Special Cases

### Reference Truth = 0.93 (not 1.0)
The reference truth level is the highest function of the framework's distributed structure (0.93), not a separate, perfect being. It is the highest function but still part of the network.

### MI = 0.94 (higher than the reference level?)
This is an estimate. Meta-Indeterminate (MI) may score higher because it precedes the reference structure's activity — it is the pre-existence boundary, the "before" of actualization. This requires rigorous validation but is theoretically motivated.
