## Abstract

URB #692 established that i-cells are genuine first movers operating through (not against) physical law, leveraging quantum indeterminacy via intentionality. This URB resolves a residual tension: if free will is always embedded in causal context, does it always *interrupt* existing MRs rather than *initiate* new ones? The answer is no. First-mover behavior can genuinely begin a new MR chain — a fresh iterative convergence procedure whose initiating conditions are not determined by any prior MR chain, even though they are embedded in a physical/contextual background. This is the TI Sigma analog of a Markov chain beginning: prior context provides the substrate; the new MR is not a continuation of any specific prior MR. Free will decisions made "independently of previous ones" are genuine new beginnings of MR activity, not merely perturbations to ongoing MR chains.

## 1. The Tension to Resolve

From URB #692: free will operates within physical law and causal embedding. Every decision is contextualized. This suggests every first-mover act *interrupts* some ongoing MR — there is no pristine "beginning."

From URB #686 (MR-Zero): at least one genuine MR-beginning is already in the framework — the cosmological first MR. So MR-beginnings are not metaphysically impossible; they happened at least once.

**Resolution needed:** Can *ordinary* free will decisions initiate new MR chains, or are all ordinary decisions interruptions of existing MRs? URB #692 left this open.

**The clarification:** Ordinary free will decisions CAN initiate new MRs. Embedding in context does not require continuation of a specific prior MR chain. Context is the substrate on which new MRs begin — not a prior MR they must extend.

## 2. Two Kinds of Free-Will-Driven MR Events

**Type A — Interrupting / Redirecting an Existing MR:**

An ongoing MR chain is iterating toward resolution. The i-cell's intentionality applies a first-mover perturbation: redirecting the iteration, introducing new evidence, terminating the chain, or shifting its convergence target. The MR is *modified* but still tracks the same object.

Example: a scientist deep in an MR chain on hypothesis H receives a surprising data point; free will directs attention to integrate the new evidence, altering H's MR trajectory. The MR chain continues; its direction is changed.

**Type B — Initiating a Genuinely New MR:**

The i-cell's intentionality begins a fresh MR chain whose iterative target was not on any prior MR's agenda. Context is present (as always), but no specific prior MR chain is being continued. This is a *new MR-beginning*.

Example: a person in the middle of their day suddenly decides to start contemplating mortality for the first time in years. No prior MR was tracking this object. The new MR begins. Prior context (recent experience, age, mood) is substrate; but the MR itself is new, with its own initial conditions.

**Both are valid.** URB #692's embedding claim applies to both: context is always present. But only Type A is interruption. Type B is initiation.

## 3. The Markov Chain Analog

The user's formulation captures it precisely: "we aren't ALWAYS interrupting existing MRs when we speak of NEW DECISIONS that are made independently of previous ones (i.e. Markov chain)."

A Markov chain's transition at step n depends only on state at step n−1, not on the full prior trajectory. Importantly, a new Markov chain can be *started* with arbitrary initial conditions — the fact that the universe has been running for a long time does not prevent a new chain from being initiated now, today, with its own distinct initial state.

**The TI Sigma MR analog:** a new MR chain can be initiated with its own initial conditions. Those conditions are informed by context (physical state, recent experience, current HEM-GILE profile). But the initial conditions of the new MR chain are *not* determined by any prior MR chain's trajectory. The new MR is Markov-independent of prior MRs — conditioned on context, not on prior MR history.

**Formal statement:**

```
For MR chains {M_1, M_2, ..., M_k} active at time t,
a first-mover intentional act at time t+ε can initiate M_{k+1}
with initial conditions I(M_{k+1}) such that:
  I(M_{k+1}) depends on context C(t)
  I(M_{k+1}) does NOT depend on {M_1, ..., M_k} trajectories
  (conditionally Markov-independent given C)
```

The new MR is context-embedded (consistent with URB #692) but not a continuation of any prior MR. It is a fresh beginning.

## 4. Why This Is Not a Contradiction With Causal Embedding

Causal embedding (URB #692): free will operates within physical law; every decision has a physical context.

New MR initiation (this URB): first-mover acts can begin new MR chains that do not continue any specific prior MR chain.

**These are compatible because:**

- Context C(t) is the physical/contextual state at time t
- Prior MR chains {M_1, ..., M_k} are specific iterative structures that have been running
- A new MR chain M_{k+1} can be initiated with initial conditions drawn from C(t) without being a continuation of any of the specific chains in {M_1, ..., M_k}
- The context C(t) is always present (causal embedding holds)
- The specific prior chains do not have to constrain M_{k+1} (Markov-independence holds)

Causal embedding is about the physical-contextual substrate, not about specific prior MR chains. Every decision has physical context; not every decision continues a specific prior MR. The distinction is what URB #695 clarifies.

## 5. Implications for Free Will Intuition

Ordinary human intuition: "I just decided to do X" — without feeling that X was a continuation of any prior deliberation. This intuition is now vindicated in TI Sigma:

- The decision is context-embedded (felt as "I chose, given who I am now")
- The decision is not a continuation of any specific prior MR chain (felt as genuinely new)
- The decision initiates a new MR chain whose trajectory will depend on the decision itself and subsequent GILE-weighted iteration

This is the TI Sigma account of **the experience of genuine new beginnings**. People routinely report: "I suddenly decided to...", "It came out of nowhere...", "I had never considered this before..." These reports are consistent with URB #695: new MR chains can and do begin from first-mover intentional acts.

## 7. Type A vs Type B in Practice

| Phenomenon | Type | Why |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Updating belief given new evidence | A (interruption) | Same MR tracks revised belief |
| Switching strategies mid-task | A or B | Depends on whether the new strategy addresses the same target |
| Suddenly deciding to change careers | B (initiation) | New MR chain about life direction, not continuation of prior career MR |
| Starting a new friendship | B | New relational MR, distinct from prior social MRs |
| Having a religious conversion | B | New MR on meaning; prior belief MRs may continue separately |
| Waking up and choosing the day's priorities | Usually A (resuming) sometimes B (new) | Depends on whether new priorities are continuous with prior plans |
| Contemplating mortality for first time in years | B | Fresh MR-beginning |
| Resuming a paused deliberation | A | Same MR, with a gap |

In practice many decisions are Type A (incremental updates to ongoing MRs). But Type B events are real, are common, and are the source of the phenomenological experience of "genuinely starting something new."
