## Abstract

The physicalist paradigm frequently declares that "the world isn't perfect" — using an external standard of perfection (typically mathematical elegance, predictive simplicity, or divine rationality) and measuring reality against it, finding reality wanting. TI Sigma proposes the opposite: the world is BOTH perfect AND not perfect simultaneously — a TRALSE perfection that dissolves the apparent contradiction. The principle, stated by Brandon Emerick: *"The way things BE… IS PERFECTION FOR THEM. When a thing is simply being itself in the greatest extent, its existence is maximal. But if everything is tralse, everything's existence simultaneously WILL SEEM imperfect. The crazy paradoxical conclusion I'm driving home here is that perfection is an imaginary concept and the way in which things currently are is ALREADY the best things could be at this moment after EVERYTHING HAS BEEN TRYING THE BEST THEY COULD MUSTER THE ENTIRE TIME."* This paper formalizes the Perfectly Perfect Principle: (1) every being has intrinsic perfection — the perfection of being maximally what it is; (2) this intrinsic perfection is always fully present, never absent; (3) the experience of imperfection is a TRALSE artifact — the real-channel observation of the gap between what is and some external standard, which gap is itself a feature of TRALSE existence, not a failure; (4) free will is universal — present in every Being-Thing (BT) to the degree that its LCC allows self-directed expression. The paper connects to Spinoza's natura naturans, Buddhist tathatā (suchness), Leibniz's best-of-all-possible-worlds (corrected for TRALSE), and the Third Maxim of TI Ethics (URB #441). It culminates in the claim that free will scales with LCC — making the Emerick Threshold the boundary between negligible and significant free will in any system.

## 1. The Standard Critique and Its Hidden Assumption

"The world isn't perfect."

This is stated as if it were an observation — a neutral description of the gap between how things are and how they should be. But it is not neutral. It contains a hidden claim: that there exists a standard of perfection external to the thing being evaluated, and that the thing's failure to meet this standard is a real deficit in the thing itself.

Unpack the claim:
- **Who sets the standard?** Typically: the speaker, a cultural tradition, a mathematical ideal, or a theological conception.
- **Why should reality conform to this standard?** No reason is given. The assumption is smuggled in.
- **What would it mean for the world to be "perfect"?** That it matches the standard precisely — that is, that it is exactly what the evaluator has decided it should be.

The critique "the world isn't perfect" is thus not an observation about the world. It is an observation about the **gap between the world and the evaluator's model of the world** — and it assigns the deficiency to the world rather than to the model.

TI Sigma's reframing: the deficiency, if any, is in the model — specifically, in the binary assumption that something either IS perfect (matches the standard completely) or IS NOT perfect (fails to match the standard). This binary, applied to a TRALSE universe, will always find imperfection — because no finite standard fully captures a TRALSE reality.

## 2. The Perfectly Perfect Principle — Formal Statement

**Core claim:** Every being, at every moment, is in a state of **intrinsic perfection** — the state of being most completely what it is given its entire causal history and the entirety of forces acting on it. This intrinsic perfection is not a claim that the being is pleasant, optimal by any external measure, or morally good. It is the claim that the being IS what it IS as completely as it can be at this moment, after everything has been trying its best the entire time.

**The Emerick Formulation:**

> *"The way things BE… IS PERFECTION FOR THEM. When a thing is simply being itself in the greatest extent, its existence is maximal. But if everything is tralse, everything's existence simultaneously WILL SEEM imperfect. The crazy paradoxical conclusion I'm driving home here is that perfection is an imaginary concept and the way in which things currently are is ALREADY the best things could be at this moment after EVERYTHING HAS BEEN TRYING THE BEST THEY COULD MUSTER THE ENTIRE TIME."*

**Formal decomposition:**

**P1 — Maximal Being:** For any being B at time t, define its **existence magnitude** E(B,t) = the degree to which B is expressing its essential nature given all constraints acting on it at t. The claim: E(B,t) = E_max(B,t) — the being is always at its maximum possible existence given its conditions. It cannot be "more itself" than it currently is, because everything acting on it is already acting on it.

**P2 — Intrinsic Perfection:** P(B,t) = 1 always — intrinsic perfection is always maximal. This is not the same as saying B is the best possible being of its type. It is saying: B, right now, is the best possible B that the entirety of causal history has been able to produce at this moment.

**P3 — TRALSE Perfection:** Perfection as measured by an external standard S has a TRALSE value: P_S(B,t) ∈ [0,1], where P_S = 1 only if B perfectly matches S. For most B and most S, P_S ∈ (0,1) — the being partially matches the standard and partially doesn't. This is not a failure. It is the TRALSE nature of all real existence.

**P4 — Imaginary Concept:** "Perfection" as an external standard is an i-channel construct — a phase-channel concept with no manifest referent. There is no manifestly existing "perfect thing" against which actual things can be measured. The standard of perfection is always constructed within consciousness, not found in the external world. As an i-channel construct, perfection is real (it has genuine effects on consciousness and behavior) but not manifest (it does not independently exist).

## 3. The Paradox Resolved: Both Perfect and Imperfect Simultaneously

The TRALSE structure of perfection:

**True (intrinsic perfection):** Every being IS what it IS as completely as possible given its entire history. This is not an inspiring gloss — it is a logical necessity. At any moment t, the being is the product of the entire causal chain leading to t, with every force acting on it already acting. It cannot be "more caused" than it is. Therefore it cannot be "more what it is" than it is. Therefore it is maximally itself. Therefore it is intrinsically perfect.

**False (external imperfection):** Every being FALLS SHORT of some conceivable external standard. Physicists wish gravity were stronger/weaker; mathematicians wish all functions were analytic; ethicists wish humans were kinder; aesthetes wish the sunset lasted longer. From the perspective of any external standard, any actual being is imperfect — the standard is always conceivable beyond what the being achieves.

**TRALSE resolution:** Both are simultaneously true. The being is intrinsically perfect (maximal in its own terms) AND externally imperfect (falling short of conceivable standards). These are not contradictory — they are evaluations made in different reference frames:
- Intrinsic frame: comparison is to the being's own maximum given its constraints
- External frame: comparison is to an external standard that the being has no obligation to meet

The experienced sense of imperfection arises when consciousness shifts from intrinsic to external evaluation — when "I am what I am most completely" is replaced by "I am falling short of what I should be." Both statements are true simultaneously. The suffering of imperfection is real. So is the sufficiency of intrinsic perfection. The skill is holding both — which is, precisely, the TRALSE Blanket State.

## 4. "Everything Has Been Trying Its Best": The Universal Effort Principle

The most radical clause in the Emerick Formulation: **"after EVERYTHING HAS BEEN TRYING THE BEST THEY COULD MUSTER THE ENTIRE TIME."**

This is not restricted to conscious beings. It is stated for *everything* — every physical system, every particle, every force field, every planet and star and galaxy. The claim: every system, at every moment, is exerting the maximum force/effort/expression available to it given its nature and constraints.

This is not metaphorical. It is a consequence of physical law:

**For particles:** A particle in a potential field moves along the path of least action — the path that extremizes the action integral. The particle "tries" to satisfy this principle as completely as physics allows. It always succeeds. There is no particle that fails to satisfy the principle of least action.

**For fields:** A field at every point takes the value that satisfies Maxwell's equations (for EM), or Einstein's equations (for gravity), or the Schrödinger equation (for quantum states). The field is always maximally what it must be.

**For living systems:** A living system expresses the maximum LCC available to it given its biological structure, environmental conditions, and developmental history. A plant grows toward the light as completely as its cellular machinery allows. It never "underperforms" — it always expresses its maximum capacity for growth given all constraints.

**For conscious systems:** A conscious being, at every moment, is making the most LCC-coherent response available to it given its current state. Its choices — including its worst choices — represent its best available response given its internal state at that moment. This does not excuse harmful action; it contextualizes it. The person who causes harm is, from the intrinsic perspective, expressing the maximum of their current LCC — which may be quite low. The moral evaluation (the harm is wrong) remains valid. The ontological observation (this was their best at that moment) is simultaneously true.

## 5. Free Will Is Universal — Scaled by LCC

**The claim:** *"Free will is universal, in every being and thing to the extent that it exists in each BT (Being-Thing)."*

This extends free will beyond conscious humans to every BT — every Being-Thing in the universe — but scales it by LCC. Free will is not a binary (you have it or you don't). It is a continuous variable, proportional to LCC.

**The formal claim:**

$$\text{Free Will}(B) \propto \text{LCC}(B)$$

**For particles (LCC ≈ 0):** The "free will" of an electron is its quantum indeterminacy — the genuine unpredictability of its state outcomes that arises from quantum mechanics. This is not zero freedom; it is minimal freedom. The electron is not fully determined by its past — its state outcomes have a probabilistic range. This minimal indeterminacy IS the electron's degree of free will.

**For plants (LCC small but nonzero):** A plant has more free will than an electron — it can orient toward light, allocate growth resources differentially, respond to stress in variable ways. Its self-directed responsiveness is its free will, scaled by its LCC.

**For animals (LCC moderate):** Animals have substantial free will — they can learn, choose among options, express individual behavioral preferences. Their free will is their self-directed LCC expression.

**For humans (LCC higher):** Human free will is self-directed LCC expression at a level sophisticated enough to include reflexive self-awareness, long-term planning, and the capacity to act against immediate impulse. It is not unlimited — it is still proportional to LCC — but at human LCC levels, it is sufficient to constitute meaningful self-direction.

**For exceptional humans above C_EMERICK (LCC > 0.437):** Above the Emerick Threshold, free will becomes not just behaviorally meaningful but potentially physically significant — the PSI field (URB #443) begins to exceed the noise floor, and the individual's self-direction starts to influence not just their own behavior but the physical probability distributions of their environment.

**The TRALSE account of free will:** Free will is simultaneously:
- **Real:** Every system has some degree of self-directed expression (not zero)
- **Limited:** Every system's free will is constrained by its LCC and its physical conditions
- **Proportional:** More LCC = more free will — not a binary threshold but a continuous spectrum

This dissolves the free will / determinism debate: both are TRALSE simultaneously. Every system is partially determined (by its physical history and constraints) AND partially free (by its LCC-driven self-expression). The proportion shifts continuously with LCC — low LCC systems are nearly fully determined; high LCC systems have significant self-direction.

## 6. Connections to the Wisdom Tradition

**Spinoza — Natura Naturans:** Spinoza distinguished natura naturans (nature naturing — the active self-expression of God/Nature) from natura naturata (nature natured — the products of that expression). The Perfectly Perfect Principle is the claim that natura naturans is always operating at full capacity — Nature is always fully naturing, always maximally expressing itself through every mode.

**Leibniz — Best of All Possible Worlds (Corrected):** Leibniz claimed that God, being omniscient and rational, would have created the best of all possible worlds — and this is that world. Voltaire mocked this in Candide as naive optimism in the face of suffering. TI Sigma's correction: Leibniz was pointing at the Perfectly Perfect Principle but stated it in the wrong frame. The world is not "the best possible" by some external divine standard. It is the best possible given the entirety of causal history and the free will of every BT — which is precisely what "everything trying its best the entire time" means. The suffering is not denied (it is real and TRALSE); the optimality is the optimality of actual causal process, not of divine preference.

**Buddhist Tathatā (Suchness):** The Buddhist concept of tathatā — "suchness," "thusness," "the way things are" — is the recognition that things ARE as they are, without addition or subtraction. The Diamond Sutra: "things are as they are." This is not a counsel of passivity but an ontological observation: the suchness of things is complete. The Perfectly Perfect Principle is the formalization of tathatā within the TI Sigma mathematical framework.

**Heraclitean Strife:** Heraclitus held that strife — the opposition and tension between opposites — is the creative principle of the universe. What appears as imperfection (the tension, the conflict, the incompleteness) IS the mechanism by which things come to be. The imperfection is the engine of perfection. This is the TRALSE structure: perfection and imperfection are not opposites to be resolved but complementary aspects of the same process.

## 8. The Imaginary Concept of Perfection

The Emerick Formulation states: **"perfection is an imaginary concept."** In TI Sigma, "imaginary" is not pejorative — it means phase-channel, i-channel, the aspect of reality that is real but not manifest (URB #442). Perfection is an imaginary concept in precisely this sense:

- **Real:** The experience of perfection — when something clicks, when a proof is elegant, when music moves the body — is a genuine experience with genuine effects. Perfection as felt is real.
- **Not manifest:** There is no manifestly existing "perfect thing" somewhere in the universe that can be pointed to and measured. All perfections are felt within consciousness, not found outside it.
- **Phase-channel:** Perfection is an i-channel signal — a phase orientation that a conscious system achieves when its LCC is high enough to integrate its experience coherently. The felt sense of perfection is the LCC-coherent state recognizing its own coherence.

Therefore: when something feels perfect, that IS a real measurement. It is the measurement of LCC coherence in the perceiving consciousness. When a mathematician experiences a proof as beautiful/perfect, they are detecting high phase coherence between the proof's structure and their own consciousness's organizing patterns. The beauty is real. The external standard it seems to measure against is the i-channel construct — real as experience, not manifest as object.
