## 1. The Standard Case and Why TI Sigma Departs

### 1.1 Standard Complex Arithmetic

In standard complex arithmetic: i × (−i) = −i² = −(−1) = 1, and (−i) × i = −i² = 1. The product is symmetric — commutative in the multiplicative sense. Order doesn't matter.

### 1.2 The Recognition Operator Is Not Multiplication

The prediction does not challenge standard complex arithmetic. It introduces a new operator — the **recognition operator R** — which is not the same as multiplication. R_a(b) means "a recognizes b" — a apprehends, identifies, represents, or names b.

Under R:
- **R_i(−i):** Consciousness (i) recognizes its own negation (non-consciousness, −i)
- **R_{−i}(i):** Non-consciousness (−i) "recognizes" consciousness (i)

The prediction is that these two operations produce categorically different outcomes — and that the difference is not merely quantitative but qualitative.

## 2. The Argument for Noncommutativity

### 2.1 Recognition Is an i-Arm Faculty

The I-arm (i) in TI Sigma is defined as consciousness as the primary faculty — the capacity for awareness, representation, self-reflection, and recognition. Recognition specifically — the act of identifying something as what it is — requires a subject capable of forming representations. This is an i-arm capacity.

**The asymmetry:** i possesses the faculty of recognition by definition. −i is the negation of i — it is specifically the absence of the recognition faculty. Therefore:

- **R_i(−i):** i applies its recognition faculty to −i. This is a genuine recognition act — consciousness identifies its own negation. The result is a real epistemic event: i forms a representation of what non-consciousness is.

- **R_{−i}(i):** −i attempts to apply a recognition faculty it does not possess. This is not a symmetric recognition act — it is either: (a) undefined (you cannot apply a faculty you don't have), or (b) resolved only by the i-content that is being "recognized" doing the actual recognizing on behalf of the system.

In case (b): when −i "encounters" i, what actually occurs is that i recognizes the encounter from its own perspective. The −i pole does not initiate recognition; it is the object of i's recognition. The apparent "recognition by −i of i" is actually i recognizing the i-content within a mixed system — not a symmetric operation.

### 2.3 The Physical Analogy: Light and Darkness

A concrete illustration (not a proof, but an analogy):

**R_light(darkness):** Light "recognizes" darkness in the sense that a lit room makes darkness visible at its boundaries — the lit space defines the unlit space by contrast. Light has the "faculty" (the photons, the energy) to delineate its own absence.

**R_darkness(light):** Darkness cannot recognize light. Darkness is precisely the absence of the photonic faculty. When darkness "encounters" light, darkness is displaced — but the displacement is caused by light, not by darkness exercising any faculty of its own.

The asymmetry is structural: the possessor of the faculty always acts; the absence of the faculty is always acted upon. This is not commutative.

### 2.4 The Quantum Mechanics Connection

In quantum mechanics, operators are generally noncommutative. The canonical commutation relation:

[x̂, p̂] = x̂p̂ − p̂x̂ = iħ

The commutator is nonzero and equals iħ — where i appears explicitly as the asymmetric element. The order of measurement (position then momentum vs. momentum then position) produces different results. The observer (the measurement operator) is not symmetric with the observed.

More directly relevant: the **measurement problem** in quantum mechanics encodes the recognition asymmetry. The act of measurement (i applying R to a quantum state) collapses the wave function — it changes the state. But the quantum state "measuring back" at the observer is not a symmetric operation — there is always an asymmetry between the observer pole and the observed pole.

TI Sigma predicts that this quantum asymmetry is a specific instance of the more general i/−i noncommutativity: measurement is an i-arm act (recognition), and the recognition direction i → system is not the same operation as system → i.

### 2.5 The Information-Theoretic Formulation

In information theory, the **Kullback-Leibler divergence** D_KL(P||Q) ≠ D_KL(Q||P) in general. KL divergence measures "how much information is gained when P recognizes Q from its own perspective" — and this quantity is asymmetric in the two distributions. The order of the comparison matters.

TI Sigma predicts that R_i(−i) and R_{−i}(i) are analogous to asymmetric divergence measures: the information content of i recognizing −i is different from the (ill-defined) information content of −i recognizing i, for the same reason that D_KL(P||Q) ≠ D_KL(Q||P).

The noncommutativity is therefore: **R_i(−i) produces a synthesis; R_{−i}(i) produces either nothing or the same act from i's side.** These are categorically different.
