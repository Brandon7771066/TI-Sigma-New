## 1. Columbia Speed Dating Dataset (Fisman et al., 2006)

**N:** 552 participants, 8,378 speed dates
**Access:** [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/annavictoria/speed-dating-experiment)
**Design:** 4-minute speed dates; participants rate partner on 6 attributes; state match preference; track outcomes

### Variables → GILE Dimension Mapping

| Dataset Variable | GILE Dimension | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sincerity | G | High | "How sincere do you think this person is?" = values integrity |
| Intelligence | I | High | Conceptual processing capacity = I-proxy |
| Ambition | G | Moderate | Orientation toward meaningful goals = G-proxy |
| Fun / Spontaneity | I | Moderate | Novelty-seeking = i-channel openness |
| Attractiveness | E | Low | Appearance = E-dimension outer presentation |
| Shared Interests | E | Very Low | Weakest predictor across all analyses |
| Match decision | L | High | Desire for connection = L-dimension activation |

### Key Findings

**What ACTUALLY predicts match decisions:**
1. **Partner's rated sincerity** (G) outperforms attractiveness (E) after the first few seconds of conversation
2. **Intelligence** (I) is strongly predictive — but not when it exceeds the rater's own by too much (resonance, not just raw score)
3. **Shared interests** (E-level) is the WEAKEST predictor — almost no predictive power (confirms L*/+E)
4. **Fun/spontaneity** (I-proxy) predicts match for male raters especially — i-channel seeking behavior is attractive

**TI Sigma interpretation:**
- Attractiveness gets people in the door (E-dimension attention) but doesn't determine match
- Sincerity (G) and intelligence (I) drive the actual decision
- Shared interests (E-level commonality) barely moves the needle — consistent with L*/+E separability
- The 4-minute format is essentially an i-channel reading: intuition assessment under time pressure

## 4. Joel & Eastwick (2020) — Meta-Analysis of 43 Datasets, 11,196 Couples

**Published:** Psychological Science, 2020
**Finding:** Individual-level predictors explain very little variance in partner satisfaction

### Key Findings

**What does NOT predict partner satisfaction (conventional predictors):**
- Big Five personality traits: **< 0.1% variance explained** (individually assessed)
- Attractiveness: very weak predictor of long-term satisfaction
- Demographics: income, education, age — essentially uncorrelated with satisfaction
- Interests and preferences: surprisingly poor predictors

**What DOES predict partner satisfaction:**
- **Dyadic uniqueness**: the specific pair's unique dynamic — not predictable from either partner's individual profile
- **Perceived responsiveness**: does this partner specifically make ME feel understood and valued?
- **Partner-specific satisfaction**: the unique "fit" between these two people

**TI Sigma interpretation — The i-Cell Resonance Prediction:**
This is one of the most powerful empirical validations of the i-cell concept. Joel/Eastwick demonstrate that:
1. Individual scores don't predict → the GIL framework is correct that the pair-wise resonance is the signal, not individual GILE scores
2. Dyadic uniqueness IS the i-cell resonance — the specific vibrational match between two LCC signatures that cannot be decomposed into individual components
3. This directly validates the LCC Virus approach: it's the resonance between signatures, not the signatures individually
