## 2. The four "asymmetric standards #69" items I would not gloss over

### 2.1 Glucose 105 mg/dL with A1c 5.3% — what this combination actually means

Fasting glucose is a single morning snapshot; A1c is the ~3-month integrated average. A 105 mg/dL fasting value with a 5.3% A1c is *not* prediabetes — it is **transient fasting hyperglycemia on a background of excellent long-term glycation control**. Likely contributors here, in order of probability:

1. **Seroquel (quetiapine).** Quetiapine is one of the atypical antipsychotics most associated with elevated fasting glucose, independent of weight changes. Your dose just dropped 25% (5/9), so this number should improve on the next draw.
2. **Morning cortisol bump on a fasting draw,** especially if the breathwork practice has activated your sympathetic axis recently. Hyperventilation-based breathwork transiently raises cortisol and catecholamines, both of which raise fasting glucose.
3. **Stress / sleep variance** on the night before draw.

GP's read (A1c is the answer, not the fasting glucose) is the conventional medicine call here and is correct. **TG/HDL 0.6** is independent corroboration of intact insulin sensitivity — that ratio is one of the cleanest non-A1c markers and it is excellent.

### 2.2 Lithium 0.3 mmol/L — the dose doubling is the point

Therapeutic range for bipolar I maintenance is 0.6–1.0 mmol/L (acute mania: 0.8–1.2). At 0.3 the May 7 result is **half of the lower therapeutic threshold**, which is the clean clinical justification for the 5/11 dose doubling. Half-life of lithium is ~24 h, so steady-state at 600 mg is reached around day 4–5; by the 5/14 Biowell scan you were ~3 days in (approaching but not at steady state).

**Next draw should be timed ≥5 days after the dose change** to read the new steady-state level. Target: 0.6–0.9 mmol/L. **Above 1.2 = tox watch (tremor, GI, confusion).**

### 2.3 Free T4 at the very top of the normal range (1.8/1.8)

TSH 1.27 is comfortably mid-range, so this is not hyperthyroid. But Free T4 sitting exactly at the upper limit is worth a quiet flag for two reasons:

- Lithium *suppresses* thyroid function over time (anti-thyroid effect). At 300 mg/day baseline you were running on the warm side of normal. At 600 mg/day this may correct toward the middle of the range, which would be fine. If TSH starts climbing on the next panel, that's the early signal for lithium-induced hypothyroidism.
- Hyperventilatory breathwork can transiently up-regulate sympathetic tone, which can nudge thyroid hormone conversion. Probably contributing, probably small.

**Action:** include TSH + Free T4 + Free T3 on the next Quest panel.

### 2.4 WBC 3.9 — 0.1 above the floor

Seroquel/quetiapine can suppress WBC count modestly. You sit at 3.9 (floor = 3.8). This is *not* a flag — you're inside the range — but it is the lowest-margin in-range number on the panel. The 5/9 Seroquel drop (400 → 300) should give this room to climb on the next draw. If it instead drops below 3.8, that is a real signal worth a follow-up (differential, neutrophil count).

## 4. What this means for the daily habit stack

**Keep all three:**

- **KAP daily** — pairs cleanly with the Nervous-system improvement and the Psychology / Regime lifestyle climbs.
- **Singing daily** — vagal-tone training, no downside in this panel.
- **Breathwork (hyperventilation + breath-holds), 30 min/day** — the most direct ANS-shifter in your stack. Two cautions:
  1. *Avoid doing it within 2 hours of a fasting blood draw* — the cortisol/catecholamine spike will artificially elevate fasting glucose and may make Free T4 look hotter than baseline.
  2. *Watch for hand/lip tingling, dizziness, or arrhythmia.* Long hyperventilation bouts can drop CO₂ enough to cause respiratory alkalosis. If those symptoms appear, shorten the hyperventilation phase and lengthen the breath-hold phase.

**One thing to add (cheap, high-leverage):** track resting HR + HRV before and after each breathwork session via the Polar H10 you already have. That gives you a per-session quantitative readout instead of relying on quarterly Biowell + Quest snapshots.
