## 1. The three real theorems behind "i completes itself"

Let z̄ denote the complex conjugate (reflection across the real axis — the "mirror").

- **Theorem C (mirror product = unity).** i · (−i) = −i² = −(−1) = **1**. More generally, for any z, z · z̄ = |z|² ∈ ℝ≥0. The imaginary unit, multiplied by its **mirror image** −i, returns the **multiplicative identity 1** — the canonical "whole." *(Proof: direct; |z|² = a²+b² for z=a+bi.)*
- **Theorem D (cyclic closure to unity).** i is a **primitive 4th root of unity**: the cyclic group ⟨i⟩ = {i, −1, −i, 1} ≅ ℤ/4ℤ, and **i⁴ = 1**. Iterating the deficit (i → i² = −1 → i³ = −i → i⁴ = 1) **closes back to unity in exactly four steps**. *(Proof: i²=−1, so i⁴=(i²)²=1; order is 4 because i,i²,i³≠1.)*
- **Theorem E (the tralse pair: annihilate additively, complete multiplicatively).** The two roots of x²+1=0 are the conjugate pair {i, −i}. They satisfy i + (−i) = **0** (return to the origin / nothing) and i · (−i) = **1** (return to unity). *(Proof: Vieta — sum = −(coeff of x)/1 = 0, product = constant/1 = 1.)* By the Galois automorphism of ℂ/ℝ (conjugation), the two roots are **algebraically indistinguishable** — there is no internal fact selecting which one is "the" i (the Benacerraf point from B171). The pair is a genuine *this-and-its-mirror* with no privileged member.

**Defining self-relation (already in B171, restated for contrast).** i · i = i² = **−1**: self-*multiplication* (not the mirror) returns the very −1 that had **no real root** — i's squaring **re-presents its own defining deficit** on the real line.

## 3. What this proves and what it does NOT (#69, both-ways)

The equations (C, D, E, and i²=−1) are **theorems**. The identification of conjugation with "self-recognition," of multiplication with "reconciliation," and of 1 with "completion/wholeness" is **interpretation (NAD-1)** — faithful, on the framework's view, but **not a corollary**. Four rails keep it honest:

1. **Two different operations — do not conflate them.** "Multiplies with itself" (i·i = **−1**) and "sees −i in the mirror" (i·(−i) = **+1**) are **distinct operations with distinct results**. The unity/completion reading rests specifically on the **conjugate** product (Theorem C), not on the plain square. The honest unifying statement is weaker and truer: *every* self-interaction of i — square, mirror-product, or 4-cycle — **collapses the imaginary back onto the real line**; squaring yields the defining deficit (−1), the mirror-product and the 4-cycle yield unity (+1). Both directions are real; they are not the same fact.
2. **z·z̄ = |z|² is a general norm, not unique to i.** *Every* complex number completes with its mirror to a nonnegative real — so mirror-completion is generic to ℂ, not special to i. (Cyclic closure is rarer: only unit-modulus numbers whose *argument is a rational multiple of 2π* are roots of unity; a generic unit number, at an irrational angle, never returns to 1. i is one of the few that closes — the primitive 4th root.) i is thus the **canonical / generating** instance (unit modulus, primitive 4th root, the defining √−1), **not the only** self-completing element. The claim is "i is the *paradigm* of self-reconciling deficit," never "only i does this."
3. **Still an interpretation, not an upgrade to proof.** IDR-2 strengthens the **resonance / aesthetic (GILE-E Elegance)** case that i *is* tralseness; it does **not** convert the i↔tralse identity into a theorem. B171's rails stand unchanged: the deficit is **ℝ-relative** (within ℂ, i is coherent, consistent, and *completes* ℝ via the FTA), and **an empty interpretive seat is not a warrant** (the math being beautiful does not make the metaphysical reading true).
4. **"Completes itself" ≠ "was never deficient."** The completion is *operational* (a product, a cycle), not a retraction of Theorems A/B (B171): i still has no real value and no signed order. The picture is *deficit → self-reconciliation → operational wholeness*, which is precisely the tralse arc (TRG-1: the canvas paints itself), **not** a proof that i was complete all along.
