## 3. The structural argument (why base rates are rejected, not merely de-emphasized)

### 3.1 Base rates presume a stable substrate that TI Sigma denies

Bayesian base rates rest on the assumption that the *frequency of occurrence* over some reference class is a real, stable feature of the world that licenses prior-probability assignment. TI Sigma's GM-Network probability-memory is **plastic and highly variable**: it is not a stable substrate. What looks like a "base rate" is a snapshot of GM-Network state that may have already shifted by the time the agent acts on it.

### 3.2 Per-event independent calculation defeats accumulation-based default

If every event is calculated "each and every time rather independently in the ever-vanishing present moment," then accumulated-frequency-as-default is structurally not the operative input. The present moment computes from C1-C4 inputs; it does not roll a historical-frequency prior forward as a separate variable.

### 3.3 Retroactive effects defeat strict-forward temporal flow assumption

Bayesian updating assumes evidence flows forward in time and updates posterior from prior. If retroactive effects (C2) are real, then "prior" and "posterior" are not strictly ordered, and the Bayesian flow chart does not apply as written.

### 3.4 LCC nonlocal correlations defeat locality assumption

Bayesian models with local-evidence-conditioning fail when nonlocal correlations are operative. C4 LCC inputs cannot be captured by a base-rate prior over local observables.

### 3.5 #69 honest counter-statement

PM-1 is NOT the claim that Bayesian methods are useless in narrow operational contexts. It is the claim that **at the foundational ontological level, TI Sigma does not posit base rates as a real variable in event calculation.** Local Bayesian fits to data can still be empirically useful as approximations; they are not foundational.
