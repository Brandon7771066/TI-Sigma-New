## Part 4: EAR Step 3 — Attempt Collapse

### 4.1 Cross-Framework Mergers

#### Merger 1: Coherence ↔ Contradiction Ratio → COHERENCE (kept, Four C's version is stricter)

**Four C's Coherence**: Claims must be mutually SUPPORTING, not just non-contradictory
**HEM D2 Contradiction Ratio**: Measures absence of internal contradiction

**EAR Verdict**: COLLAPSE into **Coherence**. The Four C's version subsumes HEM D2 because mutual support is a stricter criterion than non-contradiction. Anything that satisfies Coherence automatically satisfies low-Contradiction. The reverse is not true.

*Post-merge HEM: D1, D3, D4, D5, D6 remain*
*Post-merge Four C's: Coherence (strengthened by HEM data sources), Concreteness, Completeness, Continuity*

#### Merger 2: Concreteness ↔ Complexity — Do they collapse?

**Four C's Concreteness**: Can you form a clear understanding? Is it operationalizable?
**HEM D1 Complexity (PAS)**: How many interacting parts does the phenomenon have?

**EAR Verdict**: NO COLLAPSE. High complexity with low concreteness is possible (lots of data, poorly understood). High concreteness with low complexity is possible (a simple truth, clearly stated). They measure genuinely different things. KEEP BOTH.

#### Merger 3: Completeness × Continuity → Info Footprint?

This is the user's key insight: **Completeness + Continuity strongly predicts Info Footprint (D3)**.

**Four C's Completeness**: All dimensions addressed, limitations stated honestly
**Four C's Continuity**: Connected to past inquiry and future directions
**HEM D3 Info Footprint (AMI)**: Meaningful connections between subsystems

**Analysis**: If you have complete data (Completeness) AND it's connected across time (Continuity), you are LIKELY to have rich meaningful connections (high AMI/Info Footprint). The prediction runs primarily in one direction:

```
High Completeness + High Continuity → High Info Footprint (STRONGLY PREDICTED but not strictly necessary — 
    a complete, continuous dataset might still have low meaningful connections if subsystems are isolated)
High Info Footprint → High Completeness (NOT NECESSARILY — rich connections don't guarantee gaps are acknowledged)
High Info Footprint → High Continuity (NOT NECESSARILY — snapshot richness ≠ temporal trajectory)
```

**EAR Verdict**: STRONG CORRELATION, NOT STRICT DERIVATION. Completeness × Continuity strongly predicts Info Footprint in practice, but Info Footprint (AMI) can exist independently — a highly influential but incomplete dataset could have high AMI. The relationship is:
- Completeness + Continuity → strong Info Footprint predictor
- BUT Info Footprint retains independent information: a partial network with high influence is meaningful even if incomplete

**Result**: Keep all three. Note that Completeness × Continuity STRONGLY CORRELATES with Info Footprint, making D3 partially redundant when both Four C's are scored. D3 is best understood as an overlapping metric that adds value when Completeness or Continuity data is unavailable.

#### Merger 4: Completeness + Continuity → Relational Meaning (D4)?

The user's second insight: Completeness + Continuity predicts Relational Meaning "to some degree."

**Analysis**: If your data is complete (captures all relational dimensions) AND continuous (tracks relationships over time), you will naturally capture relational meaning. But the prediction is weaker:

```
High Completeness + High Continuity → SOME Relational Meaning (LIKELY but not NECESSARY)
High Relational Meaning → ??? (Relational meaning can be FELT without being complete or continuous)
```

**EAR Verdict**: WEAK OVERLAP. Relational Meaning is phenomenological — it captures whether love/connection was EXPERIENCED. Completeness + Continuity can capture whether that experience was DOCUMENTED. But documentation ≠ experience. Someone could have deep relational meaning in a session with terrible data quality.

**Result**: D4 Relational Meaning SURVIVES as independent. It cannot be derived from Four C's.
