## 2. Background — What "Life Reviews" Refer To

Life Reviews are a phenomenological category most famously documented in:

- **Near-death experience (NDE) literature** — typically 10-25% of NDEs include a "life review" component
- **End-of-life experience research** — including hospice studies, deathbed-vision literature
- **Cardiac-arrest survivor reports** — Pim van Lommel's *The Lancet* 2001 prospective study and follow-ups
- **Some traditional contemplative traditions** describing review processes at death or in deep meditation

The recurring features of Life Reviews:

1. **Panoramic recall** — the experiencer reports access to memories far beyond ordinary recall capacity
2. **Multi-perspective experience** — the experiencer reports feeling not just their own emotions during reviewed events but also the emotions of others present in those events
3. **Empathic completeness** — a sense that the full causal-emotional weight of one's actions on others is now experientially accessible
4. **Time-distortion** — the entire review feels nearly instantaneous from one perspective, fully detailed from another
5. **No-judgment / illuminating quality** — described as informational/educational rather than punitive

Feature **#2 (multi-perspective experience)** is the framework-relevant anchor.

The experiencer reports accessing the perspectives, thoughts, and emotions of OTHER participants in their life events — content that, by ordinary materialist memory storage assumptions, should not be retrievable because it was never stored in the experiencer's brain.

**The framework prediction is therefore independently corroborated** by a phenomenological category documented across cultures, traditions, and prospective medical studies.
