## Video 1: "What if True and False Weren't the Only Options?"

**Hook (0:00–0:15):** "Here's a question that breaks most logic textbooks: is the statement *I love this person* true or false? You probably know the answer isn't one or the other. So why do we still teach reasoning as if every claim has to be one or the other? In the next four minutes, I'll show you a simple fix that's been hiding in plain sight for 2,500 years."

**Setup (0:15–0:45):** Aristotle gave us bivalence — every well-formed statement is true or false. It works great for arithmetic and computer chips. It does *not* work for "I love this person," "this career is right," "the treatment is working," or "I'm free." These statements aren't broken — *the logic we use to evaluate them is*.

**Core idea (0:45–2:45):** TI Sigma — Tralse Informationalism — adds two more base truth values to the classical two:
1. **True** — the statement is fully aligned with reality
2. **False** — the statement is fully misaligned with reality
3. **Indeterminate (I)** — the statement is *stably balanced* between true and false in a way that more information will not resolve. This is the truth value of "I love this person" today, before more lived experience clarifies it.
4. **Double Tralse (DT)** — the statement is structurally incoherent: it both is and is not what it claims. Self-defeating statements live here. We discard these rather than reasoning with them.

The headline result: most of human life happens in the **Indeterminate zone**. That's not a bug. That's where free will operates.

**Demo (2:45–3:45):** Take a real choice — should I take this job? Classical logic says: collect more facts, decide true (take it) or false (don't). TI Sigma says: this is genuinely Indeterminate, and the right move is a *resolution procedure* called Myrion Resolution that converges on the answer through structured iteration — or honestly returns "still Indeterminate, here's what you'd need to learn for it to resolve."

## Video 2: "The Tralse: A Universal 4th Truth-Value That Changes How You Think About Honesty"

**Hook (0:00–0:15):** "Here's the most embarrassing tool in classical logic: the contradiction. *This statement is false.* If it's true, it's false. If it's false, it's true. Most logicians treat it like a glitch. TI Sigma treats it like a fundamental category — and that changes a lot."

**Setup (0:15–0:45):** Self-referential paradoxes have plagued logic for millennia. Russell's paradox broke set theory. Gödel's theorems exploited self-reference to prove arithmetic can't fully describe itself. Most responses: treat paradox as failure, restrict the language, hope the contradictions go away.

**Core idea (0:45–2:30):** TI Sigma takes a different route. **Double Tralse (DT)** is the fourth base truth value — defined formally as a statement *P* such that τ(*P*) ∧ ¬τ(*P*). Translation: the statement carries a structural imperfection (the "Tralse-quality") in two opposing directions simultaneously.

The crucial move: instead of ignoring DT, *we name it and discard it from the reasoning process*. A DT statement is structurally incoherent — it should not be plugged into further inferences. This is brutal honesty applied to logic itself.

**Demo (2:30–3:30):** Real-world DTs: *"I always lie."* *"This sentence has no meaning."* *"I am being completely honest about being completely dishonest."* These aren't deep — they're broken. Naming them as DT lets us move on instead of pretending we've discovered something profound.

The catalogue of *Meta-Truths* layered on top of base-4 (twelve in `urb_608` MR catalogue) handles cases like "the question itself is moot" — without pretending these are simple True/False answers.

## Video 4: "GILE — Four Dimensions for Measuring Anything That Matters"

**Hook (0:00–0:15):** "Most decisions in your life come down to one question: *is this good?* And most frameworks answer that question really badly. They use one dimension when they need four."

**Setup (0:15–0:45):** Pleasure-pain. Right-wrong. Profitable-unprofitable. All single-axis. None of them capture what you actually mean when you ask "is this good for me?" GILE is the four-dimensional compass that does.

**Core idea (0:45–3:00):** **GILE** = **G**oodness, **I**ntuition, **L**ove, **E**nvironment. Each dimension scores from −3 to +2 (we'll explain the asymmetry in a future video). Composite is the average.

- **G — Goodness:** measured by *outcomes*, not intentions. Did the action actually help or harm?
- **I — Intuition:** measured by *felt rightness*. Does this feel aligned, or dissonant?
- **L — Love:** measured by *connection vs separation*. Does this unite or divide?
- **E — Environment:** measured by *contextual fit*. Right tool, right time, right place?

Each dimension catches what the others miss. Goodness without Intuition is robotic; Intuition without Goodness is rationalized; Love without Environment is sentimental; Environment without Love is calculating.

**Demo (3:00–4:00):**
- Eating a healthy meal: G+1.5, I+1.0, L+0.5, E+1.5 → composite +1.1 (very good)
- Lying to a friend: G−1.5, I−2.0, L−2.0, E−1.0 → composite −1.6 (bad)
- Difficult honest conversation: G+1.0, I+1.5, L+1.0, E+1.0 → composite +1.1 (good — even though uncomfortable)

**Key insight from the last example:** high GILE doesn't mean comfortable. GILE measures alignment with reality, not pleasant feelings.

**Core idea (0:45–2:45):** The **Tralse-Joule (TJ)** is defined:

`TJ = τ(s) × δ(MR)`

Where:
- **τ(s)** is the truth-degree of the proposition the intention targets
- **δ(MR)** is the depth of Myrion Resolution applied to it

`TJ = √(τ² + δ² + α²)` where α = affective-projection

This extension is consistent with the ABC dissolution result: cognitive (τ), behavioral (δ), and affective (α) are projections of one underlying intentional state, not separate categories. The reformulation requires Brandon-authority to canonize.
