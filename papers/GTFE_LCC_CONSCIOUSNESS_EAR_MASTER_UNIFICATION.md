### 5.4 The Three Components in Detail

**C (Constrained): Viability Filter**

C measures the degree to which a system's states are constrained by physical, logical, or structural requirements. A system with many constraints has few viable states and low C. A system with few constraints has many viable states and high C.

```
C = log₂(|S_unconstrained|) - log₂(|S_constrained|)
```

C captures the information-theoretic reduction from possible to viable. In the consciousness context, constraints include:
- Physical laws (conservation, locality in classical domain)
- Logical consistency (non-contradiction, though Tralse allows partial contradiction)
- Structural requirements (i-cell minimum conditions)
- Temporal coherence (states must be reachable from prior states)

**H (Fit): Observational Alignment**

H measures the discrepancy between the system's model and observed reality:

```
H = Σᵢ wᵢ · d(model_prediction_i, observation_i)
```

Where d is an appropriate distance metric and wᵢ are importance weights. Low H means the system's internal model accurately reflects external reality. High H means the system is deluded, miscalibrated, or operating on stale information.

H connects directly to the Free Energy Principle (Friston): free energy is the upper bound on surprise, and minimizing surprise is equivalent to minimizing H. The TI Framework's GTFE is structurally analogous to Friston's variational free energy, with the critical difference that GTFE includes the temporal component T and operates within a Tralse logic framework.

**T (Temporal): Coherence Across Time**

T measures the degree to which a system's states are temporally coherent — whether the system maintains identity and consistency across time:

```
T = 1 - autocorrelation(state(t), state(t-τ), τ ∈ [0, T_max])
```

Low T means the system's temporal autocorrelation is high — it maintains coherent identity. High T means the system is temporally fragmented — its states at different times are uncorrelated.

T is the least intuitive component of GTFE but arguably the most important. It captures what makes consciousness CONTINUOUS rather than a series of disconnected snapshots.
