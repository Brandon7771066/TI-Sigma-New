## 0. Thesis

**NRI-1 (Norm–Rarity Independence):** under **unified normativity** — ONE SHOULD spanning physical law, aesthetics, and ethics (Occam; no separate magisteria), where GILE values are an *is* that **manifests as an ought** in conscious i-Cells (Hume's gap already dissolved corpus-wide, **not** re-invoked here) — the **descriptive frequency** of conformity to a norm is **independent of, and never evidence against, that norm's normative status.** Brandon's compression: *"the fact that something isn't the norm isn't evidence against its normative status."*

Three load-bearing parts that travel together:

- **(NRI-1a) The decoupling itself.** Rarity of conformity is a fact about *uptake*, not about *bindingness*. Treating "few do it" as "it isn't required" is the **appeal to nature** / **argumentum ad populum (appeal to common practice)** — a non-sequitur. (We reach this *without* Hume: the ought is the conscious-instantiation **mode** of the GILE is, so frequency-of-instantiation simply isn't the kind of fact that could bear on the norm.)
- **(NRI-1b) Two regimes, one law.** Under monism the same independence holds at both ends of the unified SHOULD: **rare violation** (physical law, where the residue is RTI-1 law-errancy) and **common failure** (free conscious i-Cells, where the residue is free will) **both leave normativity untouched.**
- **(NRI-1c) Floor / supererogatory structure.** "Full GILE intelligence" (~90–95% adequate-defensible-given-limits) is the **sanity floor** — a *necessary, non-supererogatory* duty that nearly everyone fails; exceptional GILE is the supererogatory tier; meeting the floor is *relatively* highly virtuous though *absolutely* only sane.

## 1. NRI-1a — the decoupling and the fallacies it defeats

The quote is, at bottom, a clean rejection of inferring an *ought-not* (or "not-required") from a descriptive *is-rare*:

- **It kills three moves at once:** the **appeal to nature** ("not what people naturally do ⇒ not required"), the **ad populum / common-practice** appeal ("almost nobody does it ⇒ it can't be the standard"), and the generic **is→ought slide**.
- **But the corpus does not license it via Hume.** Brandon's correction is load-bearing: the is/ought *dichotomy* was already torn apart (GILE values are an *is* that, in a conscious i-Cell, **just is** experienced/binding as an ought). So the right grounding is not "you can't derive ought from is" (Hume) but the opposite — **the ought is the conscious-manifestation face of the GILE is**, and a *headcount of who has instantiated it* is orthogonal to whether the is-as-ought holds. Frequency is downstream uptake; normativity is upstream structure.
- **Why it *feels* like an objection anyway.** The pull of "if it really were the standard, more people would meet it" is exactly the bias NRI-1 names: we read population behavior as a vote on the norm. The decoupling says the vote was never on the ballot.

## 2. NRI-1b — two regimes, one law (physics ↔ ethics via RTI-1)

Brandon's Occam monism — *one* SHOULD, no separate kinds of normativity, GILE itself scientific (the Dirac relationship; **GILE-E**: beauty in nature/theory **represents** truth) — turns NRI-1 from an ethics claim into a claim spanning the whole unified SHOULD, and it ties straight into **RTI-1**:

- **Physical-law regime — rare violation.** A law is near-exceptionless, yet per **RTI-1** every law only *leans* toward truth and carries a nonzero residual tralseness (it can in-principle "err"). That **rare** residual nonconformity does **not** impugn the law's status. Here frequency-of-conformity is ~1 and still isn't the *ground* of the norm.
- **Free-i-Cell regime — common failure.** Rationality, creativity, love, environmental-integration fail **often**, because they are **indeterminately controlled by conscious free will** (the PD/indeterminate layer; the MIM vertical-agency stack), not the autonomic layer. That **common** nonconformity equally does **not** impugn the ought.
- **The unifying insight — freedom is the *precondition* of the normative layer.** Homeostasis is reliable **because it is not free**, and precisely therefore there is **no "ought" about your kidneys** — the reliable layer is the *non-normative* one. So GILE's unreliability is not a leak in the normative claim; it is the **signature** of the only layer where oughts can live. Rarity-of-success is what an *ought addressed to a free agent* looks like from the outside. (This is the general law of which **GAR-1b** "adequate rationality is itself rare" is the special case.)
- **The coordination-convention objection, dissolved without a second kind of normativity.** *Apparent* counterexample: for "drive on the right," being-the-norm seems constitutive of the ought. But this needs **no** separate species of normativity — there, frequency is a **content-determinant** of the one SHOULD ("do what coordinates" *references* others' behavior), **not evidence against** a norm. So even the conventional case obeys NRI-1: frequency can *fix the content* a single SHOULD directs, but never serves as *evidence against* a normative status. Monism survives intact.

## 3. NRI-1c — the floor / supererogatory structure (Brandon's key new content)

The principle has bite because the *statistical* norm sits **below** the *normative* floor:

- **Floor = full GILE intelligence ≜ adequate, defensible activity *given one's limits*, ~90–95% of the time = "merely sane."** A **necessary** duty, **not** supererogatory — and one nearly everyone **fails**. (This is the meta-normative warrant for **GAR-1e**'s deontic minimum-bar: full-GILE is baseline *duty*, not bonus.)
- **Ceiling = exceptional GILE = the genuinely above-duty, particularly-virtuous tier** (the GAR-1c ingenuity tail lives here).
- **Relative-virtue corollary.** Because the floor is *rarely met*, those who merely meet it are **relatively** highly virtuous while **absolutely** only sane. *Do not conflate* "rare ⇒ relatively virtuous" with "rare ⇒ supererogatory in the absolute sense." Sanity is the floor; it just happens to be scarce. (This is the Singer-style **supererogation-creep** point: a low population baseline tempts us to mislabel basic decency as saintly.)
- **"Given one's limits" internalizes ought-implies-can.** Attainability is **built into** the floor rather than bolted on as an external caveat — the floor is **individualized**, exactly like "normal weight" indexed to height/build. This is the better design: the norm already encodes the "can," so a shortfall *within reach* is a genuine failure while a *genuinely-prevented* shortfall was never a failure (matching the **GAR-1e-i** within-reach/charity partition and **IPA-1**'s individualizing test).

## 4. #69 guardrails — where it could go wrong (equal weight)

- **(i) ought-implies-can is the ONE real limit.** Rarity-from-**unwillingness** is normatively inert (and per **TSV** can even be *value-adding*); rarity-from-**impossibility** **can** legitimately defeat an ought (no obligation to the impossible). Brandon's free-will framing + "given one's limits" supplies the "can," so his version is safe — but the principle must always **carry the attainability clause**, or it over-extends into obligations no one can meet.
- **(ii) The three analogies differ in cleanliness — rank them honestly.** **Donation-by-the-rich** is the cleanest (unambiguously volitional; the marginal-utility case makes the *ought* sharp). **GILE faculties** are clean *by stipulation* (free/indeterminate). **Weight** is the one I earlier wrongly called the weak leg — **conceded to Brandon at population level:** the decade-scale obesity rise can't be genetic drift, and its near-absence in active, well-fed hunter-gatherers shows it is **lifestyle/ancestral-mismatch**, i.e. largely volitional; "normal weight given height/build" is itself the *individualized floor*. The only residue is **individual-level charity** (his "rare exceptions" = genuine endocrine/medication cases; **IPA-1** bars population→individual condemnation; **DTHI-1** reframe-the-trait-not-the-person). Net: weight is a **faithful** analogy once the floor is individualized — the *same* structure GILE has.
- **(iii) Self-location trap (cf. GAR-1-F4).** "The floor is rarely met" is a well-supported **population** claim; it tempts the speaker to infer "∴ I am one of the few who meet it." That **self-membership** inference is *not* discharged by the population fact and must be answered **outcome-blind** (track-record / calibration / registered predictions), never from "I can see how far short everyone else falls." Keep the two strictly separate.

## 5. Falsifiers (OPEN)

- **NRI-1-F1:** a case where conformity-**frequency** is the genuine **ground** of a norm's bindingness — not a content-input (the coordination carve) and not via ought-implies-can — such that *lowering frequency lowers the ought*. Would reinstate frequency as real normative evidence and break NRI-1a. (Coordination conventions do **not** qualify: there frequency fixes content, not status.)
- **NRI-1-F2:** the GILE **sanity-floor** turns out to sit **at or below** the statistical norm (most people *do* meet full-GILE-given-limits when measured outcome-blind). Would dissolve the rarely-met-floor and the relative-virtue corollary. (Empirical, **IPA-1**-sensitive; the symmetric risk to the self-location trap.)
- **NRI-1-F3:** some region of the **unified SHOULD** where rare nonconformity **does** count as evidence against the norm **after** controlling for attainability and the RTI-1 residue. Would break the physics↔ethics span and force back the very "kinds of normativity" Occam monism rejects.

### Plain-language version

Most people's hearts and kidneys run perfectly on autopilot — but being reliably reasonable, creative, loving, and well-attuned to your situation is *rare*, because those are the parts you actually choose. Brandon's point: "rare" is not an argument against "you should." A standard doesn't stop being a standard just because few people reach it — that's true for a law of physics (almost never broken) **and** for being a good person (often not done). Doing the decent thing about 90–95% of the time, as well as your real limits allow, is the **floor** for being sane, not a gold star — even though hardly anyone clears it, which is exactly why the ones who do look impressive. The only honest catch: this holds for things people genuinely *can* do (give when you're rich, take care of yourself, think straight); you can't be blamed for failing at something truly out of reach.
