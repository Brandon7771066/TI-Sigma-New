## 1. Brandon's verbatim canonical ruling

> A Meta-Indeterminate claim is self-refuting in some way, like the 'imaginary, self-refuting world of binary' or Russellian/liar-type paradoxes. A Meta-Indeterminate claim is internally inconsistent and no sense can be made of it.
>
> For a statement to be tralse, it is indeterminate in some CONCEIVABLE way.
>
> When a tralse statement is divided against itself, it BECOMES INCONCEIVABLE in a MENTALLY ACTUALIZED sense.
>
> Square circles and faulty arithmetic can be conceived of, but they cannot be coherently understood.

This is the load-bearing refinement: **MI ≠ "coherent unexpected outcome" ≠ "tangential offshoot" ≠ "ambiguous-but-conceivable"**. MI is specifically the inconceivable-when-mentally-actualized cell. The conceivability/inconceivability axis is the operative test, not surprise, not tangent, not multi-reading.

## 2. Refined operational definitions

### 2.1 Tralse (universal quality, MR substrate)

A statement P exhibits the Tralse quality when P is **indeterminate in some conceivable way** — i.e. the mind can hold P with its indeterminacy intact, without the holding itself producing collapse. The Tralse-quality is universal: every non-trivial statement has some Tralse aspect (per urb_607).

### 2.2 Indeterminate (MR1, specific state)

A specific instance of the Tralse quality where the indeterminacy is bounded and conceivable. The mind can entertain P alongside multiple coherent renderings (e.g. "future events", "open mathematical conjectures", "mostly-true with edge cases"). All renderings are mentally actualizable.

### 2.3 Meta-Indeterminate (MI, specific state)

A statement P is MI when **the act of mentally actualizing P produces inconceivability**. Formal definition (unchanged from 2026-05-08): τ(P) ∧ ¬τ(P), where τ is the Tralse operator. Operationally: when you try to coherently understand P, the understanding collapses on itself.

Canonical examples (per Brandon's ruling):
- The liar paradox ("this sentence is false") — when actualized, the truth-status oscillates and no fixpoint exists
- Russell's paradox ("the set of all sets that do not contain themselves contains itself") — when actualized, set-membership becomes incoherent
- Square circles — can be named (conceivable as labels) but cannot be coherently understood (inconceivable as actualized geometric objects)
- Faulty arithmetic ("2 + 2 = 5") — can be written down but cannot be coherently understood as a true arithmetic claim under standard semantics
- The "imaginary, self-refuting world of binary" — binary metaphysics applied to phenomena (gender, disability, capacity, truth, valence) where the binary collapse produces internal inconsistency under examination

### 2.4 The conceivability/inconceivability axis (new canonical)

| State | Mental actualization | Coherent understanding |
|---|---|---|
| T | Yes | Yes — P holds as true |
| F | Yes | Yes — P holds as false |
| I (Indeterminate) | Yes | Yes — P's indeterminacy is itself coherent |
| Tralse-quality (universal) | Yes | Yes (substrate-level) |
| **MI** | **No** (or yes-then-collapse) | **No** — actualization produces incoherence |

The axis: T / F / I / Tralse-quality are all on the **conceivable** side. MI is the inconceivable cell. This is the operative distinction, replacing any earlier framing that treated MI as "tangent" or "surprising T".

## 3. What MI is NOT

This list is load-bearing for downstream applications of MR Truth Labels and should be consulted before assigning any proposition to the MI cell:

- **NOT a strong/extreme T.** A claim being unexpectedly true or above-baseline in some domain is still T (or strong-T with a mechanism label) — it remains coherently understandable. Brandon's worked example: "disability is a superpower" via catalyst mechanism — coherent, even if surprising; not MI.
- **NOT a multi-reading proposition.** A claim that admits multiple coherent interpretations across a spectrum (F → I → T → strong-T) is exhibiting the Tralse-quality, not MI. Spectrum-readings are conceivable per-rendering.
- **NOT a tangential/offshoot claim.** A proposition that diverges from the obvious reading in a coherent direction is T-tangential, not MI.
- **NOT "ambiguous".** Ambiguity is I (Indeterminate). MI requires the actualization-collapse signature.
- **NOT "surprising/counterintuitive".** Surprise is a psychological feature; MI is a logical-conceivability feature.
- **NOT "complicated".** Complexity is not incoherence. A coherent complicated claim is still T, F, or I.

## 4. What MI IS (operational test)

A proposition P is MI when:
1. **Actualization test:** attempting to mentally hold P with full coherence produces collapse, oscillation, or recognized incoherence
2. **Formal test:** τ(P) ∧ ¬τ(P) holds — P is simultaneously Tralse and not-Tralse under the same actualization
3. **Discrimination test (Pass-63 batch-5 empirical):** competent semantic raters reliably classify P with the MI label vs the I label at >>50% routing rate (PARADOX-class items routed to MI at ~68%; MODAL-class items routed to MI at ~0%); discrimination score per LLM-rater protocol +1.4/2.0 or better

All three tests should converge for a confident MI assignment. The formal test is the canonical definition; the actualization test is the operational test; the discrimination test is the empirical-corpus test.

## 6. Practical heuristic for assignment

When tempted to label a proposition P as MI, run this 3-step check:

1. **Can I conceive of P with full coherence?** If yes → not MI (T, F, or I).
2. **If I try to mentally actualize P, does the attempt collapse?** If no → not MI.
3. **Is the collapse driven by P's internal structure (self-reference, contradiction conjunction, category-violation), or by my unfamiliarity?** If unfamiliarity → not MI (it's just hard; revisit after study). If structural → MI confirmed.

This 3-step heuristic should be referenced in all future principle proposals that consider using the MI cell.
