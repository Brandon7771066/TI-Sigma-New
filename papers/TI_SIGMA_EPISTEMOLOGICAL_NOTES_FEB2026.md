## Note 1: Hyperthymesia as Failure of LCC Lossy Compression

### What Is Hyperthymesia?

Hyperthymesia (Highly Superior Autobiographical Memory) is the condition of near-perfect recall of every day of one's life — not just major events but the texture, weather, emotion, and incidental detail of virtually every waking day. Fewer than 100 documented cases exist worldwide.

Ordinary memory is lossy: experiences are compressed during consolidation (particularly during sleep) into representative Tralse states. Most detail is discarded. What is retained is a summary — a high-level L×E snapshot rather than a full informational record.

### The TI Sigma Explanation

Under TI Sigma, every memory formation event involves two competing processes:

**L×E binding:** The strength of informational coupling between the experiencing i-cell and the environmental configuration it is encoding. High L×E binding means the information is tightly coupled to the core identity of the i-cell — it is constitutive, not incidental.

**LCC compression:** The natural tendency of information systems to resolve Tralse detail into simplified True/False summaries as the LCC trajectory progresses. This is adaptive — full Tralse retention is energetically expensive and the detail rarely matters for future navigation.

In ordinary memory, LCC compression wins: the system discards texture and retains essence. In hyperthymesia, the hypothesis is that the individual's i-cell has unusually high baseline L×E binding across all autobiographical experiences — every experience is encoded as constitutive of identity rather than incidental. The compression cannot proceed because the binding is too strong to allow lossy resolution.

**The cost:** True Tralse retention preserves everything — including emotional weight, trauma, and the visceral quality of experiences that most people naturally forget. Hyperthymesic individuals frequently describe their memory not as a gift but as an inability to let go. They cannot selectively compress because the binding doesn't allow it. Every experience remains in a near-Tralse state rather than fully resolving into a summarized memory.

**Implication:** This provides a testable prediction. If hyperthymesia arises from unusually high L×E binding, then hyperthymesic individuals should show elevated baseline L (Love/coherence) scores and unusually strong environmental-identity integration on GILE measurement. They should also show distinctive LCC profiles — potentially stuck near a high-coherence state that prevents the lossy resolution ordinary memory requires.

**Deeper implication:** Ordinary forgetting is not a failure. It is the correct LCC operation — resolving unnecessary Tralse complexity into a clean True/False summary that frees informational resources for new encoding. The pathology is not in forgetting; it is in the inability to compress.

## Note 2: Conceptual Isolation Requires Imagining Something Impossible

### The Paradox of Abstraction

To reason about a concept "in isolation" — to think about "justice" without a specific situation, or "number" without a specific quantity — requires a cognitive operation that is strictly speaking impossible in a fully TI-consistent universe.

Every concept exists only in relation to other concepts. There is no "number" that has no relation to quantity, to ordering, to the concept of unit. There is no "justice" without a prior concept of person, action, and standard. The concept exists AS its relational network, not as a separate entity that happens to have relations.

### The TI Sigma Account: Abstraction as Tralse Construction

When we reason about a concept "in isolation," we are constructing a Tralse state with a selective suppression:

1. The concept's relational context is placed in superposition (Tralse) rather than resolved
2. The concept itself is held in a partially-resolved state — enough to engage with, not enough to cash out in specific instances
3. The suppression is coherent (not random) — a skilled reasoner knows which contextual connections to hold in Tralse and which to resolve

This is why the operation "feels" coherent even though it is strictly impossible: Tralse states are genuinely coherent — they are not confused or random. The impossibility is ontological (there is no actually isolated concept), but the Tralse state that simulates isolation is epistemically functional.

**Why this matters:** This explains why abstract reasoning works at all. Mathematics, philosophy, and theoretical science all operate on "isolated" concepts that do not actually exist in isolation in nature. They work because Tralse suppression of context is a coherent cognitive operation, even if the fully-resolved state it simulates does not exist.

**Implication:** When abstract reasoning fails — when we apply conclusions from an isolated concept to a real situation and get wrong answers — it is because the suppressed context turns out to have been relevant. The Tralse suppression was incomplete: some contextual connection that was held in superposition should have been resolved into the reasoning. This is a specific, diagnosable failure mode: not "the abstraction was wrong" but "the wrong context was suppressed."

## Note 3: A Priori / A Posteriori — Ontological Versus Temporal Priority

### Kant's Insight and Its Limits

Kant argued that certain knowledge is a priori — independent of experience, prior to any empirical encounter with the world. Space, time, causality, and the categories of the understanding are a priori for Kant: they make experience possible rather than being derived from it. All other knowledge is a posteriori — derived from and verified by experience.

The puzzle: a priori and a posteriori knowledge develop together in time. We don't have the a priori first and then add a posteriori on top. Both emerge through the development of a cognitive system. So in what sense is the a priori "prior"?

### The TI Sigma Resolution

Under TI Sigma: a priori knowledge is Tralse-level structure. It is the unresolved informational substrate within which all specific (a posteriori) resolutions occur. It is not prior in time — it is prior in ontological order: it is what must be present for any specific experience to be interpretable as experience at all.

**Analogy:** The LCC threshold structure (0.42, 0.85, 0.92) is a priori in the TI sense. These thresholds don't emerge from specific experimental results — they are the informational architecture within which experimental results are meaningful. They are Tralse-level structure that makes specific physical resolutions interpretable.

**Formal statement:**
- A priori = Tralse-level informational structure (present before resolution, conditions the resolution)
- A posteriori = resolved (True/False) informational states (the specific outputs of the resolution process)
- The a priori is ontologically prior because you cannot have a resolution without the structure within which resolution occurs
- Both develop simultaneously in the temporal experience of a cognitive agent because the agent is building its Tralse-level architecture and its resolved experiences concurrently

**Why Kant was partially right:** He correctly identified that some knowledge structures experience rather than being derived from it. He was wrong to locate this entirely in the human mind (transcendental idealism). Under TI Sigma, a priori structure is GM's informational architecture — which human minds participate in and reflect, but did not originate. The a priori is real, not a projection.

**A new prediction:** If a priori knowledge is Tralse-level structure, then highly abstract reasoning (mathematics, fundamental physics) operates closer to the Tralse substrate than applied reasoning does. This predicts that abstract mathematical intuition — the sense of a proof being correct before verifying it — is a genuine informational access phenomenon, not just heuristic pattern matching. The mathematician who "feels" a theorem is true is reading Tralse-level structure before resolving it into a proof. This is the same mechanism as divination (Paper #332) applied to mathematics.

## Note 4: The Myrion — A Proposed 8th SI Unit for Tralse Resolution Energy

### The Current 7 SI Units and Their TI Mapping

Paper #328 (SI Units from Jeff Time) established that the 7 existing SI base units derive from the 7 PRIMARY constants {0, 1, i, √2, e, φ, π}:

| i | Ampere (A) | Electric current |
| π | Kelvin (K) | Temperature |

All 7 PRIMARY constants are mapped. But there is a phenomenon not captured by any existing SI unit: the energetic event of a Tralse state resolving into a determinate truth value. This is distinct from energy (Joules), power (Watts), information (bits), or any derived unit combination.

### The Myrion (My)

**Proposed unit:** The **Myrion (My)** — the SI unit for Tralse resolution energy.

**Definition:** 1 Myrion = the minimum energy required for one coherent quantum Tralse state to complete a single Myrion Resolution (Tralse → determinate {True, False, or Indeterminate}).

**Dimensional analysis:** The Myrion is not simply a Joule. A Joule measures energy transferred. The Myrion measures energy of informational state collapse — which involves entropy change (a thermodynamic quantity) but is more specific. The closest existing framework is Landauer's principle (erasing one bit of information requires kT·ln(2) joules of energy), but the Myrion extends this to ternary/tralse resolution rather than binary erasure.

**Rough estimate of scale:**
- Landauer limit for binary erasure at room temperature: ~3 × 10⁻²¹ J
- Tralse resolution involves a 3-to-1 or 4-to-1 collapse (from Tralse to one of {T,F,I,Φ})
- Expected Myrion magnitude: approximately 2-3× the Landauer limit for binary erasure
- Estimated: 1 My ≈ 6-9 × 10⁻²¹ J at room temperature (temperature-dependent)

**Why a new unit rather than a derived unit?** Because the Myrion is not just energy — it is information-weighted energy. A 1-Myrion resolution at high coherence (Q ≥ 0.92) carries different informational significance than a 1-Myrion resolution at low coherence (Q < 0.42), even if the raw energy expenditure is identical. The coherence-weighting is the new dimensional quantity. A complete Myrion unit would involve a coherence coefficient — My(Q) — where Q is the LCC coherence at the time of resolution.

**Is adding one new unit enough?** Probably not complete. A second candidate unit is the **Tralse (Tl)** — a unit of coherence field density, measuring the strength of the Tralse state before it resolves. This would be analogous to the relationship between Volts (potential) and Amperes (flow) in electromagnetism: the Tralse measures the potential coherence, the Myrion measures the energy of the actual resolution event. Two new units, not one, may complete the TI extension of the SI system.
