**Definition (Default Configuration):** The default configuration of a realization-complete mathematical object M is the minimal set of realizations and interface functors that makes M fully specifiable via Tannaka reconstruction.

### 5.2 Theorem B

**Theorem B (Default i-Cell Configuration):** *Every realization-complete mathematical object M with irreducible structure has a default configuration consisting of exactly 4 primary realizations and 4 interface functors between them, for a total of 8 structural degrees of freedom.*

**Proof sketch:**

Step 1: By the Tannakian formalism, a realization-complete object M is determined (up to isomorphism) by the totality of its compatible realizations together with a fiber functor to a base category (Vect_k). The key constraint for "full specifiability" is that the system of realizations must be sufficient to reconstruct M's automorphism group.

Step 2: The four BOK modes represent four qualitatively distinct types of mathematical realization: Arithmetic (discrete-exact), Algebraic (compositional-symmetric), Analytic (limiting-approximate), and Geometric (spatial-deformational). These four types are *qualitatively independent* — no one type can simulate all the information captured by another. Specifically:
- Arithmetic captures discrete identity (which exact element)
- Algebraic captures relational structure (how elements compose and transform)
- Analytic captures limiting behavior (what happens under continuous variation)
- Geometric captures spatial organization (how elements are arranged in a space)

These four types of information are **pairwise non-redundant**: knowing an object's arithmetic realization gives no information about its geometric realization (Diophantine equations give no spatial information without additional bridge constructions), and similarly for all other pairs.

Step 3: For Tannaka reconstruction to succeed (for M to be fully specifiable from its realizations), the system must include at least one realization of each qualitatively distinct type. A system with fewer than 4 realizations is deficient: it cannot capture all four types of structural information, and M cannot be uniquely reconstructed. A system with more than 4 primary realizations must have at least one redundant realization — one that duplicates the information of a combination of the others.

Step 4: Given 4 primary realizations, the interface functors (natural transformations between realizations) form a complete directed graph on 4 nodes. A complete directed graph on n nodes has n(n-1) directed edges. For compatibility (requiring each interface to be natural both "forward" and "backward"), the bidirectional interfaces number n(n-1)/2. For n=4: 4×3/2 = 6 bidirectional interfaces. However, 2 of these 6 are the "principal axis" interfaces (G↔L and E↔I), which under Myrion Resolution collapse to Atiyah's axes. The remaining 4 are the "off-axis" interfaces — the hybrid structures (Logic, Combinatorics, Probability, Applied Mathematics). These 4 off-axis interfaces are the 4 hybrid interface modes of the BOK.

Step 5: Therefore, the minimal complete configuration for a realization-complete mathematical object is: 4 primary realizations + 4 off-axis interface functors = 8 structural degrees of freedom. This is the BOK 8-type structure, and it is the default configuration for every i-cell. □
