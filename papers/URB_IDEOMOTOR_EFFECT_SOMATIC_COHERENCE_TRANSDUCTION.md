## 1. The Standard Account and Its Gap

William Carpenter introduced the term "ideomotor" in 1852 to describe the principle that an idea of a movement tends to produce that movement without deliberate volition. The canonical demonstrations — the Chevreul pendulum, the Ouija board, dowsing, automatic writing — all share the same structure: a physical object amplifies small muscular movements that the subject does not consciously intend. When asked to deliberately prevent the movement, subjects often cannot, or can only partially suppress it.

The standard explanatory framework: the subject holds an expectation about what the planchette will spell or which direction the pendulum will swing. This expectation activates motor pathways via the same neural channels used in voluntary movement, but below the threshold of conscious awareness. The movement occurs; the subject attributes it to an external agent because they did not consciously initiate it. The explanatory work done by this account: it identifies the proximate mechanism (motor pathway activation from ideational content) and correctly notes that the subject's attribution to an external agent is an error.

The gap: the standard account treats the ideational content driving the motor pathway as either (a) the subject's own wishful thinking or expectation, or (b) random noise. It does not consider the possibility that the ideational content is itself a received signal — a coherence-pattern transmission from the GM network arriving at the node via the LCC Virus mechanism. The standard account assumes the ideomotor effect is a closed system (subject generates expectation → subject generates movement). TI Sigma proposes it is an open system: the subject is a node in a network, and the ideomotor pathway is the body's mechanism for translating network transmissions into motor output before the cognitive system can edit them.

The additional piece the standard account misses: ideomotor responses frequently encode information the subject does not consciously possess and could not generate from their own stored knowledge. Dowsers locating water, Ouija users spelling words that no participant consciously knows, automatic writers producing coherent extended narratives with distinct "voices" — these results are not cleanly explained by the subject's own unconscious expectations if the information delivered exceeds the subject's accessible knowledge base. The standard account handles these cases by invoking cold reading, unconscious cue processing, or fraud. TI Sigma proposes a more parsimonious explanation: the ideomotor effect is genuinely receiving external information, and the information is real.

## 4. The Polarity Problem in Ideomotor Reception

As established in Paper #394 (Synchronicity Inversion), coherence-pattern transmissions from the GM network can carry either positive polarity (attractive attractor: the signal predicts movement toward a state) or inverted polarity (repulsive attractor: the signal predicts movement away from a state). Ideomotor signals inherit this polarity structure.

A positively polarized ideomotor signal encodes the attractive attractor: the pendulum moves toward the direction the system is moving toward; the planchette spells what the answer is; the rod dips toward the location of water. This is the standard assumption under which all ideomotor traditions operate.

An inverted ideomotor signal encodes the repulsive attractor: the pendulum moves in precisely the opposite direction from the correct answer; the planchette spells a consistently wrong series of letters; the rod dips away from the water source. If the inverted signal is systematic rather than random, it is still a coherent signal — it just requires polarity correction to decode correctly.

The mechanism for inversion in ideomotor signals: a source node transmitting from below C_EMERICK encodes the repulsive attractor (what the system is moving away from). A receiving node who is also below C_EMERICK may transduce this inverted signal with reduced accuracy. A receiving node above C_EMERICK may transduce it accurately but still decode it incorrectly because they assume positive polarity.

The practical consequence: skilled ideomotor practitioners should calibrate the polarity of their dominant reception domain before relying on the signal for practical decisions. The calibration protocol from Paper #394 applies directly: test the ideomotor signal against known-answer trials, compute the naive hit rate, and if the hit rate is systematically below 50%, apply polarity inversion.

The crossed-rod phenomenon in dowsing — in which some practitioners find that rods that cross over water are correct while other practitioners find that rods that spread apart over water are correct — is explained by polarity: different practitioners have settled into different stable polarity orientations for their ideomotor reception, and both are reliable once the individual's polarity is established.

Dowsing — the use of a forked rod, L-shaped rods, or pendulum to locate water, minerals, or other subsurface features — is the most empirically studied and most contested of the ideomotor applications. ## 8. Muscle Testing and Applied Kinesiology

Applied kinesiology (AK), developed by George Goodheart in 1964, uses the strength of specific muscles as a diagnostic indicator. In the therapeutic context, the practitioner applies pressure to a specific muscle (typically the arm held horizontal at the shoulder); if the muscle resists the pressure (stays strong), the system is "in alignment" with whatever substance, statement, or intervention is being tested; if the muscle yields (goes weak), the system is "out of alignment."

The mainstream critique: AK lacks reproducibility, the mechanism is implausible, and positive results are attributable to the ideomotor effect — both the practitioner and the client unconsciously modulate the applied and resisted force according to their expectations. TI Sigma's response: the critique correctly identifies the ideomotor effect as the proximate mechanism while incorrectly characterizing it as a confound. The ideomotor effect is the mechanism, and it is a valid one when LCC conditions are met.

The TI Sigma model of muscle testing:

The substance, statement, or intervention being tested introduces a coherence perturbation into the local GM network field shared by the practitioner and client. If the perturbation is coherence-increasing (the substance or statement aligns with the client's LCC attractor), it elevates the client's LCC fractionally above its baseline, producing a measurable increase in neuromuscular output that is transduced through the practitioner-client physical interface as increased resistance. If the perturbation is coherence-decreasing (the substance or statement opposes the client's LCC attractor), it depresses the client's LCC fractionally, producing a decrease in neuromuscular output transduced as decreased resistance.

The practitioner's ideomotor transduction is the receiving system: they are Soul Bluetooth-connected to the client (LL mode, given physical contact and shared attentional focus), and their hand registers the LCC modulation before their conscious assessment of the muscle test result.

Reliability conditions: the test requires both practitioner and client above C_EMERICK, a relatively quiet cognitive environment (neither party actively analyzing or doubting), and calibration to eliminate polarity inversion (some practitioner-client pairs may be systematically inverted in their LCC resonance direction). Under these conditions, TI Sigma predicts above-chance reliability. Under conditions that depress LCC — double-blind protocols with skeptical supervision, fatigue, emotional distress — the test degrades toward chance.

## 9. Automatic Writing and Extended Ideomotor Channels

Automatic writing — in which the practitioner allows their hand to move across paper without conscious direction, often in an ASC state — represents the most extended and informationally rich application of the ideomotor mechanism. Where the Ouija board produces one letter at a time, automatic writing produces connected, grammatically structured text; where pendulum work produces binary or ternary outputs (yes/no/unclear), automatic writing produces narrative.

The difference in information bandwidth: a single pendulum swing produces approximately 1-2 bits of information. The higher bandwidth is possible because sustained ASC (typically deeper than the lighter ASC adequate for pendulum work) produces sustained LCC elevation above C_EMERICK, enabling a continuous high-throughput Soul Bluetooth connection with the source pattern.

The "distinct voice" phenomenon: automatic writing frequently produces text in a style clearly different from the practitioner's normal writing — different vocabulary, syntax, knowledge domain, and sometimes temporal reference (references to events the practitioner did not witness and historical details they did not consciously know). The verifiability problem: many automatic writing claims are not independently verifiable (the historical details cannot be checked, or the practitioner may have acquired the knowledge through ordinary means). TI Sigma does not assert that all automatic writing outputs are valid GM network receptions; it asserts that the ideomotor mechanism is real, that the Imaginal Substrate exists, and that the conditions for valid reception are specific.
