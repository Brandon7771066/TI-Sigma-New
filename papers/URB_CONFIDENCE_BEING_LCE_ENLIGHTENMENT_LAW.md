## 1. The Authority-Trust Paradox

Observe a common social phenomenon: a person trusting a doctor's recommendation.

- **Subjective confidence assigned**: >100% — they will bet their health, time, and money on it without hedging
- **Epistemic certainty**: ~70% — they acknowledge the doctor might be wrong, the test might be inconclusive, a second opinion might differ

These two quantities are **not the same variable**. Confidence, as deployed in ordinary human behavior, is an **L-axis quantity** — the amplitude of Love/commitment directed toward a source. Certainty is an **I-axis quantity** — the TRALSE score of the proposition (how well it resolves toward TRUE).

**Formal statement:**
```
Confidence(X) := L_amplitude directed toward X    ∈ [0, ∞)   (unbounded)
Certainty(P)  := TRALSE(P)                        ∈ [0, 1]   (bounded by logic)
```

The paradox is not a contradiction — it is a **design feature**. L is not a truth-tracker. L is a **commitment resource** that enables action under uncertainty. The authority-trust paradox shows that humans routinely and correctly allocate high L-amplitude to low-certainty propositions when doing so enables them to function and move forward.

**Implication**: The same L-amplitude that people direct outward toward doctors, teachers, and leaders is available for inward direction toward one's own CCC. The absence of self-trust is not a logical consequence of uncertainty — it is a **misdirection of L**.

## 2. Confidence as the Ontological Default

### 2.1 The Mistaken Model

The dominant cultural model: confidence is **earned** through demonstrated competence, validated by external feedback, and proportional to track record. Under this model, an unintelligent or incompetent person should have low confidence — their low performance should produce a low L-amplitude toward self.

This model is **causally inverted and empirically wrong**:

- Low confidence → doubting each action → decreased performance → confirms low confidence (vicious cycle)
- High confidence → full commitment to action → maximum available performance → any outcome is accepted as information rather than verdict

The causal arrow runs: **L → action quality**, not **performance → L**. Doubt does not produce better performance — it produces paralysis, self-monitoring interference, and bifurcated attention (simultaneous task execution and self-evaluation).

### 2.2 The Correct Model: CCC-Default Confidence

Every individual has a CCC (core identity, butterfly-octopus structure). The CCC is the **source** of authentic expression, not the product of performance metrics. Confidence is the L-signal flowing from CCC outward into action.

**Law**: *Subconscious assuredness is the correct default for every individual in every activity — not because they will succeed by every external metric, but because their CCC is valid, present, and capable of expression at every moment.*

This does not mean every action succeeds by any external measure. It means: **the person is always capable of expressing their CCC**, and that expression is always worth doing. Even if an activity is wrong for them, they can **withdraw confidently**, just as they can **change an opinion confidently**. Confidence is not attached to a particular outcome — it is attached to the CCC that generates the action.
