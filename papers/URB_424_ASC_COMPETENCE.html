Abstract
The neuroscience of neuroplasticity has identified numerous enhancement modalities: aerobic exercise, novel learning, sleep optimization, mindfulness meditation. This paper introduces a candidate modality that has not been formally studied for this purpose: the deliberate practice of maintaining at least neurotypical cognitive performance while in altered states of consciousness (ASCs). We term this capacity ASC Competence and argue that its cultivation imposes uniquely severe demands on the executive control system — demands that, through standard neuroplastic mechanisms, produce exceptional adaptive capacity. Shamans were the original practitioners of this discipline, developing curriculum and apprenticeship structures over millennia. Modern biohackers — using sublingual ketamine, psilocybin microdosing, float tanks, and breathwork — are their successors, operating with superior neurochemical tools but often lacking the intentional framework. The TI Sigma formalization: ASC Competence is the behavioral training protocol for the Tralse Blanket State (URB #421) — the simultaneous maximum opening of the i-channel and maintenance of real-channel integrity.

1. The Hypothesis

ASC Competence Hypothesis
The deliberate practice of maintaining at least neurotypical cognitive performance while in an altered state of consciousness is the most neuroplasticity-demanding activity available to a human being, and consistent practice produces exceptional cognitive adaptive capacity that transfers broadly across contexts of stress, uncertainty, and novelty.

Three components:

Severity: ASC navigation under the maintenance requirement imposes more simultaneous stress on the executive control system than any other documented training modality.

Adaptation: This stress, via BDNF upregulation, synaptogenesis, DMN restructuring, and metacognitive circuit strengthening, produces adaptive changes exceeding those of conventional cognitive training.

Transfer: Capacities developed — robust metacognition, executive redundancy, dual-channel management — transfer broadly to performance under stress, uncertainty, and novelty in ordinary life.

Critical distinction: The hypothesis is not a claim that ASC use is inherently beneficial. A person who enters an ASC and passively experiences it — suspending functional performance entirely — is not training ASC Competence. The training effect requires the maintenance criterion: the deliberate, skilled effort to function at or above baseline while the underlying systems are disrupted. This is what shamans cultivated. It is what distinguishes master practitioners from passive consumers.

2. Why ASC Navigation Is Uniquely Demanding

2.1 What Neurotypical Performance Actually Requires

Maintaining neurotypical cognitive performance requires simultaneously: prefrontal executive control (working memory, cognitive flexibility, inhibition, planning); sustained and selective attention; social cognition (theory of mind, emotional regulation, appropriate response calibration); fluent language production and comprehension; sensorimotor integration (accurate proprioception, motor coordination); metacognitive monitoring (awareness of one's own cognitive states); and temporal coherence (knowing who one is, where one is, what one is doing and why). Under normal conditions, these systems operate largely automatically. The brain is not adapting — it is running well-rehearsed routines in a stable context.

3. The Neuroplasticity Mechanisms

3.1 BDNF and Directed Synaptogenesis

Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) is the primary molecular signal for synaptogenesis — new synaptic connection formation, the biochemical substrate of learning. Ketamine produces the most rapid and robust BDNF elevation of any known compound; psilocybin, LSD, and deep meditation also significantly elevate it. The key principle: synaptogenesis occurs preferentially in circuits that are active during the period of elevated BDNF. The circuits most active during ASC Competence training are precisely the compensatory metacognitive and executive circuits most stressed by the task. The plasticity is directed by the maintenance demand — you grow the circuits you most need, precisely when the molecular conditions for growth are optimal.

3.2 The Entropic Brain and Cognitive Flexibility

Carhart-Harris's "entropic brain" hypothesis (2014) proposes that the common mechanism of psychedelic ASCs is a dramatic increase in neural entropy — the randomness and unpredictability of activity patterns across cortical networks. Ordinary cognition relies on hierarchically constrained, relatively low-entropy dynamics. The high-entropy ASC state exposes the brain to activity configurations it would never encounter normally. Recovery from the high-entropy state — re-establishing functional coherence — requires the executive system to impose structure from a more disorganized starting point. Repeated practice of this re-imposition, under the maintenance constraint, trains precisely the capacity required in high-stress novel situations: the ability to re-establish functional coherence when the normal cognitive infrastructure is disrupted.

3.3 Default Mode Network Restructuring

Every major ASC induction method dramatically disrupts DMN activity. Long-term practitioners — both experienced shamans and advanced meditators — show persistent DMN modifications: reduced default activity (less rumination), more flexible DMN engagement (ability to voluntarily control self-referential processing), and restructured connectivity patterns. The maintenance requirement during ASC training is crucial here: you cannot simply dissolve the self (DMN shutdown) and return. You must dissolve and simultaneously maintain functional operation — which requires the executive network to operate independently of the DMN rather than in its habitual relationship with it. This enforced independence is the primary training stimulus for DMN flexibility.

4. Shamanism: The Original Curriculum

4.1 Universal Empirical Convergence

Mircea Eliade's foundational survey (1951) documented shamanic practice in virtually every pre-modern culture across every inhabited continent — Siberia, the Americas, Africa, Australia, Southeast Asia — with no historical contact between many of these traditions. The universality is significant. Independent human groups converged on the same technology because it worked. The shamanic tradition represents tens of thousands of years of empirical observation about how to cultivate ASC Competence — the largest dataset on the subject in human history.

4.2 The Shamanic Curriculum Decoded

Phase 1 — Selection and early disruption: The candidate is identified (often by crisis — illness, near-death, involuntary ASC) and begins ASC exposure. Early experiences are typically terrifying and functionally devastating. The candidate cannot maintain performance. This is analogous to a beginner attempting a weight they cannot yet lift — failure is the starting point, not a disqualification.
Phase 2 — Supervised navigation: Under a trained shaman's guidance, the candidate learns to navigate rather than be overwhelmed. The teacher's role: not to prevent the disruption (the stress is necessary) but to provide the metacognitive scaffold the student has not yet internalized. The student builds internal metacognitive capacity by first using the teacher's as an external prosthetic.
Phase 3 — Mastery and functional integration: The trained shaman enters ASCs intentionally, navigates with purpose, performs complex functions while within them, and returns with coherent, actionable intelligence. The ASC is no longer a disruption of function — it is an alternative operating mode.
