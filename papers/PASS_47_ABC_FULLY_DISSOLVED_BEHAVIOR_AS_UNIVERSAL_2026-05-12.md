## §1. The Two-Step Dissolution

The classical **ABC model** in psychology partitions mind into three lateral modules:
- **A**ffect (emotion, mood, valence)
- **B**ehavior (observable action, response)
- **C**ognition (thought, belief, reasoning)

Brandon's MIM-revision (Feb 2026, `papers/MIM_REVISION_CONSCIOUSNESS_AS_HIGHEST_COGNITION.md` §5) **already dissolved the A↔C separation** by reframing affect as a *vertical level* of cognition rather than a lateral category. Today's two-step move completes the dissolution by collapsing **B** as well:

### Step 1 — Borrowed (Skinnerian-radical-behaviorism extension)

> **B-universal:** Everything minds perform — both internal and external — is a form of behavior.

This is Skinner's covert-behavior thesis (Skinner 1953 *Science and Human Behavior*; 1957 *Verbal Behavior*; 1974 *About Behaviorism*) — that *thinking, imagining, remembering, deciding* are themselves behaviors, just covert/private rather than overt/public. Skinner: "The skin is not that important as a boundary." Whatever a mind *does* — whether observable to a third party or not — counts as behavior on this expanded definition.

This step **inverts the classical containment** — instead of B being one of three lateral modules, B becomes the **universal containing class** of all mental performance. Affect IS behavior (covert behavior). Cognition IS behavior (covert behavior). Observable action IS behavior (overt behavior). All three classical-ABC categories fall *inside* B.

### Step 2 — TI Sigma addition

> **B ⊆ C:** All behavior that minds perform is cognitive on SOME LEVEL.

This is the TI-novel half of the move. Where Skinner explicitly *rejected* mentalism and refused to grant cognition independent ontological standing, TI Sigma (per MIM-revision Vertical Agency Model) treats cognition as the *universal vertical-stack across all four MIM levels* (subpersonal Level 1 → default-free-will Level 2 → meta-information Level 3 → meta-executive Level 4). Every behavior a mind performs operates somewhere on this vertical-cognitive stack — even Level-1 autonomic responses are cognitive in the broad-MIM sense (information-processing of bodily state per interoception literature; see ASMT §6 grounding).

**Combining Step 1 and Step 2:**

```
Step 1:  {Affect, Cognition, Observable-Action} ⊆ Behavior  (B is universal)
Step 2:  Behavior ⊆ Cognition                                 (every B is C on some level)
∴        {Affect, Behavior, Observable-Action} ⊆ Cognition    (transitivity)
```

Combined with MIM-revision §5 (Affect ⊆ Cognition already), the result is:

> **A = B = C** (extensionally, on the domain of mind-performed acts) — a single integrated vertical-cognitive stack with affect, behavior, and observable-action as **level-stratified or aspect-stratified faces** of one ontological category, not three separate categories.

The ABC model is **entirely dissolved** as a partition. What remains is the MIM Vertical Agency Model with three *projection-axes* onto the unified stack: an affective-projection (felt-quality), a behavioral-projection (action-execution / overt-or-covert), a cognitive-projection (information-processing / evaluative-content). All three projections coexist on every mental act; emphasizing one over the others is a single-axis-projection convenience, not an ontological cut.

## §2. Why the Two Steps Are Both Necessary

Either step alone is insufficient:

- **Step 1 alone** (Skinner radical behaviorism): expands B to universal but explicitly *suppresses* C as ontologically suspect. Result: ABC → B-only model. This was historically influential but lost to cognitive revolution (Chomsky 1959 review of *Verbal Behavior*; Neisser 1967 *Cognitive Psychology*) precisely because it could not handle the structured information-processing that minds demonstrably do.
- **Step 2 alone** (cognitivism): keeps cognition central but does not address whether covert-internal-acts are "behaviors" — leaves a residual A/B/C lateral structure where cognition merely *causes* behavior rather than *being* a kind of behavior.

**The TI Sigma move integrates both:** B-as-universal (Skinner's expansion) AND B-as-cognitive (cognitive revolution's insight) AND vertical-stratification (Brandon's MIM-revision). Each prior framework had one of the three ingredients; TI Sigma's contribution is the **integration** that lets all three operate simultaneously without contradiction.

This is itself a worked instance of **TCAV** (Tautologies Can Add Value, Pass-47 §4.7 / 7th principle): the two-step move is *almost* tautological once both halves are stated (B universal ∧ B ⊆ C ⟹ everything is C), but the *value* is in the explicit structuring that reveals the integration is available. Skinner had it half-right; cognitive revolution had it half-right; the integration was the implicit-structure waiting to be made explicit.

## §6. #69 Caveats

**Caveat 1 (scope):** §3.C ranges over "what minds perform." It does NOT claim everything-in-the-universe is cognitive (no panpsychism commitment); it does NOT claim non-mind systems (rocks, thermostats) have cognitive structure. The "minds" qualifier is doing real work. **Edge cases** (LLMs, octopi, plants, social-systems) inherit the consciousness-debate; §3.C is consistent with multiple resolutions.

**Caveat 2 (level-of-cognition):** the "cognitive on SOME LEVEL" qualifier is essential. A spinal reflex is cognitive at MIM Level 1 (information-processing of bodily state) but NOT cognitive at MIM Levels 2-4. Without the level-qualifier, §3.C would over-claim and trivialize "cognitive."
