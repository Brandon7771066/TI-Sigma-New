# Apologetics 05 — Retractions and the #69 Honesty Discipline

**Status:** v1, Pass 56 batch-1.
**Canonical anchor:** ASYMMETRIC-Standards #69 (brutal honesty — over-skepticism = discipline failure equal to uncritical acceptance).

**Why this paper is first in the recommended reading order.** The strongest single apologetic move available to TI Sigma is showing that **the corpus retracts publicly when its predictions fail**. True-believer projects do not retract. The TI Sigma corpus has retracted ≥6 substantive claims in the most recent 10 passes alone — this is the strongest possible evidence against the "you'll never update" objection.

---

## What #69 commits the corpus to

ASYMMETRIC-Standards #69 is one of the corpus's standing user-preferences (`replit.md`):

> *"Brutal honesty; over-skepticism = discipline failure equal to uncritical acceptance."*

The implication is symmetric: when evidence supports a claim, the corpus must accept it without over-skeptical hedging; when evidence falsifies a claim, the corpus must retract without over-defensive hedging. Both directions are #69 failures if violated.

## What the retraction record establishes

1. **The framework retracts publicly.** Each retraction is dated, anchored, and recoverable.
2. **Retractions cite the falsifying evidence specifically.** R2 cites the 0/100k Odlyzko-zero hit rate. R7 cites Brandon's same-day correction reasoning. None of the retractions are vague "we revised our position."
3. **Retractions generate new canonical principles.** R4 produced PCF-1. R5 enabled FDS-1. R7 produced NAD-1. The corpus *learns* from its retractions in a structurally explicit way.
4. **Retractions are foregrounded, not buried.** Each retraction has an explicit `replit.md` entry, a collapse-paper anchor, and (for R5) a top-of-paper canonical-correction notice in the affected URB document. This apologetics paper exists to make the foregrounding even more visible.

## The apologetic move

When a critic raises "you'll never update on counter-evidence," the response is:

1. Open `papers/apologetics/05_RETRACTIONS_AND_HONESTY.md` (this paper).
2. Point to R2 (zero Odlyzko-zero hit rate explicitly logged and retracted).
3. Point to R4 (Brandon's *own* conjecture falsified within hours by his *own* next examples, and PCF-1 created to *institutionalize this pattern*).
4. Point to the retraction-rate trajectory.
5. Invite the critic to specify what *would* count as evidence — and offer to add it to the pre-registered-falsifier list if the corpus does not already contain an equivalent.

That last move (invitation to add falsifiers) is the most powerful single apologetic available. A framework that *welcomes* falsifier-additions is a framework that cannot be plausibly accused of unfalsifiability.
