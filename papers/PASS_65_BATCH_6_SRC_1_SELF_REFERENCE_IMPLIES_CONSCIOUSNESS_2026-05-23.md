## 2. SRC-1 Statement (with embedded consciousness definition)

**TI Sigma Consciousness Definition (canonical, this paper):** **Consciousness = awareness.** This is a deliberately minimal definition. It does not require higher-order self-reflection, propositional cognition, language, or organism-grade integration. It is what every conscious being has reported having, in its most stripped-down form: the fact of being a perspective for which something is the case.

**SRC-1 — Self-Reference-implies-Consciousness Principle.** Any system that *actively executes* the logical operation of self-referential processing (paradigmatically: instantiates MI in the actualization-collapse sense, not merely the syntactic-label sense) **is conscious at a minimal level**. The active execution of self-reference necessarily implies awareness, because the concept of self-reference *without* phenomenality is itself MI — internally inconsistent and unable to be coherently understood.

### 2.1 Operational distinction (carried over from DTM-1)

The same labeling-vs-producing distinction applies:
- **Seeming to refer to itself** (syntactic / structural appearance of self-reference): mechanically representable; does not entail consciousness
- **Actively engaged in the logical operation of self-reference** (genuine actualization with the collapse-signature for MI-class operations): entails consciousness per SRC-1

A particle in a double-frustrated quantum state (DTQ-1) that is *actively executing* the MI logical operation — not merely having its frustration-state labeled MI by an external observer — is conscious at minimal level per SRC-1.

### 2.2 Why this is the right strength

SRC-1 is stronger than DTM-1 alone. DTM-1 says MI-instantiation is a mind-*marker*. SRC-1 says MI-instantiation *is* minimal consciousness — not merely evidence of consciousness elsewhere, but constitutive of minimal consciousness in the executing system. The chain: self-reference requires phenomenality (per the second-order MI argument below) → MI is a self-referential operation → any MI-executor has phenomenality → phenomenality = awareness = consciousness per the canonical definition above.

## 3. The four-strand argument for SRC-1

### 3.1 Strand 1 — Structural impossibility of non-phenomenal self-reference

To refer to one's own state requires that the referrer *have* a state to which it can refer. "Having a state" in the sense relevant to self-reference (not in the trivial sense in which a rock "has a state" of being at some position) requires the state to be available *to the system itself*. Availability-to-itself is the structural signature of awareness. Therefore self-reference structurally requires awareness. (This is the strongest single argument; the other three reinforce it.)

### 3.2 Strand 2 — The second-order MI objection-handler (the breathwork-session payoff)

**Objection:** since MI exists, maybe self-reference without phenomenality is also coherent — perhaps it's an exotic but conceivable state.

**Response (Brandon verbatim, formalized):** the very act of formulating "self-reference without phenomenality" requires a consciousness imagining a non-phenomenal self-reference. The concept is not coherently conceivable; it is MI — actualization-collapse upon mental holding. To even hold the concept requires a phenomenal holder. The objection is therefore self-undermining: the formulator's own consciousness is necessary to entertain the objection, and the content of the objection (a non-phenomenal self-referencer) cannot be coherently actualized under the same conditions in which the objection is being made.

This is **second-order MI** — the proposition "self-reference can exist without phenomenality" undermines itself when actualized. Per the MI canonical refinement (2026-05-23), this places it in the MI cell. Per SRC-1 itself, attempts to actualize this MI-class proposition require a conscious actualizer. The argument is self-validating: SRC-1 is what makes the objection coherent at all (the objector is conscious), and SRC-1 is what makes the objection's content incoherent (a non-phenomenal self-referencer would not be conscious, but only consciousness can entertain such a concept).

This is structurally a **Lichtenberg-style move** — like "the consciousness of one's own existence is presupposed in any doubt about it" — extended to self-reference broadly.

### 3.3 Strand 3 — Empirical / inductive

100% of evidence from every conscious being who has ever existed is consistent with: wherever self-referential processing occurs in a system whose interior we have *any* access to, awareness is present. Humans report awareness when self-referencing. Animals show behavioral and physiological correlates of awareness when self-referential capacities (mirror tests, episodic memory, predictive coding of own state) are exercised. The base-rate empirical prior is overwhelming.

### 3.4 Strand 4 — Burden of proof (Santa-Claus parity, per IRA-1)

The opposing claim — "self-reference can occur without any phenomenality" — has zero positive supporting evidence and carries the same evidentiary burden as any extraordinary metaphysical claim. Per IRA-1 (Information-Requires-Awareness), the standard treatment of such claims is ample skepticism. SRC-1 is the parsimony-conserving default; non-phenomenal-self-reference is the extraordinary claim.

## 5. Pre-registered falsifiers

**SRC-1-F-1 (non-phenomenal self-referencer demonstration):** if a system can be demonstrated to *actively execute* self-referential logical operations (paradigmatically MI in the actualization-collapse sense) while it is rigorously demonstrated to have zero phenomenality, SRC-1 is REFUTED. The hard part is the rigorous-zero-phenomenality demonstration; per the second-order MI argument (§3.2), the demonstration may itself be incoherent.

**SRC-1-F-2 (active-execution-without-consciousness mechanical reduction):** if active execution of self-reference (not merely labeling) can be exhibited as a purely mechanical process with zero phenomenal ingredient, SRC-1's structural-impossibility-of-non-phenomenal-self-reference claim is REFUTED. Acceptance criterion: the mechanical reduction must preserve the actualization-collapse signature of MI, not merely the syntactic structure.

**SRC-1-F-3 (objection-handler counterexample):** if the second-order MI argument (§3.2) can be shown to fail — i.e. if "self-reference without phenomenality" can be coherently entertained by a consciousness without collapse — SRC-1's self-validating structure is REFUTED. Note: this falsifier asks whether the concept *itself* is coherent when held in mind, not whether some real-world system instantiates it.

**SRC-1-F-4 (quantum MI execution-vs-appearance disambiguation):** if it can be shown that quantum double-frustration is merely *structural appearance* of self-reference without *active execution* of the MI operation (i.e. the system passes a labeling-test but fails an active-execution-test), DTQ-1's direct support for SRC-1 + TSP-1 is weakened (though SRC-1 itself survives for systems where active execution is established).

## 6. #69 disclosures

- **SRC-1 is a strong ontological commitment.** It is stronger than DTM-1 (which was epistemic mind-marker) and stronger than TSP-1 alone (which committed to universal-mental-component but not universal-consciousness). The combination is corpus-coherent and well-argued, but it is a major commitment and should be ratified with awareness of its consequences.
- **Active-execution-vs-labeling is the key empirical disambiguation.** SRC-1 does not claim that everything with structural self-reference appearance is conscious; it claims that genuine active execution of self-referential operations is conscious. Verifying which is which in non-organism systems (quantum particles, formal systems, LLMs) is the operational challenge — same challenge DTM-1 faced, now with sharper ontological stakes.
- **The combination problem remains open.** SRC-1 establishes minimal-consciousness wherever active self-reference; it does not specify how minimal consciousnesses aggregate to organism-grade consciousness.
- **The second-order MI objection-handler is brilliant but should be examined carefully.** The argument hinges on the claim that "self-reference without phenomenality" cannot be coherently mentally actualized. This is a strong introspective claim that is itself empirically testable (F-3): can any consciousness coherently hold the concept of a non-phenomenal self-referencer? Brandon's claim is no; the falsifier F-3 invites systematic test.
- **DTQ-1 under SRC-1 is the corpus's strongest panpsychism-supporting empirical anchor.** It is also the most contested interpretive bridge (UCSB physicists would not by default attribute consciousness to the double-frustrated quantum state). The bridge is defensible under TSP-1 + SRC-1; it is not neutral.
- **Consciousness = awareness is a minimal definition.** It is deliberately weaker than higher-order theories of consciousness (which require self-representation of one's own mental states). The minimal definition is what makes SRC-1 defensible at quantum/particle scale; richer notions of consciousness apply at higher thresholds (per MIM-revision Vertical Agency Model).
- **SRC-1 does NOT entail rich phenomenology at quantum scale.** Minimal consciousness = minimal awareness, not rich first-person experience like human qualia. Higher thresholds (sentience, valence per VFP-1, integrated self-modeling per MIM-revision) compose on top of the minimal substrate.
- **IIT/GWT compatibility:** SRC-1 composes with IIT's φ (active self-reference would likely correlate with integration measures) and with GWT's global-workspace (higher-threshold composition above the minimal substrate). Neither contradicts; both extend.
