The original BOK graph predicted that the four HEM-dimension loops and the four GILE-dimension loops, while potentially varying in individual concept-to-concept mappings (linear or nonlinear), maintain a **collective ratio invariance**: the aggregated HEM-weight to aggregated GILE-weight ratio is stable across a domain or a successful practitioner within that domain. ## 1. The Prediction Stated

**Collective Ratio Invariance (CRI):**

For any domain D or any successful practitioner P within D, the aggregate ratio:

```
R(D) = Σᵢ GILE-weight_i(D)  /  Σⱼ HEM-weight_j(D)
```

is stable and empirically recoverable. Individual loop-to-loop mappings (e.g., HEM-loop-k to GILE-loop-m) may be linear or nonlinear, but the collective sum over all four HEM loops and all four GILE loops yields a reliable domain signature.

**Key implications:**
- Domains have characteristic GILE:HEM ratios
- Successful practitioners in a domain exhibit that domain's ratio
- Individual concept-level mappings can vary without disrupting the collective signature
- Loops of equal length correspond to each dimension being maxed out (already established) — NOT equal weighting
- The collective ratio is the domain's structural fingerprint

## 2. Distinguishing Loop-Length from Loop-Ratio

A critical clarification from prior TI Sigma work:

**Loop length (already established):** Each of the four HEM loops or four GILE loops being equal in length indicates that dimension is maxed out — the entity has fully developed that dimension. Equal loop length ≠ equal weighting across dimensions.

**Loop ratio (this URB's prediction):** The collective sum of the four HEM loops compared to the collective sum of the four GILE loops gives a domain-specific ratio. This ratio can be 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, etc. — and it is the domain's structural signature.

These are orthogonal properties:
- A pure mathematician can have all four GILE loops maxed (GILE loops equal length) AND have a GILE:HEM collective ratio of 3:1 (much more GILE substance than HEM substance in their cognitive economy)
- A carpenter can have all four HEM loops maxed AND have a HEM:GILE collective ratio of 3:1

## 4. Empirical Strategy: Recovering Ratios from Data

### 4a. HEM Weights — Direct Measurement

HEM weights are empirically accessible because they measure existential substrate signals:
- Physical presence, embodied activity, material engagement
- Neural activation patterns (fMRI, EEG) during domain-engaged work
- Behavioral frequency, material output, resource consumption
- Duration of engagement, physical location, bodily involvement

These map directly to the four HEM loops (body, material, environment, relational embodiment — exact loop definitions per original BOK graph).

### 4b. GILE Weights — Proxy Inference

GILE weights are not directly observable but are inferable through reliable proxies:

**G (Goodness) proxies:**
- Ethical track record, pro-social outcomes, alignment with existential affirmation
- Willingness to accept cost for principle
- Long-term value generation rather than short-term extraction

**I (Intuition/Insight) proxies:**
- Novel problem recognition, anomaly detection, pattern completion beyond training data
- Speed of GINO-nucleus access in unfamiliar problems
- Quality of first-move decisions under uncertainty

**L (Love) proxies:**
- Deep engagement quality, sustained attention to specific objects
- Relational depth, mentorship output, disciple reproduction
- Emotional investment tracked over time

**E (Environment) proxies:**
- Context-sensitivity, system-level awareness
- Integration with broader ecology of the domain
- Responsiveness to feedback from environmental signals

### 4c. The Successful Practitioner Inference

A successful practitioner in domain D has, by definition, achieved enough alignment with D's structural requirements to produce domain-valid output consistently. Their cognitive-existential profile has been selected for fit with D's HEM:GILE signature.

**This means:** their individual profile is a data point for D's characteristic ratio. Measuring R(P) for successful P across many P in D yields R(D) with statistical confidence.

Just as we infer fitness landscapes from successful organisms, we infer domain GILE:HEM ratios from successful practitioners.

## 5. Testing Protocol Outline

**Phase 1 — Domain selection.** Choose 4–6 domains with clear success criteria and measurable outputs (pure math, physics, philosophy, engineering, trade craft, athletics).

**Phase 2 — Practitioner identification.** For each domain, identify 20–50 recognized successful practitioners with sufficient biographical/behavioral data.

**Phase 3 — HEM profile measurement.** For each practitioner, compute HEM-loop weights from observable substrate signals (time allocation, embodied activity, material engagement, neural data where available).

**Phase 4 — GILE proxy measurement.** For each practitioner, compute GILE-loop weights from proxy data (G: ethical track record; I: novel-problem performance; L: engagement depth; E: context-sensitivity).

**Phase 5 — Ratio computation.** R(P) = Σ GILE_P / Σ HEM_P for each practitioner P.

**Phase 6 — Domain aggregation.** Compute mean and variance of R(P) within each domain. Test whether within-domain variance < between-domain variance (the CRI prediction).

**Phase 7 — Concept-level analysis.** Examine individual loop-to-loop mappings for each practitioner to confirm: individual mappings vary (linear/nonlinear), collective ratio stable.

**Passing CRI test:** Within-domain variance in R significantly less than between-domain variance; domain means cluster at predicted ratios (or at consistent empirical values).

**Failing CRI test:** R is uniform across all domains, or varies without domain-level clustering, or individual mappings dominate the collective signal.
