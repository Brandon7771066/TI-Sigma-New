## The question (verbatim, dated 2026-05-06)

> Free will and consciousness are heavily related since they both involve self-origination, such as that found in a Markov Chain. Is there evidence of Markov Chain origination processing in the brain to support libertarian-based consciousness and free will? What is required in a hypothetical neural network for self-generated action, and does the brain meet the criteria?

## Working position (in-session, ~2 hr of agent thought, 0 API calls)

See `INSIGHTS_2026-05-06.md` §3. Summary:

- Higher-order non-Markovian self-modeling networks with system-generated noise and globally accessible workspace are closer.

## Falsifiable subquestions worth designing for

1. Does prefrontal-cortex BOLD/MEG show **non-Markovian autocorrelation structure** during volitional decision windows that exceeds what Markov-1 modeling would predict?
2. Are **agentic-feeling decisions** (high subjective ownership) accompanied by larger TJ-correlate signals (e.g., higher PFC-thalamic information integration) than non-agentic equivalents controlled for motor difficulty?
3. In RL agents trained on the same task, does adding **system-generated history-modulated noise** improve performance vs Markov-1 stochastic policy gradient — and does the resulting agent's behavior look more "free" by behavioral indistinguishability tests?

## Why this matters for the TI Framework

If the brain demonstrably exhibits non-Markovian self-origination beyond what classical neuroscience predicts, this is direct empirical support for the GILE-I (Intuition) dimension as *operationally distinguishable* from classical processing. Without it, GILE-I risks being indistinguishable from "noise we don't yet understand."
