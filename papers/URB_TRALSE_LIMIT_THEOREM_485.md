## Abstract

This paper formalizes the **Tralse Limit Theorem**: the claim that every proposition, every physical law, and every conceivable constraint has a non-zero degree of tralsity. No proposition achieves τ = 0 or τ = 1 exactly. "Binary" claims are permitted in TI Sigma as efficient rhetoric — *sufficiently true* — but the deep ontological structure is always continuous, never exactly discrete. Even the most stringent physical constant must, by necessity, have a probability of being violated — even if that probability is 1 in a googolplex or smaller.

**PRIMARY CONSTANT deployed:** 0 (the unreachable lower bound of violation probability; asymptotically approached, never reached)

## 1. Statement of the Theorem

**Tralse Limit Theorem:**

For every proposition P in any domain whatsoever:

> τ(P) ∈ (0, 1)   strictly — the open interval, excluding both endpoints.

Equivalently:

> ∀ P: τ(P) ≠ 0   AND   τ(P) ≠ 1

No proposition has exactly zero tralsity (perfectly False). No proposition has exactly unit tralsity (perfectly True). The endpoints 0 and 1 are **limit points** of the tralsity spectrum — they can be approached arbitrarily closely, but never reached.

**Immediate corollary:**

For every physical law L, defining P_L as the proposition "L holds in this instance":

> ∃ ε > 0 : P(violation of L) ≥ ε

The violation probability cannot be exactly zero for any law in any domain.

**Claim:** CCC permits tralsity in itself.

**Argument:** Perfectionism — the insistence on binary, zero-variance, exact outcomes — would be a *constraint* on CCC. A being constrained by the requirement of exactness would be less than a being that operates freely across the full tralsity spectrum, including near-zero and near-unit values. The CCC of URB #484 is *antifragile* — its floor holds under volatility, and it does not demand exactness above its floor. The eternal God-cell has a floor at +2, not a ceiling or a point value.

### 2.3 CCC's Non-Perfectionism Flows Into All Physical Laws

**Claim:** Since CCC permits tralsity in itself, CCC permits tralsity in every law downstream of it.

**Argument:** Physical laws are expressions of CCC's permissive structure. They are not imposed on CCC from outside — they are instantiated by CCC's choices about how the world operates. If CCC does not demand exactness in its own nature, CCC does not impose exactness as an absolute constraint on its instantiated laws.

**Formal structure:**
- CCC permits ε-tralsity in its own nature: τ(CCC's own constraints) > 0
- Physical laws are derived from CCC's permissive structure: L ⊆ CCC's instantiation space
- By inheritance of permissiveness: ∀ L, τ(L) > 0
- Therefore: ∀ L, P(violation of L) > 0

The "flow" from CCC to physical laws is not metaphorical. It is a containment argument: if the most fundamental level (CCC) has non-zero tralsity, no derived level can have exactly zero tralsity. Zero tralsity at a derived level would be *more constrained* than CCC itself — which is impossible, since CCC is the greatest conceivable being and therefore the most permissive possible source.

## 3. The Googolplex Caveat

The Tralse Limit Theorem does NOT claim that physical laws are likely to be violated. The violation probabilities can be:

- 1 in 10^(10^100) (one in a googolplex)
- 1 in 10^(10^(10^100)) (one in a googolplex-plex)
- Less than any finite number you can name

The theorem claims only that the probability is **not exactly zero**. The distinction matters ontologically even when it doesn't matter practically:

**Practically:** "Water will freeze at 0°C at standard pressure" is sufficiently true for all purposes. TI Sigma permits saying this. It is *efficient rhetoric*, and efficient rhetoric is legitimate within the framework.

**Ontologically:** The exact probability of water NOT freezing at those conditions is non-zero. It is less than any conceivable practical threshold, but it exists. This is the Tralse Limit Theorem's domain — the deep structure beneath the efficient rhetoric.

**Analogy:** The number 0.000...001 (with a googolplex zeros) is not zero. However close to zero it is, however many significant figures you need to distinguish it from zero, it is not zero. The Tralse Limit Theorem claims that violation probabilities of physical laws occupy exactly this territory — indistinguishable from zero practically, but not zero ontologically.

## 5. Empirical Confirmations

The Tralse Limit Theorem is not only a theoretical claim. Multiple empirical domains confirm it:

### 5.1 Quantum Tunneling
In quantum mechanics, particles penetrate barriers that classical physics declares absolutely forbidden. The probability is exponentially small for macroscopic barriers but is never exactly zero. Quantum tunneling IS the Tralse Limit Theorem in physical form: even "forbidden" processes have non-zero probability. The barrier does not have exactly τ = 0 tralsity as a passage.

### 5.2 Heisenberg Uncertainty
No physical quantity can be known or specified with exact precision. The uncertainty principle guarantees that for any conjugate pair (position/momentum, energy/time), the product of uncertainties is bounded below by ℏ/2. This means even the most precisely stated physical law has an irreducible fuzzing — the law cannot apply with exactly zero variance.

### 5.3 Gödel's Incompleteness
Gödel showed that no formal system is both complete and consistent. Every sufficiently powerful formal system contains true statements that cannot be proven within the system. This is the logical analog of the Tralse Limit Theorem: even the most rigorous logical system cannot achieve τ = 1 for all true propositions. Something always escapes perfect formalization.

### 5.4 Thermodynamic Fluctuations
The Second Law of Thermodynamics (entropy never decreases in a closed system) is violated by thermodynamic fluctuations at the microscopic level. The probability of macroscopic violations decreases exponentially with system size but is never exactly zero. The Second Law — one of the most "ironclad" physical laws — has a non-zero violation probability, exactly as the Tralse Limit Theorem predicts.

### 5.5 Cosmological Constant
The cosmological constant (dark energy density) is not exactly zero, despite many theoretical frameworks predicting it should be. The universe chose an extremely small but non-zero value — consistent with the Tralse Limit Theorem's claim that exact zeros do not appear in physical reality.
