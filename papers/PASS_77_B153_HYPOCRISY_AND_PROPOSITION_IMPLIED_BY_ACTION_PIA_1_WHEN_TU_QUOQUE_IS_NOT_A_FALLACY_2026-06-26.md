> *A hypocrisy accusation is not automatically a* tu quoque *fallacy. This paper builds the demarcation, corrects B132, and runs a small logic-harness over labelled cases.

## 1. The distinction

Two things are routinely conflated:

- **Mere *tu quoque*** (genuinely fallacious): "You do X, therefore your claim that X-is-bad is false." The opponent's behaviour is **irrelevant to the truth of the claim**. (A smoker can correctly argue that smoking is unhealthy.)
- **Performative self-refutation via the Proposition-Implied-by-Action** (legitimate): the opponent's thesis claims **universal scope that includes the opponent's own act/belief**, and that act instantiates the **negation** of a proposition the thesis **requires** to hold universally. Here the hypocrisy is not a mood-point — it is a **counterexample to a universal**, and the counterexample is the opponent's own conduct.

The decisive variable is **self-scope**. A claim with no universal self-application (smoking-is-unhealthy) cannot be touched by the arguer's conduct. A claim that quantifies over *everyone* ("there are no objective truths," "no one is responsible," "all rational belief is Bayesian") **ranges over its own author and its own assertion**, so the author's contrary act is *inside the claim's domain* and falsifies it.

## 2. PIA-1 (candidate, NOT ratified)

**PIA-1 — Proposition-Implied-by-Action.**

> An agent's action `a` **asserts** a proposition `prop(a)` (the proposition whose truth the action presupposes or enacts). "Actions speak louder than words" is the claim that, on conflict, `prop(a)` is better evidence of the agent's real commitments than their stated thesis `T`.
>
> **Legitimacy criterion (defeater test).** A hypocrisy/PIA charge against a thesis `T` is a **legitimate defeater** iff ALL of:
> 1. **self_scope** — `T` claims universal scope that includes the agent's own act/belief (`T` quantifies over everyone, hence over the agent);
> 2. **action_negates** — the agent's action instantiates `¬q` for some `q` that `T` **requires** to hold universally;
> 3. **targets_thesis** — the charge is aimed at `T`'s *truth* via this self-contradiction (performative self-refutation), not at a claim's *validity via its origin*.
>
> Otherwise the charge is a **fallacy**: a **genetic fallacy** if it judges a claim's validity by its origin while origin is irrelevant; a **mere *tu quoque*** otherwise.
>
> **Circularity corollary.** If A accuses B of circularity, and B counter-accuses A of circularity, B's counter-charge is **legitimate iff it actually defeats A** (A's argument really does presuppose its conclusion). It is *not* auto-disqualified as "you too" — *tu quoque* is fallacious only when the hypocrisy/symmetry charge **fails to defeat**; when it **succeeds**, it is just a correct refutation that happens to mirror the original.

**EVD-1 framing.** PIA-1 charges have **Evidence Status = yes** and **graded Weight** — *potentially* legitimate, decided case-by-case by the three conditions, never automatically valid (that would license every "you too") nor automatically fallacious (the B132 error). This is the same Status-vs-Weight discipline EVD-1 applies everywhere.

## 3. The B132 erratum

> **ERRATUM to `PASS_77_B132_..._2026-06-24.md` §B.4 (the "weak/position-taking" list).**
>
> **(E1)** Argument #12 ("Historical self-contradiction — Bayesianism was not adopted by Bayesian principles") was filed as a **genetic fallacy** ("how Bayesianism was *adopted* says nothing about its *validity*"). **This is mistaken** when the target is **Bayesianism-as-a-universal-norm** ("ALL rational belief-revision *must* proceed by Bayesian conditioning"). That doctrine **quantifies over its own adoption**; since the adoption was *not* itself Bayesian (it rested on intuition, informal argument, and free choice of priors/framework), the doctrine asserts the negation of one of its own universal requirements ⇒ **legitimate performative self-refutation (PIA-1)**, not a genetic fallacy.
>
> **(E2)** The genetic-fallacy label is **correct only for a different target**: the **THEOREM** (the identity `P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B)`). A *proved* theorem's validity is origin-independent, so attacking it via "Bayes reached it by intuition" *is* a genuine genetic fallacy. B132 conflated the **doctrine** (self-scoped, PIA-vulnerable) with the **theorem** (origin-independent). The distinction is exactly B132's own elsewhere-stated point that *"Bayes-theorem ≠ Bayesian-doctrine."*
>
> **(E3)** Argument #13 ("Self-defeat") was filed as **equivocation**. Refinement: there is a genuine **performative tension** for any *universal* version of the doctrine (a norm that forbids its own non-Bayesian adoption). B132's reading — "a Bayesian *lowering* credence in 'Bayesianism-as-complete' when shown failures is Bayesianism *working*" — remains correct **only for the modest, non-universal version** (Bayes as one tool among many). The universal version is self-defeating (E1); the modest version is not. The two must be kept apart.
>
> **#69 guard (no over-correction).** None of this shows "Bayesianism is refuted" tout court. It shows the **universal-normative** version is PIA-vulnerable; the **modest** version (and the theorem) survive. The charge is a *legitimate potential defeater of a specific scope of the doctrine*, graded by EVD-1 — not a blanket dismissal.

## 4. Applications (each is a self-scoped universal — so PIA-vulnerable)

| Thesis (universal) | Action implying `¬q` | Why it is a legitimate defeater (not *tu quoque*) |
|---|---|---|
| "There are **no** objective truths." | Asserts *this* as an objective truth. | The claim ranges over itself; its assertion enacts the negation. Classic self-referential incoherence. |
| "**No one** is ever morally responsible (no free will)." | Sincerely **blames/resents** people. | Blame presupposes the responsibility the thesis universally denies; the blamer's own practice is the counterexample. |
| "There are **no** moral facts." | Categorically condemns an act as *really* wrong. | Categorical condemnation presupposes the moral facts denied. |
| "Values are **fictions** — none really exist; I just *act as if* they do because it's *optimal*." (fictionalism) | Calls a policy *optimal*; sustains a *will to live*. | "Optimal" is itself a value (*optimal by what standard?*), and willing-to-live *enacts* an adopted normative principle — so denying one *has* values is self-contradicted. **Scope:** refutes value-*lessness* (a fact about the valuer), **not** anti-realism about *mind-independent* facts (the careful quasi-realist still escapes that). |
| "The external physical world is **unreal**." | Eats when hungry; dodges moving cars. | Each survival action asserts the world's reality (G. E. Moore, "Proof of an External World," 1939). |
| "**All** rational belief-revision must be Bayesian." | The doctrine was adopted non-Bayesianly. | The universal ranges over its own adoption; adoption violated it (the B132 case). |

**Contrast — genuine fallacies (controls):** "you smoke, so smoking isn't unhealthy" (no self-scope; *tu quoque*); "Bayes reached his theorem by intuition, so the theorem is suspect" (origin-judging a proved identity; **genetic**); "the scientist is pharma-funded, so ignore the safety data" (circumstantial/**genetic**). These are exactly the cases the criterion must *reject*, and does (§5).
