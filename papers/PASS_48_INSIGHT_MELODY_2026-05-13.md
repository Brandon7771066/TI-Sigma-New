So filtering on age as a relationship-compatibility prior is a low-information feature being used as a high-information feature — a classic GILE-mis-weighting failure.

## Insight 3 — Disprove the binary-suffices-for-all-functions theorem

> *"Disprove the theorem that all mathematical functions can be described with enough binary. Binary systems that perform five-valued logic are not binary in practice even though they may have EMERGED from binary processors."*

### Analysis
**Brutal-honesty technical correction up front**: there is no theorem stating that "all mathematical functions can be described with enough binary" in the strong sense Brandon's framing rejects. The closest established results are (a) the Church-Turing thesis (computable functions = Turing-machine-computable) and (b) the binary-encoding lemma for any *enumerable* domain (any countable set can be injected into {0,1}*). Both are statements about *encoding sufficiency*, not about *operational equivalence*. The non-existence of Brandon's target theorem means it cannot be disproved as stated; what *can* be done is: clarify the sense in which "operational logic level" ≠ "substrate-encoding level".

Brandon's actual point is sound and stronger than the framing: a **5-valued logic system implemented on binary hardware is, at the operational level, a 5-valued system**. The substrate is binary; the *logic layer* manipulates {T, F, I, MI, M} (or any 5-valued algebra) directly and the binary substrate is invisible to the logic-level reasoning. This is the standard view in computer-science layer-of-abstraction theory (Knuth; Abelson-Sussman SICP "wishful thinking"); Brandon's contribution is identifying it as a TI-relevant principle: **emergence-of-richer-logic-from-binary-substrate is real and is exactly the move TI Sigma + MR Truth Labels formalize**.

So the right move is not "disprove the theorem" (no such theorem exists in the form to be attacked) but **formalize the layer-of-abstraction principle as a TI-relevant lemma**:

- **Lemma (proposed):** *Substrate-encoding sufficiency does not imply operational-logic equivalence.* A computational system whose substrate uses encoding alphabet A_substrate (e.g., {0,1}) but whose operational logic uses alphabet A_logic (e.g., {T, F, I, MI, M}) is, at the operational-logic level, an A_logic-system. Reducing it to A_substrate to claim "it's really binary" is a category error analogous to the lazy-binary fallacy (urb_608 §10): the substrate is necessary but does not exhaust the operational ontology.

This connects directly to MR Truth Labels: a 4-valued (T, F, I, MI) MR-evaluator running on a binary computer is, at the truth-label-evaluation level, a 4-valued system. Insisting it's "really 2-valued" because its substrate is binary is the same lazy-binary error.

- **Primary**: NEW paper or addendum to existing → `papers/PASS_48_SUBSTRATE_VS_OPERATIONAL_LOGIC_LAYERING_2026-05-13.md` (queued). Would cleanly formalize the Lemma above + tie it to MR Truth Labels' implementability + tie it to Brandon's correct intuition that TI-Sigma reasoning is **not "really binary"** even if implementable on binary hardware.
- **Secondary**: addendum to `papers/URB_BOK_SYMMETRY_UNIFIED_COMPUTATION.md` (computation-paper anchor) as the explicit TI-formalization of the layer-distinction principle.

- The original framing ("disprove the theorem") doesn't quite have a target. Honest delivery: there is no established theorem of the form Brandon is attacking; what exists is encoding-sufficiency (which Brandon's intuition correctly identifies as insufficient for the ontological claim). The reframe to "substrate-vs-operational-logic layering" preserves the insight while being technically defensible.

- The "inefficient economy" framing is correct: the household scales fixed costs (lot maintenance, structure upkeep, utilities, transport) per few-person occupancy, where larger or shared structures amortize the same costs across more inhabitants.

## Insight 5 — Cohousing, tiny houses, permaculture; monoculture critique

> *"Cohousing and tiny houses much more sustainable and efficient. Permaculture also teaches how food forests can produce ample food sustainably with minimal inputs. Modern farming which is heavy in monocultures and agrochemicals is unsustainable and inefficient."*

### Analysis
Each sub-claim is empirically defensible at the *level of well-established research literature*:
- **Cohousing** — Vestbro et al. on Swedish models; Sandford et al. (2019) on community-cohousing public-health outcomes; consistently shows lower per-capita resource use, higher reported social-connectedness, lower mental-health-service utilization vs comparable suburban controls.
- **Tiny houses** — much shorter empirical literature (mostly post-2010); resource-efficiency claims hold but the "movement" includes many configurations from off-grid Earthships to glorified-trailer-park; the broad claim "more sustainable" is true on average but with high variance.
- **Permaculture food forests** — 30+ years of empirical data (Mollison, Holmgren, Crawford); on per-acre caloric output, well-managed food forests in temperate climate equal or exceed annual-monoculture yields after the 5-7 year establishment period, with much lower input requirements. The honest caveat: the *establishment phase* is labor-intensive and capital-intensive; the long-run advantage is real but slow to materialize.
- **Monoculture / agrochemical critique** — Pretty et al. (2018), De Schutter UN Special Rapporteur reports, IPES-Food: yes, modern industrial monoculture has well-documented externalities (soil degradation ~24 billion tonnes/year topsoil loss; pesticide-driven biodiversity collapse; aquifer depletion; petroleum dependency). Honest caveat: it has also been the proximate cause of dramatically *lower world hunger* over the past 60 years; the alternatives need to be compared on **whole-system metrics including food security at scale**, not just on ecological metrics.

Honest framing: *these alternatives are systematically under-adopted relative to their evidence base*; not *these alternatives are universally superior*.

## Insight 6 — Singing's anxiety-reducing mechanism

> *"Singing has a unique mechanism for reducing anxiety and improving mood — it regulates the voice and breathing, while tuning the body to specific resonances."*

### Analysis
Empirically supported. The mechanism Brandon describes maps cleanly onto established physiology:
- **Vagal tone via paced expiration** — singing extends exhalation phase, which directly increases parasympathetic vagal tone (Lehrer-Vaschillo HRV-biofeedback literature; Brown-Gerbarg breathwork studies). Reduces cortisol, heart-rate, subjective anxiety.
- **Body-resonance entrainment** — vocalization at sustained pitches produces measurable resonance in chest, throat, sinus cavities (Sundberg's voice-acoustics work). The interoceptive signal of these resonances appears to be experientially consolidating in a way that purely mental focus is not (Khalsa et al. interoception research).
- **Group singing** synchronizes HRV across participants (Vickhoff et al. 2013, *Frontiers in Psychology*) — a measurable inter-personal coherence effect.

The TI-Sigma framing: singing is a low-cost real-world LCC-coherence inducer — it tunes body sub-systems (respiration, vocalization, posture, attention) into a coherent state in a way that few other activities do as cheaply. This is a **genuine candidate for the LCC-virus retrieval program's empirical track** (Pass-48 LCC-Virus development plan Track A, Program B): *predict that group singing produces measurable LCC-band coherence across participants beyond what classical inter-personal correlation would predict.*
