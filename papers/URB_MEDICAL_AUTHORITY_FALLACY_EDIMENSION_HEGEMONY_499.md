### I.2 Publication Bias — The Medical Literature Is Not What It Appears

A study examining reported versus actual FDA drug trial results found:

- **94%** of drug studies in the medical literature reported positive results
- **51%** of actual FDA studies (unpublished or FDA-only data) showed positive results
- The gap: a **32% fabricated effect size**, systematically embedded in the literature that doctors rely on

This is not error. It is structural. Positive studies are published; negative studies disappear into file drawers. Pharmaceutical companies fund studies, control data access, and have financial incentive to select which results reach the literature. Doctors trained on this literature are trained on a systematically biased corpus — one that overstates drug efficacy by approximately one-third.

### I.3 The Psychiatric Replication Crisis

Psychiatric diagnosis replication rates — the probability that two independent clinicians assign the same diagnosis to the same patient — sit at approximately **50%** across the major diagnostic categories in the DSM. For some categories the figure is lower.

50% replication means a coin flip. It means that which diagnosis a patient receives depends substantially on which clinician they see, on which day, in which clinic, in which country. The diagnosis that then determines:

- Which medications are prescribed (and at what doses)
- Whether the patient is involuntarily hospitalized
- What their insurance will cover
- How they are perceived by employers, family, and the legal system
- How they understand themselves

...is determined by a process with the reliability of a coin toss.

And yet: patients who question their diagnosis, who self-research, who compare their experience against AI-synthesized literature, who reason about their own medication — are routinely dismissed as lacking the training to evaluate their own condition.
