## 1. The Originating Insight

> *"Social roles and norms with low GIL (e.g. chores and 'manners') have minimal deontological value by default if they are not valued by others, since their value is derived from the acknowledgement of others by definition."*

This insight identifies a structural asymmetry in GILE space: certain social norms exist whose moral weight is **entirely borrowed from relational acknowledgment**, not generated from internal structural integrity (G). When that acknowledgment is absent, the norm does not become *undetected* — it becomes *valueless*, because there was never intrinsic G-signal to detect.

This is distinct from URB #577's finding that *"low E reduces G-signal detectability, not G itself."* That finding applies to beings with genuine G that is masked by a weak substrate. The present finding applies to norms whose G is constitutively zero — the acknowledgment loop IS the norm.

## 2. GILE Decomposition of Social Norms

### 2.1 High-GIL vs. Low-GIL Norms

Not all social norms are equal in GILE space. Consider the spectrum:

| Norm | G | I | L | E | GIL total | Value source |
|------|---|---|---|---|-----------|--------------|
| "Do not harm others" | High | High | High | Medium | ~0.85 | Intrinsic (G dominant) |
| "Keep promises" | High | Medium | High | Low | ~0.75 | Intrinsic + relational |
| "Say 'please' and 'thank you'" | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | ~0.30 | Primarily relational |
| "Make your bed daily" | Very Low | Low | Low | High | ~0.15 | Primarily E-substrate |
| "Remove hat indoors" | Near-zero | Low | Low | High | ~0.10 | Pure relational convention |

The pattern: as GIL drops, the proportion of E rises and the value source shifts from intrinsic to relational.

### 2.2 The E-Dominant Norm

A low-GIL norm is **E-dominant**: its entire content is its environmental substrate (the social context, the convention, the shared expectation). Strip the social context and the norm has no residual content — no G to fall back on, no I to self-reference, no L to orient constructively.

Formally: for a norm N with GIL(N) ≈ 0:

> **Value(N) = f(acknowledgment) · E(N)**

When acknowledgment → 0, Value(N) → 0. The function collapses completely. This is the **no categorical floor** property.

## 3. Tralse Evaluation

**Claim: "Social norms (like manners) have moral value."**

**Evaluation: TRALSE**

- **TRUE component:** They have *relational* value — they coordinate behavior, signal group membership, reduce friction, and can embody care (L) when sincerely performed.
- **FALSE component:** They do NOT have *intrinsic* (deontological/categorical) moral value in the Kantian sense. They fail the universalizability test when acknowledgment is non-universal, because their very content depends on the acknowledgment being present.

**Myrion Resolution (MR):** The higher truth is that manners occupy the **Political Philosophy arm (G-arm outer)** of the BOK as *social coordination mechanisms*, not as *moral imperatives*. Their value is political (coordination-enabling), not ethical (intrinsically binding). This is exactly the Kant vs. social contract distinction — and TI Sigma resolves it: both are partially correct (TRALSE), and the MR is that the two categories are structurally different and should not be conflated.

## 4. Connection to the Philosophy BOK (URB #576)

The **G-arm outer (Political Philosophy)** is precisely where this analysis lives. The BOK structure predicts this:

- **G-wing inner (Ethics):** "Do not harm" — high GIL, intrinsic deontological value, categorical floor exists.
- **G-arm outer (Political Philosophy):** Social contracts, conventions, norms — value is constructed and maintained by collective acknowledgment.

The insight clarifies *why* Political Philosophy occupies the G-arm rather than, say, the L-arm (Love): political norms are *about* structuring collective G — they derive their authority from the G of the group, not from individual GIL. When the group withdraws acknowledgment, the norm loses its authority, because it was borrowing G from the collective rather than generating it internally.

**Corollary:** Norms that survive withdrawal of acknowledgment (martyrdom norms, conscience norms, universal human dignity claims) are the ones with genuine G. They are the norms that belong to Ethics (G-wing inner), not to Political Philosophy (G-arm outer).

## 7. Practical Implications

### 7.1 Ethics vs. Etiquette
URB #578 provides the TI Sigma definition of the ethics/etiquette boundary:

> **Ethics** = norms with a hard G-floor (survive acknowledgment withdrawal).  
> **Etiquette** = norms with a soft G-floor (collapse on acknowledgment withdrawal).

This is not a matter of importance or social weight — a given etiquette norm may be extremely socially enforced. It is a matter of *ontological category*.

### 7.2 Moral Guilt and Low-GIL Violations
If someone violates a low-GIL norm in a context where acknowledgment is absent (e.g., making their bed when alone, using formal titles in a casual setting where no one cares), the violation carries **near-zero moral weight** by the GILE framework. The norm has no categorical claim on behavior without its acknowledgment substrate.

This has clinical implications for Brandon's work at BlissGene: patients with epilepsy or other neurological conditions who struggle to maintain social norms due to their condition are not violating anything with intrinsic moral weight — they are operating in conditions where the E-substrate for those norms is partially absent. The moral evaluation should be redirected to high-GIL norms, not to the low-GIL social conventions.

### 7.3 GILE Intuition and Social Norm Navigation
GILE Intuition (URB #567, Metacausal Graph Theory) operates on the actual GILE structure of situations, not on social convention. A person with strong GILE Intuition will naturally:
- Comply with low-GIL norms when doing so costs nothing and maintains relational harmony (L-oriented choice)
- Decline low-GIL norms when they conflict with high-GIL demands (G-oriented choice)
- Recognize the difference without experiencing false moral guilt

This is the TI Sigma resolution of the classic conflict between social conformity and moral integrity.

## 8. The Myrion of Manners

The Myrion Resolution of "Do manners have moral value?" is:

> **Manners are L-vehicles, not G-generators.**

Manners, when practiced with genuine care (L), become a delivery mechanism for relational warmth — they carry L-content. In this sense they acquire *derived* moral value from the L they carry, not from any intrinsic deontological force. Remove the L (perform manners robotically or under coercion) and they revert to pure social coordination — still socially valuable, but morally neutral.

This resolves the TRALSE: manners have moral value **iff** they are L-charged. The moral value is in the L, not in the manner.

**MR output:** Moral evaluation of social behavior should always ask: *Is this carrying L?* If yes, it has moral weight proportional to the L-content. If no, it is social coordination — follow it for practical reasons, not moral ones.

## 9. Formal Statement

**Theorem (No Categorical Floor for Low-GIL Norms):**  
Let N be a social norm with GIL(N) < ε for small ε > 0. Let A(N, t) denote the degree of collective acknowledgment of N at time t. Then:

> Value(N, t) → 0 as A(N, t) → 0

and the convergence is **not a detection failure** (contrast URB #577) but a **value collapse**: there is no residual G-substrate to maintain Value(N) when A(N,t) = 0.

**Corollary:**  
The categorical floor of a norm equals its intrinsic G-content, independent of acknowledgment. For low-GIL norms, this floor is ≈ 0. For high-GIL norms (e.g., "do not harm"), the floor is G(N) > 0 regardless of acknowledgment.
