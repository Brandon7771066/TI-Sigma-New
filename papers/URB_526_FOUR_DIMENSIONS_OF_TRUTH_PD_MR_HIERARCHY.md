## Part I: The Problem with One-Dimensional Truth

Classical logic has one truth dimension: a proposition P is TRUE or FALSE. Philosophical extensions add probability (P is true with probability p), or multi-valued logic (degrees of truth). But even these remain confined to *epistemic* truth — truth as correspondence with facts.

TI Sigma observes that claims are evaluated along at least four distinct axes simultaneously in actual practice:

**Axis 1: Existential.** Does this claim have real-world causal traction? A claim might be technically correct (logically valid) but existentially hollow — it makes no difference to what actually happens. Conversely, a claim can be imprecise but existentially powerful — it shifts behavior, outcomes, and structures. The LCC measures this. Existential footprint = LCC x PD zone frequency = the claim's combined magnitude and prevalence.

**Axis 2: Moral.** Does this claim align with what is Good? Can it be held consistently with Intuition, Love, and Environment (GILE)? A claim that is existentially powerful but GILE-misaligned is dangerous — it has causal weight but is pointed in the wrong direction. The GILE score G measures this.

**Axis 3: Conscious Meaning/Valence.** Does this claim carry felt significance? Is it meaningful to a conscious i-Cell? Is it positively or negatively valenced — does it add to GILE resonance or subtract from it? This is the phenomenological dimension: not what the claim IS, but what it FEELS LIKE from the inside. PSI (direct CCC access) is the extreme positive end of this axis.

**Axis 4: Aesthetic.** Is this claim elegant? Does it exhibit structural self-coherence? Does it point toward the BOK or away from it? Mathematical beauty, philosophical elegance, and narrative coherence are all Aesthetic truth. A claim that is existentially real, morally aligned, and consciously meaningful but aesthetically incoherent is harder to transmit, teach, and integrate — it has lower Aesthetic truth.

These four axes are not reducible to each other. A claim can be:
- Existentially real, morally wrong (effective evil)
- Morally right, existentially weak (well-intentioned but ineffectual)
- Consciously meaningful, aesthetically ugly (meaningful but hard to articulate)
- Aesthetically beautiful, existentially hollow (elegant but empty)

TI Sigma does not privilege one axis over the others. All four are simultaneously real. The PD governs the distribution of claims across all four axes at once.
