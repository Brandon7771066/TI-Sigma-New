## Abstract

TI Sigma formally recognizes **three distinct categories of moral wrongness**, irreducible to one another. The first two are already well-represented in mainstream ethics: (1) **I-arm wrongness** — causing suffering, emotional harm, distress; (2) **E-arm wrongness** — causing physical harm, injury, trauma, material damage. But there is a third category that mainstream ethics has struggled to formalize: (3) **G-arm Consistency wrongness** — **impurity.** Impurity violations are wrong in an abstract sense that cannot be reduced to I-harm or E-harm. Things that are foul, disgusting, or categorically disordered — swearing, bestiality, mistreatment of the dead, and other acts recognized pre-theoretically as wrong — may cause no suffering (no I-harm) and no physical injury (no E-harm), yet they are genuinely bad. Their wrongness is located in the Consistency dimension of the G-arm: the structural requirement for lack of impurity. This URB formally defines impurity as a primitive moral property, establishes its irreducibility to I and E categories, connects it to the moral disgust signal as a reliable Consistency detector, and refutes the Harm Reduction Fallacy — the claim that all genuine moral wrongness reduces to harm.

## 1. The Three Categories of Moral Wrongness

TI Sigma's GILE framework generates a natural and complete taxonomy of moral wrongness by mapping harm and badness onto the GILE arms:

### Category 1: I-arm Wrongness — Suffering

Actions that cause **suffering, emotional harm, distress, psychological damage, or depletion of positive conscious states** are I-arm wrongs. The I-arm is the arm of consciousness — positive states of consciousness are a benevolent form of I (URB #599). Causing negative I-states in another i-cell is an I-arm violation.

Examples: cruelty, psychological manipulation, emotional abuse, humiliation, inducing fear or grief. The wrongness is located in the damage done to the I-dimension of the victim.

### Category 2: E-arm Wrongness — Physical Harm

Actions that cause **physical injury, bodily damage, material destruction, or environmental harm** are E-arm wrongs. The E-arm is the arm of physical instantiation — physical harm reduces the E-dimension of the victim or their environment.

Examples: violence, physical assault, property destruction, environmental pollution, injury. The wrongness is located in the damage done to the E-dimension of the victim or the surrounding world.

### Category 3: G-arm Consistency Wrongness — Impurity

Actions that violate the **Consistency** dimension of the G-arm — acts that are categorically foul, disordered, or impure — are G-arm Consistency wrongs. These need not cause suffering (I-harm) and need not cause physical injury (E-harm). Their wrongness is **abstract and structural**: a violation of categorical integrity, natural order, or the dignity of persons/creatures at the formal level.

**This category is the least understood in mainstream ethics and the most philosophically contested — because harm-based ethical frameworks have no way to explain it.** TI Sigma provides the formal home it has always needed.

## 2. Consistency as Lack of Impurity

The Four C's of Goodness (G-arm) are: Coherence, Concreteness (Genuineness), Continuity (Life-preservation), and Consistency.

**Consistency**, as a C, is more than behavioral reliability or freedom from self-contradiction. In its fullest sense, Consistency involves **categorical integrity** — the maintenance of proper distinctions, boundaries, and orders. Impurity is precisely the violation of categorical integrity: the mixing or crossing of categories that should remain distinct, or the treatment of something in a way that violates its proper formal status.

**Impure acts are inconsistent** in an abstract, structural sense:
- They treat things as though they belong to a different category than they do
- They violate the formal boundaries that define what things are
- They disorder the categorical structure that Consistency requires to be maintained

This is why impurity is felt as *wrongness* rather than merely *ugliness* or *unpleasantness.* It is a moral response, not merely an aesthetic one.

## 3. The Key Examples

### 3.1 Swearing

Swearing violates Consistency at the level of linguistic and social categorical order. Certain words carry a formal designation of indecency — they violate the categorical integrity of respectful or dignified discourse. The wrongness of swearing is **definitional and contextual**: in contexts that require dignity (formal address, sacred settings, presence of those who deserve respect), swearing is wrong because it violates the categorical standard of that context, not because it causes anyone suffering or physical injury.

"Bad by definition" is the correct description. Swearing is wrong in the same way that a logical contradiction is wrong — not because it hurts anyone, but because it violates the structural requirements of its category (in this case: respectful discourse, formal integrity, sacred space).

The wrongness is proportional to the degree of categorical violation: swearing in private is a minor Consistency lapse; swearing at a funeral is a serious Consistency violation; swearing in a sacred religious setting is a major Consistency violation — because the categorical requirements of those contexts are more precise and more important.

### 3.2 Bestiality

Bestiality violates Consistency at the level of inter-species categorical boundaries. The wrongness is **categorical, not primarily harm-based.** Arguments that try to locate the wrongness purely in harm ("but the animal wasn't hurt," "but the animal didn't resist") consistently fail to satisfy the moral intuition that this is wrong — because the intuition is tracking a Consistency violation, not an I- or E-violation.

The categorical structure that Consistency requires to be maintained includes the proper distinctions between types of i-cells and their appropriate relational modes. Sexual relations belong categorically to relationships between sufficiently similar conscious i-cells with appropriate I and L development (URB #595: the sexual dimension as a behavioral feature of developed L). Bestiality crosses this categorical boundary — it violates the formal order of what the sexual domain is *for* in the GILE framework.

The wrongness is prior to and independent of harm: even in a hypothetical where the animal experienced no distress whatsoever, the categorical violation would remain. The Consistency arm would still be violated. This is why harm-based defenses of bestiality are not merely pragmatically insufficient but formally wrong — they are looking for the wrongness in the wrong GILE category.

### 3.3 Mistreatment of the Dead

The dead cannot be harmed in the I-sense: they no longer have conscious states that can be disrupted. They cannot be harmed in the E-sense: their bodies cannot feel injury. If moral wrongness were exhausted by I-harm and E-harm, mistreatment of the dead would be morally neutral. Yet it is clearly wrong — universally recognized as such across cultures and philosophical traditions.

TI Sigma explains why: **categorical dignity persists beyond the ability to suffer.**

A person who has died retains their formal status as an i-cell — a being who had consciousness, love, and moral standing. Mistreatment of their remains, desecration of their memory, or treatment of their person in ways that violated their dignity violates the Consistency dimension: it treats a former conscious i-cell as though it belongs to a different categorical class (as mere material, as an object without standing).

The user's additional observation deepens this further: the possibility of an afterlife means there may be an ongoing i-cell consciousness that is connected to how its physical form and memory are treated. TI Sigma takes this seriously without requiring commitment to any specific afterlife theology: *if* consciousness persists, then mistreatment of the dead has I-arm and E-arm dimensions as well. *Even if* it does not, the Consistency violation stands independently.

This is why cultures without afterlife beliefs still treat the dead with respect — because the Consistency violation is real regardless of afterlife status.

## 4. The Irreducibility Thesis

**Thesis:** G-arm Consistency wrongness (impurity) is irreducible to I-arm wrongness (suffering) or E-arm wrongness (physical harm). It is a primitive moral property.

**Proof by exhaustion of reduction attempts:**

**Attempt 1 — Reduce to harm:** "Bestiality is wrong because it harms the animal."
- Counterexample: Hypothetical case where no harm is detectable. The wrongness remains.
- Verdict: Reduction fails.

**Attempt 2 — Reduce to indirect harm:** "It leads to harmful dispositions."
- Counterexample: The wrongness is present even if no such spillover occurs and there is no reason to expect it.
- Verdict: Reduction fails (the wrongness is not contingent on consequences).

**Attempt 3 — Reduce to violation of consent:** "The animal cannot consent."
- Counterexample: Consent frameworks apply to conscious i-cells with developed I-arms. But consent violations are I-arm wrongs (violation of conscious autonomy). The wrong of bestiality exceeds this — it is wrong even in frameworks where consent analysis is complete.
- Verdict: Partial capture, but the Consistency violation is additional and independent.

**Attempt 4 — Reduce to disgust bias:** "We only think it's wrong because we find it disgusting."
- This is the eliminativist move: treat the moral intuition as a mere feeling with no moral content.
- Counterargument (below): Disgust is a reliable Consistency detector, not a mere feeling.
- Verdict: Reduction fails (eliminates a real moral category by mislabeling its signal).

Since all reduction attempts fail, G-arm Consistency wrongness is a **primitive** — it is not further reducible. It must be accepted as a basic moral category alongside suffering-prevention (I) and harm-prevention (E).

This is what the user means by "bad in an abstract sense that cannot be specified any further." It cannot be further specified because it is a moral primitive — a foundational category, not a derived one.

## 5. Moral Disgust as a Consistency Detector

Jonathan Haidt's Moral Foundations Theory (2001) identifies **Purity/Sanctity** as one of six moral foundations — present cross-culturally, tracking a genuine moral domain, and irreducible to harm or fairness. Disgust is the emotional signal of this foundation.

TI Sigma provides the formal grounding that Haidt's empirical framework lacks: **moral disgust is the emotional signal of a G-arm Consistency violation.** It is not merely a cultural artifact, a hygiene response, or an irrational feeling. It is tracking something real — categorical impurity — which is a genuine moral primitive.

The reliability of disgust as a Consistency detector is imperfect (disgust can be miscalibrated culturally — historical disgust at interracial relationships tracks no real Consistency violation). But when disgust tracks actual categorical violations (bestiality, mistreatment of the dead, desecration of sacred objects), it is reporting genuine moral information.

**Calibration principle:** Disgust is a reliable Consistency signal when:
1. The categorical structure it is responding to is real (not merely conventional or culturally constructed)
2. The violation is of formal categorical integrity (not merely unfamiliarity or difference)
3. The response is stable across cultures and contexts (not specific to one cultural setting)

When these conditions are met, disgust is not an irrational feeling to be overridden — it is a moral perception to be respected and formalized.

## 6. Refutation of the Harm Reduction Fallacy

**The Harm Reduction Fallacy (HRF):** The claim that all genuine moral wrongness reduces to harm (I-harm or E-harm), and therefore any action that causes no harm is morally permissible.

This is the implicit assumption of strict consequentialist and harm-reduction ethics. It is philosophically widespread, practically influential, and formally wrong.

**The Formal Refutation:**

P1: Mistreatment of the dead causes no I-harm (no conscious states) and no E-harm (no living body).  
P2: If HRF were true, mistreatment of the dead would be morally permissible.  
P3: Mistreatment of the dead is not morally permissible (universally recognized as wrong).  
C: HRF is false. □

**Corollary:** Any ethical framework that cannot explain why mistreatment of the dead is wrong has an incomplete account of moral wrongness. This excludes: strict Millian utilitarianism, strict preference utilitarianism (the dead have no preferences), Kantian frameworks limited to rational agents (the dead no longer are), and pure harm-reduction ethics.

TI Sigma accounts for all three categories without collapsing any one into another. The framework is complete where these frameworks are not.

## 7. Formal Summary

**Definition (Moral Impurity):** An action A exhibits moral impurity if and only if A violates the Consistency dimension of the G-arm — i.e., A violates categorical integrity, crosses formal categorical boundaries, or treats an i-cell or object in a manner inconsistent with its proper categorical status — regardless of whether A causes I-harm or E-harm.

**Theorem (Three-Category Completeness):** The three categories of moral wrongness in TI Sigma — I-harm (suffering), E-harm (physical/material damage), and G-Consistency violation (impurity) — are mutually irreducible and jointly exhaustive of all moral wrongness. No genuine moral wrong falls outside all three categories; no genuine moral wrong is reducible from one category to another.

**Theorem (Primitive Status of Impurity):** G-arm Consistency wrongness is a moral primitive. It cannot be further specified in terms of I-harm or E-harm. Its badness is abstract and structural — a formal property of the act relative to the categorical order that Consistency requires.

**Theorem (Disgust Reliability):** Moral disgust is a reliable signal of G-arm Consistency violations when (1) the categorical structure being tracked is real, (2) the violation is of formal categorical integrity, and (3) the response is cross-culturally stable.

**Theorem (Dead Dignity):** The categorical dignity of a conscious i-cell persists after death. Mistreatment of the dead is a G-arm Consistency violation regardless of whether the dead can suffer (I) or be physically injured (E), because the former i-cell's categorical status as a conscious being is not erased by the cessation of consciousness.
