## Abstract

This paper establishes a formal account of **Valid Hyperbole** as a distinct speech act within TI Sigma — distinct from both lying (which asserts a false pole) and over-hedging (which refuses to commit to any pole). The central claim:

> **A hyperbole is valid when it amplifies one pole of a genuine Tralse in a context where the opposite pole has been systematically over-weighted, for the purpose of restoring epistemic balance and enabling Myrion Resolution.**

The paper proceeds in four parts. First, we distinguish Valid Hyperbole from three related but different speech acts: lying, bluntness (URB #670), and Tralse suppression (Invalid Hyperbole). Second, we formalize the **Valid Hyperbole Theorem** — the precise conditions under which pole amplification is epistemically legitimate. Third, we connect the theorem to the PD (Permissibility Distribution) as a corrective mechanism. Fourth, we illustrate with the canonical example: "not evidence of fragility whatsoever" as applied to a history of survived close calls.

## 1. The Conceptual Landscape: Four Speech Acts

Four speech acts are commonly conflated. TI Sigma distinguishes them precisely.

### 1.1 Lying

A lie asserts a claim that the speaker believes to be false, with the intention of creating a false belief in the listener. In five-valued logic terms: the speaker's internal PD has high mass on FALSE for proposition P, yet the speaker asserts TRUE(P).

Lying is a **MI move** at the communication level: the speaker simultaneously knows P is false (their internal state) and asserts P as true (their public state). This is the Tralse of sincerity — saying-true AND knowing-false — but without the valid excuse of genuine uncertainty. It is a deliberate violation of the L-dimension (Love) in GILE: it treats the listener as a means to be deceived rather than a person to be communicated with.

### 1.2 Over-Hedging (addressed in URB #670)

Over-hedging is the systematic refusal to commit to any pole. The speaker's internal PD may be well-formed (high mass on one value), but the communicated claim is so wrapped in qualifications that no definite content is expressed. This is an epistemic cowardice problem — it is technically not lying (the speaker never asserts something false) but it fails to communicate the speaker's actual belief, which is a form of dishonesty by omission.

Over-hedging fails at the level of **commitment**: the speaker refuses to nail their flag to a pole even when they have a confident view.

### 1.3 Valid Hyperbole (this paper)

Valid Hyperbole is different from both. The speaker:
- Has a genuinely Tralse assessment: BOTH poles are actually true
- Deliberately chooses to amplify one pole beyond its literal weight
- Does so because the context has systematically over-weighted the opposite pole
- Retains internal awareness that the claim is amplified, not literally complete
- Intends for the amplification to restore balance, not to deceive

Valid Hyperbole fails at neither commitment (it commits fully to the amplified pole) nor truth (the amplified pole IS true). It is not a lie and not over-hedging. It is a **PD-corrective speech act** — a deliberate adjustment to compensate for an imbalanced prior.

### 1.4 Invalid Hyperbole (Tralse Suppression)

Invalid Hyperbole occurs when the speaker amplifies one pole of a Tralse AND denies the existence of the other pole, claiming the amplified version is the complete and exclusive truth. This is a MI move: asserting one pole as exhaustive while simultaneously knowing the other pole is real.

The difference from Valid Hyperbole is **meta-awareness and intent**: Valid Hyperbole knows it is amplifying; Invalid Hyperbole claims to be reporting the whole truth.

## 2. The Formal Structure of Tralse in Communication

### 2.1 Communicative Tralse

A **Communicative Tralse** is a situation in which both of the following are simultaneously and genuinely true:

$$P_1: \text{"Subject S exhibits property X"}$$
$$P_2: \text{"Subject S exhibits property NOT-X"}$$

And neither P_1 nor P_2 is merely apparent — both have independent causal/evidential support.

The paradigm case from §5: 
- P_1 (Fragility): "A system with many close calls IS fragile — the vulnerabilities are real, the dangers were real each time."
- P_2 (Indestructibility): "The pattern of survivals IS strong evidence of structural resilience — the returns were not all luck."

Both P_1 and P_2 are true. The situation is a genuine Tralse at the level of characterization. Any *single-pole* description of such a system is, in isolation, incomplete. But this does not mean both poles must be given equal weight in every communicative context.

### 2.2 The Discourse Prior

Define the **Discourse Prior** D_P as the probability distribution over poles that the listener holds *before* receiving the current communication. The Discourse Prior is formed by:
- The listener's previous conversations on this topic
- The dominant framing in the relevant institutional/cultural context
- The listener's own experiential priors
- Media, clinical, and social narrative exposure

The Discourse Prior may be severely imbalanced even when the truth is a genuine Tralse. If years of cultural narrative have hammered one pole relentlessly, the listener's D_P can be:

$$D_P(P_1) \approx 0.95, \quad D_P(P_2) \approx 0.05$$

even when the epistemically correct posterior (given all evidence) should be something like:

$$D_P^*(P_1) \approx 0.55, \quad D_P^*(P_2) \approx 0.45$$

The Discourse Prior is miscalibrated. It has systematically under-weighted one pole of the Tralse.

## 3. The Valid Hyperbole Theorem

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem (Valid Hyperbole):** A hyperbolic assertion of the form "X is entirely/completely/not-at-all Y" is epistemically valid if and only if all of the following conditions hold:

**Condition 1 (Genuine Tralse):** The proposition "S exhibits Y" and the proposition "S does not exhibit Y" are both genuinely true — the situation is a real Tralse, not a false dichotomy.

**Condition 2 (Discourse Prior Imbalance):** The listener's Discourse Prior D_P has systematically over-weighted the pole opposite to the one being amplified. There exists a measurable gap between D_P and the epistemically correct D_P*.

**Condition 3 (Speaker Meta-Awareness):** The speaker is internally aware that the claim is an amplification of one pole, not a literal complete description. The speaker does not believe their own hyperbole to be the full truth.

**Condition 4 (Corrective Intent):** The speaker's intent is to reduce the gap between D_P and D_P* — to move the listener's understanding toward the more balanced posterior, not to create a new imbalance in the opposite direction.

**Condition 5 (Tralse Preservation):** The speaker does not, in the same communicative context, actively suppress or deny the existence of the other pole when it is directly engaged. If the listener asks "but isn't it also true that S is Y in some sense?" the valid hyperbolist acknowledges the Tralse rather than doubling down on suppression.

### 3.2 Validity Score

We can formalize the validity of a hyperbolic claim H as:

$$\text{Validity}(H) = \frac{|D_P - D_P^*|}{|D_P^* - D_H|}$$

Where:
- |D_P − D_P*| = the magnitude of the Discourse Prior imbalance being corrected
- |D_P* − D_H| = the magnitude of the hyperbole's over-correction beyond the true posterior

A Valid Hyperbole has Validity(H) > 1: the correction it applies is smaller than the imbalance it is correcting. An Invalid Hyperbole has Validity(H) < 1: it over-corrects beyond the true posterior, creating a new imbalance.

The maximally valid hyperbole exactly corrects the Discourse Prior to D_P* — it asserts the under-weighted pole so forcefully that the listener arrives at the balanced posterior. In practice, since overcorrection is possible, the valid hyperbolist aims for Validity(H) >> 1 (large imbalance being corrected, modest overcorrection).

## 4. Connection to PD, MR, and HEAR

### 4.1 Valid Hyperbole as PD-Corrective Speech

The Permissibility Distribution (PD) in TI Sigma assigns probability mass across truth values for any event or claim. A listener's D_P is their working PD on the proposition in question. When D_P is severely miscalibrated, the epistemically responsible move is not merely to state the calibrated posterior — it is to apply a corrective force proportional to the miscalibration.

This is what Valid Hyperbole does at the communicative level: it acts as a **PD correction signal**, pushing the listener's implicit distribution toward accuracy by temporarily amplifying the under-represented pole.

### 4.2 Valid Hyperbole and Myrion Resolution

In MR terms, the listener's D_P represents their current truth-state assignment for the proposition. A severely imbalanced D_P is a **Dottie Trap state**: the listener has stabilized at a false metastable equilibrium (one-sided view) that feels complete but is not.

Valid Hyperbole is a **forced MR step**: it provides the epistemic energy needed to push the listener past the false equilibrium toward a more resolved (balanced Tralse-acknowledged) view. Just as kumbhaka (breath retention) in pranayama forces the I-channel open by applying direct pressure, Valid Hyperbole forces the listener's PD toward accuracy by applying amplified counter-pressure.

Without this forceful counter-pressure, the listener's Dottie Trap D_P may never spontaneously resolve. The balanced truth — "it is BOTH fragile AND indestructible" — requires energy to arrive at if one has been told only "it is fragile" for years. Valid Hyperbole provides that energy in the form of an amplified claim.

### 4.3 The HEAR Connection

The HEAR process includes the Emerick Existence Amplification Razor: amplify what exists; prune what does not. At the communicative level, this translates to:

**Amplify the under-weighted truth; prune the dominance of the over-weighted framing.**

Valid Hyperbole is the HEAR principle applied to discourse. When the existing discourse has amplified one pole far beyond its epistemic warrant, HEAR prescribes amplifying the other pole to restore balance. The hyperbole is not excess — it is the appropriate corrective amplitude.

## 5. The Canonical Example: Fragility and Indestructibility

### 5.1 The Tralse

From the previous discussion (URB #677 context):

**P_1 (Fragility pole):** A system — whether a person with a history of suicidal ideation, a species that has survived mass extinctions, or a civilization that has survived nuclear near-misses — IS fragile by definition. The close calls were real. The vulnerabilities are real. The danger was not imaginary.

**P_2 (Indestructibility pole):** The pattern of N survivals is strong Bayesian evidence of structural resilience. After 10 close calls all survived, P(Indestructible) > 99.9% under a flat prior. The Dottie attractor is operating. The I-dimension is demonstrably active.

Both are true. The situation is a genuine Communicative Tralse.

### 5.2 The Discourse Prior Imbalance

The conventional clinical, cultural, and social narrative has, for decades, hammered the fragility pole relentlessly:
- Clinical framing: "history of SI = elevated future risk" (P_1 dominant)
- Medical framing: "near-extinction events = evidence of precarity" (P_1 dominant)
- Cultural framing: "close calls are warnings, not evidence" (P_1 dominant)

A person who has lived under this framing has D_P heavily weighted toward P_1. The P_2 pole — indestructibility, Bayesian evidence of resilience, robust I-dimension — is essentially unrepresented in their prior.

### 5.3 The Valid Hyperbole

The statement: **"Not evidence of fragility whatsoever"** is a hyperbole. Literally it is false — the close calls ARE evidence of fragility (P_1 is true). But it is a VALID hyperbole because:

1. **Genuine Tralse:** P_1 and P_2 are both true (check)
2. **Discourse Prior Imbalance:** D_P is dominated by P_1; P_2 is near zero (check)
3. **Speaker Meta-Awareness:** The speaker knows P_1 is true and is deliberately amplifying P_2 (check)
4. **Corrective Intent:** The amplification aims to move the listener toward D_P* ≈ (0.55, 0.45) from D_P ≈ (0.95, 0.05) (check)
5. **Tralse Preservation:** When directly engaged ("but isn't it also true that close calls indicate risk?"), the speaker immediately acknowledges: "Yes — that is valid. The truth is a Tralse" (check)

The Valid Hyperbole is maximally efficient here: a softer statement ("close calls are ALSO evidence of resilience") would not penetrate the heavily imbalanced Discourse Prior. The amplified form cuts through the noise and delivers the corrective signal at the required amplitude.

### 5.4 The Immediate Self-Correction

Crucially, the speaker immediately identified and named the hyperbole:

> *"'Not evidence of fragility whatsoever' is a VALID HYPERBOLE. The truth is that a system with many close calls is fragile by definition... but the other end of the tralsity is what ought to be emphasized hyperbolically to get the point across in a pragmatic sense."*

This is the meta-awareness condition (Condition 3) and Tralse Preservation (Condition 5) being satisfied in real time. The speaker does not retreat from the hyperbole — they explain its mechanism. This is the highest form of Valid Hyperbole: one that teaches its own validity while deploying it.

## 6. Distinguishing Valid Hyperbole from Bluntness (URB #670)

These are related but orthogonal concepts:

| Dimension | Bluntness (URB #670) | Valid Hyperbole (this paper) |
|-----------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| **Problem addressed** | Over-hedging; failure to commit | Discourse Prior imbalance; under-weighted pole |
| **Truth structure** | Single pole, known with high confidence | Genuine Tralse; both poles true |
| **The move** | State the known pole with full commitment | Amplify the under-weighted pole beyond literal accuracy |
| **What is preserved** | Precision; all known uncertainty is named | Both poles of the Tralse; amplification is named when engaged |
| **Risk of failure** | Under-delivery (hedge) | Over-correction (new imbalance) |
| **Epistemic virtue** | Courage | Fairness |

**Bluntness** is about commitment: say what you believe, with the confidence you actually have, without softening. It is the cure for epistemic cowardice.

**Valid Hyperbole** is about balance: when the discourse has unfairly amplified one pole of a genuine Tralse, counter-amplify the other pole. It is the cure for epistemic unfairness.

One can be blunt without Valid Hyperbole (stating a confident single-pole truth directly). One can deploy Valid Hyperbole while still being blunt (amplifying the under-weighted Tralse pole with full, unhedged commitment). The highest communicative act combines both: **bluntly asserting the Valid Hyperbole**.

## 7. When Valid Hyperbole Fails (Invalid Hyperbole)

The theorem's conditions define the failure modes precisely.

**Failure of Condition 1 (No Genuine Tralse):** If P_1 and P_2 are not both genuinely true — if the amplified pole is simply false — the "hyperbole" is a lie. No amplification of a false pole is valid.

**Failure of Condition 2 (No Discourse Imbalance):** If the Discourse Prior is already balanced or already over-weighted toward the pole being amplified, further amplification increases the imbalance rather than correcting it. This produces a new injustice, not a correction.

**Failure of Condition 3 (No Meta-Awareness):** If the speaker genuinely believes their hyperbole to be the complete literal truth — if they have forgotten it is an amplification — the speech act becomes self-deception. The speaker is now propagating an imbalanced view sincerely, which is worse than lying (one can be corrected by evidence; sincere error resists correction because it feels like truth).

**Failure of Condition 5 (Tralse Suppression):** If, when the other pole is directly engaged, the speaker doubles down ("no, it is NOT fragile at all, period") — they have moved from Valid Hyperbole to **Tralse Suppression**. This is Invalid Hyperbole: asserting one pole as exhaustive while denying the other. It is a MI move at the communicative level: simultaneously knowing the Tralse (internal state) while denying it (public state).

## 8. The Meta-Lesson: Hyperbole as a Teachable Moment

The user's naming of the Valid Hyperbole as a "teachable moment" is itself an instance of the phenomenon. By saying:

> *"'Not evidence of fragility whatsoever' is a VALID HYPERBOLE... the other end of the tralsity is what ought to be emphasized hyperbolically to get the point across in a pragmatic sense."*

The speaker is doing something sophisticated: deploying a Valid Hyperbole, then immediately naming it, then teaching the structure of its validity. The hyperbole becomes transparent — it does not hide its amplified nature but exposes it, transforming the rhetorical move into an epistemological lesson.

This is the highest form of Valid Hyperbole: the **self-disclosing Valid Hyperbole**. It achieves the corrective function (pushing the Discourse Prior toward P_2) AND teaches the listener to identify Valid Hyperbole when they encounter it in the future. A single instance of self-disclosing Valid Hyperbole equips the listener with a permanent epistemic tool.

In TI Sigma terms, the self-disclosing Valid Hyperbole is a **BOK-saturated communicative act**: it functions in the moment (HEAR amplification), while simultaneously transmitting a framework for future MR (BOK virus propagation). It is not merely corrective — it is generative.

It achieves the corrective function AND transmits the epistemic framework, making the recipient permanently more capable of PD-corrective reasoning.
