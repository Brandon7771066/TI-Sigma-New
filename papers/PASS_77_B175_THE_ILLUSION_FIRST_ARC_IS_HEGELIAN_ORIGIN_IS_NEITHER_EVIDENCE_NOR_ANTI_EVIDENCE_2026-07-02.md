## 2. The independent grounding: Hegel's dialectic

The claim *"seeing the truth is first seeing that it is an illusion"* — that truth is reached only by passing through and reconciling its opposite — is a central structure of **G. W. F. Hegel's** philosophy (*Phenomenology of Spirit*, 1807; *Science of Logic*, 1812–1816). Its precise machinery:

- **Determinate negation** (*bestimmte Negation*): the negation of a concept is not empty nothing but a *specific* positive result — the opposite carries content forward.
- **Negation of the negation**: a concept passes into its opposite, and that opposite is in turn negated, yielding not a return to the start but an **enriched affirmation**.
- **Sublation** (*Aufhebung*): Hegel's term for a move that simultaneously **cancels, preserves, and lifts up** — the opposite is not discarded, it is carried into the higher unity.

**Structural rhyme with the rotation (flagged as resonance, not derivation).** The B174 arc has the same shape: falsehood (−1) is not deleted but **turned** — negated *as* a phase and sublated into truth (+1), a higher affirmation reached *through* the negation, not around it. The illusion is "passed through, not skipped" (TRG-1) — which is precisely Hegel's point that the negative is a *moment* of the true, not merely its absence.

**Honest attribution.** The popular slogan *thesis–antithesis–synthesis* is commonly pinned on Hegel but is more accurately traced to **Fichte** and to **Chalybäus's** 1837 systematization (*Historische Entwicklung der spekulativen Philosophie von Kant bis Hegel*); Hegel's own vocabulary is *Aufhebung* and the *negation of the negation*, and in the **Preface to the *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807)** he polemicizes precisely against the mechanical application of a triadic formula as a "lifeless schema" (*lifeless formalism*). We use his terms, not the slogan.
