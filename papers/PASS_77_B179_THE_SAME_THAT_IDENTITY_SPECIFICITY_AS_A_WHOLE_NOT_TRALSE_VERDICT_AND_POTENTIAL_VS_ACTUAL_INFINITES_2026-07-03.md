## 1. "THE SAME THAT" — identity-specificity of the referent (NEW today)

The framework's most inclusive definition of tralse (DTR-1, ch02) is *"tralse is **that** which represents a deficit in reality."* The upgrade makes explicit that the word **THAT** in that definition, in the N/A definition, and in the MI definition is **one and the same THAT**, and that it is **always specific to the object in question** — never generic. Stated in the framework's object-language:

- **tralse** = "the **THAT** which represents a deficit of reality" (the object carrying the deficit);
- **N/A** = "the **THAT** which is tralse **and may (overall) not be** tralse";
- **MI** = "the **THAT** which **is and is not** tralse (overall)".

**Why this is load-bearing.** Because *that* is pinned to a **single object**, the two verdicts it is made to carry collide **on one referent**. That collision *is* the **identity conflict** at the heart of MI and of certain N/A cases: MI is the object that both is and is not tralse; the "certain N/A" case is the object that is tralse yet may (overall) fail to be. If "that" were permitted to **drift** to a *different* object between the two halves, there would be **no** conflict — the two halves would simply be about two different things. Object-specificity is therefore the precise mechanism that manufactures the conflict, and naming it is what lets the framework distinguish a genuine self-collision from a mere change of subject. This complements — does not replace — the formal MI definition `MI(P) ⟺ τ(P) ∧ ¬τ(P)` (ch07) and the corpus-overview gloss "coherence-collapse between concepts whose fundamental nature is incompatible": the object-language sharpens *whose* natures collide (one THAT's, overall).

## 2. The "as-a-whole" not-tralse verdict — two tralsities, not the same THAT (made earlier; now APPLIED in the book)

This upgrade was ruled earlier but had **not been applied in the book**; B179 applies it. The verdict **"not tralse"** attaches to the **statement AS A WHOLE** in the case where **two or more indeterminate statements are taken together** and judged **incompatible through self-defeat** (excessive, nonsensical incoherence). The consequences the book must carry:

1. We are **always dealing with two (or more) genuine tralsities** first — each half is a real deficit-in-reality attaching to *its own* THAT, individually legitimate.
2. Only their **combination**, judged as a whole, earns the label **not tralse**; the label is never passed on either half alone.
3. The tell-tale is that **the THAT is *not* the same** across the two self-referring propositions — each "that" points at a *different* object, and forcing a single overall statement to own both incompatible referents at once is exactly the identity conflict MI names.

This is why MI's object-language says "…is and is not tralse **overall**": the *overall* verdict is a whole-statement verdict built from two component tralsities whose THATs differ. The reading is stated in ch07 as a general rule ("keep this in view wherever the framework passes a not-tralse verdict") and foreshadowed in ch02, so every concept that inherits a not-tralse verdict inherits the two-tralsity reading with it.

**Relation to prior canon (no double-count).** This does not add a label or change the pipeline (N/A → MI → T/F/I). It refines *how* an MI verdict is understood to be generated (two-tralsity, whole-statement, differing THATs), consistent with the I/MI leeway demarcation (#17: annihilated leeway → MI) and PSM-1's I↔MI frontier work. Count stays **80**.

## 3. Potential-vs-actual infinites — a *revision with caveat* of "absolute infinites don't exist" (NEW today)

**Prior stance (URB #433):** completed/actual infinity is physically unmotivated and not an existent; potential infinity (limits) retained; the one honestly-argued actual infinity = the *future* (Myrion's immortal truth-seeking). The user now **revises** the blunt "absolute infinites do not exist" **given the mature Platonism reading** (B159 Platonism-as-tralse; TSG-1 generativity; EPE-1 Existence-Potential):

- **The *potential* of any infinite is truly real.** The notions everything such a hypothetical would entail **exist in potential** — genuine, contemplable possibilities held in the Existence-Potential (the "potential-until-instantiated" that Linnebo's mathematical potentialism defends — real cites: Linnebo & Shapiro, "Actual and Potential Infinity," *Noûs* 53(1), 2019; Linnebo, *Thin Objects: An Abstractionist Account*, Oxford University Press 2018; also carried from B159's Platonism-as-tralse ruling). Potential infinity (the limit/process) is retained in full.
- **Only the *actualized* aspects can be said to exist** (existence-weight ≈ 0 until tapped/instantiated). A *finished, completed* infinite has no confirmed instantiation.
- **Actual infinites therefore remain MI** — the THAT that *both is and is not* tralse overall (real as potential, non-existent as finished object): exactly the MI signature, not a data-gap N/A. The *future* exception (potential infinity lived forward, never a completed set) is preserved.

Corrected slogan: **"the potential of an infinite is real; its completion is MI."** This costs the framework nothing operational (every construction it uses is finite or a limit) and squares ch16 with the Platonism-as-tralse ruling. It **supersedes** the unqualified "absolute infinites don't exist" wording of URB #433 §(A) while keeping §(B) potential-infinity-retained and §(C) future-as-the-one-actual-infinity intact.
