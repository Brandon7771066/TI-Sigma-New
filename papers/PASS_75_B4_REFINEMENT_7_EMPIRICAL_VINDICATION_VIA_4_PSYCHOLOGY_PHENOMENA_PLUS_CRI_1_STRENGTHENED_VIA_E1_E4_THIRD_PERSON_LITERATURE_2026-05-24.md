### §3.1 E1 — Schaffner & Luks 2018 "Misinformation or Expressive Responding?" (Trump crowd-size worked-example)

**Citation:** Schaffner, B. F., & Luks, S. (2018). Misinformation or Expressive Responding? What an Inauguration Crowd Can Tell Us about the Source of Political Misinformation in Surveys. *Public Opinion Quarterly*, 82(1), 135-147.

**Study design:** N=1388 representative sample shown 2 photos (Obama 2009 inauguration LARGER crowd vs Trump 2017 inauguration SMALLER crowd) without labels; asked which photo had more people.

**Result:** Trump-voters significantly more likely than Clinton-voters to identify the SMALLER (Trump) crowd as LARGER. Effect persisted after controls for guessing/political-signaling.

**Refinement #7 framing:** The reported perception was GENUINELY DIFFERENT for Trump-voters on the imaginary axis. It's NOT merely (a) lying for political-signaling (the design controlled for this), NOR (b) cognitive-bias-failure (the perception was internally-consistent and group-correlated), BUT (c) genuine imaginary-axis truth-value-differentiation modulated by consciousness-state (motivated-perception + group-identity-receptiveness).

**Empirical-class:** N=1388 controlled-study; published peer-review; politically-neutral analytical treatment (the study finding holds REGARDLESS of which group is studied — equivalent motivated-perception findings exist for any in-group/out-group dyad in social psychology literature; Trump example is illustrative not partisan).

### §3.2 E2 — Hasher/Goldstein/Toppino 1977 illusory-truth-effect + dozens of replications

**Citation:** Hasher, L., Goldstein, D., & Toppino, T. (1977). Frequency and the conference of referential validity. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 16(1), 107-112. + extensive replication literature (Dechêne et al. 2010 meta-analysis 167 effects; Fazio et al. 2015 expanded scope; etc.)

**Effect:** Statements rated more truthful after repeated exposure, INDEPENDENT of objective truth-value. Effect robust across (a) plausible AND IMPLAUSIBLE statements, (b) statements participants KNOW to be false, (c) various exposure-intervals.

**Refinement #7 framing:** Repetition modulates imaginary-axis truth-value toward "perceived-true" — even for hyperimaginary-end content (objectively-false / objectively-incoherent claims). Directly vindicates Brandon's "ANY claim repeated enough - no matter how nonsensical - can eventually make people believe it."

**Empirical-class:** Dozens of replications across cultures/decades; 167-effects-meta-analysis; canonical psychology finding.

### §3.3 E3 — Hypnosis literature (consciousness-state-modulated perception)

**Citations:** Decades of hypnosis literature spanning Hilgard 1965 (neodissociation theory); Spanos 1986 (sociocognitive); modern neuroimaging (Oakley & Halligan 2009; Jiang et al. 2017 fMRI of hypnotic suggestion). APA Division 30 (Society of Psychological Hypnosis) consensus literature.
