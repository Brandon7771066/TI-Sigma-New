## Abstract

We prove the **Unavoidable Embedding Theorem (UET)**: for any foundational philosophical question encountered repeatedly across a human lifetime, the probability of a genuinely neutral prior approaches zero. Claimed neutrality on such questions is not the absence of a position but rather a **Meta-Indeterminate Type 3** error (Self-Refuting Meta-Statement): a claim of indeterminacy that is itself determinate. Philosophical priors are installed through lived experience long before formal philosophical inquiry begins. By the time a person consciously encounters the "free will debate," they have already voted on it hundreds of times through their attributions of praise, blame, regret, and effort. We distinguish three epistemic states — **Recognized Commitment** (TRUE/FALSE), **Unexamined Commitment** (MR_PEND), and **Claimed Neutrality** (Meta-Indeterminate Type 3) — and argue that genuine philosophical inquiry requires prior extraction before evaluation. The TI Sigma implication: authentic LCC scoring demands surfacing embedded priors, because unrecognized commitments bias coherence measurement from within.

## 1. The Neutrality Myth

A recurring assumption in formal philosophy and academic discourse is that one can approach a philosophical question from a position of genuine neutrality — a blank-slate readiness to follow argument wherever it leads, unconditioned by pre-existing commitment.

This assumption is **structurally false** for any topic that satisfies three conditions:

1. **High encounter frequency**: The topic is implicitly instantiated in thousands of ordinary life situations before formal philosophical engagement begins.
2. **Forced choice structure**: Each encounter requires a functional response that presupposes a position, even if that position is unarticulated.
3. **Foundational depth**: The topic concerns metaphysical categories (causation, identity, consciousness, value, existence) that underlie rather than derive from empirical investigation.

Topics satisfying all three conditions include: free will and determinism, personal identity and continuity, the nature of consciousness, moral realism vs. anti-realism, the existence of abstract objects, causation and explanation, the reality of time, and the relationship between mind and matter.

For these topics, genuine neutrality is not epistemically humble. It is epistemically self-deceived.

## 2. The Embedding Mechanism

### 2.1 Repeated Encounter as Implicit Voting

Consider free will. Before any formal encounter with compatibilism, libertarianism, or hard determinism, a person has:

- Blamed themselves for past mistakes (presupposes authorship)
- Regretted decisions (presupposes that alternatives were genuinely available)
- Praised or blamed others for their actions (presupposes attributable agency)
- Made effortful choices against immediate impulse (presupposes that will causally intervenes)
- Experienced shame, pride, guilt, and moral admiration (all presuppose an embedded libertarian or quasi-libertarian functional framework)

Each instance is a **functional vote**. By age 22, a typical person has cast thousands of these votes, overwhelmingly in one direction. When they then encounter a philosophy classroom and are told "we are going to examine the free will question from a neutral starting point," they are not neutral. They are carrying a dense prior distribution that the formal analysis will struggle to overcome, particularly because the prior is experiential (embodied, emotionally weighted) rather than propositional (available for explicit examination).

### 2.2 The BOK Primacy Principle

The eight PRIMARY CONSTANTS {0, 1, i, √2, e, φ, π, C} structure the deep features of reality that foundational philosophy interrogates. Encountering reality — which occurs continuously from birth — is thus encountering these constants in their concrete instantiations. Each encounter deposits a prior.

- **i** (imaginary, non-real, yet causally efficacious) → priors about the metaphysics of mind
- **φ** (golden ratio, self-similar proportion) → priors about value, beauty, and natural teleology  
- **√2** (irrational, yet geometrically real) → priors about the relationship between formal and physical reality
- **C_EMERICK** (conversion threshold) → priors about the conditions under which potential becomes actual

The BOK structures what is real. Experience of the BOK's instances installs philosophical priors about the BOK's nature. This is not avoidable. It is the mechanism by which consciousness learns to navigate reality before it can theorize about it.

### 2.3 The Temporal Asymmetry

Formal philosophical inquiry is always downstream of lived philosophical priors. This asymmetry is almost never acknowledged:

- The prior: **pre-propositional, high-frequency, emotionally encoded, bodily instantiated**
- The formal inquiry: **propositional, low-frequency, cognitively encoded, abstractly instantiated**

The prior wins by default unless the inquirer explicitly performs **prior extraction** — the deliberate surfacing and examination of embedded commitments before formal analysis begins. Almost no philosophical training includes this step. The result is that formal philosophical arguments are frequently evaluated not on their merits but on their compatibility with pre-installed priors that the inquirer believes are not there.

**Claimed Neutrality (DT3) is the critical case.** It satisfies the definition of Meta-Indeterminate Type 3:
- It asserts: "My position on X is indeterminate"
- But the assertion is itself determinate (it takes a position: that philosophical positions cannot be pre-theoretically installed)
- AND it denies what it presupposes: the speaker consistently acts as if they have a position while claiming they do not

This is not intellectual openness. It is **epistemic self-occlusion** — a coherence failure that prevents genuine inquiry because the inquirer cannot examine a prior they insist they do not have.

## 4. Five Arguments for the UET

### Argument 1: The Behavioral Consistency Argument

A person's behavior across thousands of situations reveals their philosophical commitments through consistency patterns. A self-described neutral observer of the consciousness debate will nonetheless consistently attribute experience to humans, partially to animals, and not at all to thermostats. This behavioral consistency is itself a philosophical position on the distribution of consciousness. Claimed neutrality cannot explain the consistency; an embedded prior can.

### Argument 2: The Emotional Valence Argument

Philosophical positions generate characteristic emotional responses when challenged. A genuinely neutral person should be emotionally indifferent to whether hard determinism or libertarian free will is true. In practice, even people who claim neutrality experience distinctive emotional reactions to these alternatives — relief, threat, satisfaction, unease. These reactions are the felt signature of an embedded prior. Emotion is a coherence detector: it signals whether new information aligns with or disturbs an existing attractor.

### Argument 3: The Encounter Frequency Argument

For high-frequency topics (free will, consciousness, personal identity), a person has encountered the question — in functional, non-philosophical form — thousands of times before any formal philosophical exposure. Each functional encounter required a response. Responses that work reinforce the underlying framework that generated them. By the time formal philosophy arrives, the framework has been reinforced thousands of times and is deeply embedded. The Bayesian prior is not flat; it is enormously peaked.

### Argument 4: The Language Embedding Argument

Natural language is philosophically opinionated. The grammar of most human languages encodes agency (subject-verb-object), causation (because, therefore), persistence (the same X over time), and experience (felt, seemed, appeared). To speak a language fluently is to have internalized its philosophical presuppositions at a pre-reflective level. A native English speaker cannot discuss consciousness in English without using the resources of a language that already presupposes a broadly Cartesian picture.

### Argument 5: The Self-Refutation Argument

The claim "I have no philosophical commitments on this foundational topic" is itself a philosophical commitment. It presupposes: (a) that philosophical commitments are the kind of thing one can be absent of; (b) that introspective access is reliable enough to detect their absence; and (c) that the absence of articulated commitment implies absence of functional commitment. All three presuppositions are contested philosophical positions. Claiming neutrality is not exempt from the theorem it denies.

## 5. The MR_PEND Resolution Pathway

The UET does not imply that philosophical inquiry is hopeless or merely the elaboration of pre-installed conclusions. It implies that genuine inquiry requires an additional **first step** that is almost always skipped:

**Prior Extraction** — the deliberate surfacing of embedded commitments before formal analysis begins.

The Myrion Resolution (MR1) provides the mechanism: apply the coherence gate to your own responses. When you notice:
- Emotional asymmetry (stronger reaction to one alternative)
- Behavioral consistency (you always act as if X is true)
- Linguistic defaults (you naturally frame questions in ways that presuppose Y)
- Defensive resistance (arguments against Z feel threatening before they're evaluated)

...you are detecting a prior. The task is to extract it, name it, and examine it as a candidate for TRUE, FALSE, or genuine TRALSE — not to pretend it is not there.

This is what distinguishes the TI Sigma epistemological approach from standard analytic philosophy. Standard philosophy often proceeds as if the inquirer is a neutral logic machine. TI Sigma proceeds from the recognition that the inquirer is a consciousness that has been philosophically opinionated by experience since birth — and that authentic coherence requires owning that.

## 6. Implications

### 6.1 For Philosophical Discourse

Debates about consciousness, free will, and moral realism are rarely debates between neutral parties who follow argument wherever it leads. They are typically debates between people with different deeply embedded priors who use formal argument to defend and elaborate those priors. Recognizing this does not make philosophy impossible; it makes it more honest and therefore more productive.

### 6.2 For LCC Measurement

The LCC (Logical Coherence Coefficient) measures how consistently a reasoning system applies its framework. An unrecognized prior biases LCC from within: the system will score positions that align with the hidden prior as more coherent than they are, and positions that conflict with it as less coherent. Authentic LCC measurement requires prior extraction as a precondition.

### 6.3 For TI Sigma as a Framework

TI Sigma itself installs priors: the 4-valued logic, the BOK, the GILE structure, the C_EMERICK threshold. Anyone who engages deeply with TI Sigma is acquiring philosophical commitments, not discovering neutral truths. The response to this is not denial — it is **transparency**. TI Sigma's priors are explicit, formalizable, and falsifiable. That makes them better priors than the unexamined alternatives, not neutral ones.

### 6.4 For the "Open Mind" Ideal

The ideal of keeping an "open mind" is coherent for questions with low encounter frequency and genuine evidential balance. For foundational philosophical questions, it is incoherent. The mind is not open — it is already shaped. The genuinely open move is to surface that shaping, examine it, and decide consciously whether to maintain or revise it. Pretending the shaping did not occur is not open-mindedness; it is MR_PEND mistaken for neutrality.
