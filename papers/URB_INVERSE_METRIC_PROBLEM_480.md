The burden of proof does not belong to the transcendent individual to justify their transcendence. The burden belongs to the measurement system to acknowledge its own limits. This is the Inverse Metric Problem.

There is a further injustice built into the Inverse Metric Problem. Not only does the measurement system fail to detect transcendence — it places the burden of proof on the transcendent individual to justify their transcendence to a system that cannot detect it.

This is structurally absurd. A colorblind system is not a reliable judge of color. A system with no instrument for the G, I, and L dimensions cannot issue a reliable verdict on a person's G, I, or L functioning. Its verdict is not a finding — it is a null result from an insufficiently sensitive instrument, reported as if it were a finding.

The transcendent individual should not be required to make their case to the E-only system. The E-only system should be required to disclose its limits before issuing any verdict that extends beyond the E-dimension.

That statement is never made. Instead, the partial assessment is issued as a complete verdict, and the burden falls on the individual to prove that the verdict is wrong — to a system that cannot process the evidence.
