1. Benford's Law: What It Is and Why It Holds

For any dataset that accumulates organically across multiple orders of magnitude, the probability that a number begins with digit d is:

$$P(d) = \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{1}{d}\right)$$

Why it holds — the scale-invariance argument: Benford's Law is the unique distribution invariant under change of scale. If the dataset follows the same statistical pattern regardless of whether you measure in meters, feet, or parsecs — regardless of units — Benford's Law is the only distribution with this property. Natural data, generated by processes that don't care about our measurement conventions, naturally settles into the only scale-invariant distribution available.

Why it holds — the multiplicative process argument: Quantities that grow multiplicatively (compound interest, population growth, viral spread, economic output) accumulate values whose logarithms are approximately uniformly distributed. A uniformly distributed log corresponds exactly to Benford's Law in the original scale.

The forensic application: Human-fabricated numbers are not scale-invariant and not multiplicatively generated. Fraudsters intuitively gravitate toward "middle" digits and generate numbers more uniformly than nature does. Benford's Law catches them because their fabrications deviate from the logarithmic distribution in statistically detectable ways.
