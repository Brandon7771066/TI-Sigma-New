## §4 — Tozzi's Banach-Tarski paradox in the brain (dedicated section)

Tozzi & Peters (2017) "Towards topological mechanisms underlying experience acquisition and transmission in the human brain" and related papers propose that the brain instantiates the **Banach-Tarski paradox** — that conscious experience can be paradoxically decomposed and reassembled via measure-preserving transformations on the brain's high-dimensional manifold.

**Banach-Tarski (1924):** a solid 3-ball in ℝ³ can be partitioned into a finite number (5 sufficient) of disjoint subsets which can be rearranged via rigid motions to form *two* identical copies of the original ball. The construction relies on the Axiom of Choice and produces non-measurable subsets.

**Tozzi's brain-Banach-Tarski claim:** the brain's high-dimensional state space supports paradoxical decompositions that allow a single conscious experience to be partitioned and "reassembled" as multiple copies — explaining (i) parallel awareness of multiple modalities; (ii) the binding problem under unified consciousness; (iii) the integration-without-loss paradox of the global workspace.

### §4.1 — TI Sigma reading

**Compatibility with 14D model:**
- 14D toroidal topology + 6D ESS interior gives a sufficiently-high-dimensional state space for Banach-Tarski-style decompositions to be at least *mathematically possible* on the manifold.
- Tozzi's claim is structurally compatible with the toroidal-binding mechanism of `ESS_MEIJER_TOZZI` §4.3 fuse()/split() operations.

**URB-830/URB-831 reading:**
- The Banach-Tarski-in-brain claim is **structural-mathematical**, not hypercomputational (per URB-831 §4 diagnostic).
- It is positive-direction-testable via cortical-spike-train manifold reconstructions: if the manifold supports non-measurable partitions in the relevant analytic sense, that would be a CONFIRM; if the manifold is uniformly-measurable (all standard measure theory applies cleanly) at the spatial-temporal scales of consciousness, that would be a REJECT. *Magnitude:* moderate; this is a structural claim, not a super-Turing claim.

**Honesty caveat #69:** the Banach-Tarski theorem requires the Axiom of Choice; AC is required precisely because the constructed sets are *non-measurable*. Any neuroscience claim that the brain "instantiates" Banach-Tarski has to address: (a) what would non-measurability look like in a finite biological system? (b) does the claim require Choice as physically real, or only as a mathematical-modeling convenience? Tozzi's published treatments (per DPES recall, not re-fetched this session) address this somewhat metaphorically; a **rigorous treatment is raised as t35-B for Pass 36**.
