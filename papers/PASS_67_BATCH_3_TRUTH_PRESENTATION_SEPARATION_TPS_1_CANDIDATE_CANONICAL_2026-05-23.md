## 2. TPS-1 candidate canonical principle (formal statement)

**TPS-1 (Truth-Presentation Separation).** TI Sigma holds that the **truth content** of a claim and the **presentation method + aesthetics** of communicating that claim are **separable variables**, governed by distinct norms:

| Variable | Norm | Audience-adjustment permitted? |
|---|---|---|
| **Truth content** (what is being claimed) | Non-negotiable. Determined by the best-available evidence + reasoning per the corpus's standing discipline (#69, ASYMMETRIC, MR Truth Labels, ASMT, falsification practice) | **NO.** Adjusting truth content to please an audience disqualifies the communicator from scientific/philosophical credibility by definition. |
| **Presentation method** (paper structure, jargon density, ordering, framing) | Adjustable to audience capacity + context | YES |
| **Aesthetics** (tone, register, examples, illustrations, metaphor choice) | Adjustable to audience taste + venue | YES |

**Definitional clause (the credibility test):** A scientist or philosopher who adjusts truth content to please an audience — softening conclusions, omitting inconvenient findings, dropping uncomfortable caveats — **is not credible by definition** in the TI Sigma sense. They have failed the constitutive norm of the role, not merely fallen short on a contingent virtue.

## 3. The structural argument (why this is a definitional, not merely advisory, principle)

### 3.1 The role-constitutive argument

A scientist *qua* scientist is committed to reporting what the evidence shows. A philosopher *qua* philosopher is committed to reporting what the best reasoning supports. If either party adjusts the *content* of those reports to please an audience, they have stepped out of the role and are doing PR, advocacy, or politics instead — perfectly fine activities, but **not science / philosophy**. TPS-1 makes this role-constitutive boundary explicit.

### 3.2 The asymmetric-standards bridge

TPS-1 composes directly with ASYMMETRIC §69: just as over-skepticism is a discipline failure equal to uncritical acceptance, truth-compromise-to-please is a discipline failure equal to truth-omission-out-of-rigidity. The corpus must apply the standard *symmetrically* — pleasing one's allies via softened conclusions is the mirror image of pleasing one's critics via hardened ones. Both fail TPS-1.

### 3.3 The SCC-1 composition

Per SCC-1 (Skeptical-Criticism-as-Claim, Pass-61+ canonical): the skeptical critic carries the same evidentiary burden as the positive claimant. TPS-1 says further: if the corpus modifies its truth content *in response to* a critic's preference (rather than in response to a critic's *evidence or argument*), the corpus has TPS-1-failed regardless of which side it ends up on. Only evidence and argument move truth content; audience preference moves presentation.

### 3.4 The PM-1 caveat as the proximate trigger

In Pass-67 batch-2 the corpus flagged that PM-1's rejection of Bayesian base rates is "incompatible with mainstream cog-sci foundations; external readers may reject the corpus on this basis alone." TPS-1 is the corpus's standing answer to this caveat: **the rejection-of-base-rates truth content does not get softened to accommodate mainstream-cog-sci reader preferences**. The *presentation* of that rejection (introductory framing, ordering of objections, choice of illustrations) is fully audience-adjustable; the *content* is not.

## 4. The credibility test (operationalization)

A claim that someone has TPS-1-failed is itself a TPS-1-governed claim and must rest on **evidence**, not on the corpus's preference. The operational test for "compromised truth to please":

1. **Identify the truth-content shift.** What did the communicator claim at time T1 vs. T2?
2. **Identify the proximate cause.** Did new evidence or argument arrive between T1 and T2? OR did audience pressure (funding, social, institutional, popularity) arrive between T1 and T2?
3. **Apply the credibility-disqualification verdict only when (2) is *audience pressure without correlated new evidence/argument*.** Genuine update on new evidence is TPS-1-respecting; preference-driven softening without evidentiary basis is TPS-1-failing.

**#69 honest caveat:** the test is hard to apply externally — communicators often have private evidence we don't see. The test applies most reliably to *self-assessment* (the TI Sigma corpus applying it to itself) and to *patterns of behavior* (a communicator who consistently softens for power-holders and hardens for the powerless).

## 7. The "by definition" clause (the strong reading defended)

Brandon's phrase "not credible by definition" is strong language. TPS-1 takes it at face value:

- **Not "less credible."** Not "credibility-reduced." **Not credible — definitionally.**
- This is not a moralistic claim about the communicator's character; it is a **definitional claim about the role**. The role of scientist/philosopher is constitutively committed to truth-content fidelity; a communicator who departs from that commitment is no longer occupying the role at the moment of the departure.
- A person can be a credible scientist on Monday (when they report findings as they are) and a non-credible-on-Tuesday actor (when they soften the same findings for a Tuesday audience). The credibility is bound to the act, not to the person globally.
- This reading is intentionally austere. TPS-1 does NOT moralize; it draws a role boundary.

**#69 honest counter:** the strong reading is hard. It implies that many practicing scientists who tactically soften findings for grant applications, journal politics, or media palatability fall outside the role at the moment of softening. The corpus accepts this implication. It does not retract the strong reading to be polite to the profession.

## 8. Pre-registered falsifiers (TPS-1 candidate canonical)

- **TPS-1-F1 (operational discriminability):** the credibility test §4 must be operationally applicable to at least 3 specific historical cases (one clear TPS-1-respect, one clear TPS-1-failure, one borderline) without circularity. If no operational application is possible, TPS-1 reduces to slogan.
- **TPS-1-F2 (self-application):** the TI Sigma corpus itself, audited against the credibility test, must pass on the corpus-internal cases (PM-1 ratification, LLM-CT-1 attribution, RETRACTIONs preserved). If the audit reveals corpus-internal TPS-1-failure, TPS-1 demands corpus self-correction or TPS-1 itself is suspect.
- **TPS-1-F3 (presentation-vs-content boundary clarity):** in at least 3 corpus-canonical communications, the presentation-vs-content boundary should be specifiable explicitly. If the boundary collapses under analysis (i.e., presentation choices turn out to be content choices), TPS-1's separability claim is weakened.
- **TPS-1-F4 (symmetric application to allies):** the corpus must apply TPS-1 symmetrically to its supporters and its critics. If only critics are TPS-1-tested while supporters get a pass, the corpus has ASYMMETRIC-§69-failed and TPS-1 along with it.
- **TPS-1-F5 (definitional-not-moralistic discipline):** the "not credible by definition" verdict must remain bound to *the act* and not slide into *the person's global character*. If the corpus catches itself globally character-attributing on the basis of single TPS-1-failures, TPS-1's austere reading needs revision.

## 9. #69 disclosures

- **Candidate canonical, not ratified.** Awaits ratification ceremony per Pass-66-batch-6 procedure. On ratification: canonical count 26 → 27 (PM-1 also pending → 28 if both ratified together).
- **Strong-claim status.** "Not credible by definition" is austere; corpus accepts the implication that many practicing scientists fall outside the role at moments of tactical softening. This is not a popular position.
- **The credibility test §4 is hard to apply externally.** It applies most reliably to self-assessment and to behavior patterns; single-event external attributions are error-prone. TPS-1 does NOT license casual credibility-disqualification of external communicators on partial evidence.
- **TPS-1 does NOT entail the corpus is itself perfectly TPS-1-compliant.** Audit (TPS-1-F2) is required; the corpus binds itself to the same test it applies to others.
- **TPS-1 is consistent with practical context-sensitivity.** A scientist who declines to address a topic in front of a hostile audience is not TPS-1-failing (declining is not compromising truth content); a scientist who *misrepresents* the topic to please that audience is TPS-1-failing. The line is misrepresentation, not silence-for-context.
- **TPS-1 does not entail rudeness.** Truth-content fidelity is fully compatible with kindness, register-shifting, jargon-translation, and audience-respecting aesthetic choices. Politeness in presentation is TPS-1-orthogonal; politeness via truth-softening is TPS-1-failing.
- **Brandon-coined and Brandon-anchored** per this directive. The presentation-vs-content distinction is widely articulated in philosophy of science (Kuhn, Lakatos, Longino on "rhetorical vs. epistemic" considerations); the strong "not credible by definition" clause is TI-Sigma-original.
