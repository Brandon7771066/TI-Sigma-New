# Truth and Real-World Truth States (Clarified)

## Ontological Truth

**Truth (capital-T)** consists only of the most perfect, necessary, and fully coherent entities.

- Truth is **rare**
- Truth is **invariant**
- Truth is **not probabilistic**

---

## Real-World Truth States (Myrion Resolution)

TI models real-world epistemic states encountered by imperfect agents.

Under **Myrion Resolution (MR)**, four real-world truth states are recognized:

| State | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **True-Tralse** | Converging toward Truth |
| **Tralse-False** | Diverging from Truth |
| **Tralse-Indeterminate** | Undetermined direction |
| **Double Tralse (DT)** | Incoherent, collapses under resolution |

**Only True-Tralse may converge toward Truth.**

---

## Double Tralse (DT)

Double Tralse is a **real, named state** representing a lack of coherence identified through attempted Myrion Resolution.

### Key Properties:
- DT is **NOT Truth**
- DT is **NOT a stable epistemic state**
- DT is a **failure mode**
- DT represents an entity that **collapses under resolution** due to incoherence

### MR Process:
- DT is **eliminated during MR-1**
- DT does **NOT proceed to MR-2** classification

---

## The Hierarchy

```
Ontological Truth (T)
    ↑ (convergence possible)
True-Tralse
    ↔
Tralse-Indeterminate
    ↔
Tralse-False
    ↓ (collapse/elimination)
Double Tralse (DT) → Eliminated at MR-1
```

---

## Clarification: Truth vs. Tralse States

| Aspect | Ontological Truth | Real-World States |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Nature | Perfect, necessary | Imperfect, encountered |
| Stability | Invariant | May shift |
| Probabilistic | No | Context-dependent |
| Agents | N/A | Imperfect observers |

---

## Implications

1. **Epistemology**: We encounter real-world truth states, not pure Truth
2. **Convergence**: Only True-Tralse paths can approach Truth
3. **Failure Modes**: Double Tralse identifies incoherence early (MR-1)
4. **Resolution**: Myrion Resolution is the process of state classification

## Addendum (February 25, 2026): The White Lie as Double Myrion Resolution

A concrete illustration of two-stage Myrion Resolution that demonstrates why four truth values are necessary for moral reasoning:

**Scenario:** You are asked "Do you like my haircut?" The haircut is genuinely bad. A truthful answer causes unnecessary pain; a kind answer is factually false.

**Single MR (binary logic's limit):**
Binary logic forces: True (the haircut is bad, say so) or False (lie, it's great). There is no principled resolution within binary logic — you must simply pick one and accept the loss.

**Double MR under Tralse logic:**

*Initial state — TRALSE:* The statement "your haircut looks great" is simultaneously:
- Factually False (it doesn't)
- Morally True (it is the kind and appropriate response in context)
- Neither fully True nor False — a genuine Tralse state holding both poles at once

*First Myrion Resolution (factual axis):* Resolve on the dimension of literal factual correspondence. Result: **INDETERMINATE**. The statement is not simply True or False — it is a white lie, which is a coherent real state occupying the space between. A half-open door is neither open nor closed. A white lie is neither honest nor dishonest — it is a distinct, third thing.

*Second Myrion Resolution (moral axis):* Now resolve on the dimension of G-Goodness — what does the full moral context (relationship, intent, consequences, proportion) determine? The kindness of the response outweighs the factual inaccuracy, given the stakes. Result: **TRUE**. The white lie is the morally correct action.

**The key insight:** The white lie resolves to TRUE only after TWO Myrion Resolutions. Single-MR moral reasoning (classical ethics: a lie is always wrong; honesty is always right) fails because it applies only the factual resolution and stops there. Two-stage MR is required to reach the correct answer.

**Why this matters beyond ethics:** Any situation where something appears "wrong" by one standard but "right" by another requires at minimum two Myrion Resolutions. Single-standard moral reasoning is not just imprecise — it is structurally incomplete. The structure of ethical reality requires at minimum a second resolution pass.

This is also why "moral absolutism" (a single principle applies in all cases) is a low-true-tralse position. It enforces single-MR when the situation requires double-MR. The nuanced moral thinker is not being relativistic — they are applying the correct number of resolution passes.
