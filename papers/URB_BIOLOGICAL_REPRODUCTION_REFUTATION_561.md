## 1. The Target: Physicalism's Implicit Criterion

Physicalism is the position that only physical things are real — that mental, conceptual, or mathematical objects are derivative, secondary, or simply not real in the full sense. Its working criterion, stated or unstated, is:

> **Physicalism's Criterion (PC):** X is real ↔ X has physical existence independent of any mind.

By this criterion, unicorns are not real (mind-dependent concepts). Mathematical objects are not real (abstract, non-physical). Fictional characters are not real. Primary constants like φ and π are, at best, useful fictions.

This paper presents a single, decisive refutation of PC using physicalism's own most cherished example: biological reproduction.

---

## 2. The Refutation

**Claim:** Biological reproduction is mind-dependent by physicalism's own criterion. Therefore PC is self-refuting.

**Proof:**

**(Step 1 — Intentional agency):**
Biological reproduction in sexually reproducing organisms requires intentional acts by conscious beings. Two minds choose, respond, and act. The process is initiated and sustained by agents bearing subjective experience. By PC, anything produced by the intentional acts of minds is mind-dependent — and therefore, by PC, not fully real.

**(Step 2 — Information substrate):**
DNA is not a physical object in the relevant sense. It is a **code** — a sequence of symbols whose meaning is entirely relational, determined by the biochemical reader that interprets it. Remove the reader (the ribosome, the cell, the developmental context) and the DNA is just a polymer chain with no more biological meaning than random noise. The information content of DNA is mind-like in precisely the way language is mind-like: it requires interpretation. By PC, information-dependent processes are not fully real.

**(Step 3 — Recognition and pattern-matching):**
The development of a new organism from a fertilized cell is driven entirely by pattern recognition — the immune system distinguishing self from non-self, morphogenetic fields establishing body plans, neural development following topological maps. These are all computational, mind-like processes. By PC, processes that operate through recognition and interpretation are mind-dependent.

**(Step 4 — Conclusion):**
A newborn child is produced by:
- intentional acts of two conscious beings (Step 1)
- encoded in a purely informational substrate (Step 2)
- assembled by interpretive, recognition-based processes (Step 3)

By PC, the newborn child is a mind-dependent artifact — and therefore, by PC, **not fully real.**

This is the refutation. Physicalism's criterion for reality, applied consistently, eliminates newborn children. A position that cannot accommodate newborn children as real is not a coherent ontology. It is a prejudice masquerading as a criterion. □

## 3. The Deeper Problem: "Real" Is a TRALSE Term

The refutation above shows that PC generates a contradiction. But why does it generate a contradiction? Because **"real" is not a binary term.**

In the TI Sigma framework, truth values are five-fold:

"Is X real?" is a **TRALSE** question for most interesting X. Not FALSE (unicorns clearly exist as causally potent concepts) and not TRUE in the physicalist's narrow sense. TRALSE: genuinely real AND generative of further reality through the minds that hold them.

Physicalism tries to answer a TRALSE question with a binary. The result is always contradiction — because TRALSE propositions, forced into TRUE/FALSE, oscillate and destabilize. > **Definition (vern):** An entity that IS without acting and persists without being a thing. A state of pure existence-in-progress. Grammatically and ontologically between noun and verb.

The vern ontology replaces the binary real/unreal with a single question:
