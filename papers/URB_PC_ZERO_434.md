## 1. Mathematical Definition

**0 is the unique element of any additive group satisfying:** x + 0 = x for all x.

Properties:
- 0 × x = 0 for all x (annihilation)
- 0 + x = x for all x (identity)
- x − x = 0 for all x (inverse)
- 0^0 = 1 (by convention; the indeterminate form points toward 1, not 0)

**Why 0 is not trivial:** The existence of an additive identity is one of the deepest structural facts about any algebraic system. Number systems without zero cannot express the concept of "no change" — they can describe only positive or negative movement, never rest. The introduction of zero into European mathematics (via Arabic mathematicians, via India) was one of the most consequential intellectual events in history. The medieval European resistance to zero was not superstition — it was a recognition that zero is philosophically unusual: it is the thing that is nothing, the number that is not a number, the quantity that has no quantity.

**2.2 Absolute Zero:** The third law of thermodynamics states that absolute zero (0 K) is unattainable — you can approach it asymptotically but never reach it. This is the only "achieved" zero in nature.

**2.3 Symmetry and Zero:** Noether's theorem establishes that every continuous symmetry of a physical system corresponds to a conserved quantity — a quantity whose net rate of change is zero. Conservation is the physical expression of zero: not nothing, but no net change. The total momentum, total energy, total charge of a closed system is zero in its rate of change. The universe conserves by maintaining zero rate of change for its fundamental symmetry-linked quantities.
