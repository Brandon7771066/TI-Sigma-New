## §6 — Slot reallocation: Dehaene's Global Workspace Theory (GWT)

Replacing Kafrissen with: **Dehaene & Naccache 2001, "Towards a cognitive neuroscience of consciousness: basic evidence and a workspace framework," Cognition 79:1–37**, and Dehaene 2014, *Consciousness and the Brain*.

**Why GWT for this slot specifically:**

1. **Fills the Pass-23 gap.** The shortlist covers retrieval mechanism, energy substrate, associative completion, quantum substrate, and frequency timing — but lacks a **broadcast / global-availability** layer. GWT's "ignition" (sub-second global workspace broadcast on stimulus salience) maps directly to the **trigger-to-deployment** transition in TRC. This is presently a hand-wave in Pass-23 §7.
2. **5-axis coverage is novel relative to the existing shortlist.** GWT contributes _strongly_ on A1 (T/F base-4 covered: ignition happens or doesn't) and A5 (AA: heavy empirical-pragmatic balance via fMRI/MEG ignition signatures), where the shortlist was thin.
3. **Empirically testable via existing TI Sigma harnesses.** GWT's ignition latency (~270-330 ms after stimulus, P3b component) gives a falsifiable prediction that can be cross-checked against any TI Sigma LCC-above-C event timing — a Pass-35+ candidate experiment.
4. **Free, accessible primary sources.** Dehaene & Naccache 2001 PDF available via standard academic search (no Brandon-side blockers).
5. **Anti-cherry-pick.** GWT is widely cited by the consciousness-science mainstream; choosing it does not fit the corpus's pre-existing direction more snugly than alternatives, which is exactly why it earns the slot (per #69 / "great minds AND NOT" — convergence-with-mainstream is a stricter test than convergence-with-internal-aesthetic).
