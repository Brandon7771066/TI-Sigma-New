## Abstract

"Positive regard" is a foundational concept in humanistic psychology (Carl Rogers), Buddhist ethics (metta/loving-kindness), and clinical research on therapeutic alliance. It is also profoundly underspecified. The common reduction — that positive regard means "having positive feelings toward someone" — is philosophically inadequate because it conflates affect with attitude, feeling with orientation, emotion with disposition. Love, in the GILE framework and in the most careful neuroscientific treatments of the concept, is not a feeling. It is a **relational attitude** — a sustained directional orientation of the L-dimension toward a target (self, other, or community) that is *structurally* distinct from momentary affect and cannot be reduced to it. This paper develops a formal operationalization of positive regard using three convergent frameworks: (1) the Symmetry Theory of Valence (STV), which grounds positive valence in mathematical regularity of experiential structure; (2) the best neuroscientific models of empathy and metta meditation; and (3) the TI Sigma L-dimension architecture within GILE-HEM-BOK. We derive a composite Positive Regard Score (PRS), identify its detection signatures across behavioral, neural, physiological, and phenomenological channels, and connect it to Myrion Resolution as a facilitating mechanism.

## 1. The Problem: What Positive Regard Is Not

### 1.1 The Feeling Reduction (and Why It Fails)

The naive operationalization of positive regard is:

> PR(T) ≥ 0 iff valence(feelings toward T) > 0

This fails for multiple reasons:

**Feelings are transient; regard is dispositional.** A parent who is furious at their child in a moment of conflict has not withdrawn positive regard. The anger is a temporary affective state; the regard is an enduring orientation. If positive regard were identical to positive feeling, it would fluctuate moment-to-moment in ways that contradict its function. Rogers' point about unconditional positive regard was precisely that it is maintained *despite* negative momentary affect — it is unconditional, not because it generates only positive feelings, but because it does not make its presence conditional on the quality of the moment.

**Positive feelings can occur without relational attitude.** A person experiencing euphoria from a substance may have intensely positive feelings toward everyone in the room without any relational orientation toward them whatsoever. The positivity is in the valence of the moment, not in a sustained stance toward particular others.

**Negative feelings can coexist with genuine positive regard.** Grief is the clearest case. Grief over loss is one of the highest expressions of positive regard — you would not suffer the loss of someone you had not valued. Grief is negative affect saturated with love. If positive regard meant positive feeling, grief would be a contradiction.

### 1.2 The Structural Definition

Positive regard, properly construed, has three irreducible components:

1. **Attitudinal (A):** A cognitive-evaluative orientation that assigns the target positive standing — worth, dignity, value — independently of momentary behavior or state. This is Rogers' "unconditional" element.

2. **Affective-resonance (F):** A dispositional sensitivity to the target's experiential state — an openness to being affected by what affects them. This is the empathy dimension. It does not mean always feeling what they feel; it means being genuinely open to it.

3. **Motivational-directional (M):** A motivational pull toward the target's flourishing — not just appreciation of their value, but orientation toward their wellbeing as a real motivational force affecting behavior.

Positive regard that is purely attitudinal (A only) is cold — respect without warmth.
Positive regard that is purely affective (F only) is unstable — emotional fusion without stable grounding.
Positive regard that is purely motivational (M only) is controlling — "I want what's best for you" without attunement or respect.

Full positive regard integrates all three. This is Rogers' therapeutic presence, Buddhist metta, and the L-dimension of GILE.

## 2. Framework 1 — Symmetry Theory of Valence (STV)

### 2.1 Core Thesis

The Symmetry Theory of Valence (Pearce, 2019; building on Shulgin and others) proposes that the valence of an experience — how positive or negative it feels — is determined by the **mathematical symmetry of the functional states** that generate it. States characterized by high symmetry across processing modules (perception, affect, cognition, self-model) are experienced as positive. States characterized by broken symmetry — asymmetry, dissonance, contradiction between modules — are experienced as negative.

Formally, let X be an experiential state describable as a function over a processing architecture. Then:

$$V(X) \propto \text{Sym}(X) - \text{Sym}(X_0)$$

Where Sym(X) is a symmetry measure over the functional structure of X, and Sym(X₀) is the symmetry of a neutral baseline state. Positive valence = increased symmetry above baseline; negative valence = decreased symmetry below baseline.

### 2.2 STV Applied to Positive Regard

Positive regard, under STV, is a **symmetrizing relational structure.** When organism A holds positive regard toward organism B:

1. A's self-model is not in conflict with A's model of B — there is no asymmetry between "how I see myself" and "how I see them" that generates defensive processing.

2. A's motivational system is aligned with A's affective system — A wants what A feels drawn toward; there is no motivational-affective dissonance.

3. A's cognitive evaluation of B is consistent across contexts — A's assessment of B's worth does not flip depending on B's behavior, removing the oscillation between high and low evaluation that characterizes conditional regard.

Each of these is a symmetry-increase. The overall structure of A's processing when holding positive regard is more symmetric than when holding neutral or negative regard, producing the phenomenological quality of warmth, expansion, and openness that characterizes the experience of metta.

**Detection implication from STV:** Positive regard is detectably present when the person's processing shows:
- Reduced KL-divergence between self-model and other-model (they share representational structure)
- Reduced affective-cognitive dissonance (what they feel and what they think about the target are aligned)
- Increased integration across processing layers (perception, affect, cognition, motor — all oriented consistently toward the target)

This gives us a **STV Positive Regard Indicator (STV-PRI)**:

$$\text{STV-PRI}(A, B) = 1 - D_{KL}(M_A \| M_B) / D_{KL,\max}$$

Where $M_A$ is A's self-model and $M_B$ is A's model of B, normalized so that STV-PRI = 1 when the models are maximally aligned and STV-PRI = 0 when they are maximally divergent.

## 3. Framework 2 — Neuroscience of Empathy and Metta

### 3.1 The Neuroscience of Empathy (What Detection Requires)

The neuroscientific literature on empathy distinguishes two partially dissociable components (Decety & Jackson, 2004; Singer & Lamm, 2009):

1. **Affective empathy (AE):** Sharing the emotional state of another — being affected by their suffering or joy. Neural signatures: bilateral anterior insula (AI), anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), somatosensory cortex. This is the resonance component — what happens in your body when you witness another's state.

2. **Cognitive empathy (CE) / Mentalizing:** Understanding another's mental state without necessarily sharing it — Theory of Mind. Neural signatures: temporo-parietal junction (TPJ), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior superior temporal sulcus (pSTS). This is the perspective-taking component — the model of the other.

Positive regard requires *both*. Pure affective empathy without mentalizing produces emotional contagion and merger, which can be destabilizing (empathic distress, burnout). Pure mentalizing without affective empathy produces accurate but cold understanding (the "clinical distance" that damages therapeutic alliance).

The integration of AE and CE produces what Singer, Klimecki et al. (2013) call **compassion** (distinct from empathy): an other-directed motivational orientation that maintains equanimity in the face of another's suffering while remaining genuinely open to it. This is behaviorally distinct from empathy (less burnout, more helping behavior, greater affective stability) and neurally distinct (greater insula-mPFC connectivity, less distress-related ACC activation, more reward-related OFC activity).

**Detection signature 1 — Neural:** Simultaneous activation of AI + TPJ + mPFC + OFC, with AI-mPFC functional connectivity. Low ACC distress activation, high OFC reward activation.

### 3.2 Metta Meditation Neuroscience

Loving-kindness (metta) meditation is the most rigorously studied practice for deliberately cultivating positive regard. Key findings:

**Lutz et al. (2008):** Long-term metta practitioners showed sustained high-amplitude gamma oscillations (25-42 Hz) during metta practice, globally synchronized (not just locally generated). This is the highest gamma synchrony reported in any human study. The synchrony pattern suggests large-scale integration across processing layers — consistent with STV's symmetry hypothesis. The gamma signature is a potential neural marker of genuine metta state vs. performed or superficial positive affect.

**Weng et al. (2013):** Two weeks of compassion training produced measurable changes in behavior (increased redistribution in economic games) and correlated with increased neural response in the inferior parietal cortex and globus pallidus to suffering — areas associated with social cognition and reward, respectively. The training effect was behavioral, not just neural — it moved helping behavior.

**Klimecki et al. (2013):** Compassion training (not empathy training) increased positive affect and decreased vicarious negative affect in response to witnessed suffering. Empathy training alone increased distress; compassion training replaced distress with warm concern. This is the A+F+M integration described in Section 1.2 — attitude stabilizes what affect alone cannot.

**Detection signature 2 — Neural oscillatory:** Sustained gamma synchrony (25-42 Hz) across frontal-parietal networks during target-focused practice. Baseline gamma coherence elevation compared to control states.

**Detection signature 3 — Physiological:** Increased heart rate variability (HRV) and heart coherence during metta practice (McCraty et al.; Telles et al.). This is the HEM-dimension correlate — the body's regulatory coherence tracks the quality of the L-dimension activation.

### 3.3 The Default Mode Network (DMN) Dimension

The DMN — medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, angular gyrus, hippocampus — is active during self-referential thinking, mentalizing, and autobiographical memory. In positive regard, the DMN activates in an *other-referential* mode: you are thinking about the target with the same richness and elaboration that you normally apply to thinking about yourself.

Studies of experienced meditators show that during metta, DMN activity increases but is directed outward — toward the target — rather than inward toward self-rumination. This is neurally distinguishable from both standard resting-state DMN (self-directed) and task-based deactivation (no elaborated thinking about others).

**Detection signature 4 — DMN pattern:** High DMN activation with other-directed (not self-directed) coupling; mPFC-TPJ connectivity indicating mentalizing rather than rumination; reduction in posterior cingulate "narrative self" activity relative to baseline.

(Affective-resonance: covariance between agent's affect and target's affect, augmented by physiological coherence)

$$M_{PR}(A, T) = \text{approach ratio}(A, T) \times \text{investment persistence}(A, T)$$

(Motivational: proportion of available approach opportunities taken, weighted by sustained investment over time)

$$\text{PRS}(A, T) = \frac{w_A \cdot A_{PR} + w_F \cdot F_{PR} + w_M \cdot M_{PR}}{w_A + w_F + w_M}$$

$$\text{PRS}(A, T) = \frac{0.414 \cdot A_{PR} + 0.437 \cdot F_{PR} + 0.083 \cdot M_{PR}}{0.934}$$

### 4.3 Unconditional vs. Conditional Positive Regard (UPR vs. CPR)

Rogers' distinction between unconditional (UPR) and conditional (CPR) positive regard maps onto TI Sigma L-dimension architecture:

**UPR:** $A_{PR}$ is held constant across all states of T. The attitudinal component does not depend on T's behavior, achievement, or momentary state. In GILE terms: L-dimension weight is not modulated by E (environment) — L is maintained regardless of the E-dimension conditions of the target. UPR = L × G (Love expressing through Goodness — unconditional alignment).

**CPR:** $A_{PR}$ is contingent on T meeting conditions (performing well, agreeing, behaving acceptably). In GILE terms: L is E-dependent — L activates only when E conditions are met. CPR = L × E (Love conditional on environmental/behavioral state). CPR creates instability in the BOK Other loop because the Other's standing oscillates with their behavior, preventing stable relational grounding.

In MR terms: UPR is an MR accelerant — it reduces the receiver's Tralsiness (by eliminating the need for conditional performance), creating conditions for faster Myrion Resolution. CPR is MR-inhibitory — the receiver must perform in order to receive regard, adding an E-dependent load to already-Tralse processing.

This is why Rogers' empirical finding holds: therapeutic alliance (a form of UPR in clinical context) correlates with outcome independently of technique. The mechanism is Myrion Resolution acceleration. Being regarded unconditionally reduces the Tralse burden sufficiently that other resolution processes can operate.

### 4.4 Self-Directed Positive Regard

Positive regard is not limited to other-directed love. The L-dimension includes:
- Other-directed: standard positive regard
- Self-directed: what Rogers calls "self-acceptance" and what Buddhist psychology calls "metta toward self"

Self-directed positive regard is the attitudinal, affective, and motivational orientation toward one's own flourishing that parallels the structure of other-directed regard. Its TI Sigma formula is identical to PRS(A, T) with T = A.

The key result: **self-directed PRS and other-directed PRS are not independent.** The BOK Being-Other-Knowledge framework predicts they co-develop. Low self-directed PRS limits other-directed PRS capacity — a well-documented empirical finding (you cannot sustainably give what you do not have). High self-directed PRS is not narcissism (which is high attitudinal component with negligible affective and motivational components) — it is full-spectrum self-love with appropriate affective resonance to one's own experience and genuine motivation toward one's own flourishing.

### 5.4 Self-Report / Phenomenological

## 7. The Mirror: What Absence of Positive Regard Looks Like

Negative regard is not the opposite of positive regard on one dimension. It is the breakdown of the three-component integration:

- **Attitudinal collapse:** The person loses standing — they are devalued, dismissed, categorically reduced. The self-model and other-model diverge maximally (high KL-divergence). STV-PRI → 0.
- **Affective inversion:** Resonance becomes aversion — other's positive states produce envy or indifference, not warmth. HRV coherence collapses during target salience.
- **Motivational withdrawal or reversal:** Active avoidance, or worse, active interference with the target's flourishing.

The Tralse state for a specific relational target is exactly this: the person simultaneously holds residual positive regard (they matter to you, you are not indifferent) and active negative regard (aversion, resentment, rejection). The Tralse resolution for negative regard is not forced positivity — it is genuine acknowledgment of the negative components, followed by MR processing, followed by either (a) resolution into positive regard or (b) honest withdrawal into neutral/no-regard (ending the relationship). The Tralse-preserved "I hate them but I love them" is addressed by moving through MR to resolution, not by performing positivity over the Tralse substrate.
