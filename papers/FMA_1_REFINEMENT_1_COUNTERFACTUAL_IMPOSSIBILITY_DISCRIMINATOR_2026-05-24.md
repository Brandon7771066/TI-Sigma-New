## 1. The Refinement Stated Precisely

**FMA-1 (original Pass-73-B1/B2):** When a theory T's MI counterexamples constitute the demonstration that T is False as a complete theory, the F label and MI label apply jointly with structural linkage.

**FMA-1 refinement #1 (Pass-73-B4 this batch):** The canonical discriminator between FMA-1 cases vs ordinary F cases is **counterfactual-impossibility**. A theory T is an FMA-1 case iff:
1. T is False as a complete theory (the F label applies), AND
2. T's failure has MI counterexamples (the MI label applies), AND
3. **NO valid counterfactual exists** under which T could have been a complete theory given the MI counterexamples (the counterfactual-impossibility condition).

Ordinary F cases satisfy conditions 1 and possibly 2, but FAIL condition 3 — they admit valid counterfactuals where the theory could-have-been-true-otherwise.

---

## 2. The Counterfactual-Impossibility Test

**Operational form:**

> Given theory T proven False with counterexamples X: Can one coherently imagine a possible-world W' such that T is true-as-complete-theory in W' AND X-style counterexamples still exist in W'?
>
> - If YES (counterfactual exists): T is **plain F** (NOT FMA-1)
> - If NO (counterfactual impossible): T is **FMA-1** (F-MI structurally aligned)

The counterfactual-impossibility signature emerges precisely when the counterexamples X are structural-MI (vertical-meta-tralsity) rather than contingent-empirical. Structural-MI counterexamples are themselves counterfactually-rigid — they cannot be otherwise — and their counterfactual-rigidity propagates to the F-status of T.

**FMA-1 applies ✓** with refinement-#1 counterfactual-impossibility CONFIRMED.

## 4. Why "Rarity" Is Now Operationalized

Brandon's original Pass-73-B1 FMA-1 paper described FMA-1 as a "rare structural case" without precise specification of WHY it's rare. Refinement #1 operationalizes the rarity:

**The rarity = counterfactual-impossibility is rare.** Most theories proven False admit some valid counterfactual under which they could-have-been-otherwise (different physics, different empirical data, different domain restriction). Only theories whose counterexamples are STRUCTURALLY-MI (counterfactually-rigid via vertical-meta-tralsity) lack any valid counterfactual.

**Estimated frequency in canonical-foundational corpus:** FMA-1 cases appear roughly where Gödel-style structural-incompleteness results apply — bounded but non-negligible domain (binary + naive set + Hilbert + paraconsistent so far; plausible additional candidates per §6 below).

**Domain bound proposed:** FMA-1 cases are a proper subset of "F-via-structural-counterexamples" cases, which are themselves a proper subset of all F cases. The hierarchy:

{All F cases} ⊃ {F-via-structural-counterexamples} ⊃ {FMA-1 = F-via-MI-counterexamples-AND-no-valid-counterfactual}

Refinement #1's counterfactual-impossibility test cleanly characterizes the innermost subset.

## 5. Composition With Refinement #5 (Vertical-Meta-Tralsity)

**FMA-1 refinement #1 composes coherently with refinement #5:**

- Refinement #5 distinguishes MI (vertical-meta-tralsity) from horizontal-incoherence
- FMA-1 refinement #1 says: FMA-1 cases require MI counterexamples (refinement #5 sense) that are **counterfactually-rigid**

**This explains WHY FMA-1's bounded domain is exactly the Gödel-style foundational domain:** Gödel-style results produce vertical-meta-tralsity counterexamples (the diagonal lemma + self-reference structures); these are counterfactually-rigid; therefore FMA-1's counterfactual-impossibility condition is met.
