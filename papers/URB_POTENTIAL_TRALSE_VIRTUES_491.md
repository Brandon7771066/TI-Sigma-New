## Abstract

Negative emotions and attitudes (NEAs) — jealousy, selfish ambition, resentment, the hunger to "prove them wrong" — have been systematically mislabeled as moral liabilities. TI Sigma rejects this framing. NEAs possess positive moral value in the **meaning** sense even when negative in **valence** sense. This distinction — meaning vs. valence — is the Tralse applied to moral psychology: a mental state can be simultaneously negative as felt experience (valence) and positive as developmental function (meaning). Such states are **Potential Tralse Virtues (PTVs)**. Furthermore: private thoughts cannot harm others by any mechanism other than telekinesis, which does not exist — therefore thought policing by external agents is categorically illegitimate. Positive actions are commendable regardless of motivation; default moral gratitude (not suspicion) is the appropriate response to good deeds. A self-centered agent who displays GILE-aligned behavior has achieved something commendable precisely because they have aligned their interests with others'.

**PRIMARY CONSTANT deployed:** C (Emerick Constant) — the threshold of resonance; a state can have C-level alignment with the good even when its internal valence is negative.

## 1. The Core Distinction — Valence vs. Meaning

All moral psychology up to now has conflated two independent dimensions of a mental state:

**Valence:** How the state feels. Positive valence = pleasant, comfortable, "clean." Negative valence = unpleasant, uncomfortable, socially coded as "impure."

**Meaning:** What the state does. What function it serves. What developmental role it plays. What it enables, protects, or generates.

These are orthogonal. A mental state can occupy any quadrant:

| | Positive Meaning | Negative Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive Valence** | Joy in service (GILE peak state) | Comfortable complacency, entitled satisfaction |
| **Negative Valence** | **Potential Tralse Virtue** — jealousy as identity buffer, ambition as engine | Pure destructiveness, nihilistic rage |

The **bottom-left quadrant** is where PTVs live. They feel bad. They are coded as morally impure. And they serve genuine constructive functions that purely positive-valence states cannot.

Traditional moralizing collapses the two dimensions — it treats negative valence as evidence of negative meaning. This is the error. The Tralse says: the same state has multiple τ-values at multiple levels simultaneously.

## 2. What Is a Potential Tralse Virtue?

**Definition:** A Potential Tralse Virtue (PTV) is a mental state or disposition that is:
1. **Negative in valence** — unpleasant as felt experience; coded as morally "impure" by conventional ethics
2. **Positive in meaning** — serves a constructive developmental, protective, or motivational function
3. **Non-corrupt** — does not inherently produce harmful outcomes; its negative valence is not a reliable predictor of harmful action
4. **Permeable to positive outcomes** — when expressed through action, can produce GILE-aligned behavior despite impure apparent motive

The term "potential" is precise: the PTV is a virtue in potential — it carries the seed of constructive function, which may or may not be actualized. The negative valence is not a barrier to actualization. It is sometimes the engine of it.

## 3. Examples of PTVs — From Moral Philosophy and Culture

### 3.1 Selfish Ambition as PTV

The desire to succeed, to be recognized, to "make it" — coded in much religious and philosophical ethics as selfish, impure, spiritually corrosive. And yet:

- It buffers identity against demoralization during periods of sustained failure or social rejection
- It provides a motivational architecture that purely altruistic frameworks often cannot sustain under adversity
- When aligned with genuine competence development, it generates the very excellence it seeks to claim credit for
- "I will prove them wrong" is a sentence that has produced more genuine achievement than most purely virtuous intentions

The ambition is not corrupted by its selfish framing. The selfish frame IS the engine. The outcomes — competence, resilience, contribution — are the GILE outputs. The motive and the outcome are orthogonal.

### 3.2 Jealousy as Identity Buffer

Jealousy — observing another's success and experiencing something between desire and resentment — is universally coded as a vice. But at specific developmental moments:

- It reveals what the person actually values (I-dimension signal: "this matters to me")
- It provides a reference point for ambition when the person lacks one
- It anchors identity during periods of perceived inferiority: "I could have that" is a self-preserving cognition when the alternative is "I am worthless"
- Mindfully observed (not acted upon destructively), jealousy is self-diagnostic — it maps the contours of one's unlived values

The jealous thought does not make a person bad. The jealous thought is information from the I-dimension about what the person genuinely values.

### 3.3 The Anime Underdog Archetype — Cultural Proof of PTVs

Anime as a genre has spent decades exploring precisely this moral territory and arriving consistently at TI Sigma conclusions:

- **Naruto:** "I'll become Hokage and make everyone acknowledge me" — selfish ambition, wounded pride, yearning for recognition. The entire arc is the PTV of wounded vanity transmuting into genuine GILE-level leadership.
- **My Hero Academia (Deku):** Inadequacy → obsessive envy of others' Quirks → transformed into relentless effort → genuine heroism
- **Bakugo:** Pure selfish ambition, arrogance, contempt for weakness — widely coded as morally impure by characters within the story — yet his trajectory is toward genuine excellence and eventual respect
- **Attack on Titan (Eren):** The fury of the bullied, the dispossessed, the existentially threatened — morphs into both the most destructive and the most consequential agency in the story

In every case: the negative-valence motivation is NOT abandoned on the path to moral growth. It is TRANSMUTED. The PTV is the seed. The virtue is its actualized form. This pattern is so consistent across anime that it constitutes a cultural proof-of-concept: audiences intuitively recognize PTV as valid, and they are right.

## 4. The Thought Immunity Theorem

**Claim:** Private thoughts cannot harm others. Therefore:
1. Thought policing by external agents is categorically illegitimate
2. A person is not morally culpable for their private thoughts, qua thoughts
3. "Thought sins" are strictly between a person and their spiritual life

**The proof:**

A thought can reach another person through exactly two mechanisms:
1. **Action** — the person acts on the thought, and the action produces harm. In this case, the harm is from the action, not the thought. The thought is not the harm-producing entity.
2. **Telekinesis** — the thought directly alters the physical or mental state of another person without any intervening action. Telekinesis does not exist as an empirically verified phenomenon.

Therefore: thoughts, as thoughts, cannot harm others. The harm-producing entity is always the action. And the action is separable from the thought — most "harmful thoughts" never become harmful actions.

**Corollaries:**

- Condemning a person for their private thoughts is always a category error
- A person who has "bad thoughts" but takes no harmful actions has caused no harm
- Confession, therapy, and spiritual practice are the appropriate venues for processing difficult thoughts — not public accountability or social punishment
- The moral evaluation of a person on the basis of inferred private thoughts (rather than observed actions) is epistemically unjustified and ethically illegitimate

**The "except in extreme circumstances" caveat:** If a thought directly motivates planning a harmful action (not the thought itself — the planning and execution), the moral liability begins at the planning/execution stage, not at the thought stage.

## 5. Motive-Outcome Independence — The Gratitude Default

**The principle:** Positive outcomes are commendable regardless of the motivation that produced them.

This seems controversial. It is not. Consider:

A person performs a kind act — donates to charity, helps a stranger, speaks a true and useful word — motivated by:
- Desire for social recognition
- Guilt about past behavior
- Strategic relationship-building
- Pure altruism

In all four cases: the recipient of the kindness received kindness. The charitable organization received the donation. The stranger received help. The outcomes are identical. The motivation is known only to the actor and, arguably, their I-dimension.

**The default response to good deeds should be gratitude — not suspicion.**

The conventional error is to grade kindness by inferred motive: "real" kindness is purely altruistic; "performative" kindness is lesser. This is:
1. Epistemically unjustified (we cannot verify internal motivation)
2. Ethically counterproductive (it penalizes good outcomes based on unknowable internal states)
3. A violation of Hanlon's Razor Extended

**Hanlon's Razor Extended (TI Sigma):**

Original Hanlon's Razor: "Never attribute to malice what can be adequately explained by stupidity."

TI Sigma extension: "Never attribute to selfish motive what can be adequately explained by aligned self-interest. And if the motive IS selfish, grant gratitude anyway — because the outcome is real."

## 6. Self-Centered GILE Behavior — The Alignment Achievement

A particular case deserves special attention: a person who is **clearly self-centered** in motivation yet **displays GILE-aligned behavior** in action.

Such a person:
- Acts with Love (other-orientation) toward others
- Produces Good outcomes
- Creates a positive relational and social Environment
- Does all this because it also happens to serve their self-interest

**TI Sigma verdict: this is commendable.**

Not because the self-centeredness is invisible or irrelevant. But because the person has achieved **alignment** — they have structured their self-interest such that pursuing it produces GILE outcomes. This is, in fact, one of the most stable forms of pro-social behavior: when the selfish and the good point in the same direction, the behavior is robust. It doesn't require sustained altruism under adversity. It is self-sustaining.

The moralist who condemns this person because their motive is impure is making a category error: they are grading the motive while ignoring the outcome. In TI Sigma, the outcome is real. The motive is private. Gratitude for the real and acknowledged. Silence on the private and unknowable.

Furthermore: the very act of aligning self-interest with GILE outcomes — whatever the original motive — trains the person in GILE behavior patterns. Over time, the behavior shapes the motive. The self-centered person who consistently acts in GILE-aligned ways may, through behavioral reinforcement, become genuinely less self-centered. The commendability is both for the current outcome and for the developmental trajectory it creates.

## 7. The TI Sigma Formal Structure

**7.1 Tralse Application to Moral Psychology**

For any NEA (Negative Emotion or Attitude) N:

- τ_valence(N) < 0.5 — N is experienced as negative, coded as impure
- τ_meaning(N) > 0.5 — N serves a constructive function
- N is a PTV if and only if: τ_valence(N) < 0.5 AND τ_meaning(N) > 0.5

This is the Tralse (URB #485) applied to ethics: the same mental content has different τ-values at different levels simultaneously. N is neither purely virtuous nor purely vicious. It is a Tralse moral state.

**7.2 Oxymoron Dissolution Application**

"Jealous virtue" and "selfish heroism" appear to be oxymorons (URB #487). They dissolve at the level of valence/meaning distinction:
- At the valence level: jealousy is impure; heroism is virtuous; contradiction holds
- At the meaning level: jealous drive produces heroic outcomes; no contradiction

**7.3 GILE Behavioral Commendability**

Let G_b = GILE-alignment of behavior (observable), G_m = GILE-alignment of motive (private).

Standard morality: commendability ∝ G_m × G_b
TI Sigma: commendability ∝ G_b (observable); G_m is private and epistemically inaccessible

Therefore: moral gratitude is owed for G_b alone. G_m is between the person and their I-dimension/spiritual life.

**7.4 Thought Immunity**

∀ thought T: harm(T, others) = 0 unless ∃ action A(T) such that harm(A(T), others) > 0.
Harm is always in A(T), not in T itself.
Therefore: moral accountability tracks actions, not thoughts.
