## §2. B34b — Multi-FAAH Targeting (faah-1 + faah-2 Paralog Cross-Product)

### §2.1 Why multi-FAAH targeting matters

C. elegans has four FAAH paralogs (`faah-1` through `faah-4`). Per WormBase + Pastuhov 2016, `faah-1` is functionally dominant in the canonical NAE-hydrolysis pathway, but `faah-2` contributes a non-trivial residual activity. A monogenic `faah-1` knockdown construct may leave 20-40% residual NAE-hydrolysis from faah-2, blunting the AEA-elevation phenotype. Multi-FAAH-targeting constructs (single RNAi cocktail or dual-gRNA CRISPR vector) close this gap.

This matters directly for BlissGene's translational arc: human FAAH-OUT (Jo Cameron's locus) controls FAAH expression; a multi-targeting approach in the worm validates the *principle* of paralog co-suppression before mammalian-paralog work begins.

### §2.2 Surrogate model

**Independent-action combined-effect model:**
$$AEA_{elevation}(kd_1, kd_2) = 1 - (1 - w_1 \cdot kd_1)(1 - w_2 \cdot kd_2)$$

with `w₁ = 1.0` (faah-1 dominant) and `w₂ = 0.4` (faah-2 modest). Combined effect feeds the same Hill function (K=0.40, n=2.0) used in B33.

**Sweep grid:** kd_faah1 ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 0.8, 0.95} × kd_faah2 ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 0.8, 0.95} × 100 seeds × 12 readouts = **19,200 simulated worm-runs**.

### §2.3 P-MULTI-1 pre-reg + verdict

**P-MULTI-1:** dual knockdown (kd_faah1=0.80, kd_faah2=0.80) produces stronger osmotic-aversion signal than single knockdown (kd_faah1=0.80, kd_faah2=0) — specifically, |g_dual| − |g_single| ≥ 0.15.

| Condition | osmotic-aversion |g| vs WT | pct WT |
|---|---:|---:|
| Single (kd_faah1=0.80) | 1.369 | 77.9% |
| Dual (kd_faah1=0.80, kd_faah2=0.80) | 1.569 | 76.2% |
| **Gap** | **0.199** | — |

**P-MULTI-1 VERDICT: PASS** (gap = 0.199 ≥ 0.15 pre-reg threshold).

### §2.4 What this informs

The B32 Phase-3 construct-design queue should prioritize **multi-targeting constructs (RNAi cocktails covering faah-1 + faah-2, or dual-gRNA CRISPR vectors)** ahead of single-target faah-1 constructs. Expected wet-lab benefit per construct: small but measurable (~20% additional effect-size at saturating per-gene knockdown), consistent with independent-action additivity. Bigger benefit may appear at sub-saturating per-gene levels where residual paralog activity matters more.

**B34b also opens the falsifier path:** if Phase-2 wet-lab single vs dual comparison shows *no* additional effect from dual-targeting, either (a) the surrogate's `w₂=0.4` overestimates faah-2's role, or (b) functional redundancy is engaged via other paralogs. Either way, the surrogate gets recalibrated and the construct queue re-prioritized — exactly the iteration loop the in-silico-first pipeline is designed to enable cheaply.

## §3. B34c — Longevity + Stress-Response Surrogate Layer ⚠️ MAJOR #69 FINDING

### §3.1 The counterintuitive direction

The single most important biology lesson in this batch:

> **Per Lucanic et al. 2011 *Nature* 473:226, N-acylethanolamine signaling REDUCES C. elegans lifespan.**
>
> NAE depletion (via dietary restriction or fat-7 manipulation) EXTENDS lifespan by ~20-30%, partly via dauer-pathway-adjacent mechanisms. Conversely, **NAE elevation — exactly what FAAH-1 knockdown produces — should REDUCE worm lifespan, not extend it.**

This is the **opposite** of what naive intuition (or my own B32 §3.4 pre-reg P4 phrasing — "top construct extends mean lifespan ≥ 10% vs WT") would predict. **B32 §3.4 P4 should be inverted.** Acknowledged as a #69 finding requiring B32 errata.

### §3.2 Why this is a major BlissGene clinical-translation flag

If the worm result is mechanistically conserved — and the NAE-lifespan link has partial mammalian-pathway analogues in the FAAH/2-AG/AMPK axis — then **chronic FAAH inhibition in humans, the Jo Cameron mimicking strategy, may carry a longevity cost**. Jo Cameron herself was 71 at the time of the case report (Habib 2019); she is **lifetime-FAAH-deficient**, so her observed phenotype includes whatever longevity penalty exists already. The case report does not flag accelerated aging, but n=1 cannot detect a 10-20% mean-lifespan effect.

**This is exactly the kind of trade-off BlissGene's URB #603 first-mover thesis must engage with honestly:** "permanent wellbeing" cannot mean "extended wellbeing at the cost of shortened life" without disclosure. The clinical-translation arc (B32 Phase 5) must include a longevity-safety endpoint or explicitly characterize the trade-off in informed consent.

The worm work surfaces this *now*, before any mouse or human commitment. **This is the single highest-value insight from B34** — a $0 surrogate sweep just surfaced a translational safety concern that, if confirmed in mouse, reshapes BlissGene's go-to-market positioning. ASYMMETRIC §69 in action: brutal honesty as discipline.
