## 2. Anchor 1 — Damasio somatic-marker hypothesis + Iowa Gambling Task

### 2.1 What I can confidently state

- **Authors and date:** Antonio Damasio; the canonical Iowa Gambling Task paper is Bechara, Damasio, Damasio, and Anderson 1994, *Cognition* 50:7-15, "Insensitivity to future consequences following damage to human prefrontal cortex"
- **Core finding:** patients with bilateral ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) damage show normal IQ, normal working memory, normal explicit-reasoning ability — but persistent failure on the Iowa Gambling Task (preference for high-payout-high-penalty decks over the long-run-profitable low-payout-low-penalty decks)
- **Mechanism claim:** somatic markers — bodily/affective signals associated with prior outcomes — normally guide rapid decision-making; vmPFC damage disrupts the marker-integration, leaving cold reasoning intact but choice-quality degraded
- **VFP-1 relevance:** this is the canonical anchor for "valence carries information that cold reasoning misses". The vmPFC-damage pattern is a natural-experiment dissociation: when valence-integration is selectively disrupted while cold reasoning is preserved, decision quality drops. This is exactly the prediction VFP-1 makes.

### 2.2 What needs PDF verification before external publication

- Exact effect sizes from the 1994 paper (I recall the deck-A+B vs deck-C+D preference pattern was substantial but I don't remember the exact percentages — claim "substantial dissociation" rather than a specific number)
- Subsequent replication record: I believe Bechara et al. 1997 *Science* "Deciding advantageously before knowing the advantageous strategy" extended the finding to skin-conductance-response anticipatory markers, but the exact SCR-effect timing claim should be checked
- Contemporary critique status: I recall Maia & McClelland 2004 *PNAS* challenged the "unconscious" interpretation arguing subjects do have explicit knowledge — VFP-1 should engage this critique rather than ignore it. The critique sharpens VFP-1 toward "valence carries decision-relevant information" without committing to "valence is unconscious", which is the stronger and more defensible position.

### 2.3 #69 status assessment

The Damasio anchor is **well-established as an empirical finding** with **active mechanistic dispute** about interpretation. VFP-1 doesn't need the "unconscious somatic marker" reading; it needs the weaker dissociation claim (valence-integration impairment degrades decisions despite intact cold-reasoning), which is robust. Confidence: **high** for the weaker reading; **moderate** for the stronger Damasio-original reading.

## 3. Anchor 2 — Aesthetic neuroscience / default-mode recruitment

### 3.1 What I can confidently state

- **Citation claimed:** Vessel, Starr, Rubin 2012 *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*. Edward Vessel is a real aesthetic-neuroscience researcher; the 2012 paper title I recall is approximately "The brain on art: intense aesthetic experience activates the default mode network"
- **Core finding (as recalled):** intense aesthetic responses to artworks recruit the default-mode network (DMN), which is more typically associated with self-relevant processing and mind-wandering than with stimulus-driven sensory processing. The interpretation offered is that deep aesthetic engagement is structurally self-relevant — the artwork is integrated into the viewer's self-model, not merely evaluated as external object.
- **VFP-1 relevance:** if aesthetic-valence response is mere epiphenomenal byproduct, the neural signature should look like sensory-evaluation circuits. The DMN recruitment instead suggests aesthetic valence is functioning as identity-relevant information processing. This is consistent with VFP-1's "valence carries information about self-environment fit", though it doesn't prove the stronger functional claim.

### 3.2 What needs verification

- Exact 2012 paper title and findings — I have moderate confidence on the gist but lower confidence on specific quantitative claims (cluster locations, percentage of DMN voxels recruited, etc.)
- Subsequent replication: there is a broader "neuroaesthetics" literature (Chatterjee, Skov, Nadal, Pearce) that has expanded and complicated the 2012 picture; VFP-1's anchoring should note this rather than cite Vessel 2012 in isolation
- DMN-vs-task-positive interpretation contested: some readers interpret DMN recruitment as task-disengagement rather than self-relevant processing; the aesthetic-as-self-relevant reading is one interpretation among several

### 3.3 #69 status assessment

The aesthetic-neuroscience anchor for VFP-1 is **weaker** than the Damasio anchor. The empirical findings exist; the interpretive bridge to "valence is functional information processing" is one defensible reading rather than the dominant one. VFP-1 should cite it as **suggestive, not load-bearing**, with the Damasio anchor doing the heavy lifting. Confidence: **moderate** on existence of finding; **lower** on interpretation-as-VFP-1-evidence.

## 4. Anchor 3 — Awe research and downstream behavior

### 4.1 What I can confidently state

- **Authors:** Dacher Keltner (Berkeley) and Jonathan Haidt are the canonical awe-emotion theorists. The 2003 paper I cited is approximately Keltner & Haidt 2003 *Cognition & Emotion*, "Approaching awe, a moral, spiritual, and aesthetic emotion"
- **Core theoretical claim:** awe is characterized by (1) perceived vastness and (2) need for cognitive accommodation. It is distinguished from related positive emotions (joy, gratitude, admiration) by these two features.
- **Piff et al. 2015 citation:** Piff, Dietze, Feinberg, Stancato, Keltner 2015 *J. Personality and Social Psychology*, "Awe, the small self, and prosocial behavior". Reported finding: awe-induction (via standing under tall trees, watching nature videos, recall of awe experience) produces measurable downstream increases in generosity, ethical behavior, reduced entitlement, willingness to volunteer time.
- **VFP-1 relevance:** if awe were epiphenomenal — a mere byproduct of perception with no functional role — these downstream behavioral effects would not be expected. The behavioral effects are direct evidence that awe (a conscious valence state) carries information that shapes action.

### 4.2 What needs verification

- Replication record on Piff et al. 2015: there has been a broader replication crisis in social psychology; the specific awe-induction effects should be checked against multi-lab replication efforts (Many Labs initiatives) before citation in external publication
- Effect sizes: I recall the awe-prosociality effects are in the small-to-medium range (d ≈ 0.20-0.40) rather than dramatic; this is consistent with VFP-1 (valence is informative, not deterministic) but should be stated with the actual effect-size range
- Mechanism vs correlation: the Piff studies are correlational/quasi-experimental on awe-induction → behavior; causal mediation through specific cognitive variables is less well-established

### 4.3 #69 status assessment

The awe anchor is **moderately well-established** with **replication-crisis caveats**. The directional finding (awe-induction → prosocial-shift) is robust enough to anchor VFP-1's "valence shapes action" claim. The exact effect sizes and mechanistic pathway should be cited cautiously. Confidence: **moderate**, with explicit caveat on replication-crisis context.

## 5. Steelman of the anti-VFP-1 position (anti-prior)

A responsible VFP-1 statement must engage the contrary literature, not ignore it.

### 5.1 Affect-heuristic and System-1 errors

- Kahneman *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) synthesizes decades of work showing systematic errors driven by fast emotional/intuitive processing
- Slovic, Finucane, Peters, MacGregor 2007 *European Journal of Operational Research*, "The affect heuristic" — documents specific contexts where affect-driven judgment is biased relative to deliberative judgment
- Anchoring effects, availability heuristic, framing effects — all involve fast-affective shortcuts producing measurable errors

### 5.2 Why this doesn't refute VFP-1

VFP-1 does NOT claim valence is always reliable. It claims valence is *informative* and that *suppression loses information*. Both can be true: valence shortcuts can be wrong in some contexts (Kahneman/Slovic) AND valence-integration can outperform pure cold-reasoning in others (Damasio). The competent agent integrates V and R with awareness of which contexts favor which weighting — exactly the Policy-M Tralse-Middle position from the DSB arc.

The conventional-academic posture VFP-1 critiques is not "be aware of affect-heuristic failures" (which is sensible) but "treat cold reasoning as the epistemic gold standard and emotion as bias to be eliminated" (which is the binary inversion of Policy-W).

### 5.3 Where VFP-1 would be falsified

The pre-registered falsifiers (F-1, F-2, F-3 from batch-3 paper) remain the operative test. The affect-heuristic literature is consistent with VFP-1 as currently stated — it documents valence-as-misleading-in-some-contexts, not valence-as-pure-noise-everywhere.
