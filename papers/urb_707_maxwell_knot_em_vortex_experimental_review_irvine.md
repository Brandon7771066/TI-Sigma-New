## 2. Background: The Hopfion and Higher Knotted Solutions

In 1989, **Antonio Rañada** showed that Maxwell's equations admit exact solutions in which the field lines of E and B form **linked Hopf circles** — the simplest non-trivial knotted electromagnetic configuration, called a **Hopfion**. Subsequent work extended this to **trefoil knots, torus knots, and higher braided configurations**.

These are not approximations or perturbations: they are **exact, finite-energy, topologically protected solutions of the source-free Maxwell equations** in vacuum. Their existence was widely regarded as a mathematical curiosity until the 2010s, when **William Irvine and co-workers showed they could be produced experimentally** using structured laser beams and superconducting plasmas.

## 3. The Irvine Group's Experimental Program (Selected Highlights)

A non-exhaustive list of published experimental confirmations relevant to URB #573:

### 3.1 Hopfion in laser optics (Kedia, Bialynicki-Birula, Peralta-Salas, Irvine — 2013)
*"Tying knots in light fields"* (Phys. Rev. Lett. 111, 150404, 2013) — explicit construction of Hopfion configurations in classical optics with linked E and B field lines, demonstrating **stable propagation** of the knot for finite distances.

### 3.2 Vortex knots in fluid analog (Kleckner & Irvine — 2013)
*"Creation and dynamics of knotted vortices"* (Nature Physics 9, 253, 2013) — used the **hydrodynamic-EM analogy** (Helmholtz vorticity = magnetic field in dual formulation) to create knotted vortex tubes in water. **Showed that knotted topology is preserved under propagation** for hundreds of vortex periods, with eventual reconnection events that reduce topological complexity.

### 3.3 Knotted superfluid vortices (Kleckner, Kauffman, Irvine — 2016)
*"How superfluid vortex knots untie"* (Nature Physics 12, 650, 2016) — extended to superfluid analogs, demonstrated **the topological invariant (linking number) is preserved between reconnection events**, with quantized changes at reconnections.

### 3.4 Knotted plasma fields (Smiet et al. — 2015, 2017)
Various groups have demonstrated that **plasma confinement geometries (e.g., spheromaks, tokamak edge fields)** naturally form knotted configurations matching Maxwell-knot solutions, with stability properties predicted by topology.

### 3.5 Knotted phase singularities in optics (Larocque et al. — 2018, ongoing)
*"Reconstructing the topology of optical polarization knots"* (Nature Physics 14, 1079, 2018) — directly imaged polarization-knot topology in structured laser beams, **including verification of trefoil and figure-eight knot configurations**.
