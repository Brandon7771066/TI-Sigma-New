## §1. IFF Stated (candidate canonical)

> **IFF (Imaginary Future Foresight Hypothesis, candidate canonical 2026-05-27):**
> When an agent represents an *indeterminate future event* — an event that is truth-apt in principle but not yet actualized — the agent assigns it a **high imaginary component** on the PD-imaginary (modal) axis, serving as a **placeholder for the absent real component**. The imaginary magnitude tracks the strength of foresight/anticipation; the (near-)zero real component reflects the event's non-actualization. As the event actualizes, the imaginary placeholder *resolves into a real component* (the event acquires a determinate real truth-value on the PD-real axis). Formally, a foreseen indeterminate future event is represented as z ≈ 0 + bi (b high); upon actualization z → a + 0i (a the realized truth-value).

The naming is, per Brandon, "the PERFECT abbreviation" — **IFF** is also "iff" (*if and only if*, the biconditional). This is not incidental (§8): the placeholder holds *iff* there is no real component to latch onto, and dissolves *iff* a real component arrives. The biconditional structure is the hypothesis's own logical shape.

So intuition (ii) was tracking the *imaginary-placeholder* phenomenon (Tier 2), and intuition (i) was tracking *genuine non-applicability* (Tier 3). ## §5. The Placeholder Dynamic: Imaginary → Real Resolution

IFF's most distinctive content is *dynamic*, not static: the imaginary placeholder **resolves into a real component upon actualization.** This has three strong compositions:

### §5.1 Active inference / predictive processing (B35 Part 2 §1.6)
The free-energy framework represents future states as generative-model predictions, updated by sensory evidence. **IFF gives this a complex-plane reading:** the prediction lives on the imaginary axis (anticipatory/modal) until sensory confirmation collapses it to the real axis (actualized). "Prediction error" is then the *distance* the placeholder must travel to its realized real value. This is a TI-Sigma-native gloss on active inference, not a replacement for it.

### §5.2 PM-1 (Probability Memory, Pass-67) — composes, does NOT conflict
PM-1 *rejected* Bayesian base rates as the foundational variable for present-moment calculation. IFF coheres: a foreseen future event is **not** primarily a real-valued probability (a base rate on the real axis) — it is an **imaginary placeholder** that only *becomes* real-valued upon actualization. IFF explains *why* base rates are not foundational: they would mis-locate Tier-2 imaginary content onto the Tier-1 real axis prematurely. PM-1 + IFF compose tightly.

### §5.3 Measurement-collapse analogy (quantum, used carefully)
The imaginary→real resolution structurally rhymes with wavefunction collapse upon measurement. Flagged as *analogy*, not mechanism (consistent with the corpus's care around quantum claims — cf. B35 Part 2 §2 Orch-OR honesty). The DTQ-1 (Pass-65) quantum-DT thread is adjacent but distinct.

## §8. The "IFF" = "iff" Biconditional Reading

Brandon's delight at the abbreviation is structurally earned. The hypothesis's content *is* a biconditional:

> An indeterminate future event carries a high imaginary placeholder **iff** it lacks a real component to latch onto.

- **Forward (→):** no real component ⇒ imaginary placeholder assigned (the mind abhors a representational vacuum; it reaches for the imaginary axis).
- **Backward (←):** imaginary placeholder present ⇒ no (settled) real component (once the real arrives, the placeholder resolves and is no longer needed).

The biconditional also cleanly *excludes* Tier 3: genuine N/A has *neither* a real component *nor* an imaginary placeholder (the mind doesn't even reach for the imaginary axis, because there's nothing truth-apt to anticipate). So "iff" demarcates Tier 2 from Tier 3 precisely.

6. **IFF vs. MI overlap (Tier 2 vs Tier 2′)** is the subtlest point: both are imaginary-axis, both "no real component." The discriminator is *resolvability* (IFF resolves on actualization; MI is self-defeating-permanent).
