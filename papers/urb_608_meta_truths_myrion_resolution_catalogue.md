## Abstract

Myrion Resolution (MR) is the process by which a Double Tralse (DT) proposition — one that is incoherent, contradictory, or truth-absent — is examined and either resolved, dissolved, or accepted as irreducibly indeterminate. MRs can proceed iteratively: a second MR examines the first, a third examines the second, and so on, until contemplation ceases. A **Meta-Truth (MT)** is introduced as a formal term for any MR at the third level or higher that substantially contradicts a previous MR. MTs represent refinements toward a convergent Permissibility Distribution (PD) value — they are not aberrations but the mechanism by which the PD converges to its true value. This paper introduces the concept, defines the MT threshold criterion, and provides the complete catalogue of major MT types organized into six categories: Reversal, Dissolution, Scope-Shift, Contextual, Acceptance, and Integration MTs. The iterative MR process terminates either by convergence (successive MRs confirm rather than contradict) or by deliberate cessation of contemplation, at which point the current PD value is treated as the operative truth-state.

## 1. The Iterative Structure of Myrion Resolution

### 1.1 First MR: Base Resolution

The first Myrion Resolution addresses a Double Tralse (DT) proposition directly — one that is incoherent, simultaneously affirmed and denied, or categorically ill-formed. The first MR asks: what is the truth-state of this proposition once the contradiction is examined?

**First MR outcomes:**
- **Resolve:** The apparent DT dissolves when examined — one arm was correct, the other was an error. Outcome: T, F, or Tralse.
- **Moot:** The proposition, upon examination, was not meaningful enough to have a truth-state. Outcome: the question is set aside.
- **Persist:** The DT is genuine — the contradiction is real and unresolvable at this level. The proposition remains DT; iterative MR continues.

### 1.2 Second MR: Examination of the First

The second MR examines the first MR's outcome or process. It asks: was the first MR's resolution correct, complete, or appropriate?

**Second MR function:** Quality assurance and depth-checking on the first MR. It may confirm the first MR's conclusion (convergence) or substantially modify it.

### 1.3 Third MR and Beyond: Meta-Truths

The third MR — and any MR after the second — is a **Meta-Truth** when it substantially contradicts a previous MR. At this level, the reasoning is no longer about the original proposition but about the reasoning process itself. The third MR asks: was the entire prior resolution framework appropriate? Have we been answering the right question?

**Key property:** MRs can proceed indefinitely. There is no logical ceiling on the number of MRs. The process terminates when:
1. **Convergence:** Successive MRs confirm rather than substantially contradict — the PD is stable.
2. **Deliberate cessation:** One decides to cease contemplation, accepting the current PD value as the operative truth-state.

## 2. Defining Meta-Truths

**Meta-Truth (MT):** A Myrion Resolution at the third level or higher that **substantially contradicts** a previous MR's conclusion.

**The substantiality threshold:** "Substantial" means the contradiction meaningfully changes the operative truth-state or the recommended action. Small refinements within the same truth-region (e.g., adjusting a PD weight from 0.65 to 0.70 toward T) are not MTs — they are ordinary convergence refinements. A MT changes the direction, reverses the conclusion, dissolves the question, or shifts the framework in a way that would alter what one does or believes as a result.

**MT function:** MTs signal that higher-order contemplation has produced a meaningful update. They are not failures of the earlier MRs — they are the mechanism by which the Permissibility Distribution refines toward its true value. Each MT is a data point that the initial framing required revision.

**The convergent PD:** Each MT shifts the PD. The process converges when the PD value stabilizes across successive MRs. The final stable PD value is the operative truth-state of the original proposition under full iterative MR.

---

## 3. Special Case: If a Previous MR Is Deemed Moot by a Later MT

If the third (or any higher) MR concludes that the second MR's resolution was itself **Moot** — that the second MR's conclusion was not meaningful, applicable, or did not actually resolve anything — the result is:

**"Indeterminate overall" as the meta-truth.**

This is because Moot applied to a previous MR means: the resolution process itself produced no net truth-content at that stage. The original question remains genuinely open — neither resolved nor dissolved — which is the definition of Tralse/Indeterminate at the meta-level.

This is a clean result: **Moot(MR_n) → Tralse at the meta-level.**

## 4. Complete Catalogue of Major Meta-Truths

### Category A: Reversal MTs
*The third MR substantially reverses the direction of the second MR's conclusion.*

---

**MT-A1: "Worth Doing Anyway" (WDA)**

**Trigger:** The second MR concluded against an action or position (F or strong F-Tralse). The third MR recognizes that despite the analysis, the action is still worth pursuing — the second MR's objections, though valid, are outweighed by considerations the second MR did not fully incorporate.

**Common structure:** The second MR found a genuine objection. The third MR weighs the full context and determines the objection, while real, is not decisive. The error in the second MR was overweighting one arm of the analysis.

**Example:** Second MR concludes "this research direction has a low probability of success." Third MR recognizes that even low-probability, high-impact directions are worth pursuing given the specific circumstances. MT = WDA.

**PD outcome:** Shifts F-ward PD back toward T or Tralse; operative conclusion reverses to "proceed."

---

**MT-A2: "Not Worth Doing After All" (NWDA)**

**Trigger:** The second MR concluded for an action or position (T or strong T-Tralse). The third MR recognizes that despite the apparent justification, the action is not worth pursuing — the second MR missed a decisive objection or overweighted the case for action.

**Common structure:** The second MR found a genuine case for action. The third MR discovers a cost, consequence, or constraint that the second MR underweighted or missed entirely.

**Example:** Second MR concludes "publishing this paper is clearly worth doing." Third MR recognizes it would prematurely reveal the strategic rollout before institutional allies are in place. MT = NWDA.

**PD outcome:** Shifts T-ward PD back toward F or Tralse; operative conclusion reverses to "do not proceed."

---

### Category B: Dissolution MTs
*The third MR dissolves the question or the previous MR itself.*

---

**MT-B1: "Moot" (Moot-MT)**

**Trigger:** The third MR determines that the second MR's resolution was itself not meaningful — the conclusion did not actually resolve anything, or the resolution was about a question that doesn't need answering.

**Common structure:** The second MR answered a question with apparent confidence. The third MR recognizes the question was the wrong one, or that the answer doesn't connect to any action or belief that matters.

**Meta-truth result:** Indeterminate overall. The original question is genuinely open because the resolution process failed to close it.

**PD outcome:** Convergence to center Tralse (PD weight concentrated at Indeterminate).

---

**MT-B2: "Wrong Question" (Dissolve)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the original proposition — and therefore all previous MRs — was answering the wrong question. The proposition itself was ill-formed in a way that previous MRs did not recognize.

**Common structure:** The DT arose from a category error in the original question, not from genuine complexity in the subject matter. The resolution should have been dissolution from the start.

**Example:** Extended MR process about "whether consciousness is physical or non-physical" — third MR recognizes that "physical" in this question is being used incoherently; the question dissolves, not resolves.

**PD outcome:** The PD is reset; a new, correctly formed question replaces the original.

---

### Category C: Scope-Shift MTs
*The third MR changes the level of stakes or the appropriate analytical framework.*

---

**MT-C1: "Escalate" (Stakes Are Higher)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the stakes of the decision are substantially higher than the second MR assumed — the conclusion requires a different framework, more evidence, or higher standards of justification.

**Common structure:** The second MR treated the question as a low-stakes local decision. The third MR recognizes it is actually a high-stakes precedent-setting decision that requires different consideration.

**Example:** Second MR resolves "this communication strategy is fine." Third MR recognizes the communication will become part of the permanent public record and sets precedent for how TI Sigma responds to critics. MT = Escalate.

**PD outcome:** Conclusion not necessarily reversed, but the PD narrows (requires higher confidence) and the action may be delayed for deeper analysis.

---

**MT-C2: "Descale" (Stakes Are Lower)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the second MR was operating with a framework calibrated to higher stakes than the situation requires — overthinking a low-stakes decision.

**Common structure:** The second MR applied a rigorous multi-arm analysis to a question that, upon reflection, doesn't warrant it. The analysis was correct but disproportionate.

**Example:** Second MR produces an elaborate GILE analysis of a minor wording choice. Third MR recognizes: this is a low-stakes decision; either option is fine; proceed with any reasonable choice.

**PD outcome:** Convergence to T quickly; reduces paralysis from over-analysis.

---

### Category D: Contextual MTs
*The third MR reveals that the conclusion is context-dependent in ways the second MR did not capture.*

---

**MT-D1: "Context-Dependent" (CD)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the second MR produced a universal-seeming conclusion that is actually context-specific. In different contexts, the opposite conclusion applies.

**Common structure:** The second MR resolved to T or F. The third MR identifies the hidden context variable that the second MR treated as fixed but is actually variable.

**Example:** Second MR concludes "this intervention is beneficial." Third MR recognizes: beneficial for I-arm dominant individuals, but possibly harmful for E-arm dominant individuals. MT = Context-Dependent.

**PD outcome:** PD splits by context — T in Context A, F in Context B, Tralse when context is unknown.

---

**MT-D2: "Asymmetric" (Direction-Dependent)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the second MR's conclusion is valid in one direction but not in the reverse — the relationship or principle is non-commutative (see URB #605).

**Common structure:** The second MR resolved a symmetric-seeming question as if both directions yielded the same answer. The third MR recognizes a fundamental asymmetry.

**Example:** Second MR concludes "consciousness and its physical substrate are equivalent." Third MR recognizes: physical substrate is necessary for this-level consciousness (T), but not sufficient for consciousness in general (F in reverse). MT = Asymmetric.

**PD outcome:** Two separate PDs — one for each direction of the proposition.

---

### Category E: Acceptance MTs
*The third MR determines that further MR is inappropriate — acceptance is the correct response.*

---

**MT-E1: "Accept Imperfection" / "Good Enough" (GE)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the ideal resolution is not achievable given current information, time, or stakes — and that further contemplation produces diminishing returns. The current PD value, while not perfect, is actionable.

**Common structure:** The second MR produced a Tralse outcome (genuinely indeterminate) and implied further analysis is needed. The third MR recognizes this question may never be fully resolved, and action must proceed on the current best estimate.

**Example:** Second MR: "We cannot determine with confidence whether this outreach strategy will succeed." Third MR: "We cannot know in advance; the current strategy is our best estimate; proceed and update based on evidence." MT = Good Enough.

**PD outcome:** PD locked at current value; deliberate cessation of contemplation; action proceeds.

---

**MT-E2: "Irreducible" / "Paradox Stable" (PS)**

**Trigger:** The third MR determines that the DT is genuinely irreducible — the contradiction is not a result of error or insufficient analysis but is a real feature of the situation. The appropriate response is to live with the contradiction rather than force a resolution.

**Common structure:** Multiple MRs have each produced different conclusions; the instability is itself informative — this proposition genuinely admits no stable resolution. Forcing one would be distortion.

**Example:** "Is consciousness physical?" — after repeated MRs, each producing contradictory resolutions, the third MR recognizes: the question is genuinely paradox-stable at current understanding. Neither collapse is warranted. MT = Paradox Stable.

**PD outcome:** PD remains centered on Tralse; DT is accepted as the stable state; no further resolution is attempted.

---

### Category F: Integration MTs
*The third MR finds a synthesis that transcends the prior MR's framing.*

---

**MT-F1: "Transcend" (Both Were Partial)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the second MR's resolution was correct at one level but missed a higher-level synthesis that incorporates both arms of the original contradiction. Neither previous MR was wrong — they were both incomplete.

**Common structure:** The DT arose from two genuine partial truths. The second MR chose one. The third MR finds the frame in which both are preserved.

**Example:** DT: "This system must be both rigorous and accessible." Second MR resolves: "Prioritize rigor; accessibility is secondary." Third MR recognizes: there is a framing in which rigor and accessibility are not in tension — the apparent conflict was a false dilemma. MT = Transcend.

**PD outcome:** PD resolves to T at the higher-level synthesis frame; the original contradiction dissolves.

---

**MT-F2: "Both True at Different Levels" (BT)**

**Trigger:** The third MR recognizes that the contradiction in the DT was real, but the two contradicting claims operate at different levels of description — both are true within their appropriate domain.

**Common structure:** The apparent contradiction arose from applying the same proposition across levels where different GILE weights apply or different existence footprints are relevant.

**Example:** DT: "Consciousness is fundamental (T)" vs. "Consciousness is generated by physical processes (T)." Both claims are true — at different levels of description (phenomenological vs. mechanistic). MT = Both True at Different Levels.

**PD outcome:** PD resolves to T for Claim A within Domain A, T for Claim B within Domain B; apparent contradiction dissolves into level-specification.

## 6. The Iterative Convergence Process

The full iterative MR process with MTs can be represented as:

**MR₁** → first resolution (resolve / moot / persist)  
**MR₂** → second resolution (confirm / modify)  
**MR₃...MRₙ** → Meta-Truths (substantially contradict previous MR) or convergence refinements (do not)

**Convergence criterion:** When successive MRs fail to produce a MT — i.e., when the PD value is stable across n consecutive MRs — the process has converged. The stable PD value is the operative truth-state.

**Deliberate cessation:** One may choose to cease contemplation at any point. The current PD value at cessation is the operative truth-state. This is the appropriate response when MT-E1 (Good Enough) applies — recognizing that perfect convergence may require indefinite contemplation, and action has its own value.

**The deepest MTs:** The higher the MR level, the more the MT reflects meta-cognitive insight rather than object-level analysis. Very high-level MTs (MR₅ and above) typically produce Category F MTs (Transcend, Both True at Different Levels) — the highest-level synthesis insights that reframe the entire prior analysis.

## 7. Indeterminate as the Epitome of Tralse — Canonical Formalization (Brandon 2026-05-12)

**Source:** `papers/PASS_47_INSIGHTS_AROUSAL_BREAKING_PLUS_LAZY_BINARY_TRALSITY_2026-05-12.md` §4.2, ratified by Brandon 2026-05-12. Added to URB #608 per Brandon's authorization in the same exchange.

### 7.1 Core Claim

Among the canonical base-4 truth-labels {True (T), False (F), Indeterminate (I), Double Tralse (DT)} ratified in `papers/MR_TRUTH_LABELS_CANONICAL_RULING_2026-05-08.md`, the label **Indeterminate (I)** uniquely maximizes the product of two structural quantities:

> **VALID TRALSENESS(P) := τ(P) × stability(P)**

where:
- **τ(P)** = the *tralseness measure* of proposition P (the degree to which P resists collapse to classical binary T/F).
- **stability(P)** = the persistence of P's truth-state under self-application or iteration; equivalently, the absence of paradoxical self-destabilization (¬paradoxical ⇒ stable).

**Theorem (informal, proposed canonical):** Indeterminate uniquely maximizes valid tralseness.

### 7.2 Derivation

| Label | τ(P) | stability(P) | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T** | Low (binary collapse to True occurred) | High (stable under iteration) | LOW |
| **F** | Low (binary collapse to False occurred) | High (stable under iteration) | LOW |
| **DT** | High (τ ∧ ¬τ ⇒ maximal tralseness, paradox-encoded) | LOW (paradoxical, destabilizing under iteration / MR) | LOW-TO-MODERATE |
| **I** | High (truth-state genuinely unsettled = high tralseness) | High (terminal label per Validly-Indeterminate Stance §2.3b of source paper; not paradoxical) | **MAXIMAL** |

**Sketch of argument:**
1. T and F have *low* τ because binary collapse has already occurred — the proposition has been resolved into one of the two classical poles, leaving no residual tralseness.
2. DT has *high* τ because it encodes paradoxical bothness (τ(P) ∧ ¬τ(P)) which is the maximally tralse structural form. However, DT has *low* stability because under iterative MR the paradox tends to either (i) resolve via a Reversal/Dissolution/Synthesis MT (Categories A/B/F), (ii) stabilize as a genuine paradox via MT-E2 (Paradox Stable), or (iii) convert to Indeterminate via MT-B1 Moot. In all three cases the DT itself is unstable — it transitions to a different state under contemplation.
3. I has *high* τ because the proposition's truth-state is genuinely unsettled — it has not collapsed to a binary pole and is not encoding a paradox; it is the "open question" state, which carries genuine tralseness. I has *high* stability because it is a *legitimate terminal truth-label* per the Validly-Indeterminate Stance (§2.3b of source paper) — making an Indeterminate assertion AND STOPPING THERE is a complete honest epistemic act, not an incomplete one.

The product τ × stability is therefore maximized uniquely at I.

This is the *specific structural prediction* of the max-valid-tralseness theorem applied at the meta-level — NOT a tautological "any-result-confirms" claim. - T or F at meta-level → would refute "TI Sigma contains its own negation"
- DT at meta-level → would imply destabilizing self-paradox; corpus would not be operationally usable
- **I at meta-level → max-valid-tralseness state realized; predicted state**

**For routine MR work:** When iterative MR converges on I (rather than T, F, or DT), the convergence is to the *highest-tralseness stable state*. This reframes I from "the disappointing fallback when nothing else fits" to "the structurally optimal terminal state when genuine unsettledness is the truth of the matter."
