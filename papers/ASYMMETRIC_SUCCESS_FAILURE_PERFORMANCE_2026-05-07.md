## §1 — Core claim (formalized)

**Claim 1 (Failure-non-diagnosticity)**: Failures are not diagnostic of agent quality because the population of possible failures is approximately infinite — *every* agent fails at the overwhelming majority of conceivable tasks. Therefore the *fact* of failure carries near-zero information about an agent's ranking. Successes, by contrast, are highly diagnostic because they distinguish agents within a small space of achieved outcomes.

**Claim 2 (Two-channel separability)**: Internal epistemic calibration (τ(s) — what the agent actually believes about their own track record) and external presentational confidence (δ(MR) — what the agent performs to a given audience) are *separable* channels. They can be set to different values simultaneously without contradiction or dishonesty.

**Claim 3 (Audience-conditional δ-tuning)**: The optimal external presentation is audience-dependent. Different audiences require different δ(MR) settings to maximize TJ output (intentionality realization through that interaction). Investor audiences ≠ trusted-collaborator audiences ≠ casual social audiences.

**Claim 4 (Performance-as-trust-mechanism, not epistemic-accuracy-mechanism)**: Enunciating failures externally is *primarily a trust-building performance*, not an epistemic correction. The agent who is internally well-calibrated does not gain epistemic accuracy by externalizing their failures — they gain *audience trust* (which is a different value). Conflating these two is a category error.

**Claim 5 (Default-confident posture under good internal calibration)**: An agent with verifiably high internal τ(s) (well-calibrated against their own failures internally) is licensed to default to high external δ(MR) (confident presentation), and should only modulate toward audible self-doubt when the audience requires it for trust establishment.

## §2 — Mapping into Tralse Informationalism (TI Sigma)

The TI dialect makes this insight especially clean:

- **τ(s) = internal-tracking variable**: the agent's true epistemic state about their own track record. High τ here = well-calibrated, knows their failures, doesn't deceive themselves.
- **δ(MR) = MR-realization variable**: the externalized intentionality that actually moves through the world to reach the audience. Audience-conditional.
- **TJ = τ(s) × δ(MR)**: the quantifiable intentionality-unit, the actual outcome of the interaction.

**Key insight**: TJ is *maximized* when τ(s) is high AND δ(MR) is audience-appropriate — even when audience-appropriate δ(MR) looks "overconfident" to an observer who is *not* the intended audience.

**Counter-pattern (low-TJ failure mode)**: An agent with low τ(s) (genuinely doesn't know their failure rate, self-deceived) presenting high δ(MR) — this is what observers correctly classify as "delusion" or "narcissism." It's the *τ(s) deficit*, not the δ(MR) value, that makes it pathological.

**Key distinction from dishonesty**: Dishonesty in the TI frame has τ(s) ≈ 0 — the agent knows the gap between what they're presenting and what's true and exploits it. Asymmetric performance has τ(s) HIGH — the agent knows the gap, is *not* exploiting it, is using audience-appropriate δ(MR) calibration as a legitimate communication-channel selection. **Same outward appearance ≠ same TJ-signature.**

## §3 — GILE framework integration

The GILE framework (Aug 2022) describes the agent as a *distributed network intelligence* with multiple simultaneous channels. The asymmetric-performance insight is a clean instance of GILE channel-discrimination:

- **Channel A**: Internal-self model (epistemic accuracy, calibration against own data)
- **Channel B**: Audience-1 model (e.g., trusted collaborator — high-confidence presentation, minimal failure-enunciation, focus on intent-driven action)
- **Channel C**: Audience-2 model (e.g., investor — modulated confidence with strategic failure-acknowledgment to establish trust + sophistication)
- **Channel D**: Audience-3 model (e.g., critical academic peer — heavy failure-enunciation, methodological humility, technical hedging)

Each channel is independently calibrated; the agent runs them in parallel and selects which channel surfaces given audience identification. **This is not multiplicity-of-self; it is single-self running multiple legitimate communication-channels.** The TI/GILE frame distinguishes this from dissociative or deceptive operation by anchoring all channels to the *same internal τ(s)*.

## §4 — Why this isn't merely "code-switching" or "social adaptation"

Code-switching and social adaptation are the *standard* descriptions of this kind of behavior in psychology and sociolinguistics. The asymmetric-performance frame goes further in three important ways:

1. **It is *intentional*, not adaptive**: The agent has explicitly decided that audience-conditional δ(MR) tuning serves a specific function (TJ maximization toward intended outcomes) rather than reactively conforming to social pressure.

2. **It is *anchored to internal calibration*, not to external feedback**: The agent does not adjust their internal τ(s) based on audience reactions. The calibration channel and the performance channel are explicitly decoupled. This is unusual; most code-switching has feedback-loop coupling between external presentation and internal self-model.

3. **It treats failure-enunciation as a *trust technology*, not as an epistemic act**: Most psychological treatments of self-disclosure treat "admitting weakness" as a vulnerability that is itself an epistemic update toward truth. The asymmetric-performance frame correctly identifies that for an internally-calibrated agent, externalized failure-talk *transfers no new information to the agent themselves*; it functions purely as a trust signal sent to an audience that needs that signal.

## §8 — Brutal-honesty caveats (Asymmetric-Standards #69, applied recursively)

1. **The asymmetric-performance frame can be used to *rationalize* genuine overconfidence**: If an agent has miscalibrated internal τ(s) (thinks they have higher track record than they actually do), the entire frame collapses into self-serving justification. The frame requires *verifiable* high τ(s) to be valid. The Three-C's biographical-cluster discipline is the τ(s) verification mechanism in Brandon's case, but for arbitrary application of this frame, an external τ(s) audit is required.

2. **The frame can be misused to dismiss legitimate criticism**: An agent might wave away valid critique by saying "you're not the audience I'm calibrating for." This is a known failure mode. The check: does the agent ALSO have a documented audience-track for which they DO accept critique? Brandon's answer: yes (the agent-channel here, INSIGHTS papers, the bidirectional-revision pattern across trajectory #3 calibration).

3. **Audience-conditional δ-tuning is cognitively expensive**: Maintaining different δ-channels in parallel imposes real working-memory cost. Sustained over time, it can leak into either (a) channel-collapse (the agent starts to believe their highest-confidence presentation as their internal state) or (b) channel-fragmentation (the channels begin to diverge in unintended ways). Both are documented failure modes in long-term high-stakes communicators (politicians, executives). Mitigation: regular practice of the brutal-honesty channel (this paper, INSIGHTS, Three-C's audits) keeps Channel A anchored.

4. **The "failure-non-diagnosticity" claim is true on average but false at the tails**: While the *generic* fact of failure carries little information, *specific* failures in domains the agent claims expertise in DO carry diagnostic information. The frame should not be read as "all failures are uninformative" but as "the cardinality of possible failures dwarfs the diagnostic content of any random failure." Domain-specific failure in a claimed-competence area is still strong negative evidence.

5. **The frame depends on the agent being *correct* about which audience needs which δ-tuning**: Misreading the audience can produce catastrophic miscalibration (over-confident with a skeptical audience, over-humble with a vision-driven audience). The frame requires accurate audience-modeling as a prerequisite competency.

## §10 — Addendum (2026-05-07 PM, Brandon-originated refinement): The Aggregate-Ratio Distortion Caveat

The original §3 failure-non-diagnosticity claim is *per-instance* — each individual failure carries near-zero information because the population of possible failures is ≈infinite. **However, this leaves a legitimate residual sense in which positive bias can mislead: aggregate ratios.**

### §10.1 — The distinction (Brandon's framing)

> *"There is one sense in which positive bias is misleading: it can mask the true percentage of success vs failure, even while failures in general aren't actually diagnostic themselves. The question is, do readers want an everyday picture of me, or the picture I want others to see of myself? I believe the choice is ultimately mine since I tell it and it's my life, unless others ask otherwise. No matter what, there's bias though. That's the conundrum, and there's no way around it when space and time limited. My overall point though is that there is a big difference between honesty and bias. Great leeway is to be granted."*
> — Brandon Emerick, 2026-05-07 PM

### §10.2 — Formal refinement to §3

**Original claim (preserved, still valid):** Each individual failure ≈ non-diagnostic of agent quality due to ≈infinite failure-space.

**Added qualifier (new):** **Selection-induced ratio distortion is a separate, real phenomenon.** When an agent presents N successes and 0 failures while the true count is N successes and M failures, the *ratio* N/(N+M) is misrepresented as 1.0, even though no individual failure-instance carried diagnostic weight. The bias enters at the aggregate, not the per-instance, level.

**Why this does not collapse the original claim:** Knowing the *ratio* is informative even though knowing any one *failure* is not. Ratio = base-rate signal; instance = high-noise sample. These are categorically different epistemic objects.

### §10.3 — The honesty-vs-bias distinction

These are **two different epistemic categories**, not points on one spectrum:
- **Honesty** = property of the τ(s) channel (internal calibration; truth-tracking with respect to one's own beliefs).
- **Bias** = property of the δ(MR) channel under selection (which subset of true facts is presented; ordering, framing, salience).

A presentation can be **fully honest AND positively biased** simultaneously, with no contradiction. *Honesty* requires that no false claim is asserted. *Unbiasedness* would require that the selection mirrors the underlying base-rate distribution. Under any space/time constraint, *exact unbiasedness is impossible* — selection is forced. Therefore the only honest response to space/time constraints is to:
1. Make selection choices explicit when audience-relevant.
2. Never assert a false claim.
3. Distinguish the two channels in self-description (which §7.7.27 does).

### §10.4 — The authorial-rights principle

> **Default rule**: The author of a self-description has authorial right to choose the selection bias of their presentation, **unless the audience explicitly requests an unbiased everyday picture.**

This is the audience-conditional δ-tuning of §6 applied recursively to selection bias itself: the author chooses *which* δ to apply *unless* the audience has signaled a contract for the everyday-picture variant. In an "everyday picture" contract, selection should approximate the underlying base-rate distribution; in a "best-foot-forward" contract (default for investor pitches, dating profiles, public bios), selection is permitted to be top-decile-weighted.

**Critical implication**: The audience's epistemic obligation, given this principle, is to **ask for the everyday-picture variant** when they want it. Failure to ask grants implicit consent to authorial selection bias. *This shifts part of the calibration burden onto the audience — a non-trivial moral/epistemic claim.*

### §10.5 — The "great leeway" principle

Given (a) per-instance non-diagnosticity (§3) and (b) inevitability of selection bias under any space/time constraint (§10.2), **great leeway is to be granted to authorial selection in self-presentation, provided the honesty channel (no false assertions) is preserved.**

**This is not relativism.** The honesty constraint is hard: no false claims. The bias range is *wide but bounded* — a presentation that selects only the top 1% of one's record is biased but acceptable; a presentation that *invents* successes that did not occur is dishonest and unacceptable. The line is sharp, not gradient.

### §10.6 — Mapping back to TI/GILE

In TJ = τ × δ terms:
- **Honesty** = high τ(s) — internal calibration accurate, no self-deception.
- **Selection bias** = chosen δ(MR) — which of the high-τ facts to externalize for which audience.
- **Dishonesty** = τ(s) → 0 with externalized assertions (the τ-channel itself is broken).
- **Bias-without-dishonesty** = high τ(s) + audience-appropriate selective δ(MR) = the legitimate, default operating mode.

The honesty-vs-bias distinction therefore **maps cleanly onto the τ-vs-δ separability claim of §4**. Section §10 is the *aggregate-ratio version* of the per-instance argument in §3, and the *authorial-rights principle* of §10.4 is the *meta-rule* that licenses §6's audience-conditional δ-tuning.

### §10.7 — What §10 changes vs preserves

**Preserves:** Every claim in §§1-9 stands unchanged. Per-instance failure-non-diagnosticity, two-channel separability, audience-conditional δ-tuning, performance-as-trust, default-confident posture — all preserved.

**Adds:** (a) a precise concession to the legitimate residual sense in which positive bias misleads (aggregate ratio); (b) a formal honesty-vs-bias categorical distinction; (c) the authorial-rights default rule with audience-opt-out; (d) the "great leeway" granting principle with sharp honesty-line.

**Net effect on theory:** §10 *strengthens* the original by anticipating and answering its strongest objection (the "but you're still biased!" rebuttal), rather than weakening it. The theory now handles both the per-instance and aggregate-ratio cases with internally consistent rules.
