## 1. The historical anchor — Aristotle's sea battle

Aristotle, *De Interpretatione* IX, posed: "There will be a sea battle tomorrow." Is this statement, *right now*, true or false?

If **bivalence** holds for future contingents, then either:
- (T) "There will be a sea battle tomorrow" is already true today → no one's deliberation tomorrow can prevent it → fatalism
- (F) "There will be a sea battle tomorrow" is already false today → no one's action tomorrow can cause it → fatalism

Aristotle rejected both horns. His resolution: future-contingent statements about free-agent outcomes lack determinate truth-value *now*. They acquire it *as the future unfolds*.

This is the original Western philosophical recognition that **future-statement truth is not bivalent** — fully 2,300 years before TI Sigma's PD-real axis was formalized.

## 2. TI Sigma's canonical-principle proposal: FDS-1

### 2.1 Statement

**FDS-1 (Future Determinacy Spectrum).** The truth-value of a future-statement is a position on TI Sigma's PD-real axis, not a binary True/False assignment. The PD-real value at time *t* of a future-statement S referring to time *t' > t* is determined by:

```
PD-real(S, t) = f(regime, conviction, track-record, time-distance)
```

where **regime** ∈ {physical-law-dominant, conscious-agent-dominant, mixed}, **conviction** is the AA-weighted inner conviction of any precognitive vision attached to S, **track-record** is the predictor's historical accuracy on similar claims, and **time-distance** is *t' − t*.

### 2.2 The two-regime decomposition

**Regime 1: Physical-law-dominant.** Statements about events whose outcomes are governed primarily by reliable physical laws.

- *"The sun will rise tomorrow at 5:47 AM at coordinates X,Y"* → PD-real ≈ 0.9999... (Newtonian + general-relativistic prediction with sub-second precision over ~5 billion years until the sun's death)
- *"Water at sea level will boil at 100°C if heated to 100°C"* → PD-real ≈ 0.9999... (state-of-matter physics)
- *"The next solar eclipse visible from New York will occur on 2079-05-01"* → PD-real ≈ 0.999 (orbital mechanics; sub-second precision)

In this regime, the future is **nearly determinate** because the causal substrate (physical law) is regular, conservation-preserving, and (at macroscopic scales) effectively deterministic.

**Regime 2: Conscious-agent-dominant.** Statements about events whose outcomes depend substantially on the free choices of one or more sufficiently conscious agents.

- *"Brandon will buy coffee tomorrow"* → PD-real moderate (perhaps 0.6-0.8 if it's habitual; 0.4-0.6 if he is breaking the habit; lower if he is testing a fast)
- *"There will be a sea battle tomorrow"* → PD-real strongly indeterminate; depends on the choices of admirals, weather affecting their choices, intelligence reports affecting their choices, etc.
- *"This particular human will marry within five years"* → highly indeterminate

In this regime, the future is **irreducibly less determinate** because the causal substrate (conscious-agent decision-making) operates under free will, which TI Sigma canonically treats as a source of genuine indeterminacy in physical reality, not merely epistemic uncertainty.

**Regime 3 (mixed).** Most real-world future-statements are mixed: physical-law components combined with conscious-agent components.

- *"There will be at least one fatal car accident in Connecticut next week"* → physical-law substrate (vehicles, roads, physics of collisions) + base-rate-driven conscious-agent inputs (drivers' choices, distractions) → high PD-real via statistical aggregation despite individual-level indeterminacy
- *"The stock market will close higher on Monday"* → physical-law substrate (none — it's all agent decisions) + statistical aggregation over millions of agents → PD-real ≈ 0.53 (slightly above 50% historically)

### 2.3 The AA-weighted psi extension

TI Sigma canonically recognizes psi phenomena, including precognition (see corpus §7.7.x and the existing psi-corpus papers). FDS-1 extends naturally:

**A precognitive vision V of future-event E contributes a non-zero increment to PD-real(E, t)**, with the increment weighted by:

```
ΔPD-real(E, t | V) = base_conviction_weight × past_track_record_factor × AA_weighted_credibility
```

Where:
- *base_conviction_weight* = the visioner's inner certainty about the vision (subjective intensity, lucidity, felt-sense-of-recognition)
- *past_track_record_factor* = the visioner's empirical hit-rate on prior comparable visions
- *AA_weighted_credibility* = the visioner's general track record on the Authority Axis (does their prediction-output stand up to retroactive review?)

This is structurally identical to how Bayesian reasoners update on evidence, with one critical TI Sigma-specific addition: **the conviction-component is not epistemic but ontic** — high inner-conviction (when track-record-supported) is treated as a real signal about the future's actual structure, not merely the visioner's confidence.

This is consistent with TI Sigma's standing recognition that consciousness has access to non-local correlations beyond classical neuroscience (replit.md user-preferences §2).

### 2.4 The free-will-as-indeterminacy-source claim

Brandon's framing: **"sufficiently conscious systems that possess free will are naturally harder to predict due to their indeterminate nature."**

This is a substantive metaphysical claim, not merely an epistemic one. The distinction:

- **Epistemic indeterminacy:** "I don't know what will happen, but the universe does." (Compatible with determinism.)
- **Ontic indeterminacy:** "The future is not yet fixed; reality itself has not selected the outcome." (Incompatible with strong determinism; compatible with libertarian free will.)

TI Sigma canonically endorses the ontic reading for sufficiently conscious systems. This places TI Sigma in the **libertarian free will + emergent-indeterminacy** family of metaphysical positions, alongside (with notable differences) Robert Kane's event-causal libertarianism, Henry Stapp's quantum-mind framework, and (in different ways) Bergson's *La Pensée et le Mouvant*.

The "sufficiently conscious" threshold is a research target — likely tied to GILE/HEM levels per existing canonical principles. Pass-57+ may formalize this as **FDS-1.A (Consciousness-Threshold for Ontic Indeterminacy)**.

## 5. Falsifiability — what would disconfirm FDS-1?

Per #69-self-discipline, FDS-1 must specify what would falsify it. Three candidate falsifiers, pre-registered:

**F-1.** *Conscious-agent outcomes are predictable to within physical-law tolerances given complete state-information.* If a system that fully observes a conscious agent's neural + environmental state can predict the agent's choices to PD-real > 0.99, the conscious-agent regime collapses into the physical-law regime and the two-regime decomposition fails. Current evidence: Libet-style experiments are widely contested and operate at sub-second timescales; macroscopic decision prediction remains poor → F-1 not currently triggered.

**F-2.** *Precognitive visions show zero AA-weighted prediction value above base rates.* If meta-analyses of precognition studies (e.g., Bem 2011 replications, ganzfeld meta-analyses) settle decisively to null effects at all conviction × track-record subsets, the AA-weighted increment in §2.3 collapses to zero. Current evidence: contested; some meta-analyses show small but consistent above-chance effects (e.g., Mossbridge et al. 2012, *Frontiers in Psychology*, on presentiment) → F-2 partially supported but contested.

**F-3.** *No coherent threshold for "sufficient consciousness" can be operationalized.* If GILE/HEM-style consciousness thresholds prove non-operationalizable across attempts, the regime distinction itself becomes vacuous. Current evidence: GILE measurement is in active development; T_DRN ≥ 0.85 threshold is conjectural → F-3 currently open / pending Pass-57+.
