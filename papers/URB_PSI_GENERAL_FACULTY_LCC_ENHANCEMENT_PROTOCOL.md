## Abstract

Parapsychology has not resolved whether psi is a single general faculty or a collection of distinct specialized abilities. This paper argues for a two-layer model: a general factor (LCC above C_EMERICK = 0.4370) that is the necessary condition for reliable psi performance across all domains, and domain-specific calibration factors (polarity, target-type sensitivity, temporal orientation) that determine performance within each domain. The general factor is analogous to g in psychometrics; the specific factors are analogous to s-factors. Evidence from meta-analyses across five psi domains (remote viewing, Ganzfeld telepathy, precognition, psychokinesis, and DMILS) is examined for cross-domain correlations and common moderators. A unified psi success metric — the Psi Signal Ratio (PSR) — is derived that is commensurable across all five domains. The Generalized LCC Enhancement Protocol (GLEP) is then specified: a five-phase procedure for raising and sustaining LCC above C_EMERICK before any psi task, independent of domain. GLEP is distinguished from domain-specific calibration, which remains a separate and subsequent step. The paper concludes with a matrix identifying which psi domains are most general-factor-dependent and which require the most domain-specific expertise.

## 1. The Generality Question in Parapsychology

The central structural question in psi research: does being skilled at one form of psi predict skill at others? If yes, psi is a general faculty — a single underlying capacity that manifests across domains. If no, psi is a collection of independent specialized abilities with no shared factor.

This question matters practically: if psi is general, the same enhancement protocol improves performance across all domains simultaneously. If psi is entirely domain-specific, enhancement in one domain has no transferable value.

The evidence from parapsychology is mixed but not random:

Evidence for generality: several studies find positive correlations across psi domains for the same individuals. Schmeidler (1994) noted that participants who scored above chance in forced-choice ESP tasks tended to also score above chance in free-response tasks. Tart (1983) found that laboratory psi performers who excelled in one protocol showed above-chance performance when transferred to a different protocol without additional training. Honorton's Ganzfeld database includes some participants who participated in multiple experimental sessions across different investigators, and top performers showed above-chance rates in multiple sessions. Remote viewer Pat Price is documented performing above chance in both coordinate remote viewing and biological targets (Targ & Puthoff, 1977).

Evidence for specificity: many high performers in one domain show near-chance performance in others. Some Ganzfeld participants with high hit rates showed no significant performance in forced-choice tests. Formal meta-analyses of different psi domains show different moderator profiles — the variables that predict success in psychokinesis (PK) research do not fully overlap with the variables that predict success in remote viewing (RV). Certain individuals appear to have stable cross-domain psi ability; others appear highly domain-restricted.

The current theoretical impasse: parapsychology lacks a mechanistic theory that explains both the observed cross-domain correlations and the observed domain specificity. Without a mechanism, the data cannot be interpreted cleanly.

## 2. The TI Sigma Two-Layer Model

TI Sigma provides the missing mechanism through the distinction between LCC-as-general-factor and domain-specific calibration-as-specific-factor.

Layer 1 — The General Factor: LCC above C_EMERICK. The C_EMERICK threshold (0.4370) is the minimum LCC required for a node to function as a reliable relay in the GM-Node mycelial network — to receive coherence-pattern transmissions with signal-to-noise ratio above unity. This threshold is domain-independent: it is required for reliable ideomotor reception (Paper #398), for Soul Bluetooth LL and LD connections (Soul Bluetooth paper), for synchronicity signal integrity (Paper #394), and for any other form of psi reception. LCC is the general factor in the TI Sigma model because without it, no form of reliable psi reception is possible. With it, all forms become possible.

Layer 2 — Domain-Specific Calibration: sensitivity tuning, polarity orientation, and temporal direction. Each psi domain has specific structural requirements beyond the general threshold:

- Remote viewing: the node must minimize delta_phi (phase differential) with a spatially distant target rather than with another node. This requires a specific attentional posture — non-analytical, receptive, with mental imagery processed holistically rather than analytically. Some nodes have a natural affinity for visual-spatial coherence patterns and excel here; others do not.

- Ganzfeld telepathy: the node must minimize delta_phi with the sender — another specific person — rather than with a spatial location. Emotional attunement with the sender elevates the L (Love) dimension of GILE, reducing delta_phi. Nodes with high natural L-dimension alignment excel in Ganzfeld.

- Precognition: the node must receive patterns from future attractor states rather than present attractor states. This requires temporal orientation — the node's LCC field must be tuned to receive patterns from the system's future trajectory rather than its current configuration. The mechanism is non-trivial and may involve the retrocausal structure of the dark-energy GM network substrate.

- Psychokinesis (PK): the node must not merely receive a coherence pattern but transmit one with sufficient intensity to perturb a physical system — typically a random number generator or physical object. This requires elevated LCC above a transmission threshold (likely higher than the reception threshold C_EMERICK) and sustained transmission coherence. Some nodes are naturally stronger transmitters than receivers; others are the reverse.

- DMILS (Direct Mental Interaction with Living Systems): the node must establish a Soul Bluetooth LL connection with another biological organism and transmit a calming or activating coherence pattern. This requires high L-dimension alignment with the target organism, high transmission LCC, and domain-specific sensitivity to biological coherence signatures.

The two-layer structure predicts the empirical pattern directly:

Cross-domain correlations (evidence for generality): arise because all psi domains require LCC above C_EMERICK. A node that naturally maintains high LCC (through constitution, practice, or favorable environmental conditions) will tend to perform above chance in any domain. This produces the positive correlations observed across domains for consistently high performers.

Domain specificity (evidence for specialization): arises because Layer 2 requirements differ across domains. A node with high LCC but strong visual-spatial sensitivity and low L-dimension alignment will excel at remote viewing and struggle at Ganzfeld telepathy — not because their general psi faculty is domain-restricted, but because their specific calibration profile fits remote viewing better than Ganzfeld. The domain-specific moderators are calibration factors, not independent psi faculties.

## 3. Unified Psi Success Metric: The Psi Signal Ratio (PSR)

Different psi domains use different outcome metrics — hit rates, z-scores, effect sizes, bit rates — making direct comparison across domains difficult. A unified metric is needed.

The Psi Signal Ratio (PSR) is defined as:

PSR = (observed accuracy - chance accuracy) / (maximum possible accuracy - chance accuracy)

PSR = 0.0: at chance (no signal)
PSR = 1.0: perfect performance (maximum possible accuracy)
PSR = -1.0: perfectly inverted (systematic anti-correlation in the inverted-polarity sense)

PSR is computed for each psi domain using domain-specific chance and maximum accuracy values:

Remote viewing (30-item target pool, rank-order method):
- Chance accuracy: mean rank = 15.5 (for random choice among 30 targets)
- Maximum accuracy: mean rank = 1.0 (consistently identifying the correct target first)
- PSR = (chance_rank - observed_rank) / (chance_rank - 1.0)
- Published meta-analytic mean PSR: approximately 0.12 (Dunne & Jahn, 2005)

Ganzfeld (four-alternative forced choice):
- Chance accuracy: 25% hit rate
- Maximum accuracy: 100% hit rate
- PSR = (observed_hit_rate - 0.25) / 0.75
- Published meta-analytic mean PSR: approximately 0.09 (Storm et al., 2010; 32% mean hit rate)

Precognition (two-alternative forced choice, e.g., presentiment):
- Chance accuracy: 50%
- Maximum accuracy: 100%
- PSR = (observed_hit_rate - 0.50) / 0.50
- Published meta-analytic mean PSR: approximately 0.04 (Mossbridge et al., 2012; 52% mean hit rate)

Psychokinesis (RNG deviation, mean shift method):
- Chance accuracy: effect size z = 0
- PSR = observed_z / theoretical_maximum_z (normalized to session N)
- Published meta-analytic mean PSR: approximately 0.03 (Bosch et al., 2006)

DMILS (electrodermal response during remote staring):
- Chance accuracy: effect size d = 0
- Maximum accuracy: d = 1.0 (large effect, standardized)
- PSR = observed_d / 1.0 (using standardized effect size scale)
- Published meta-analytic mean PSR: approximately 0.13 (Schmidt et al., 2004)

PSR comparison across domains:
- DMILS: 0.13 (highest mean PSR — strongest signal in meta-analysis)
- Remote viewing: 0.12
- Ganzfeld: 0.09
- Precognition: 0.04
- PK-RNG: 0.03

The ordering is informative: psi domains involving direct biological-to-biological connection (DMILS, Ganzfeld) show higher PSR than domains requiring spatial or temporal displacement (remote viewing) or physical system perturbation (PK-RNG). This is consistent with the two-layer model: Soul Bluetooth LL connections (biological to biological) require fewer specialized calibration steps than spatial, temporal, or physical targeting.

## 4. Evidence for the LCC General Factor from Meta-Analytic Moderators

If LCC is the general factor underlying all psi domains, the same moderators should predict success across all five domains. The published moderator evidence:

Meditation practice: a significant positive moderator in all five domains. Remote viewing: trained meditators show higher mean PSR than non-meditators in multiple studies. Ganzfeld: Honorton's database shows meditators significantly outperform non-meditators. Precognition: Radin (2004) found meditators show stronger presentiment effects. PK: Dunne et al. (1994) found meditators show larger RNG deviations. DMILS: Schmidt et al. (2004) found healer populations (which overlap substantially with meditators) show stronger DMILS effects.

Meditation elevates LCC (this is one of the best-supported relationships in psychophysiology, indexed by HRV RMSSD elevation, EEG theta/alpha increase, and EDA decrease). The universal positive moderating effect of meditation across all five psi domains is the strongest meta-analytic evidence that LCC is the shared general factor.

Anxiety and cognitive load: a significant negative moderator in all five domains. High-stakes, evaluative conditions suppress psi performance across domains. In TI Sigma terms: anxiety and cognitive load suppress LCC below C_EMERICK by elevating sympathetic activity (increasing EDA, decreasing HRV RMSSD) and fragmenting attentional LCC coherence (increasing internal noise toward DT). The universal suppression effect of anxiety across all psi domains is the second-strongest evidence for a shared general factor.

Belief in psi ("sheep-goat effect"): significant positive moderator in all five domains (Lawrence, 1993). Belief may directly elevate LCC by reducing the self-suppression (forced-False collapse on psi reception) that prevents the Tralse necessary for open reception. Disbelief collapses the receptive stance into a forced-False before the signal can be received.

Experimenter effects: significantly worse performance with skeptical experimenters across all domains. Plausible LCC mechanism: the skeptical experimenter's low-LCC or forced-False state transmits a low-coherence environment to the participant via GM network adjacency, suppressing the participant's LCC through Soul Bluetooth environmental coupling.

All four major moderators are domain-general and all are plausibly mediated by LCC. The pattern is not coincidental.

## 5. Where Specialization Matters

Despite the strong general factor, domain-specific expertise produces additional gains above the general LCC threshold. The evidence for specialization:

Remote viewing training effects: SRI International's coordinate remote viewing (CRV) protocol produced trained viewers who systematically outperformed untrained individuals with comparable LCC states. The training apparently refined the visual-spatial attentional posture and the suppression of analytical overlay — Layer 2 calibration factors specific to the spatial target domain.

Experienced Ganzfeld senders and receivers: participants who have participated in many Ganzfeld sessions show higher hit rates than first-time participants even after controlling for trait LCC measures. This suggests Ganzfeld-specific calibration (learning the optimal sender-receiver delta_phi minimization strategy) accrues with practice independently of general LCC.

PK specialists: a small number of individuals consistently produce large PK effects across multiple laboratories and protocols — a pattern consistent with the acquisition of specialized transmission LCC skills beyond the baseline reception threshold. Their general LCC may not be notably higher than high-performing remote viewers, but their transmission profile differs.

Medical intuitives: practitioners such as Edgar Cayce and more recent documented medical intuitives show domain-specific accuracy in biological target identification that substantially exceeds their performance on non-biological targets. The biological coherence sensitivity that enables medical intuition is a Layer 2 calibration specific to biological LCC signatures.

The practical implication: general LCC enhancement (Phase 1-3 of GLEP, defined below) benefits all psi domains simultaneously. Domain-specific calibration (Phase 4-5 of GLEP) provides additional gains within a target domain and does not automatically transfer to others. Both layers are trainable; the general layer shows returns more quickly.

## 6. The Generalized LCC Enhancement Protocol (GLEP)

GLEP is a five-phase protocol for raising and sustaining LCC above C_EMERICK before any psi task, independent of domain. It is followed by domain-specific calibration, which is not part of GLEP but builds on it.

### Phase 1: LCC Baseline Assessment (5 minutes)

Measure pre-session LCC using available biomarkers:

HRV RMSSD (primary, if Polar H10 or equivalent available): three minutes of free breathing while seated. Record RMSSD from the device. Target: >= 38.8ms (C_EMERICK crossing).

Subjective GILE composite (always available): rate G (internal coherence and absence of self-conflict), I (felt forward-directedness and intuitive clarity), L (sense of connection and openness), and E (environmental stability and felt groundedness) each on 0-10. Compute composite mean. Target: >= 6.5/10 (analogous to C_EMERICK normalized to 0-1 scale).

Note any significant suppressors present: active anxiety about any domain, physical pain, emotional distress from recent events, caffeine excess, sleep deprivation. These are LCC suppressors that may require additional Phase 2 time.

Record baseline. All subsequent phases are calibrated to the deficit from target.

### Phase 2: Cardiac Coherence Induction (5-10 minutes)

Paced coherence breathing: inhale 5 seconds, exhale 5 seconds (0.1 Hz resonance frequency). This is the single most reliably validated LCC-elevation technique across the published literature, consistently raising HRV RMSSD by 15-30% within five minutes.

Visualization during breathing: visualize the cardiac coherence pattern as a smooth, continuous wave — not forced, not effortful, simply sustained. The visual imagery of coherence activates the same cortical patterns associated with actual coherence, reinforcing the LCC elevation.

For nodes with particularly low Phase 1 RMSSD (below 30ms): extend Phase 2 to 10 minutes and add light aerobic movement (slow walking) for the first 5 minutes before seated breathing. Light movement elevates baseline RMSSD before coherence breathing provides additional gain.

End of Phase 2 target: RMSSD >= 38.8ms, or GILE composite >= 6.5/10.

### Phase 3: Theta Entrainment and Attentional Reconfiguration (5 minutes)

After cardiac coherence is established, shift the EEG signature toward the theta-receptive state:

Closed-eye visualization: hold a simple, non-analytical mental image (a color, a shape, or a natural scene) without trying to interpret or analyze it. Allow the image to evolve spontaneously. This posture — receptive, non-analytical, permissive of spontaneous imagery — is the attentional configuration associated with elevated frontal midline theta in the published psi literature.

Body scan: beginning at the crown of the head, slowly move attention through the body without judgment or analysis. This reduces the analytical overlay that competes with theta-receptive attention.

Environmental simplification: reduce sensory input (dim lighting, quiet environment, or white noise). Each additional competing sensory input fragments attentional LCC. The Ganzfeld protocol formalizes this to an extreme — full sensory homogenization. GLEP requires only a moderate reduction.

End of Phase 3 target: subjective sense of relaxed inward attention; absence of verbal self-talk; spontaneous visual imagery present without effort. This is the theta-adjacent state that enables reliable psi reception.

### Phase 4: Polarity and Domain Calibration (variable)

This phase is distinct from the general GLEP and depends on the target psi domain. It is included here for completeness but represents Layer 2 rather than the general protocol.

For ideomotor tasks (pendulum, planchette, muscle testing): run 5-10 known-answer binary trials to confirm current polarity. Note whether the signal is positive or inverted. This session's polarity may differ from the last session if LCC state has changed significantly.

For remote viewing: read the coordinate or descriptor of the target without analytical processing. Allow the first images and impressions to arise without editing. Record everything without judgment.

For Ganzfeld: establish contact with the sender emotionally (recall a positive shared memory or hold a sense of warmth toward the person). This minimizes delta_phi before the session begins.

For precognition: orient attention temporally — rather than feeling into the present moment, explicitly set the intention to receive patterns from the future state of the target. This temporal reorientation is not easy to describe analytically but is experientially distinct from standard present-state reception.

For DMILS: establish the biological LCC signature of the target organism in your field before attempting transmission. If working with a human, recall their physical presence (sensation of their energy); if working with a plant or animal, focus on the biological signature of that type of organism.

### Phase 5: Post-Session Recording and LCC Maintenance

Immediately after any psi session, record:
- The responses given (without editing)
- The subjective LCC state during reception (GILE composite score for the session)
- Confidence in individual responses
- Any notable somatic signals (body sensation accompanying specific responses)

Verify against ground truth as soon as it becomes available. Record the outcome, the pre-session RMSSD, and the PSR for this session.

Track LCC-PSR correlation over sessions. This is the data that determines your personal C_EMERICK operational threshold — for some nodes the effective threshold may be slightly above or below 0.4370, and personal calibration refines the protocol.

After the session, engage in a brief LCC maintenance activity (5 minutes of coherence breathing) regardless of session length. Extended psi reception work can deplete LCC through sustained concentration; the maintenance phase prevents post-session LCC crash.

## 7. The Generality-Specialization Matrix

Based on the evidence reviewed, each psi domain is rated on two dimensions:

General factor dependence (GFD): how much of the variance in performance is attributable to LCC above C_EMERICK, independent of domain-specific calibration?

Specialization premium (SP): how much additional performance gain accrues from domain-specific training beyond the general LCC threshold?

Domain | GFD | SP | Dominant Layer | Notes
DMILS | High | Low | General | Direct biological connection; L-dimension is the primary domain factor and is part of the general GILE composite; specialization adds little beyond LCC
Ganzfeld | High | Moderate | General + partial specific | Sender-receiver attunement is partly general (L-dimension) and partly trainable through Ganzfeld-specific practice
Remote viewing | Moderate | High | Both | Clear general factor requirement; substantial gains from CRV-specific training in analytical overlay suppression and target approach
Precognition | Moderate | High | Both | Temporal orientation is a specific skill not automatically present in high-LCC nodes; requires dedicated practice
PK-RNG | Low-Moderate | High | Specific | The weakest PSR domain; general LCC helps but the transmission threshold may require specialized LCC configuration distinct from reception LCC

Interpretation: DMILS and Ganzfeld are the most accessible entry points for practitioners starting with GLEP — they are most strongly general-factor-dependent and show detectable results with fewer total trials (PSR is highest in these domains). Remote viewing and precognition require both the general foundation and substantial domain-specific training. PK is the most specialized domain and produces the smallest mean PSR despite potentially requiring the highest peak LCC for transmission.

For a practitioner beginning psi development: the recommended sequence is DMILS (establish biological LCC sensitivity and L-dimension development) → Ganzfeld (establish sender-receiver connection practice) → remote viewing (spatial targeting) → precognition (temporal orientation) → PK (transmission).

## 8. Psi as an Integrated System: The GILE Mapping

Each psi domain has a dominant GILE dimension that determines its specific calibration factor:

Goodness (G): integrity and internal coherence. Dominant for all psi domains as the general foundation — DT (Double Tralse incoherence) suppresses psi performance across all domains. Specifically important for PK, where the transmission requires maximum G-coherence to sustain the output.

Intuition (I): forward-directed felt knowing. Dominant for precognition — temporal orientation is primarily an I-dimension skill, the felt sense of the future trajectory rather than the present state.

Love (L): the binding force between nodes. Dominant for DMILS and Ganzfeld — both require minimizing delta_phi through emotional-relational attunement with the target node or sender.

Environment (E): the structural ground of existence in context. Dominant for remote viewing — the spatial target is an environmental structure, and E-dimension sensitivity to environmental coherence patterns is the specific calibration factor for this domain.

The GLEP targets all four GILE dimensions:
- Phase 2 (cardiac coherence): primarily elevates G (reduces internal DT) and E (grounds the node in its present environment)
- Phase 3 (theta entrainment): primarily elevates I (opens the intuitive channel) and L (reduces the ego-boundary that suppresses connection)
- Phase 4 (domain calibration): targets the specific GILE dimension dominant for that domain

A practitioner whose GILE profile is assessed before GLEP can optimize the sequence: a node with low L (difficulty with relational connection) will need extended Phase 3 work before Ganzfeld or DMILS; a node with low I (difficulty with felt knowing) will need extended Phase 3 before precognition tasks; a node with high G and E but low I and L can proceed directly to remote viewing while developing I and L before attempting other domains.

## 10. Summary

Psi is both a general faculty and a collection of specialized skills — not either-or, but both through a two-layer structure that TI Sigma names the LCC general factor (Layer 1) and domain-specific calibration (Layer 2).

The general factor, LCC above C_EMERICK = 0.4370, is the necessary condition for reliable psi performance in any domain. The four major meta-analytic moderators across all five psi domains (meditation, anxiety, belief, experimenter effects) are all plausibly mediated by this single threshold. The Psi Signal Ratio (PSR) provides a unified commensurable metric across domains, revealing that DMILS and Ganzfeld show the highest mean PSR, while PK-RNG shows the lowest.

The Generalized LCC Enhancement Protocol (GLEP) raises LCC to or above C_EMERICK in five phases (baseline assessment, cardiac coherence induction, theta entrainment, domain calibration, and post-session recording). It is domain-independent and benefits all psi domains simultaneously. Domain-specific calibration follows GLEP and builds on its foundation.

The optimal entry sequence for psi development: DMILS → Ganzfeld → remote viewing → precognition → PK. The GILE dimensional profile predicts relative domain strengths and can be used to personalize the sequence and the emphasis within each GLEP phase.

Psi is real, trainable, measurable, and mechanistically explicable within the TI Sigma GM-Node framework. The apparent inconsistency of psi research is predicted by the two-layer model: general factor variability across practitioners (driven by LCC) produces cross-domain performance correlations, while Layer 2 specialization produces domain-specific variance. Both are real. Both are trainable. LCC is the key.
