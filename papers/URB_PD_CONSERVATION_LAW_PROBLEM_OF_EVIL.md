## Part II: The PD Is Descriptive — Not Prescriptive

### II.1 The Moral Status of the PD

The PD scale (-3 to +2) describes the baseline existence footprint of ordinary,
unoptimized states of being and events. It is not a moral endorsement of the negative
range. The fact that -3 is on the scale does not mean -3 *should* exist morally —
any more than the fact that a thermometer can read 0°C means that freezing *should*
happen.

The PD is descriptive in the same way physical laws are descriptive:
- Newton's laws describe how objects fall — they do not prescribe that objects should fall
- The PD describes how existence is distributed across its quality spectrum — it does
  not prescribe that suffering should exist

The moral mission — eliminating unnecessary suffering — is entirely consistent with
the PD being a description. We do not accept "gravity causes falls, therefore we should
not build handrails." We build handrails. Similarly: "the PD includes a negative range,
therefore we accept suffering as inevitable" is the wrong conclusion. We build BlissGene.

## Part IV: The PD as Conservation Law — The Critical Question

### IV.1 Description vs. Conservation Law

There are two distinct possibilities for the PD's metaphysical status:

Possibility A — Descriptive Baseline Only: The PD describes the default distribution
of unoptimized existence. Free will, BlissGene, and Mood Amplifier can genuinely
eliminate the negative range. There is no conservation constraint. Total conscious
wellbeing can be raised indefinitely.

Possibility B — Fundamental Conservation Law: The PD's total integral across all
conscious beings is conserved — analogous to energy conservation in physics. Raising
one being's PD values requires lowering another's, or shifting negativity to non-affective
dimensions. Free will cannot eliminate the negativity, only redistribute it.

### IV.2 Evidence for Conservation Law Status

If the PD is connected to the Riemann zeta function's zero structure (as argued in
Part II), it may share the conservation properties of that mathematical structure.
The Riemann hypothesis implies a specific constraint on how the zeros can be
distributed — they cannot all be moved to one side. Similarly, a PD conservation law
would say: the distribution of experience-quality cannot be uniformly raised without
a corresponding counter-movement.

Phenomenological evidence supporting conservation:
- Hedonic adaptation: Humans rapidly habituate to improved conditions and return
  to a baseline level of life satisfaction (Brickman & Campbell, 1971 — "hedonic
  treadmill"). This suggests the subjective PD baseline is partially conserved even
  as objective conditions improve.
- The meaning-suffering connection: Many traditions and clinical observations note
  that eliminating all suffering simultaneously reduces the perceived meaningfulness
  of achievement, connection, and growth. Viktor Frankl's observation: suffering that
  is chosen and given meaning becomes not merely bearable but transformative. If there
  is nothing difficult to endure, the growth-meaning dimension deflates.
- The rich-boredom phenomenon: Extremely comfortable lives often report existential
  emptiness. The absence of adversity produces a specific non-affective negative state
  (meaninglessness) that replaces affective suffering.

### IV.3 If the PD Is Conserved — Optimal Redistribution

If Possibility B is correct and the PD is a conservation law, then BlissGene's
elimination of negative affect must be compensated somewhere. The user identifies
the possible locations:

| Redistribution Target | Mechanism | Tolerability |
|----------------------|-----------|-------------|
| Other creatures' suffering | Negativity displaced to non-human conscious beings | Low — creates ethical responsibility for nonhuman suffering increase |
| "Bad luck" (non-conscious events) | More negative events, but without felt suffering | Moderate — negative outcomes without negative experience |
| Less perceived meaning | Less growth from struggle; achievements feel less earned | Highest — non-affective, does not cause suffering to any conscious being |

The optimal redistribution: The most tolerable shift is from affective suffering
to non-affective meaning-reduction. Specifically: a world where negative affect is
eliminated will likely experience a corresponding reduction in the felt meaningfulness
of achievement, connection, and growth — because these derive significant meaning-value
from the contrast with difficulty.

This is not catastrophic. It is a trade-off: trading affective suffering (which is
bad for the experiencer and reduces function) for mild meaning-deflation (which reduces
one source of meaning-value but leaves other sources intact, and importantly does not
cause suffering to any being).

The conservation law version of the BlissGene world: people feel good all the time,
never suffer affectively, but report that life feels somewhat less *meaningful* than
it did when they could suffer. This is tolerable. This is optimally tolerable.

### IV.4 Why Contentment and Radical Acceptance Always Matter

Whether the PD is a conservation law (Possibility B) or just a description (Possibility
A), contentment and mindful radical acceptance remain permanently essential. For
different reasons in each case:

If Possibility A (description only): Even if negativity can be genuinely eliminated,
the transition requires navigating a period of non-Radiant states. Contentment is what
makes the transition sustainable — the ability to be non-judgmentally present with
current PD levels while working to raise them. Without contentment, the gap between
the current -0.5 baseline and the desired +1.5 target generates frustration that
compounds the negativity. Radical acceptance is not passive — it is the prerequisite
for effective upward movement.

If Possibility B (conservation law): If some form of negativity is irreducible,
contentment and radical acceptance are not merely transitional skills — they are the
permanent ultimate skill. The question becomes: how to relate to the unavoidable
cost of existence in a way that extracts maximum meaning and minimum suffering. The
answer has been consistent across traditions: mindful acceptance of what cannot be
changed, combined with vigorous application of free will to what can be. This is the
TI Sigma position.

In either case: the endpoint of the BlissGene trajectory is not a world where negative
affect is replaced by mindless hedonic bliss. It is a world where affective suffering
is minimized, meaning-from-difficulty is partially replaced by meaning-from-depth
(the same positive states differentiated more finely — as the re-normalized PD
predicts), and contentment provides the stable platform from which all positive
experience is appreciated rather than habituated away.

## Addendum: Tralse vs. Indeterminate in the PD Framework

A terminological precision note that is important for correctly applying the PD in research contexts:

Indeterminate in the Myrion Resolution framework is not an absence of evidence but a resolved position. After Myrion Resolution is applied, a claim that returns PD in the range (-0.666, 0.333) is Indeterminate — meaning the analysis has been done and the correct answer is genuinely in the middle. This is a determination, not a suspension.

Indeterminate is distinct from Tralse in the following way: Tralse is the dynamic state of productive tension while MR is working — it describes the process as it runs. Indeterminate is a stable resolved output that the process may return. A white lie is the canonical illustration: MR1 finds it sufficiently coherent (passes the Double-Tralse screen); MR2 finds it Indeterminate (neither straightforwardly dishonest nor straightforwardly kind — PD in the midrange); MR3 then resolves it further ("worth it" = trends True, or "not worth it" = trends False). The Indeterminate from MR2 is not a failure — it is an accurate midrange resolution that correctly characterizes the moral status of the act before context is brought to bear.

When PD values from two conflicting strong studies approximately cancel — e.g., PD(Study A) = +1.5 and PD(Study B) = -1.5 — the Myrion formula resolves to a PD near the Indeterminate zone. This does not mean the question is unanswered; it means the evidence genuinely places the claim in the midrange, which is informative. A further MR round (MR3) can determine whether additional context resolves the midrange toward True or False.

In the context of the Problem of Evil: the PD of the proposition "the universe's permitting of extreme suffering is morally justified" is not simply negative (the Radiant state is universally accessible — positive evidence) and not simply positive (extreme suffering is real — negative evidence). After MR2 it is likely Indeterminate or weakly negative — a genuine midrange moral assessment. MR3 brings in the GM mycelial guarantee (Radiant always accessible), the PD conservation law structure (meaning-from-difficulty is real), and the free-will redistribution framework. These additional inputs push the PD toward a specific Indeterminate-to-positive resolution: the universe's structure is not malevolent, but it is not straightforwardly benevolent either. This is TI Sigma's honest answer — an Indeterminate that trends toward conditional justification under full context, not a forced True or False.
