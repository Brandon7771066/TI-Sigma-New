## §0: Motivation — The Gap Friston's FEP Leaves Open

Friston's Free Energy Principle (FEP) is one of the most ambitious unification attempts in contemporary science. It proposes that any system that persists over time must minimize variational free energy — equivalently, must maintain a boundary (Markov Blanket) across which internal states remain conditionally independent of external states. But the FEP has three unresolved gaps that the TFEP addresses:

**Gap 1: Biological restriction.** Markov Blankets are defined statistically — as conditional independence boundaries in probabilistic graphical models. **Gap 2: Binary ontology.** The FEP operates within classical probability theory — states are either in the blanket or outside it, internal or external. It cannot natively represent the four-valued Tralse structure (TRUE / TRALSE / FALSE / MR_PEND) that TI Sigma establishes as fundamental.

**Gap 3: No connection to the 5-zone PD.** The FEP predicts no specific zone frequency distribution for a system's states. It says systems minimize surprise but gives no closed-form prediction of *how* states distribute at equilibrium. The Permissibility Distribution provides exactly that — and the TFEP explains why.

The TFEP resolves all three gaps by replacing statistical Markov Blankets with topological i-Boundaries, classical probability with 4-valued Tralse logic, and unspecified surprise-minimization with a dynamical law whose stationary distribution is provably the PD.

## §2: i-Boundaries — TI Sigma's Markov Blankets

In Friston's FEP, Markov Blankets are statistical: a set of states B such that internal states μ are conditionally independent of external states η given B. This is:

```
P(μ | B, η) = P(μ | B)
```

i-Boundaries are the topological generalization. An i-Boundary Ω(ψ) of i-Cell ψ is the informational interface through which ψ exchanges Tralse-structured signals with the environment:

```

```

Key properties of i-Boundaries that distinguish them from Markov Blankets:

**Property 1 — Topological, not statistical.** i-Boundaries are defined by informational topology — the BOK structure of the i-Cell's connectivity — rather than by conditional independence in a probability model. **Property 3 — Self-referential.** An i-Cell's i-Boundary is part of what the i-Cell IS (UOP). The boundary and the cell are not separate — the cell's identity is constituted by its boundary structure. This follows from the BOK's 4-large + 4-small loop topology, where the boundary loops are as fundamental as the interior loops.

**Theorem 2.1 (i-Boundary Reduction).** At the biological scale, i-Boundaries reduce to Markov Blankets. Specifically, for Level 4 i-Cells (biological organisms) with stationary statistics, the TT score of i-Boundary states is measurable as conditional independence, recovering the classical FEP formulation.
