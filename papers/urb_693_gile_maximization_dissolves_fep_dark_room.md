## 1. The Dark Room Problem and FEP's Inadequate Responses

**FEP claim:** Biological systems minimize variational free energy — equivalent to minimizing surprise / prediction error over time.

**Dark room problem:** If true, the optimal strategy is to find a dark, predictable, sensorily-impoverished room and remain there. Maximum predictive accuracy. Minimum surprise. Minimum free energy. But organisms emphatically do not do this.

**Standard FEP responses (and why they fail):**

1. **Active inference / "I am an explorer" priors:** The organism has built-in priors that include exploration. *Circular* — this just embeds the explanation as a brute prior; it doesn't explain why such priors evolved.

2. **Epistemic value / information gain:** Exploration reduces long-term free energy by gathering information. *Insufficient* — many high-risk exploratory behaviors produce no usable predictive information (extreme sports, mystical practices, philosophical inquiry into unanswerable questions). Information gain cannot account for the magnitude of risk humans accept.

3. **Hierarchical priors and curiosity:** Higher-level priors override lower-level surprise minimization. *Regress* — what evolutionary pressure produced the higher-level priors in the first place?

The deepest problem with FEP responses: they treat the dark room as a theoretical puzzle to be patched. But the **evolutionary** form of the question is decisive: **why did evolution produce entities that opposed dark-room equilibria when bacterial-level optima were available?**

## 2. The Evolutionary Form of the Critique

Evolution had access to many possible local optima. Among them:

- **Bacterial equilibrium:** Continuous adaptation, no consciousness, no risk-taking, near-perfect free energy minimization for the local environment
- **Plant equilibrium:** Increased complexity without nervous system, no exposure to predator risk, near-perfect free energy minimization
- **Eusocial insect equilibrium:** Distributed intelligence, role specialization, low individual risk, high colony stability

Any of these could have been evolution's terminal answer. Bacteria are wildly successful — they outnumber all other life by orders of magnitude. Plants dominate biomass. Insects are evolutionarily older than mammals.

**Yet evolution also produced:** organisms that voluntarily accept enormous risk, organisms that pursue suffering for non-survival reasons, organisms that undergo developmental phase transitions (puberty, midlife, mystical experience) that are existentially disruptive, organisms that originate questions about the nature of existence itself with no survival value.

**The evolutionary question:** What selection pressure produces beings that violate FEP optima? FEP cannot answer because *FEP itself names the optimum these beings violate.*
