## 1. Brandon-Originated Sub-Insights (3 distinct candidate principles)

### 1.1 GHMR-1 — Gender as Domain-Bounded HMR

**Statement:** Gender identity is natively a Hybrid-MR (HMR) construct for at least a non-trivial sub-population. The cardinality is **bounded above by 3** for any single agent's gender characterization, even when meta-identities and dysphoria are included.

**Worked examples (Brandon-originated):**

| **GHMR-3** | {T_male-identity, T_nonbinary-identity, DT_dysphoria-gradient} | The DT label represents the *gradient* of dysphoria — a structural tension between identity-as-experienced and identity-as-socially-categorized; genuine τ ∧ ¬τ at the moments of dysphoric experience per Pass-65 DT canonical refinement |
| **NOT GHMR-4+** (Brandon empirical claim) | — | Brandon hypothesizes no agent's gender characterization natively requires 4+ simultaneous MR labels |

**Why this is distinct from HMR-5.1 ("God exists"):**
- HMR-5.1 spans 5 *framings* (substrate / analytic-philosophy / self-reference / definition / decision-utility) but is about an *external* proposition.
- GHMR is about *internal-identity-characterization* — a different ontological category. Brandon's empirical claim is that internal-identity HMR cardinality is intrinsically capped at 3 (perhaps because identity is a CDA-1 Stratum-3 construct and self-reference saturates earlier than external propositions).

### 1.2 GHMR-2-MT-SWITCH — Simultaneous vs Rapid-Switching Distinction (multitasking constraint)

**Statement:** Whether an HMR characterization is realized as (a) genuinely-simultaneous label-holding or (b) rapid-switching-that-appears-simultaneous depends on the agent's multitasking capacity. **Approximately 2% of the population** can genuinely multitask per neuroscience research (the "supertasker" phenomenon, Watson & Strayer 2010 PLoS ONE; Medeiros-Ward et al. 2015).

**Implication:** HMR is an **operational construct**, not a phenomenological-only construct. For 98% of agents, HMR-k realizes as rapid-switching-between-labels at sub-second timescales; for ~2% supertaskers, HMR-k realizes as genuine simultaneous-holding.

**This is a refinement of HMR-1 composition rule R1 (cross-level preservation):** the cross-level preservation is *information-theoretic* (the labels must all be preserved in any complete characterization) regardless of whether the agent realizes them simultaneously or sequentially. The HMR label-set is invariant under realization-mode.

**Why this matters for canonical:** the HMR-1 canonical (Pass-70 B0) was implicitly mode-agnostic. GHMR-2-MT-SWITCH makes this explicit: HMR is mode-agnostic at the corpus level but mode-dependent at the agent level. **This composes with TPI-1 #32 canonical** (HEM-axis-asymmetric-tradeoffs may include "realization-mode capacity" as an H-component).

### 1.3 GHMR-3-EMOTION — Mixed Emotions as Universally-Attainable HMR

**Statement:** While gender-HMR may be rare in any single agent, **mixed emotional states are universally attainable** across the population. Mixed-emotions are the most accessible empirical anchor for HMR.

**Examples (Brandon-listed):**
- **Bittersweet** = HMR-2 {T_sweet, T_bitter} at single-experience level (Williams & Aaker 2002 J Consumer Research; Vaillant 2008 *Spiritual Evolution*)
- **Sad happiness** = HMR-3 {T_happiness, T_sadness, MT-F1_transcend-at-higher-frame} — apparent contradiction reconciled at higher frame (e.g., bittersweet nostalgia, posttraumatic growth)
- **Fiery love** = HMR-3 {T_passion-attraction, T_aggression-protective, MT-F1_compound-into-intensity}

**Why mixed-emotions is the canonical empirical anchor for HMR:** the **disability paradox** (Albrecht & Devlieger 1999) shows 54% of disabled patients self-rate QOL as "good or excellent" — a population-wide mixed-state phenomenon. **Mixed emotions are NOT marginal** — they are the modal emotional state in adult humans per the Russell-Carroll affect-grid literature.

**Composition with VFP-1 canonical (#26, Pass-65):** valence-as-functional implies mixed-valence states have functional roles. The mixed-emotion HMR-k characterization explains *why* mixed states are functional — they preserve information from multiple valence-dimensions simultaneously, enabling more flexible behavioral response.
