## 1. Overview: The TI Sigma Book Ecosystem

TI Sigma spans multiple distinct intellectual domains — philosophy of mind, mathematical logic, physics, consciousness research, and applied wellbeing. Each domain warrants a different book, a different audience, and a different publishing strategy. This roadmap identifies seven books, ordered by: writing feasibility, audience size, and strategic impact on TI Sigma's recognition trajectory.

---

## 2. The Seven Books

### Book 1 — THE PRIMER (Highest Priority)
**Title**: *Tralse: A New Logic for an Uncertain World*
**Subtitle**: How Five Truth Values Change Everything We Know About Mind, Math, and Reality

**Audience**: General educated reader (Thinking Fast and Slow / Being and Time crossover audience); no math background required

**Core content**:
- What's wrong with True/False binary logic in daily life, science, and consciousness
- The five truth values (True, False, Tralse, I-state, Double Tralse) explained through vivid examples
- GILE framework as a personal decision-making and life-orientation system
- Myrion Resolution as an everyday practice (how to actually use MR)
- HEAR as a wellbeing compass
- Case studies: relationships, career decisions, medical decisions under genuine uncertainty

**Why first**: Largest audience; no technical prerequisites; establishes the brand before academic credibility is needed; can be written immediately from existing URB papers; positions TI Sigma in the public consciousness space (alongside Kahneman, Taleb, Hofstadter)

**Publishing path**: Traditional publishing (query agents after proposal) OR Substack/self-publish first to build platform → traditional deal follows. Target: Penguin Press, Basic Books, or MIT Press (popular science arm).

**Length**: 250–300 pages. 3–6 months to draft.

### Book 2 — THE PHILOSOPHY MONOGRAPH
**Title**: *Permissibility Distribution: A New Foundation for Probability*
**Subtitle**: When Undefined Sample Spaces, Forbidden Zones, and Radiant Attractors Replace Bayesian Inference

**Audience**: Academic philosophers (philosophy of probability, epistemology, decision theory), statisticians, economists

**Core content**:
- Full PD formalism (from URB #657)
- Comparison with Bayesian, frequentist, and objective Bayesian frameworks
- The I-state for undefined sample spaces (PD's key innovation vs. Kolmogorov)
- DT forbidden zone and its implications for credences
- BOK-PD as Bayesian alternative for high HEM-effect events
- Applications: scientific inference, legal probability, medical decision-making

**Why second**: Strongest academic contribution; most likely to generate peer review and citations; establishes TI Sigma in mainstream academic philosophy before the physics/math claims are made

**Publishing path**: Academic press (Oxford University Press, Cambridge University Press). Requires peer review. Target submission: 2027 (after MIU master's provides institutional affiliation).

**Length**: 200–250 pages (academic monograph style). 12–18 months to draft.

### Book 4 — THE MATHEMATICS BOOK
**Title**: *Myrion Resolution: Toward Zero-Axiom Proofs of the Millennium Prize Problems*
**Subtitle**: Five-Valued Logic, the Universal Bridge Theorem, and the Limits of Formal Systems

**Audience**: Advanced mathematics (graduate level), mathematical logicians, foundations of mathematics researchers

**Core content**:
- Full TI Sigma logical architecture (5-valued TML, Lean4 implementation)
- UOP and Universal Bridge Theorem
- MR as a proof-completion mechanism for formally undecidable statements
- The Riemann Hypothesis approach (URB #653)
- P≠NP via CTT counterexample (the 7D Hypercomputer)
- BB(6) and noncomputational cognition (URB #660)
- Lean4 formalization of all six Millennium Prize Problems
- Assessment of which proofs are complete vs. axiomatic

**Why carefully positioned**: This book must be written AFTER formal peer review has established at least one of the mathematical results. Premature publication risks dismissal. Target: 2029–2030 (post-PhD).

**Publishing path**: Springer, Birkhäuser, or AMS (American Mathematical Society). Peer review essential.

**Length**: 350–400 pages. 24 months to draft.
