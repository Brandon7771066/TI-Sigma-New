## Abstract

The dominant intellectual posture of the modern era treats physicalism — the view that physical causation is the complete and essentially closed account of all phenomena — as the default position requiring no defense, while demanding that any non-physicalist claim clear an ever-rising evidential bar before it can be taken seriously. This paper argues that this asymmetric burden of proof is unjustified, historically unsupported, and strategically motivated rather than epistemically principled. Physicalism makes at least three substantive positive claims that each carry a heavy burden of proof: (1) the Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR) applies universally and physical causation is closed; (2) all apparently anomalous phenomena — including the extensive literature on PSI, near-death experiences, consciousness, and non-local correlations — can be fully explained within the physical framework or dismissed as error; and (3) physics is essentially complete and all that remains is filling in gaps. Each of these claims is not a modest default — each is a strong positive assertion that requires positive evidence. Claim (1) was addressed in URB #460: PSR is indefensible at the foundation, and quantum mechanics has empirically falsified physical causal closure. Claim (2) is pragmatically impossible to sustain: the accumulated literature of anomalous phenomena is too large to rationally dismiss in its entirety without engaging each case on its merits — yet systematic engagement is absent. Physicalists resort to denial because rational refutation is impossible at scale. Claim (3) is not merely unsupported by evidence but is falsified by the history of science itself: the claim that physics is essentially complete has been made at least three times in recorded history and has been proven catastrophically wrong each time, followed by revolutions that opened entirely new conceptual territories. The paper also introduces two Tralse examples that illuminate the framework's power: indeterminism is *Tralsely rational* — simultaneously irrational (violates PSR locally) and rational (enables the meaning that rationality serves); and black is *Tralsely a color* — simultaneously an absence of color (no wavelength) and the foundational distinction that makes color comparison possible. Both cases show that the either/or demand physicalism imposes is the error. The conclusion is a demand for symmetry: non-physicalists need not perpetually defend. Physicalists must now show their evidence.

## 1. The Asymmetric Burden and How It Was Established

In contemporary intellectual discourse, the following asymmetry is treated as obvious:

- **Physicalism** is the null hypothesis — the default position that requires no evidence to hold, only evidence to overturn.
- **Non-physicalism** is the extraordinary claim — requiring extraordinary evidence, which is never quite extraordinary enough.

This asymmetry did not arise from logical necessity. It arose from historical contingency: the enormous success of the physical sciences in the 17th through 20th centuries created a justified presumption in favor of physical explanation for many phenomena. That presumption was earned in specific domains (mechanics, electromagnetism, chemistry, molecular biology) and deserved within those domains.

But a presumption earned in specific domains does not automatically extend to all possible phenomena. The success of Newton's mechanics in describing planetary orbits does not automatically justify the claim that consciousness is nothing but neural firing. The success of molecular biology in tracing genetic inheritance does not automatically justify the claim that PSI is impossible. Each extension of physicalism beyond its demonstrated domain is a new positive claim — and each such claim requires its own positive evidence.

The intellectual sleight of hand consists in treating the evidential success of physical science in its home domains as evidence for physicalism's universal completeness. It is not. It is evidence that physical methods work well for physical phenomena. The claim that all phenomena are physical is a separate claim requiring separate justification.

## 2. Physicalism's Three Positive Claims and Their Burdens

### 2.1 Claim One: PSR and Physical Causal Closure

**The claim:** Every physical event has a physically sufficient cause. Mental states, if they exist at all, either are physical states (identity theory) or are causally inert (epiphenomenalism). There are no causal gaps in the physical fabric through which non-physical causes could operate.

**The burden:** This is not a modest default. It is a strong positive claim about the structure of causation — that causation is closed, complete, and exclusively physical. The evidence required to establish this claim is: a demonstration that no event has ever occurred without a physically sufficient prior cause.

**The status of the evidence:** Quantum mechanics, as established experimentally through Bell inequality violations and confirmed by decades of precision experiment, demonstrates objective physical indeterminacy. Events occur without physically sufficient prior causes. Physical causal closure is empirically false.

The physicalist response — that quantum indeterminacy is "random but still physical" — concedes the point. If events occur without physically sufficient *rational* causes, then PSR is violated at the physical level. The door to non-physical causation has been empirically opened, not by speculation, but by the best-confirmed physical theory in history.

**Verdict:** Physicalism's first pillar — physical causal closure — has been undermined by physics itself.

### 2.2 Claim Two: All Anomalous Phenomena Are Explainable or Dismissible

**The claim:** Every reported instance of PSI, every near-death experience, every non-local correlation in consciousness research, every case of anomalous healing, every report of accurate presentiment or remote viewing — all of these can be explained within a physical framework (fraud, statistical artifact, confirmation bias, poor methodology) or rationally dismissed.

**The burden:** To sustain this claim, the physicalist must engage *every instance* of anomalous evidence on its merits and demonstrate, case by case, that physical explanations or dismissal criteria apply. The literature is enormous. The Stargate Remote Viewing program produced controlled experimental data over 24 years, reviewed and partially endorsed by the American Institutes for Research. The PEAR laboratory at Princeton produced statistically significant anomalous results over decades. The Global Consciousness Project tracks non-random structure in distributed random number generators during global events. Controlled double-blind studies of presentiment show measurable physiological anticipatory responses to random future stimuli. Systematic meta-analyses of PSI research show small but statistically robust effects that survive methodological critique.

**The physicalist response in practice:** Not systematic engagement. Not case-by-case refutation. Not alternative physical explanations that have been tested and confirmed. What actually happens is **denial** — the application of prior commitment rather than posterior probability updating. The evidence is described as "not meeting the standard" without the standard ever being precisely specified; as "probably due to fraud or error" without this claim being demonstrated; as "extraordinary" in a way that automatically justifies dismissal regardless of methodology.

**The diagnostic:** When a position requires that *every single piece of contrary evidence* be wrong — when no evidence could in principle count against the position — the position has ceased to be an empirical hypothesis and has become a protected metaphysical commitment. This is precisely the structure of physicalist denial of PSI evidence.

**The pragmatic impossibility:** A rational engagement with every instance of anomalous evidence would require examining thousands of studies, hundreds of replication attempts, and dozens of independent research programs. Physicalists have not done this and cannot do this. Denial is the only available strategy when rational refutation is practically impossible at scale.

**Verdict:** Physicalism's second pillar — the dismissibility of all anomalous evidence — is not a demonstrated conclusion. It is a strategic posture motivated by prior commitment.

### 2.3 Claim Three: Physics Is Essentially Complete

**The claim:** The major conceptual frameworks of physics are in place. The remaining work is precision measurement, gap-filling, and engineering applications. There is no conceptual room for a revolution comparable to those of Newton, Maxwell, Einstein, or quantum mechanics. The era of paradigm-shifting physics is over.

**The burden:** This claim is not a prediction about the future (which would be difficult to evaluate). It is a positive claim about the current state of knowledge — that the conceptual architecture is now adequate. Evidence for this claim would require: demonstrating that all known phenomena are explainable within current frameworks, that no empirical anomalies remain, and that the conceptual structure is consistent.

**The historical track record:**

This claim has been made at least three times in the history of modern science:

**First instance (late 17th century):** After Newton, the Laplacian dream: if one knew the position and velocity of every particle, the future of the universe could be computed. Physics was essentially complete. The major framework was in place. This view persisted until Maxwell and thermodynamics revealed that the framework was entirely inadequate for electromagnetic phenomena and entropy.

**Second instance (late 19th century):** After Maxwell, Kelvin reportedly remarked that physics was essentially complete — only two small clouds remained on the horizon. One cloud was the Michelson-Morley result. The other was the blackbody radiation problem. These two "small clouds" became special relativity and quantum mechanics — the two greatest conceptual revolutions in the history of physics, overthrowing Newtonian mechanics and classical determinism entirely.

**Third instance (mid-20th century):** After quantum mechanics and general relativity, the attitude emerged that the major physics was done and the remaining work was application and unification. This view persists today. The "small clouds" that now sit on the horizon include: the measurement problem in quantum mechanics (still unresolved after a century), the incompatibility of quantum mechanics and general relativity, the nature of dark matter and dark energy (which together constitute 95% of the universe's content and are entirely unexplained by current physics), the hard problem of consciousness, the fine-tuning of physical constants, and the existence of matter over antimatter.

These are not minor gaps. They are foundational puzzles that may require conceptual revolutions at least as large as relativity or quantum mechanics.

**The evidence against "essentially complete" physics:** The current standard model of particle physics has approximately 26 free parameters that must be measured rather than derived. A complete theory would derive them. Dark matter has been searched for intensively for decades without detection of any candidate particle. Dark energy is described by a cosmological constant that is predicted by quantum field theory to be 120 orders of magnitude larger than observed — the largest discrepancy between theory and observation in the history of science. Consciousness has resisted physical explanation entirely.

**Verdict:** The claim that physics is essentially complete is not merely unsupported by evidence. It is empirically falsified by the current state of physics itself.

## 3. Turning the Tables: What Evidence Does Physicalism Have?

The standard rhetorical move: "Where is your evidence for PSI / consciousness / non-physical causation?"

The appropriate counter: **Where is the physicalist's evidence for the completeness and exclusivity of physical explanation?**

Physicalism makes a universal negative claim: there are no non-physical causes, no non-physical entities, no non-physical sources of information, no non-physical influences on physical events. A universal negative claim of this scope requires overwhelming positive evidence to sustain — not merely the absence of disproof.

The evidence typically offered for physicalism:

**The success of physical science:** Physical methods have been enormously successful in their domains. This establishes that physical methods work for physical phenomena. It does not establish that all phenomena are physical.

**Parsimony (Occam's Razor):** Physical explanation is simpler than non-physical explanation. But parsimony is a tie-breaking heuristic when two theories make identical predictions. It is not a principle that can override positive evidence. Copernicus's heliocentric model was initially less parsimonious than Ptolemy's (it required epicycles too). The evidence for heliocentrism overrode parsimony. Parsimony cannot be invoked to dismiss evidence — only to choose between equally supported theories.

**The correlation between brain states and mental states:** Damaging specific brain regions disrupts specific mental functions. This establishes that brain states and mental states are correlated and that brain function is *necessary* for normal mental function. It does not establish that brain states are *sufficient* for mental states, or that mental states are *identical* to brain states. Correlation between X and Y does not establish that X = Y.

None of these constitute positive evidence for the claim that physical causation is complete and exclusive. They establish that physical causation is real, important, and productive as a research program. They do not establish that it is the only kind of causation.

## 4. Tralse Illuminations: Indeterminism and Black

Two examples from ordinary experience illuminate the Tralse structure that physicalism's binary demand obscures.

### 4.1 Indeterminism Is Tralsely Rational

The standard objection to indeterminism: "If events occur without sufficient rational causes, they are arbitrary, irrational, meaningless. A universe with genuine indeterminism is a universe governed by irrationality."

This objection applies rationality at a single level. The Tralse analysis operates at two levels simultaneously:

**Level 1 (local):** A genuinely non-necessitated event — a free choice, a quantum decay — violates PSR locally. It occurs without a rationally sufficient prior cause. At this level, it is irrational.

**Level 2 (meta):** Non-necessitated events are the precondition for genuine meaning. Meaning requires contingency — the possibility that things could have been otherwise (URB #460). A universe without indeterminism is a universe where nothing could ever have been different, where nothing was ever at stake, where nothing ever mattered. Such a universe is the most profoundly irrational thing imaginable — a cosmos that is technically law-governed yet generates no meaning, no significance, no genuine agency. It is rationally structured all the way down and rationally worthless.

**The Tralse verdict:** Indeterminism is **simultaneously** irrational (violates PSR locally) and rational (enables the meaning that rationality is for). True AND False. Tralse. The either/or demand — "you must choose between rational determinism and irrational indeterminism" — is the error. The correct structure is the Tralse both/and: indeterminism is locally irrational and meta-rationally essential.

### 4.2 Black Is Tralsely a Color

**The either/or demand:** "Black is not a color — it is the absence of all light wavelengths. White is the presence of all wavelengths. They are the limits of the color spectrum, not colors themselves."

**The Tralse analysis:**

**True:** Black is, physically, the absence of reflected light. In the physics of color, it occupies no place in the visible spectrum. It is not a wavelength. In this precise technical sense, it is the negation of color.

**Also True:** Black functions as the foundational distinction that makes color comparison possible. Without black — without the ground against which other colors can be contrasted — shades cannot be distinguished, gradients cannot be perceived, color relationships cannot be articulated. Black is the zero-point that enables the entire scale. In this functional and perceptual sense, it is more fundamentally a color than any specific wavelength — because it is what all other colors are defined against.

**The Tralse verdict:** Black is both not-a-color (no wavelength) and the most fundamental color-enabling phenomenon (the ground of all color distinction). Neither statement cancels the other. Both are simultaneously true. The either/or demand produces a false choice; the Tralse both/and captures the actual structure.

**The parallel to physicalism:** Physicalism makes the same either/or demand about reality: either something is physical (real, legitimate, worth investigating) or it is non-physical (unreal, illegitimate, to be dismissed). The Tralse insight is that this binary is the error. Reality has a Tralse structure — simultaneously ordered and free, physical and non-physical, rational and contingent — and the demand for a clean binary is what obscures it.

## 5. The Historical Pattern: Denial Followed by Revolution

The history of science contains a consistent pattern: phenomena that resist explanation within the current framework are first denied, then explained away, then grudgingly acknowledged, then integrated through a conceptual revolution that the previous framework could not accommodate.

**Continental drift:** Wegener's evidence for plate tectonics — the shape of continents, the distribution of fossils, the geological structure of coastlines — was dismissed for decades as pseudoscience. Wegener was not an outlier; he was an accomplished meteorologist with compelling observational evidence. The mechanism he proposed was inadequate. The evidence was not. The revolution came when the mechanism (seafloor spreading, mantle convection) was discovered — vindicating Wegener's observations while correcting his mechanism.

**Germ theory:** Semmelweis demonstrated that handwashing by doctors dramatically reduced puerperal fever mortality. He was ridiculed, dismissed, and eventually died in a psychiatric institution. The evidence was compelling. The mechanism (invisible microbes causing disease) violated the received framework. It took Pasteur and Koch, with direct microscopic evidence, to force acceptance of a mechanism that the evidence had required decades earlier.

**Quantum mechanics itself:** The double-slit experiment, photoelectric effect, and blackbody radiation produced results that violated every expectation of classical physics. The response was not immediate acceptance but years of denial, modification, and attempted accommodation within the classical framework. The revolution required abandoning classical determinism entirely.

**Prions:** The idea that proteins could cause infectious neurological disease without any nucleic acid — without DNA or RNA — was considered so impossibly anti-mechanistic that Prusiner's Nobel Prize-winning work was initially dismissed as quackery.

**The pattern:** In each case, the evidence came first. The mechanism came later. The demand for mechanism before evidence — "show us how it works before we'll look at whether it happens" — is not a scientific principle. It is a strategy for preserving the current framework against challenge.

The physicalist demand for a mechanism before acknowledging PSI evidence follows exactly this pattern. "Show us the physical mechanism for remote viewing and then we'll look at the data." This inverts the scientific process. The data comes first. If the data is robust, the mechanism must be sought — even if that search requires revising the framework.

## 6. The Asymmetry Corrected: A Symmetrical Challenge

The corrected epistemological stance is not "physicalism is wrong" — it is "physicalism and non-physicalism must be held to the same evidential standard."

**The symmetrical challenge:**

To the physicalist: Provide positive evidence for each of your three claims:
1. Physical causal closure is complete (address quantum indeterminacy, explain the measurement problem)
2. All anomalous phenomena are explainable within the physical framework (engage the evidence case by case)
3. Physics is essentially complete (explain dark matter, dark energy, the cosmological constant discrepancy, consciousness, the fine-tuning of constants)

To the non-physicalist: Provide positive evidence for non-physical causation that meets the same methodological standards applied to physical science (replicability, controls, pre-registration, independence of research teams).

The non-physicalist, under this symmetrical standard, has significant evidence to offer — the accumulated literature of PSI research, consciousness studies, and anomalous phenomena. That literature has methodological problems, as does all frontier science. But it is not categorically weaker than the evidence physicalists offer for their positive claims.

**The practical conclusion:** Non-physicalists need not perpetually defend. The appropriate response to "where is your evidence?" is "where is yours?" — specifically, positive evidence for the three pillars of physicalism enumerated above. In the absence of that evidence, physicalism is not the null hypothesis. It is one hypothesis among several, to be evaluated on its evidential merits alongside its competitors.

## 7. Summary

| Claim | Physicalist Assumption | Actual Status |
|---|---|---|
| Physical causal closure | Default requiring no evidence | Empirically falsified by quantum mechanics |
| All anomalous evidence is dismissible | Default requiring no engagement | Pragmatically impossible to demonstrate; denial used instead |
| Physics is essentially complete | Default requiring no defense | Empirically falsified by current physics (dark matter, dark energy, consciousness, measurement problem) |
| Burden of proof | Entirely on non-physicalists | Should be symmetrical; physicalists owe positive evidence for each pillar |
| Historical precedent | Physicalism has always been right | History shows repeated "essentially complete" claims followed by revolution |
| Indeterminism | Irrational (violates PSR) | Tralsely rational — locally violates PSR; meta-rationally enables meaning |
| Black as color | Not a color (no wavelength) | Tralsely a color — absence of wavelength yet foundational to all color distinction |

Physicalism is not the null hypothesis. It is a strong metaphysical claim making assertions that are either empirically falsified, pragmatically impossible to sustain, or simply undefended. The demand that non-physicalists bear the entire burden of proof is a rhetorical strategy, not an epistemic principle.

The appropriate response is symmetry: the same evidential standards, applied in both directions. When that symmetry is applied, the non-physicalist position is not weaker than the physicalist one. In several critical areas, it is stronger.
