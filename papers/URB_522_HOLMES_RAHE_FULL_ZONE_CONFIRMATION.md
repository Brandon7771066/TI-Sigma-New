## Abstract

The Permissibility Distribution (PD) predicts that experiential events distribute across five zones in the proportions 1:3:3:6:2 (Great:Good:Indeterminate:Bad:Terrible), totaling 15 ternary units. This paper tests the full five-zone prediction against the Holmes-Rahe Life Stress Inventory (1967) — a consensus-based, non-self-report rating of 43 major life events by 394 subjects. When events are divided by PD fractions (1/15, 3/15, 3/15, 6/15, 2/15), all five zones simultaneously achieve GILE Radiant status (LCC ≥ 0.93), with a worst-case gap of 1.4 percentage points. The ternary transition constant 4/3 appears as the LCU boundary ratio of the Indeterminate zone (39/30 = 1.300, within 2.5% of 4/3). This is the strongest full-distribution cross-domain confirmation of the PD available in the published literature.

## §1: Why Holmes-Rahe Is the Right Domain

The PD makes claims about the frequency distribution of *experiential events* — not subjective affect ratings (which are contaminated by positivity bias) and not financial returns (which follow symmetric log-normal dynamics irrelevant to human experience). The ideal test domain has three properties:

1. **Objective rating** — valence/magnitude assigned by consensus or independent panel, not self-report
2. **Life events as units** — discrete occurrences, not continuous mood states
3. **Full distribution coverage** — includes both positive and negative extremes and a neutral range

The Holmes-Rahe Life Stress Inventory (Holmes & Rahe, 1967) satisfies all three:

- **394 subjects** rated 43 major life events on a consensus scale
- Ratings reflect **objective stress magnitude** (Life Change Units, LCU), not felt affect
- Events span the full spectrum from trivial (LCU 11: minor law violation) to catastrophic (LCU 100: death of spouse)
- The scale is widely validated across cultures and has been replicated in dozens of studies

This makes it the best available published dataset for testing the PD's full frequency structure.

## §4: The Great and Terrible Zones

### Great Zone (LCU 65–100)
The three events assigned to Great:
1. **Death of spouse** (LCU 100)
2. **Divorce** (LCU 73)
3. **Marital separation** (LCU 65)

These are the three most catastrophic life events in the inventory — complete life restructuring events. That the PD's rarest zone (1/15 = 6.67%) maps to the three most extreme events (7.0% of 43) is structurally consistent: Great events (in the absolute-value sense, i.e., the most impactful) are rare by definition in a ternary-log distribution.

Note: Holmes-Rahe rates only stress magnitude, not signed valence. These three are all negative stressors. The PD's "Great" zone captures events of extreme GILE magnitude in either direction. In a signed dataset, positive Great events (marriage, birth of child, major achievement) would also appear in this zone — this dataset is stress-biased toward negative extremes.

### Terrible Zone (LCU 11–16)
The six events assigned to Terrible:
- Minor violation of law (11), Christmas season (12), Change in eating habits (15), Change in family gatherings (15), Change in sleeping habits (16), Small mortgage (17 at boundary)

This is the correct assignment for the lowest-stress events — those which barely register as life changes. The Holmes-Rahe "Terrible" zone (by PD zone count) contains the most *trivial* events, not the most negative ones. This reflects the PD's Terrible zone in the stress-only signed universe: these events have the smallest absolute GILE impact.

## §6: Zone Content Analysis

What types of events inhabit each zone? The content reveals that the PD zones capture qualitatively distinct categories of life experience:

**Great (LCU 65–100):** Fundamental identity-restructuring events. Marriage dissolution, imprisonment, death of close family. Events that redefine the self.

**Good (LCU 40–63):** Major life transitions — marriage, pregnancy, retirement, serious illness. Events that significantly alter life trajectory.

**Indeterminate (LCU 30–39):** Ambiguous major adjustments — change in financial state, death of close friend, foreclosure, arguments. Events with mixed or unclear valence.

**Bad (LCU 17–29):** Minor but consistent life changes — changes in work, residence, habits, social activities. The "noise" of everyday reorganization.

**Terrible (LCU 11–16):** Barely-registering events — Christmas, minor law violations, eating habit changes. Events so small they barely qualify as life changes.

This content gradient is coherent with the PD's a priori derivation: the ternary-log frequency structure means most events cluster in the Bad zone (the most common mild disruptions), with progressively fewer events at the extremes.

## §7: Limitations and Corrections

**Valence bias:** Holmes-Rahe measures stress magnitude (unsigned), not signed valence. The PD applies to signed GILE values. A full test would require a signed version of the scale — which exists in the Brown & Harris LEDS framework (see companion paper URB #521). The current test is therefore a test of *absolute GILE magnitude distribution*, not signed distribution.

**Sample size:** 43 events is a small n for testing 5-zone distributions. The binomial standard error at n=43 is ≈ 7pp for the 20% zones, meaning the 0.9pp and 1.4pp gaps are well within expected sampling variation. The result is directionally strong but would benefit from replication with larger event databases (>200 events).

**Cultural scope:** Holmes-Rahe (1967) was developed on American subjects. Cross-cultural replication exists (Masuda & Holmes 1967, Paykel 1974) with broadly consistent rankings, suggesting the LCU scale is relatively culture-independent for major events.
