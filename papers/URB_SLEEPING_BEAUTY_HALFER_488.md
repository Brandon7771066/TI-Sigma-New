## Abstract

The Sleeping Beauty Problem is not genuinely paradoxical. It is an Oxymoron (URB #487) generated by conflating two entirely different questions — one about a fair coin, one about an awakening protocol — within the same formal probabilistic framework. The "Thirder" answer (P(Heads) = 1/3) is the correct response to the question "in what fraction of awakenings will you be in a Heads-world?" IFF the coin is **truly fair**, then P(Heads) = 1/2 is a physical ground truth — not an epistemic claim — and no awakening protocol whatsoever can alter a fact about a physical coin. The Thirder position, in treating awakening events as Bayesian evidence about the coin, commits a category error: it upgrades a guaranteed event into informative evidence. This is a living refutation of naive Bayesianism. The paradox dissolves completely at the level of question-disambiguation.

**PRIMARY CONSTANT deployed:** 1/2 — the exact midpoint of the Tralse spectrum; the unique probability that is its own complement; the fair coin's physical ground truth.

## 1. The Setup

**The experiment:**
- Sunday: Sleeping Beauty is briefed on the protocol. A fair coin is flipped. She is put to sleep.
- **If Heads:** She is woken once on Monday. The experiment ends.
- **If Tails:** She is woken on Monday, put back to sleep with complete amnesia about the Monday waking, woken again on Tuesday. The experiment ends.
- Each time she wakes: she is asked **"What is your credence that the coin landed Heads?"**

She cannot tell what day it is. She cannot remember prior awakenings (if any). All she knows is that she is awake and the protocol is as described.

**The two camps:**

| Camp | Answer | Core argument |
|---|---|---|
| **Halfer** | P(Heads) = **1/2** | The coin is fair; she has no new information; credence stays at 1/2 |
| **Thirder** | P(Heads) = **1/3** | There are 3 equally likely awakening-states; only 1 is a Heads-state; so 1/3 |

## 2. The TI Sigma Diagnosis — Two Different Questions

This is an Oxymoron (URB #487): apparent contradiction generated by failing to distinguish the LEVEL at which the question is being asked.

**Question A:** "What is the probability that the coin, which is physically fair, landed Heads?"
→ **Answer: 1/2.** This is a fact about the coin's physics, fixed before any awakening.

**Question B:** "What fraction of your awakenings across many repetitions of this experiment occur in Heads-worlds?"
→ **Answer: 1/3.** By the structure of the protocol, 1 in 3 awakenings falls in a Heads-world.

These are different questions with different answers. Both answers are correct at their respective levels. The "paradox" is the Oxymoron of pressing both answers against each other as if they are competing claims about the same thing.

The question actually asked — "what is your credence that the coin landed Heads?" — is Question A. The answer is 1/2. The Thirder answers Question B.

By URB #487 (Oxymoron Dissolution), the resolving level is: **question-disambiguation**. At that level, there is no paradox. Both camps are right about their respective questions. The controversy exists only for as long as the two questions remain conflated.

## 3. The Physical Ground Truth Argument

**IFF the coin is TRULY FAIR:**

A fair coin has P(Heads) = 1/2 as a physical fact — not as an epistemic estimate, not as a prior to be updated, but as a feature of the physical object and the flipping process. This is prior to any probabilistic formalism. The formalism is supposed to CAPTURE the physical fact, not override it.

The question "what is your credence that the coin landed Heads?" is asking Sleeping Beauty to report her best estimate of a physical fact about an already-completed coin flip. That physical fact is P(Heads) = 1/2.

Nothing about her awakening status changes the physical fact. The coin landed how it landed before she was woken. Her being awake is causally downstream of the coin flip, not upstream of it. Downstream events do not retroactively alter the probabilities of upstream causes — at least not in any standard causal structure.

**The Thirder's error stated precisely:** The Thirder treats "I am currently awake" as evidence that updates P(Heads). But this is only legitimate Bayesian updating if being awake is more likely under one hypothesis (Tails) than another (Heads). Let's check:

- P(awake at some point | Heads) = 1 (she will definitely be woken)
- P(awake at some point | Tails) = 1 (she will definitely be woken)

The likelihood ratio = 1/1 = 1. By Bayes' theorem, evidence with likelihood ratio 1 provides **zero information** and produces **zero update**. P(Heads | awake) = P(Heads) = 1/2. The Halfer is correct as a matter of strict Bayesian mathematics.

## 4. The Refutation of Naive Bayesianism

The Thirder position is a **living refutation of naive Bayesianism** precisely because a formal Bayesian analysis — correctly applied — gives the Halfer answer, not the Thirder answer.

**Where the Thirder goes wrong in Bayesian terms:**

The Thirder treats being awake on a particular occasion as evidence — specifically, the evidence that "I am awake right now, in this specific awakening." They then apply indifference across the three awakening-states to get 1/3.

But this move conflates two things:
1. **"I am awake"** — guaranteed regardless of coin outcome; likelihood ratio = 1; zero information
2. **"I am awake on THIS specific occasion, either Monday or Tuesday"** — she doesn't know which, so this evidence is not available to her

She can observe (1) but not (2). She knows she's awake. She doesn't know which awakening she's in. The Thirder's indifference argument applies only if she can distinguish the awakening-states — which she cannot by stipulation (amnesia, no external information).

**The Bayesian framework properly applied:**

Prior: P(Heads) = 1/2
Evidence: "I am awake" (guaranteed; likelihood ratio = 1)
Update: none
Posterior: P(Heads | awake) = 1/2

The Thirder's 1/3 requires importing information she doesn't have: the ability to treat "this awakening" as a sample from the set of all awakenings. But she cannot locate herself within that set. She knows she's in some awakening; she doesn't know which one. The indifference principle across awakening-states requires being able to identify the relevant reference class — which requires distinguishing the awakenings — which she cannot do.

## 7. The "Truly Fair Coin" — Physical vs. Epistemic Probability

Brandon's formulation is precise: "IFF the coin is TRULY FAIR, then the coin is — in the most definite sense possible — 50/50 no matter how or when she wakes up."

This distinguishes two senses of probability:

**Physical probability (objective):** A property of the coin and flipping process. 1/2 for a truly fair coin. This is NOT updated by observation — it is a fact about the device.

**Epistemic probability (subjective):** A credence state in a rational agent. This CAN be updated by evidence. This is what Bayesianism governs.

The Thirder treats P(Heads) as purely epistemic and updates it. But P(Heads) for a truly fair coin has a physical component that is not subject to epistemic updating. The coin landed how it landed. The agent's uncertainty is about discovering a fact, not about a fact that is itself uncertain.

When physical and epistemic probabilities align — as they do for a fair coin with no additional evidence — the result is 1/2. The Thirder argument is most compelling if you are a pure subjectivist about probability (all probabilities are epistemic). But if probabilities track physical facts — as they must if they are to be useful — the fair coin's 1/2 is not up for epistemic revision without new physical evidence.
