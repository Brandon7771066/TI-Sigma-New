## Abstract

The neuroscience of neuroplasticity has identified numerous activities that enhance brain function: aerobic exercise, novel learning, sleep optimization, mindfulness meditation. This paper introduces a candidate modality that has not been formally studied for this purpose: the deliberate practice of maintaining at least neurotypical cognitive performance while in altered states of consciousness (ASCs). The key criterion is not the ASC itself but the maintenance requirement: the deliberate refusal to allow functional performance to degrade below baseline. No other lifestyle generates comparable multi-system stress on executive function simultaneously. Shamans were the original practitioners of this discipline, developing curriculum, apprenticeship structures, and cultural frameworks over millennia. Modern biohackers — using sublingual ketamine, psilocybin microdosing, float tanks, and breathwork — are their successors, operating with superior neurochemical tools but often lacking the intentional framework. The TI Sigma formalization: ASC Competence is the behavioral training protocol for the Tralse Blanket State (URB #421) — the simultaneous maximum opening of the i-channel and maintenance of real-channel integrity.

## 1. The Hypothesis

### 1.1 Core Claim

**The ASC Competence Hypothesis:** The deliberate practice of maintaining at least neurotypical cognitive performance while in an altered state of consciousness is the most neuroplasticity-demanding activity available to a human being, and consistent practice of it produces exceptional cognitive adaptive capacity that transfers broadly.

The hypothesis has three components:

1. **Severity claim:** ASC navigation under the maintenance requirement imposes more simultaneous stress on the executive control system than any other documented training modality.

2. **Adaptation claim:** This stress, through standard neuroplastic mechanisms (BDNF upregulation, synaptogenesis, DMN restructuring, metacognitive circuit strengthening), produces adaptive changes that exceed those produced by conventional cognitive training.

3. **Transfer claim:** The capacities developed through ASC Competence training — robust metacognition, executive redundancy, dual-channel management — transfer broadly to performance under stress, uncertainty, and novelty in ordinary life.

### 1.2 What the Hypothesis Is Not

The hypothesis is not a claim that drug use is beneficial per se. The ASC is a training context, not the point. A person who enters an ASC and passively experiences it — who suspends functional performance entirely — is not training ASC Competence. They are doing the cognitive equivalent of sitting in a sauna: potentially useful for other reasons, but not the high-demand adaptive stress that produces the training effect.

The training effect requires the maintenance criterion: the deliberate, skilled effort to function at or above baseline while the systems underlying that function are disrupted. This is what shamans cultivated and what distinguishes the best biohackers from those who merely consume substances recreationally.

## 2. Why ASC Navigation Is Uniquely Demanding

### 2.1 What Neurotypical Performance Requires

To appreciate the severity of the demand, consider what maintaining neurotypical cognitive performance requires simultaneously:

- **Prefrontal executive control:** Working memory, cognitive flexibility, inhibition, planning
- **Attention regulation:** Sustained attention, selective attention, attention shifting
- **Social cognition:** Theory of mind, emotional regulation, appropriate social response calibration
- **Language system:** Fluent production and comprehension
- **Sensorimotor integration:** Accurate body-world mapping, proprioception, motor coordination
- **Metacognitive monitoring:** Awareness of one's own cognitive states, detection of errors and biases
- **Temporal coherence:** Maintenance of narrative continuity — knowing who one is, where one is, what one is doing and why

Under normal conditions, these systems operate largely automatically. The challenge is low. The brain is not adapting to anything — it is running well-rehearsed routines in a stable physiological context.

### 2.2 What ASCs Disrupt

Every ASC induction method disrupts multiple systems from the above list simultaneously:

**Ketamine (NMDA antagonist):**  
Disrupts: working memory (NMDA-dependent), temporal coherence (dissociative disconnection of sequential experience), sensorimotor integration (dissociation), and executive control. Simultaneously elevates BDNF to the highest levels of any known rapid-acting compound — creating a state of maximal neural plasticity concurrent with maximal executive disruption.

**Psilocybin / LSD (5-HT2A agonists):**  
Disrupts: attention regulation (attention becomes involuntary and global), sensorimotor integration (perceptual distortions), social cognition calibration (radically altered emotional valence), and temporal coherence. Dramatically increases neural entropy — the randomness of activity patterns across cortical networks — producing a "high-entropy brain" that has lost its default hierarchical control structure.

**Meditation (deep states):**  
Disrupts: the default mode network — the network responsible for self-referential thought, narrative continuity, and temporal projection (past/future). In jhana and similar deep states, the ordinary executive architecture becomes inactive, replaced by modes of awareness that have no analogue in ordinary cognition.

**Holotropic breathwork / hyperventilation:**  
Disrupts: CO₂/O₂ balance → altered blood pH → massive changes in neural firing thresholds across all cortical regions simultaneously. Produces experiences clinically indistinguishable from psychedelic states.

### 2.3 The Compound Demand

When a person in any of these states attempts to maintain neurotypical performance, they are asking their executive system to do something it was never designed to do: **compensate for the disruption of multiple of its own subsystems using the remaining, partially disrupted subsystems as the compensatory tools.**

This is the cognitive equivalent of:
- Running a marathon while periodically losing proprioception in one leg — you must continuously compensate and recalibrate
- Performing a complex musical improvisation while your short-term memory is intermittently dropping — you must develop new strategies for maintaining coherence without the tools you normally use
- Navigating a conversation while your emotional regulation system produces systematically anomalous outputs — you must monitor, interpret, and re-map those outputs in real time

No other training modality creates this kind of demand. Conventional cognitive training (n-back tasks, dual-task paradigms, chess, foreign languages) operates in a stable physiological context, targeting specific subsystems while the rest of the architecture remains intact. ASC Competence training disrupts the entire architecture simultaneously and demands that the system perform anyway.

## 3. The Neuroplasticity Mechanisms

### 3.1 BDNF and Rapid Synaptogenesis

Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) is the primary molecular signal for synaptogenesis — the formation of new synaptic connections. It is the biochemical substrate of learning at the cellular level. Higher BDNF = higher capacity for new connection formation = higher neuroplasticity.

Key finding: ketamine produces the most rapid and robust BDNF elevation of any known compound. Within hours of a ketamine dose, BDNF in prefrontal cortex increases dramatically, and rapid synaptogenesis follows (Duman, Aghajanian, Sanacora & Bhagya, 2019). This is the mechanism of ketamine's rapid antidepressant effect — it is not primarily about the subjective experience but about the neural reorganization that occurs while BDNF is elevated.

**The ASC Competence amplification:** If synaptogenesis occurs preferentially in circuits that are active during the period of elevated BDNF, then the circuits engaged by maintaining performance during ketamine intoxication — precisely the compensatory metacognitive and executive circuits most stressed by the task — are the circuits that undergo the greatest synaptogenesis. The plasticity is directed by the demand.

This is the training principle: the stress (ASC) creates the plasticity window (BDNF/synaptogenesis), and the maintenance requirement directs that plasticity toward the most useful circuits (executive compensation, metacognitive monitoring).

### 3.2 The Entropic Brain and Cognitive Flexibility

Robin Carhart-Harris's "entropic brain" hypothesis (2014) proposes that the mechanism common to all psychedelic ASCs is a dramatic increase in neural entropy — the randomness and unpredictability of activity patterns across cortical networks. Ordinary cognition relies on hierarchically constrained, relatively low-entropy neural dynamics. Psychedelic ASCs disrupt this hierarchy, releasing lower-level networks from top-down control.

This temporary high-entropy state has an important implication for training: it exposes the brain to activity configurations it would never encounter in normal physiological states. Recovery from the high-entropy state — the process of re-establishing functional coherence — requires the executive system to rebuild structure from a more disorganized starting point. Doing this repeatedly, under the constraint of maintaining functional performance, trains the executive system's ability to impose structure under conditions of internal disorder.

This is precisely the capacity required in high-stress novel situations in ordinary life: the ability to re-establish functional coherence when the normal cognitive infrastructure is disrupted by stress, fatigue, emotional flooding, or radical uncertainty.

### 3.3 Default Mode Network Restructuring

The Default Mode Network (DMN) is the brain's "self-referential" network — active during mind-wandering, autobiographical memory, social cognition, and narrative self-construction. It is also implicated in rumination, depression, anxiety, and cognitive rigidity. Every major ASC induction method dramatically disrupts DMN activity:

- Psychedelics: acute suppression of DMN coherence, followed by reorganization
- Meditation: gradual restructuring of DMN patterns over years of practice
- Ketamine: acute DMN suppression via NMDA antagonism
- Breathwork: CO₂-mediated DMN disruption

Long-term practitioners of ASC-inducing activities — both shamans and experienced meditators — show persistent DMN modifications: reduced default activity (less mind-wandering and rumination), more flexible DMN engagement (ability to turn on and off self-referential processing at will), and restructured DMN connectivity patterns that correlate with well-being and cognitive flexibility.

The maintenance requirement during ASC training is crucial for DMN restructuring: you cannot simply dissolve the self (DMN shutdown) and return. You must dissolve and simultaneously maintain functional operation — which requires the executive network to operate independently of the DMN rather than in its usual default relationship with it. This enforced independence is the training stimulus for DMN flexibility.

## 4. Shamanism: The Original Curriculum

### 4.1 The Universality of the Shamanic Tradition

Mircea Eliade's foundational survey (1951) documented the presence of shamanic practice in virtually every pre-modern culture across every inhabited continent: Siberia, Central Asia, indigenous Americas, Africa, Australia, and Southeast Asia. The techniques vary — drum-induced trance, plant medicines, fasting, extreme cold or heat exposure, darkness — but the core training structure is remarkably consistent across cultures with no historical contact.

This universality is significant. It suggests that shamanism is not a cultural artifact but a discovery — a technology that independent human groups converged on because it worked. The shamanic tradition represents the accumulated results of tens of thousands of years of empirical observation about how to cultivate ASC Competence.

### 4.2 The Shamanic Curriculum

Shamanic initiation across traditions follows a recognizable structure:

**Phase 1 — Selection and early disruption:** The candidate is identified (often by crisis — illness, near-death, involuntary ASC) and begins exposure to ASC induction. Early experiences are typically terrifying, disorienting, and functionally devastating. The candidate cannot maintain performance. This is analogous to a beginner attempting to lift a weight they cannot yet manage — failure is the starting point, not a disqualification.

**Phase 2 — Supervised navigation:** Under the guidance of a trained shaman, the candidate learns to navigate the ASC rather than be overwhelmed by it. The crucial teacher's role: not to prevent the disruption (you need the stress) but to provide the metacognitive scaffold that the student has not yet internalized. "This is what you are experiencing. This is what it means. Here is how to maintain orientation." The student is building internal metacognitive capacity by first using the teacher's metacognition as an external prosthetic.

**Phase 3 — Mastery and functional integration:** The trained shaman can enter ASCs intentionally, navigate them with purpose, perform complex social and healing functions while in them, and return with coherent and often actionable information. The ASC is no longer a disruption of function — it is an alternative operating mode. This is ASC Competence at its highest expression.

## 5. Modern Biohacking: The Technological Succession

### 5.1 The Successor Tradition

Modern biohackers who use ASC-inducing tools — sublingual ketamine, psilocybin microdosing, float tanks, holotropic breathwork, LSD microdosing — are the technological successors to the shamanic tradition. They have access to:

- **More precise dosing:** Sublingual ketamine can be titrated to specific plasma concentrations; psilocybin synthesized to exact purity; dose-response curves documented
- **Mechanistic understanding:** The 5-HT2A receptor, NMDA antagonism, BDNF cascade — the biohacker can understand what is happening neurochemically and use that understanding to optimize the training context
- **Reduced cultural friction:** No need to navigate the social structures of a traditional shamanic community; protocols are accessible via the internet
- **Combination tools:** A biohacker can combine sublingual ketamine with theta-frequency binaural beats, float tank sensory reduction, and specific cognitive tasks — designing a training stimulus that no traditional shamanic curriculum could assemble

### 5.2 What the Biohacker Often Lacks

The shamanic tradition had things the biohacker community has largely lost:

**Intentional framework:** Shamanic ASC use always occurred within a clearly defined purpose — healing, divination, community service, initiation. The purpose was not incidental to the practice; it was the training demand. Maintaining the purpose while in the ASC is what produced the maintenance requirement. A biohacker who uses ketamine recreationally — without a maintained task, without a functional demand — is not training ASC Competence. They are exploring the ASC passively.

**Supervised initiation:** The shamanic apprentice's early terrifying experiences occurred with a master present who could provide metacognitive scaffolding. Many biohackers navigate early high-intensity ASC experiences alone, without the externalized metacognitive support that would accelerate the internalization of navigational capacity.

**Cultural integration:** Shamanic practice was embedded in community. The shaman's ASC competence was regularly tested by community demands — healing sessions, ceremonial leadership, interpretive work. This provided continuous performance testing. The biohacker's ASC competence is often untested in any demanding social or functional context.

### 5.3 The Integration Protocol

The optimal modern ASC Competence training protocol synthesizes shamanic structure with biohacker precision:

1. **Clear functional demand** (the maintenance requirement): a specific cognitive task to be maintained at or above baseline during the ASC — conversation, creative problem-solving, a work output, a musical performance
2. **Calibrated induction** (biohacker tool): sublingual ketamine at a specific dose targeting a specific experiential intensity; psilocybin at a microdose or low full dose; float tank; theta-frequency entrainment
3. **Metacognitive monitoring** (the shaman's second-order awareness): explicit, practiced attention to one's own cognitive state — noticing what is disrupted, what is available, and recalibrating continuously
4. **Post-session integration** (the shamanic debrief): structured reflection on what was maintained, what was lost, what was learned about one's own compensatory strategies — this is the explicit learning that converts the experience into durable adaptive capacity
5. **Escalating intensity** (progressive overload): as competence grows, the ASC intensity increases — maintaining performance at deeper disruption levels, just as a weight-lifter increases load as strength grows

## 7. The Research Gap and Why It Exists

Despite the potential significance of this hypothesis, there is essentially no formal research on ASC navigation as a cognitive training modality. The reasons for this gap are revealing:

**The wrong outcome measures:** Psychedelic research has focused on therapeutic outcomes (depression, PTSD, addiction) and subjective experience quality. It has not measured the cognitive skill of maintaining performance during the ASC — because the assumption has been that ASC = impaired performance, and impaired performance is a side effect to minimize, not a training stimulus to leverage.

**Prohibition and stigma:** The controlled substance status of most potent ASC inducers has made systematic research nearly impossible for decades. The research that does exist — Hopkins, NYU, MAPS — has focused on establishing safety and therapeutic efficacy. Cognitive training applications are next-generation research that requires a normalized regulatory environment.

**The passive ASC model:** The dominant cultural model of ASC use (both in recreational contexts and in most research contexts) is passive: you take the substance and observe what happens. The active model — you take the substance and attempt to maintain a specific functional performance standard — is rarely studied or even conceptualized.

**The shamanic knowledge problem:** The people with the deepest empirical knowledge of ASC navigation — experienced shamans, trained psychedelic guides, long-term meditators in deep-state traditions — are not publishing in peer-reviewed journals. Their knowledge exists in apprenticeship traditions and direct transmission, largely inaccessible to formal research.
