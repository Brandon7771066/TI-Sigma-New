### §4.4 Where the 17% MI→F and 13% NA→F errors come from (#69 disclosure)

Inspection of misclassifications reveals two interpretable patterns rather than rater noise:

- **MI → F (10/60 ratings, 17%):** dominated by the propositions where the contradiction is *arithmetically* presented rather than semantically-paradoxically. E.g., "2 + 2 = 5 by definition of the standard arithmetical operators on natural numbers" can be rated F (it's mathematically-false) OR MI (it's incoherent-under-actualization given the qualifier "by definition of the standard operators"). Raters split. This is a **boundary-case feature**, not a discrimination-failure of the 5-tier system — it indicates the **MI/F boundary near arithmetic contradictions** is genuinely fuzzy and requires a sharper-definitional treatment (queued: candidate falsifier F-MR-MI-F-1 for Pass-78+).

- **NA → F (8/60 ratings, 13%):** dominated by propositions whose category-mistake CAN be read as "trivially false because the predicate doesn't apply to that subject" (logically-positivist treatment of category-mistakes-as-false). E.g., "the number 7 smells distinctly of vanilla" can be rated NA (category-mistake; numbers don't have olfactory properties) OR F (no number actually has any smell so the predication is false). This is the canonical **NA-vs-F philosophical boundary** that Carnap/Ryle/Strawson debated mid-20th century. Raters split; this is a **philosophical-substantive disagreement**, not a discrimination-failure (queued: candidate falsifier F-MR-NA-F-1 for Pass-78+).

Both error-patterns are **interpretable in favor of the 5-tier system rather than against it**: the categorical-distinctions correspond to philosophically-substantive boundary-cases where intelligent-raters can principled-disagree. Pass-63-B5 had analogous boundary-disagreements (PARADOX→I 5%, MODAL→MI 0%) and treated them as features-not-bugs; same treatment here.
