## 1. The Empirical Record

### 1.1 Core Finding

The Maharishi Effect was first predicted by Maharishi Mahesh Yogi in 1960 and first studied scientifically in 1974 (Borland & Landrith, 1976). The finding:

> When approximately the square root of 1% of a population (√(0.01 × N) individuals) practices TM together, measurable reductions in crime, accidents, hospital admissions, and social stress appear in the surrounding population.

**For a population of N, the required group size is:**
```
n_threshold = √(0.01 × N) = √N / 10
```

Examples:
| Population N | Required group | Ratio |
|-------------|---------------|-------|

| 10,000 | 10 | 0.10% |
| 100,000 | 31.6 ≈ 32 | 0.032% |
| 1,000,000 | 100 | 0.010% |
| 8,000,000,000 (global) | 8,944 | 0.00011% |

### 1.2 Key Studies

| Study | Location | Group Size | Effect | Significance |
|-------|---------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Borland & Landrith (1976) | 11 US cities | √1% practitioners | Crime rate reduced vs matched cities | p < 0.05 |
| Dillbeck et al. (1981) | National US | √1% nationally | Reduced traffic fatalities, crime | p < 0.01 |
| Hagelin et al. (1999) | Washington DC | 4,000 TM-Sidhi practitioners | 23% crime reduction over 8 weeks | p < 0.0001, controlled for 8 variables |

The Washington DC study (Hagelin et al., 1999) is particularly rigorous: it was pre-registered, controlled for weather, holidays, police staffing, and 5 other variables, used an independent review board, and produced a 23% violent crime reduction during the 8-week intervention — reversing when the group dispersed.
