## 1. Background and Motivation

| Value | Symbol | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| True | T | Definitely true |
| False | F | Definitely false |

| Neither | N | Neither true nor false — unknown or irreducible |

**Tralseness** (T_r) is the *degree* to which a system occupies the Tr state — how much it holds contradictions simultaneously. For a pair of subjects (persons, ideas, forces), Tralseness measures the magnitude of their mutual contradiction across GILE dimensions.

Formally, for a pair (A, B):

```

```

Where A_d, B_d are the GILE dimension scores of each subject. T_r ranges from 0 (identical) to 1 (maximally contradictory).

**Low Tralseness:** Two nearly identical people. Complementary but not contradictory.  
**High Tralseness:** Two deeply different people. They contradict each other across multiple dimensions. Each is "both right and wrong" from the other's frame.

### 2.2 Derivation of the Squared Relationship

**Why squared, not linear?**

Consider the Tr state as a **potential energy well**. Two poles A and B, separated by Tralseness T_r, store energy proportional to:

```

```

This is the standard form for any restoring-force potential (spring, electric dipole, chemical bond). The stored energy *always* scales as the square of the separation — this is fundamental to physics, chemistry, and information theory alike.

**Information-theoretic derivation:**

The Shannon entropy of a Tr system with two poles each with probability p and (1-p):

```
H(p) = -p log₂(p) - (1-p) log₂(1-p)
```

This is maximized at p = 0.5 (maximum uncertainty, maximum Tr). The *information released* upon resolution from H_initial to H_final = 0 is:

```
ΔI = H_initial = -p log₂(p) - (1-p) log₂(1-p)
```

For a two-pole Tr system with equal balance (p=0.5): ΔI = 1 bit — the full reduction from maximum entropy to certainty. For unbalanced Tr: ΔI < 1 bit. The *maximum possible information released* scales with the depth of the initial uncertainty — i.e., with T_r.

When both poles are present simultaneously (Tr state), the information released upon synthesis is **greater** than simply the sum of the two individual contributions, because the synthesis contains a *third entity* not present in either pole alone — the MR output itself. This third entity's information content ∝ T_r² because it must encode both poles AND the relationship between them.

**Physical analogy:**

When two *similar* gases mix: ΔS ≈ small (low Tralseness → low entropy generation)  
When two *highly different* elements react: ΔE >> T_r(A) + T_r(B) (high Tralseness → disproportionate energy release)

The binding energy of a molecule scales quadratically with the difference in electronegativity between atoms — high Tralseness chemistry produces the most stable and energetic compounds (e.g., NaCl = maximum ionic Tr → most stable salt).
