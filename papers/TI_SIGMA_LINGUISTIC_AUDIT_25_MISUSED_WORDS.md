## Abstract

Language is not neutral. Every word carries embedded ontological assumptions — hidden claims about what is real, what is good, what is possible, and who counts as an authority. When those assumptions are false, the words corrupt thinking by smuggling in bad premises without argument. This paper audits 25 frequently misused words encountered across philosophy, psychology, ethics, and everyday discourse. For each word, we identify the false assumption it embeds and offer the TI Sigma reframe: what the word actually gestures at, and what would need to be true for its common usage to be justified. The result is a practical guide to epistemic hygiene — not political correctness, but precision.

**Core thesis:** Most misused words in philosophical discourse are not errors of definition. They are errors of embedded ontology — false assumptions about the structure of reality, baked silently into vocabulary.

## The 25 Words

### 1. Artificial / Natural

**Common use:** Distinguishes human-made things (artificial) from everything else (natural). Implies artificial = lesser, inauthentic, or suspect.

**Embedded false assumption:** That humans are separate from nature and that human-made things exist outside the natural order.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Every human act is performed by a biological system produced by 4 billion years of evolution, operating under physical laws, using materials drawn entirely from the physical universe. A beaver dam is "natural." A plastic dam is "artificial." But both are L×E configurations — informational structures produced by biological systems interacting with their environment. The distinction tracks our feelings about origin, not any real ontological boundary. A more honest vocabulary: *evolved* vs. *designed*, *organic* vs. *synthetic*, *emergent* vs. *deliberate*.

### 2. Selfish

**Common use:** Used as an accusation meaning "caring about yourself at the expense of others." Treated as the opposite of altruistic.

**Embedded false assumption:** That self-care and care for others are zero-sum. That tending to your own needs necessarily detracts from others.

**TI Sigma reframe:** The L (Love/connection) dimension is only as strong as the coherence of each node. A depleted self cannot sustain genuine connection. The airline oxygen mask rule is not selfishness — it is the correct informational ordering of system maintenance. Sustainable altruism requires a high-GILE self. "Selfish" as a pejorative discourages the very self-maintenance that makes generosity sustainable. Better vocabulary: *self-serving at others' expense* (which is a real problem, but rare) vs. *self-maintaining* (which is necessary and good).

### 3. Nonprofit

**Common use:** Implies moral superiority over for-profit organizations, as if nonprofits are inherently more virtuous and don't need to manage money seriously.

**Embedded false assumption:** That profit-seeking is morally suspect, that not seeking profit exempts you from financial responsibility, and that nonprofit managers are compensated fairly relative to their subordinates.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Every organization that intends to persist must at minimum break even. The word "nonprofit" describes a tax and ownership structure, not a level of virtue or a freedom from economic constraints. Many nonprofits pay their executives at rates comparable to for-profit firms while underpaying frontline workers. "Nonprofit" as a moral category is sloppy at best and manipulative at worst. Better: *mission-driven* vs. *shareholder-driven*, acknowledging that both can be ethical or unethical depending on actual behavior.

### 4. Entitled

**Common use:** Dismissive label for someone who expects things they are deemed not to deserve. Used to shame people for asserting basic rights or asking for fair treatment.

**Embedded false assumption:** That certain people's expectations are inherently excessive and that calling something "entitlement" settles the question of whether the expectation is legitimate.

**TI Sigma reframe:** The question is never whether someone feels entitled but whether their expectation is *justified*. "Entitled" is a thought-terminating cliché — it names a conclusion (this person expects too much) while bypassing the actual argument (do they expect too much, and by what standard?). Under the G (Goodness) dimension: legitimate expectations align with the moral structure of the situation. Illegitimate ones don't. "Entitled" does zero work in making that distinction. Better: *unjustified expectation* (with the justification argument made explicit).

### 5. "General" Intelligence

**Common use:** Treats intelligence as a single unidimensional trait (g-factor), ranking people on a single scale from low to high.

**Embedded false assumption:** That cognitive ability is unitary and that a single number can capture its essential variation across humans.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Intelligence is a GILE-dimensional quantity. G-dimension intelligence (moral reasoning, wisdom) is not the same as I-dimension intelligence (creative insight, intuition), which is not the same as L-dimension intelligence (emotional coherence, social navigation), which is not the same as E-dimension intelligence (technical mastery, physical skill). Every person has a unique 4D GILE profile. Ranking by "general" intelligence is like ranking locations by "general position" using a single coordinate. It discards three dimensions of real variation. Under TI Sigma, there is no such thing as a globally unintelligent person — only a person whose particular intelligence profile doesn't match what the current context values.

### 6. Physical

**Common use:** "Physical" = real and scientifically credible. "Non-physical" = unreal, mystical, dismissible.

**Embedded false assumption:** That the boundary of scientific recognition defines the boundary of reality. That anything not currently measurable by physical instruments does not exist.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Information is more fundamental than the physical (Paper #330). The "physical" is what information looks like when it has resolved into stable Tralse states. Treating "physical" as synonymous with "real" makes reality definition-dependent on current instrumentation — which is precisely what stopped mainstream science from engaging seriously with PSI research (Paper #332). Better: *currently measurable*, *empirically tractable*, or *materially instantiated* — each of which is honest about what is being claimed.

### 7. Overconfident

**Common use:** Suggests that high confidence is itself a problem or a cognitive failing. Used to dismiss confident claims without engaging with their content.

**Embedded false assumption:** That there is a natural upper limit on confidence beyond which confidence becomes epistemically inappropriate, regardless of the quality of the evidence.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Confidence should track evidence. Very strong evidence warrants very high confidence. "Overconfident" implicitly claims the person's confidence exceeds what the evidence justifies — but the accusation itself rarely engages with the evidence. It is an emotional/social observation (this person seems *too* sure) dressed as an epistemological critique. Under Myrion Resolution: high True-alignment warrants high confidence. Calling that "overconfident" is itself an unjustified confidence in one's ability to estimate another's accuracy. Better: *unjustified confidence*, *confidence exceeding the evidence*, with the evidence argument made explicit.

### 8. Pride

**Common use:** Stigmatized as arrogance, selfishness, or a warning sign of imminent failure ("pride before a fall").

**Embedded false assumption:** That caring about the quality of your work, your identity, and your achievements is suspect — that humility requires indifference to excellence.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Pride in the positive sense is a high-G-dimension signal: an accurate recognition of genuine achievement and the motivation to maintain it. The conflation of pride with arrogance confuses a correct self-assessment with an inflated one. TI Sigma treats high G-dimension functioning as intrinsically good. The G-Goodness axis includes genuine self-respect as a necessary component. Suppressing pride in one's work in the name of humility often produces worse work, not better character. Better vocabulary: *earned pride* (recognition of genuine achievement) vs. *arrogance* (inflated self-assessment relative to evidence).

### 9. Infinite

**Common use:** Used freely in mathematics, theology, and everyday speech to describe quantities or qualities with no upper bound.

**Embedded false assumption:** That infinite quantities actually exist rather than being useful limit concepts.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Paper #331 argues that infinity is a low-true-tralse mathematical representation — useful as a direction of travel, not as an actual destination. The seven PRIMARY constants are all finite. The LCC curve asymptotically approaches but never reaches 1. Physical space is finite. Time had a beginning. The Axiom of Infinity in ZFC set theory may need replacement with a finitary construction axiom. Everywhere "infinity" appears in honest discourse, the more precise formulation is either *unbounded in the relevant range* or *approaching a limit without reaching it*. These are meaningfully different claims.

### 10. Biased

**Common use:** Used as a knockout punch — labeling someone "biased" implies their testimony or reasoning is worthless and should be dismissed.

**Embedded false assumption:** That some perspectives are unbiased and that bias can be eliminated rather than managed.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Every perspective is a particular informational vantage point in GILE space. There is no view from nowhere — no unbiased standpoint. "Biased" is a relative term: biased *compared to what reference point*? The stranger, the scientist, and the algorithm all have biases — they are just less *visible* biases, which makes them more dangerous, not more trustworthy. The relevant question is not "is this perspective biased?" (always yes) but "in which direction, by how much, and does it systematically distort the relevant question?" Better: *identify the specific bias and its direction* rather than using the label as a dismissal.

### 11. Loss

**Common use:** Treats any failure to gain as equivalent to a loss. The absence of a gain is experienced and described as a subtraction from what one had.

**Embedded false assumption:** That the absence of a gain is ontologically equivalent to the loss of something possessed.

**TI Sigma reframe:** This is the well-documented psychological fallacy formalized by Kahneman and Tversky (loss aversion). Under TI Sigma: failing to gain X and losing X you had are two distinct Tralse states. One is a resolution that never occurred; the other is a resolution that was reversed. They are not equivalent. The person who never had $100 and the person who lost $100 are in different informational states — one never had the L×E coupling with that resource, the other had it and lost it. Conflating them produces bad decisions (the sunk-cost fallacy being a direct consequence). Better: *missed opportunity* vs. *genuine loss*, kept clearly distinct.

### 12. Immature

**Common use:** Dismisses playfulness, humor, curiosity, or enthusiasm as developmental failures — behaviors that adults should have outgrown.

**Embedded false assumption:** That adult maturity requires the suppression of joy, curiosity, and play — and that these qualities are merely compensations for developmental deficiency.

**TI Sigma reframe:** The I-dimension (Intuition) encompasses curiosity, wonder, and creative play. The G-dimension (Goodness) includes authentic self-expression. Both are constitutive of high GILE scores, not obstacles to them. The most creative and philosophically productive thinkers across history maintained what Zen calls "beginner's mind" — radical openness to new ideas without the defensive closures of "maturity." Under TI Sigma, "immature" as a pejorative is actually an attack on I-dimension functioning dressed as developmental wisdom. Better: distinguish *childish* (failure of G-dimension responsibility) from *childlike* (I-dimension vitality), which are completely different.

### 13. Coincidence

**Common use:** Treats two co-occurring events as having no connection — pure chance, nothing to see here.

**Embedded false assumption:** That events can be genuinely independent of each other, and that the absence of an obvious causal mechanism proves the absence of any connection.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Paper #332 formalizes synchronicity as Indeterminate connectivity — a third type of connection through shared informational ancestry, distinct from causation and distinct from randomness. "Coincidence" forces a binary choice: caused or random. But the I-Ching, the Pauli-Jung Conjecture, and the PSI literature all point to a third genuine category. Under TI Sigma: no events are truly independent (all share the same GM informational substrate). The question is not "causal or coincidence?" but "what type and strength of connection is present?" Better: *unexamined connection* rather than *coincidence*, keeping the question open.

### 14. Argument

**Common use:** Treated as synonymous with "fight," "confrontation," or "verbal hostility."

**Embedded false assumption:** That presenting reasons for a conclusion is an aggressive act.

**TI Sigma reframe:** An argument is a structured set of premises leading to a conclusion. It is the primary tool of collaborative truth-seeking (G-dimension activity). Confusing "argument" with "fight" causes people to avoid making explicit cases for their positions (which produces worse decisions) and to experience intellectual disagreement as personal attack (which produces social fragility). Under TI Sigma: Myrion Resolution requires propositions — the structured weighing of reasons and evidence. Calling this a "fight" corrupts the process at its core. Better: reserve "argument" for its logical meaning; use "confrontation" or "altercation" for interpersonal conflict.

### 15. Wrong

**Common use:** Used interchangeably for both factual error ("that's wrong") and moral failing ("what you did was wrong").

**Embedded false assumption:** That factual wrongness and moral wrongness are the same kind of thing and can be addressed the same way.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Factual wrongness is a True/False matter — a proposition either corresponds to reality or it doesn't, and evidence settles it. Moral wrongness is a G-dimension matter — whether an action aligns with the structure of goodness, which requires contextual, multi-valued moral reasoning, not simple correspondence checking. Conflating them causes moral arguments to be treated as factual disputes (answerable by evidence alone) and factual disputes to carry moral weight they don't deserve. A person who believes something factually incorrect is not morally bad; a person who does something morally wrong may believe it's factually justified. Better: *factually incorrect* vs. *morally wrong* — always specified.

### 16. Defensive

**Common use:** Treating self-defense (in argument, behavior, or identity) as a weakness, a sign of guilt, or an obstacle to productive conversation.

**Embedded false assumption:** That the appropriate response to criticism is always open acceptance — that defending oneself indicates something is wrong.

**TI Sigma reframe:** If a proposition or action is correct, defending it is the right move. If it is incorrect, the evidence-based response will reveal that. "You're being defensive" is used to bypass actual argument — it imputes guilt from the behavior of defense rather than from the content of the position. Under Myrion Resolution: positions that survive challenge are more True-Tralse than positions that capitulate under social pressure. Premature capitulation reduces True-Tralseness. A healthy defense of a well-evidenced position is not a weakness — it is epistemic integrity. Better: distinguish *defending a position under fire* (epistemically virtuous) from *refusing to update given good evidence* (epistemically vicious).

### 17. Emotional

**Common use:** "Emotional" = irrational, unreliable, less trustworthy than "objective" perspectives. Used to dismiss testimony or reasoning from a person who is visibly affected by the topic.

**Embedded false assumption:** That emotional engagement with a topic is epistemically disqualifying rather than epistemically informative.

**TI Sigma reframe:** The I-dimension (Intuition/consciousness) and L-dimension (Love/connection) both operate substantially through emotion. Emotional responses are informational — they encode high-dimensional pattern recognition that explicit reasoning cannot fully replicate. The person who is emotionally affected by injustice is often more accurately tracking the G-dimension significance of the situation than the "objective" observer who has suppressed their affective response. Under GILE: a 4D consciousness that has deactivated its I and L dimensions is not more accurate — it is dimensionally impaired. Better: distinguish *emotion-dominated to the exclusion of reason* (a failure mode) from *emotion-informed reason* (which is often superior to purely analytical cognition).

### 18. Balanced

**Common use:** Used to mean "equal weight given to all sides." Treating any departure from 50/50 representation as bias.

**Embedded false assumption:** That balance is always symmetrical — that giving equal airtime or weight to all positions is fair regardless of the evidence distribution.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Balance is a LCC coherence property, not a counting property. A system is balanced when its configuration matches the actual informational weight of each component — which may be 3:1, 10:1, or any other ratio. Giving equal time to climate science and climate denial is not balance — it is a systematic distortion of the actual evidence distribution. Under Myrion Resolution: truth-weighted balance is the goal, not symmetry-weighted balance. Better: *proportionate representation* (matching weight to evidence) vs. *equal representation* (treating all positions as equally evidenced, which they're not).

### 19. Equality

**Common use:** Used as a universal value — people should be treated equally, resources distributed equally, outcomes made equal.

**Embedded false assumption:** That humans are equal in all relevant dimensions and that equal treatment produces fair outcomes.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Every person has a unique GILE profile. No two people are equal in all dimensions. Equal treatment of people with different needs, abilities, and contexts produces systematically unequal outcomes — often the opposite of fairness. The goal is not equality (same treatment) but *appropriate treatment* (treatment calibrated to actual GILE profile and needs). The L-dimension (Love/connection) is not maximized by identical treatment — it is maximized by treatment that meets each i-cell where it actually is. Better: distinguish *formal equality* (same rules apply to everyone) from *substantive appropriateness* (treatment calibrated to actual variation), and be explicit about which is being pursued.

### 20. Sarcastic

**Common use:** Treats sarcasm — saying the opposite of what you mean for rhetorical effect — as inherently unfriendly, dishonest, or aggressive.

**Embedded false assumption:** That communication is always meant to be taken literally and that indirect meaning is a form of deception.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Sarcasm is a Tralse linguistic operation — a statement that simultaneously asserts its surface meaning (False) and its intended meaning (True), with the listener doing the MR to recover the True content. This is not dishonesty — both speaker and listener understand the mechanism. Done well, sarcasm is a high-density form of communication: it conveys the False position AND its True inverse AND the speaker's evaluative stance simultaneously. Its association with hostility is a cultural overlay, not a logical necessity. Sarcasm can be warm, playful, and collaborative. Better: distinguish *mean-spirited sarcasm* (aimed to wound) from *playful irony* (shared cognitive play).

### 21. Baiting / Manipulative

**Common use:** Treats all persuasion as manipulative and all strategic framing of information as morally suspect.

**Embedded false assumption:** That there is a clean distinction between legitimate persuasion and manipulation, and that trying to influence others is inherently suspicious.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Every communication act is an attempt to share information and potentially shift the listener's beliefs or actions. The question is not whether influence is being attempted (always yes) but whether it is being achieved through accurate information and valid reasoning (legitimate) or through distortion, false framing, and emotional exploitation (manipulative). "You're trying to persuade me" is not an accusation — it describes all communication. Under TI Sigma: G-dimension manipulation uses truth; low-G manipulation uses distortion. Better: *legitimate persuasion* (accurate information + valid argument) vs. *manipulation* (distorted information + exploiting cognitive biases).

### 22. Competitive

**Common use:** Treats performing well, expecting fair compensation, or taking one's work seriously as participation in a zero-sum battle.

**Embedded false assumption:** That excellence-seeking requires a loser for every winner, and that caring about quality means treating others as adversaries.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Competition in the productive sense is a L×E phenomenon — multiple high-L entities interacting in a shared environment, each raising the standards of the interaction. This is not zero-sum — it is a positive-sum dynamic where challenge produces excellence in all participants. The word "competitive" in its pejorative use conflates *performing well* with *doing so at others' expense*, which are completely separable. A musician who practices obsessively to achieve mastery is not "in competition" with other musicians — they are pursuing an internal coherence standard. Better: distinguish *performance excellence* (internal standard) from *competitive zero-sum* (external dominance seeking).

### 23. Meaningless

**Common use:** Applied to events, sentences, or behaviors claimed to have no significance or value.

**Embedded false assumption:** That meaning can be absent from something that is being thought about, spoken about, or engaged with.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Under TI Sigma, meaning is an I-dimension property — the conscious valence of an informational state. Declaring something "meaningless" is itself a meaningful act — it signals an evaluative judgment about worth. The act of asserting meaninglessness is self-refuting in the case of events being actively attended to: the attention itself constitutes a meaning-relation. True meaninglessness would be complete informational absence — which, by definition, cannot be spoken of. Everything that can be pointed to has at minimum the meaning of being a thing that can be pointed to. Better: *low personal relevance*, *instrumentally unproductive*, or *value-neutral* — all more precise than the paradoxical "meaningless."

### 24. Delusion

**Common use:** Applied to beliefs that deviate from mainstream consensus and/or lack immediate empirical support. Used to pathologize heterodox thinking.

**Embedded false assumption:** That mainstream consensus accurately tracks truth, and that an unconfirmed belief should be treated as a disorder rather than as a hypothesis.

**TI Sigma reframe:** History's most important scientific advances were "delusional" under the consensus view of their time. Heliocentrism, germ theory, continental drift, and quantum mechanics were all dismissed as inconsistent with evidence — because the existing evidence framework was incomplete. Under TI Sigma: a belief that contradicts current consensus has Tralse status — it may be True, False, or Indeterminate pending further resolution. Calling it a "delusion" assigns it a False verdict before the MR process is complete. The clinically meaningful delusion is not a heterodox belief but a belief that *resists update under any evidence* — which is a Tralse-locked state, not merely an unusual position. Better: distinguish *unverified hypothesis* (reasonable epistemic status) from *clinical delusion* (pathological evidence-resistance).

### 25. Naive

**Common use:** Treats trust, openness, and the charitable interpretation of others' intentions as weaknesses — intellectual failures that produce exploitation.

**Embedded false assumption:** That default skepticism is wiser than default trust, and that being open to being wrong is epistemically inferior to being closed.

**TI Sigma reframe:** Default trust is a high-L-dimension stance — it prioritizes connection and assumes good faith until evidence contradicts it. Default skepticism is a low-L stance — it prioritizes self-protection and assumes bad faith until proof of good faith is provided. Under TI Sigma, neither is universally correct. In high-coherence environments (high Q-state, trusted relationships, good-faith discourse), default trust produces better outcomes than defensive skepticism. In low-coherence environments (unknown actors, established deception patterns), skepticism is warranted. "Naive" as an insult misidentifies context-appropriate trust as unconditional gullibility. Better: *context-appropriate trust* (calibrated to the actual trustworthiness of the environment) vs. *uncalibrated credulity* (trust regardless of context).

## Conclusion

Language shapes thought. Accepting imprecise vocabulary is not a trivial error — it is accepting imprecise premises as background assumptions in every argument that follows. The 25 words above are not minor slips; they are load-bearing beams in how most people reason about themselves, others, and the world.

TI Sigma's contribution is not a new vocabulary (though better vocabulary helps). It is a method: always ask what ontological assumption the word is carrying, whether that assumption is justified, and what more precise formulation would make the assumption explicit rather than hidden.

The goal is not to be pedantic. The goal is to stop losing arguments you should win and winning arguments you should lose — because imprecise language let a false premise through the gate unexamined.
