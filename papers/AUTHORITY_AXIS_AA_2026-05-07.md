## §1 — Origin and Name

### §1.1 — Source

The Authority Axis (AA, "Double A") was named by Brandon Emerick on 2026-05-07 PM as a refinement of the 5th orthogonal TI Sigma truth-axis identified earlier the same evening in `ASYMMETRIC_SUCCESS_FAILURE_PERFORMANCE_2026-05-07.md` §13 (originally called "pragmatic-vs-epistemic certainty"). The renaming carries genuine theoretical content — it identifies *what the axis tracks* with greater precision than the original framing.

### §1.2 — Definition

**Authority Axis (AA)**: the dimension along which an agent grants **operative authority** to a proposition — the willingness to act on it, defer to it, build on it, treat it as a load-bearing premise — *independent* of whether the proposition is epistemically settled.

AA is **orthogonal** to:
- **PD real axis** (degree of trueness)
- **PD imaginary axis** (modality / DT)
- **Tralse five-valued logic** (categorical truth-state: {Nonsense, Moot, True, False, Indeterminate}; MR-gate scheme per `TI_SIGMA_FIVE_AXIS_TRUTH_RICHNESS_REVIEW_2026-05-07.md` §4, corrected per Brandon 2026-05-08; classical T/F explicitly rejected)
- **τ/δ separability** (internal calibration vs external selection from `ASYMMETRIC` §4)

A TI Sigma truth-claim's full description is therefore a **5-dimensional vector**, with AA as the 5th coordinate.

### §1.3 — The naming improvement over "pragmatic-vs-epistemic certainty"

The original §13 framing — *pragmatic-certainty vs epistemic-certainty* — described the **contrast** the axis introduces (action-enabling commitment vs absolute factual completeness) but did not name *the underlying quantity being varied*. The Authority Axis names the quantity directly: **operative authority granted**. This is a strict improvement because:

1. It identifies what is being measured (authority-grant), not just what is being contrasted.
2. It generalizes naturally to inter-agent settings (see §2), which "pragmatic certainty" did not.
3. It connects cleanly to existing literature on epistemic authority, expert deference, and trust calibration.

## §2 — Dual-Applicability: Self-Authority and Other-Authority

The original §13 framing covered only the **self-authority** case (an agent granting operative authority to its own current position). The renamed AA naturally covers **two symmetric cases**:

### §2.1 — Self-Authority (AA-self)

The agent grants operative authority to its own current understanding. High AA-self → agent acts on its own position with conviction; low AA-self → agent treats its own position as provisional, awaiting further evidence.

Example: *"I've figured out this solution"* (Brandon, §13.1) is a high-AA-self statement. The agent commits to its own framing as load-bearing, even while leaving epistemic room for revision.

### §2.2 — Other-Authority (AA-other)

The agent grants operative authority to another agent's claim, source, expert testimony, institution, lineage, or transmission. High AA-other → agent defers, builds on the other's claims, treats them as load-bearing; low AA-other → agent treats the other's claims as provisional or requires independent verification.

Examples:
- A student granting AA-other to a textbook claim while solving a problem on top of it.
- A practitioner granting AA-other to a transmission lineage (Reiki, fNIRS protocol, etc.) while building further work on top of it.
- A reader granting AA-other to a peer-reviewed publication while citing it.

### §2.3 — The Symmetry Principle

**Self-authority and other-authority operate by the same structural rules.** What governs honest AA-self calibration also governs honest AA-other calibration. The simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle (§3) applies symmetrically: an agent that grants AA-self without subconscious doubt-reservation is dogmatic about its own position; an agent that grants AA-other without subconscious doubt-reservation is dogmatic about the other's position. Both are the same failure mode, expressed on different sub-axes.

This symmetry is what makes AA a unified axis rather than two separate axes — the *quantity being varied* (operative authority granted) is the same; only the *target of the grant* differs.

## §3 — The Simultaneous-Belief-and-Doubt Principle

### §3.1 — Brandon's formulation

> *"Whether it's believing oneself or someone else, you MUST believe in what you currently are entertaining as well as leave subconscious room for doubt."*
> — Brandon Emerick, 2026-05-07 PM

This is the **operating principle** of the Authority Axis. Honest AA-calibration **requires both simultaneously**:

1. **Operative belief in the foreground** — the agent acts on, builds on, defers to the proposition.
2. **Subconscious doubt-reservation in the background** — the agent maintains an unspoken hold-open of the possibility that the proposition is wrong.

Both must be present at all times in any honest AA-grant. This is a **requirement**, not a possibility.

### §3.2 — Why both are necessary (the two failure modes)

**Failure mode 1 — Over-grant (dogmatism)**: Operative belief without subconscious doubt-reservation. Agent treats the proposition as unrevisable. Symptoms: defensiveness when challenged, refusal to update on new evidence, attribution of disagreement to bad faith. Applies symmetrically to AA-self (self-righteousness) and AA-other (uncritical deference to authority).

**Failure mode 2 — Under-grant (paralysis)**: Subconscious doubt without operative belief. Agent treats the proposition as too provisional to act on. Symptoms: chronic deferral of decisions, infinite-regress demands for further evidence, inability to build on partial conclusions. Applies symmetrically to AA-self (chronic self-doubt) and AA-other (chronic skepticism preventing learning).

The simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle is the **knife-edge between these two failure modes**. Both registers must be active simultaneously.

### §3.3 — The two-register architecture (cognitive plausibility)

The principle is cognitively plausible because **belief and doubt operate on different cognitive registers**:

- **Foreground operative register**: drives action, deployment of cognitive resources, behavioral commitment. This is where AA-belief lives.
- **Background reservation register**: maintains the hold-open of alternative possibilities, monitors for disconfirming evidence, preserves revision-readiness. This is where AA-doubt lives.

That these registers can run simultaneously without contradiction is precisely what `ASYMMETRIC` §13.2 established for the simultaneous hold of P1=*"I've figured it out"* + P2=*"something is missing"* — they live on different axes (here, different registers within the same axis) and are therefore not classically contradictory.

### §3.4 — Connection to the τ/δ separability of `ASYMMETRIC` §4

The two-register architecture of AA mirrors the two-channel architecture of τ/δ separability:

| `ASYMMETRIC` §4 | AA (this paper) |
|---|---|
| τ-channel: internal calibration (no false claims) | Background register: doubt-reservation |
| δ-channel: external selection (which subset of true facts to present) | Foreground register: operative belief |

Both architectures resolve apparent contradictions by recognizing that the conflicting demands operate on **separable channels/registers**, not on a single shared axis. This is itself an instance of the axis-addition heuristic of `ASYMMETRIC` §11.3 applied at the cognitive-architecture level.

## §4 — Connections to Existing Literature

The simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle is well-established philosophical territory; the contribution of this paper is the **TI Sigma formalization** (axis identification + symmetry across self/other + register-architecture mapping), not the underlying insight.

- **Fallibilism (Peirce, Popper)**: hold beliefs operatively while remaining open to revision. This is exactly the simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle, formulated within the foreground register only.
- **Epistemic humility (Socratic tradition)**: *"I know that I know nothing"* operates as a background register reservation that does not prevent operative engagement with any specific question.
- **Trust calibration literature (Lee & See 2004, Hoff & Bashir 2015)**: in human-automation and human-AI trust, calibrated trust = trust matched to actual capability. AA-other formalizes the "calibrated" requirement as the simultaneous foreground-grant + background-doubt-reservation.
- **Bayesian epistemology**: a posterior of *p < 1* is structurally an AA grant with subconscious doubt-reservation = (1−p). The TI Sigma formulation does not require the doubt to be quantified, only present.
- **Buddhist "beginner's mind" (shoshin)**: granting operative authority to current understanding while preserving openness to it being wrong — direct mapping to the simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle.
- **Authority and deference in social epistemology (Hardwig 1985, Goldman 2001)**: when is it rational to defer to expert testimony? AA-other formalizes the structural requirement (calibrated grant + doubt-reservation) without requiring a specific decision rule for the calibration value.

What is **TI-Sigma-novel** in this paper:
1. The identification of authority-grant as the underlying quantity (not just the contrast it introduces).
2. The unification of self-authority and other-authority as one axis with two sub-dimensions sharing the same operating principle.
3. The mapping of the simultaneous-belief-and-doubt principle to the τ/δ separability of `ASYMMETRIC` §4 via a shared two-register / two-channel architecture.
4. The formal-orthogonality justification of AA against the four pre-existing TI Sigma truth-axes (PD-real, PD-imaginary, tralse five-valued, τ/δ).

## §5 — Worked Examples

### §5.1 — Self-authority, calibrated

Brandon's claim *"I've figured out this solution"* (the §13 originating quote) is a high-AA-self grant with explicit subconscious doubt-reservation: *"There's probably something I'm still missing."* Both registers active. Honest AA-self calibration. Enables iteration (§13.3.1 inter-iterational progress) without dogmatism.

### §5.2 — Self-authority, over-grant (failure)

A theorist who treats their own framework as unrevisable and attributes all challenges to misunderstanding. AA-self foreground at maximum, background doubt-reservation absent. Failure mode 1 (dogmatism). Common in cult-leader pathology, academic territorialism, and over-fitted single-author theoretical work (#69 self-flag for TI Sigma itself: this is a known risk of single-author research programs and is partially mitigated here by explicit doubt-reservation discipline).

### §5.3 — Self-authority, under-grant (failure)

A theorist who refuses to commit to any of their own positions pending "more evidence" indefinitely. AA-self background doubt-reservation at maximum, foreground operative belief absent. Failure mode 2 (paralysis). Prevents iteration entirely.

### §5.4 — Other-authority, calibrated

A practitioner reading a peer-reviewed neuroscience paper, granting operative AA-other (citing it, building on it, deferring on technical points outside their expertise) while maintaining background doubt-reservation (recognizing the replication crisis, p-hacking risk, paradigm-dependence). Both registers active. Enables productive use of others' work without uncritical deference.

### §5.5 — Other-authority, over-grant (failure)

A practitioner who treats a single transmission (a guru, a textbook, a single paper) as unrevisable. AA-other foreground at maximum, background doubt-reservation absent. Failure mode 1 (dogmatism). Common in cult-membership pathology, citation-cargo-culting, and uncritical institutional deference.

### §5.6 — Other-authority, under-grant (failure)

A practitioner who refuses to learn from any external source until they have personally derived it from first principles. AA-other background doubt-reservation at maximum, foreground operative belief absent. Failure mode 2 (paralysis). Prevents productive use of cumulative human knowledge.

## §7 — Open Questions

1. **Quantification**: AA is presented as a one-dimensional axis but no measurement scale is specified. Should AA-grants be quantified (e.g., 0-1, with calibration against base rates) or remain qualitative? Bayesian epistemology suggests the former; phenomenological practice suggests the latter is often sufficient.

2. **AA-self vs AA-other coupling**: do agents tend to over- or under-grant AA-self and AA-other in correlated ways, or are the two sub-dimensions independently regulated? Empirical question.

3. **Trust networks and AA-other propagation**: when agent A grants AA-other to agent B, who grants AA-other to agent C, does A's effective AA-other grant to C inherit any structural properties from the chain? (Connection to social epistemology and PageRank-like trust propagation.)

4. **AA and the τ/δ separability**: §3.4 maps AA's two-register architecture to τ/δ's two-channel architecture. Are AA and τ/δ genuinely orthogonal axes, or is AA a special case of τ/δ at the cognitive-internal level? This deserves separate analysis. **Tentative claim**: they are orthogonal because τ/δ governs *what an agent presents externally* while AA governs *what an agent treats as load-bearing internally* — different operating domains.

5. **AA dynamics under iteration**: how does AA-self update across iterations of additive theory-growth (per §13.3.1)? Does honest AA-self grant grow monotonically with iteration count under the inter-iterational progress claim, or does it require periodic recalibration via doubt-register-driven challenges?
