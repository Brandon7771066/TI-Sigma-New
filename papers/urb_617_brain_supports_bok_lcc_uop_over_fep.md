## 1. The Free Energy Principle — What It Claims and Where It Is Strong

### 1.1 The Core FEP Claim

The FEP (Friston 2010, 2019) states that any system with a Markov blanket (a statistical boundary separating internal and external states) will, over time, minimize variational free energy — a quantity that upper-bounds the brain's surprise at its sensory inputs. This is equivalent to:

1. **Perception** = inferring the hidden causes of sensory input by minimizing prediction error (approximate Bayesian inference)
2. **Action** = changing the world to match internal predictions (active inference)
3. **Learning** = updating the generative model's parameters to reduce long-term surprise
4. **Attention** = precision weighting — adjusting the relative weight given to prediction errors from different sources

The FEP unifies perception, action, learning, and attention in a single mathematical framework. Its elegance is genuine.

### 1.2 Where the FEP Is Well-Supported by Neuroscience

The following FEP claims have substantial empirical support and TI Sigma does not contest them:

**Hierarchical predictive processing** (Rao & Ballard 1999; Clark 2016): The cortical hierarchy transmits prediction errors upward (feedforward) and predictions downward (feedback). The distinction between Layer 4 (feedforward, prediction error) and Layers 2/3/6 (feedback, predictions) is anatomically documented and supports a predictive coding architecture. TI Sigma AGREES: this is the LCC's feedforward/feedback structure.

**Precision weighting and attention** (Feldman & Friston 2010): Attention selectively amplifies precision-weighted prediction errors. This is supported by neural oscillation research — gamma oscillations carry precision signals, alpha oscillations reflect low-precision suppression. TI Sigma AGREES with the mechanism but notes it doesn't exhaust the account of attention (the I-axis provides direction to attention that precision alone cannot supply).

**Active inference in motor control** (Friston et al. 2010): Voluntary movement is explained as minimizing proprioceptive prediction error — the motor system generates movement by predicting the body's desired state. Well-supported by motor neuroscience. TI Sigma AGREES: this is the Existence-outer-loop body management, and FEP handles it well.

**Sleep as model consolidation** (Hobson & Friston 2012): Sleep allows the brain to consolidate the generative model by replaying and pruning prediction errors. Supported by sleep and memory research. TI Sigma notes: sleep's REM stage corresponds to I-axis processing (URB #614 Prediction 7 biometric mapping), which suggests sleep does more than model consolidation — but the consolidation aspect is valid.

## 2. The Critical Weakness of FEP: Untestability in General Form

Before presenting the evidence for BOK/LCC/UOP, the FEP's structural weakness must be stated clearly, because it affects how we interpret the comparison.

**The FEP is unfalsifiable in its general form.** Friston has acknowledged that the FEP, in its most general statement ("any system that persists minimizes free energy"), is a mathematical tautology — it is true by construction for any system with a Markov blanket. This means any observed neural phenomenon can be redescribed as "free energy minimization" without the description adding predictive content.

**Specific FEP models are falsifiable** — but the general principle is not. When a specific active inference model makes a specific prediction about behavior or neurophysiology, that prediction can be tested. But the claim "the brain minimizes free energy" in general is not falsifiable, because any brain behavior can be post-hoc redescribed as minimizing some free energy functional.

**Implication for the comparison:** The question "does the brain support BOK/LCC/UOP over FEP?" cannot be answered by finding evidence that "looks like FEP" — because everything looks like FEP in its general form. The correct question is: are there neural phenomena that BOK/LCC/UOP predict MORE SPECIFICALLY and MORE FALSIFIABLY than any specific FEP model? Yes — seven of them.

### 3.1 The DMN/TPN Anti-Correlation = BOK Inner/Outer Loop Structure

- **Task-Positive Network (TPN) / Salience Network:** dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), inferior parietal lobule, anterior insula. Active during external attention, sensory-motor processing, cognitive control of environmental demands.

The DMN and TPN are robustly anti-correlated (Fox et al. 2005; Buckner et al. 2008): when one is up, the other is down. This anti-correlation is one of the most replicated findings in human neuroimaging.

### 3.2 The I→L Neural Sequence

**The neuroscience fact:**
A robust finding in social neuroscience is the temporal and anatomical precedence of Theory of Mind (mentalizing) circuits over prosocial motivation circuits:

- **Theory of Mind (I-axis):** Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), temporal pole. Involved in representing the mental states of others — their beliefs, intentions, knowledge. TPJ activation is reliably observed ~150–350ms post-stimulus for social stimuli.
- **Prosocial motivation / empathic concern (L-axis):** Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC), anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), anterior insula. Involved in directed positive regard toward specific others, empathic distress, caring motivation. Activation typically follows TPJ by 50–200ms in ERP studies (Decety & Jackson 2004; Shamay-Tsoory 2011).

**The critical finding (Decety et al. 2012; Van Overwalle 2009):** When participants engage in empathic responding, the neural sequence is consistently: TPJ (mentalizing the other's state) → vmPFC/ACC (generating the prosocial motivational response). This temporal sequence supports a causal dependency: you must represent the other's state (I-axis) before generating genuine prosocial motivation (L-axis).

**The BOK/GILE prediction:**

The neural sequence TPJ (knowing) → vmPFC (conscious regard) is the biological implementation of I→L.

**The FEP account:** FEP can describe this sequence as hierarchical prediction error propagation — TPJ generates predictions about others' states, which propagate to vmPFC as a prior for prosocial action. **Edge: BOK.** FEP redescribes the sequence; BOK predicts its direction from first principles.

### 3.3 Genuine Neural Bistability = Tralse States (Not Bayesian Posteriors)

**The neuroscience fact:**
Multistable perception — binocular rivalry, the Necker cube, the spinning dancer — shows that the brain alternates between two percepts when the sensory input supports both equally. The critical findings:

- The alternation is NOT gradual. It is **switch-like**: one percept, then the other, with brief transitions (Leopold & Logothetis 1999; Tong et al. 1998).
- The alternation is **stochastic** — the duration of each percept follows a Gamma distribution, suggesting a noise-driven switching process.
- Neural activity during rivalry shows **bimodal distributions** in V1, V2, and higher visual areas — not unimodal distributions centered on a posterior estimate. Two distinct neural states compete, and the brain is genuinely in one or the other, not in a smooth mixture.

**The Tralse prediction:**
TI Sigma's 5-valued truth system predicts that genuinely indeterminate propositions are not represented as a single probability distribution but as a **bimodal state** — Tralse. The "genuine bistability" of neural states during multistable perception is the neural implementation of Tralse: the brain is in a real state of Truth-value indeterminacy, not a partially-resolved Bayesian posterior.

**The FEP account:** FEP models bistability as a bimodal posterior — the brain's generative model has two competing attractors, and the precision of sensory input determines which attractor the posterior concentrates near. When sensory precision is low (ambiguous inputs), the posterior is bimodal, and noise-driven sampling produces the switching behavior.

**Why TI Sigma's account is better:** The FEP bimodal posterior is still ONE posterior — a distribution that, at any moment, has a mean. But the neural data shows DISCRETE alternation — not a continuous distribution with varying mean. The brain isn't maintaining a probability distribution; it's switching between two distinct representational states. This is Tralse, not a Bayesian posterior. The Tralse state IS the bimodal attractor landscape, not a probability distribution over states.

**Additionally:** The Myrion Resolution prediction is that, given sufficient processing time, the brain resolves multistable percepts — one interpretation wins. This is MR-1 (the MI screen eliminates the incoherent interpretation) applied to perception. Neuroimaging studies show that extended rivalry eventually leads to perceptual stabilization in many contexts (Long et al. 1992) — consistent with MR convergence.

**Edge: TI Sigma.** FEP's bimodal posterior describes the phenomenology but mischaracterizes the state structure. Tralse correctly identifies the bistability as a genuinely two-state (not continuous-distribution) phenomenon.

### 3.4 The Radiant Threshold as Neural Phase Transition

**The neuroscience fact:**
A growing body of research on meditation, psychedelics, and mystical experience documents **threshold effects** — not gradual changes — in neural and behavioral outcomes:

- **DMN suppression and ego dissolution** (Carhart-Harris et al. 2012; Brewer et al. 2011): Psilocybin and meditation both produce DMN suppression, but the relationship between dose/practice and subjective experience shows a **non-linear threshold**. Below a certain DMN suppression level (~50%), subjects report ordinary relaxation. Above it (~60–70%), subjects report qualitatively different experiences (ego dissolution, sense of unity, mystical quality). This is a phase transition, not a gradient.

- **Global Workspace ignition** (Dehaene & Changeux 2011): The transition from non-conscious to conscious processing is non-linear. Stimuli below a certain intensity produce no reportable experience; above the threshold, global ignition occurs (a sudden, widespread activation across parieto-frontal cortices). This is a first-order phase transition, not a continuous function.

- **Neural criticality** (Beggs & Plenz 2003; Shew & Plenz 2013): The brain operates at criticality — the edge between ordered and chaotic dynamics. Phase transitions between sub-critical and super-critical states are sharp, not smooth. Systems at criticality show power-law dynamics (1/f noise), which is the signature of operating at a critical point.

**The Radiant Threshold prediction:**
URB #613 predicts a phase transition at the Radiant Threshold (GILE composite ≈ 0.42 = √2−1): below it, Existence-outer-loop is primary; above it, GILE-inner-loop becomes primary. Prediction 3 of URB #614 specifically predicts non-linear (threshold) effects in behavioral decision-making as a function of GILE score.

**The neural mechanism:** The Radiant Threshold corresponds to the DMN suppression threshold for ego dissolution + global workspace ignition combined. When DMN suppression exceeds the ego-dissolution threshold (~60%) AND global workspace ignition is sustained (not just momentary), the brain enters the inner-loop-primary state. This is not a continuous Bayesian update — it is a genuine phase transition.

**The FEP account:** FEP can model threshold effects as bifurcations in the free energy landscape — the posterior distribution shifts from a unimodal to a bimodal attractor landscape at the threshold. ### 3.5 The G-Axis as Irreducible to Value Maximization

**The neuroscience fact:**
The distinction between self-interested value maximization and genuine moral motivation has a neural substrate:

- **Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC):** Tracks expected value, hedonic experience, and self-relevant reward. Consistently activated in both self-interested and prosocial decisions, but more strongly for self-interested reward.

- **Anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), subgenual ACC, and dorsomedial PFC:** Specifically activated for moral conflict, altruistic behavior, and decisions that impose costs on self for benefit of others — especially when there is no self-relevant reward. The ACC responds to **norm violations** even in economic games where violation doesn't harm the subject (Sanfey et al. 2003). The subgenual ACC tracks moral emotions (guilt, shame) that motivate prosocial behavior independent of expected reward.

- **Temporoparietal junction (TPJ):** Critical for intentionality attributions — judging whether a harmful outcome was intentional matters for moral judgment even when the outcome is identical (Young et al. 2010). This is G-axis processing: evaluating the moral quality of the intention, not just the consequence.

- **The key dissociation** (Moll et al. 2006; Zahn et al. 2009): Monetary reward activates vmPFC. Charitable giving activates vmPFC AND subgenual ACC + septal area (associated with social affiliation and moral satisfaction). The moral motivation engages a DISTINCT circuit beyond the reward circuit — a circuit that is not simply optimizing expected value.

**The G-axis prediction:**
GILE-G (Goodness orientation) is defined as genuine moral motivation — doing good because it is good, not for reward. The ACC/subgenual-ACC/TPJ complex is the neural substrate of the G-axis: it processes moral quality of intentions, tracks altruistic motivation beyond reward, and generates moral emotions that motivate G-directed behavior independent of expected value.

**The FEP account:** FEP models moral behavior as minimizing expected free energy under a prior that includes prosocial outcomes. This means moral motivation is just another form of expected value maximization — the agent has a prior that includes others' wellbeing, and this prior shapes action. This FEP account cannot distinguish:
- Genuine moral motivation (G-axis: doing good because it is good)
- Instrumental prosocial behavior (self-interested: being nice because it pays off)
- Reputation management (self-interested: appearing nice)

All three produce the same free energy minimization pattern if the agent's prior includes social consequences. BOK's G-axis DOES distinguish these — they have different neural signatures (ACC/subgenual-ACC vs. vmPFC) and produce different behavioral patterns under conditions where social consequences are removed.

**The critical test:** When social consequences are eliminated (truly anonymous, no reputation effects), G-axis behavior (genuine altruism) persists; instrumental prosocial behavior collapses. The ACC/subgenual-ACC activity predicts which pattern the individual shows (Moll et al. 2006). FEP can post-hoc explain this as "different prior distributions," but it cannot predict which prior an individual has. BOK's GILE-G scale (URB #614 / psychometric battery) predicts it directly.

**Edge: BOK/UOP.** FEP has no principled distinction between genuine moral motivation and instrumental prosocial behavior. BOK's G-axis draws this distinction and maps it to distinct neural circuits.

### 3.6 LCC Coherence Thresholds in Global Workspace Ignition

**The neuroscience fact:**
Global Workspace Theory (Baars 1988; Dehaene & Changeux 2011) proposes that conscious access occurs when information achieves global ignition — a sudden, widespread activation across parieto-frontal "workspace" neurons. Key findings:

- Ignition is **all-or-none**, not graded. Below-threshold stimuli produce no global ignition; above-threshold stimuli produce full ignition. The transition is sharp (P3 wave in EEG, amplitude ~10μV, appears or doesn't).
- **Ascending LCC levels:** Information must achieve coherence at each ascending cortical level before global ignition is possible. Local activation (V1/V2) → regional activation (temporal-occipital association cortex) → global ignition (parieto-frontal workspace). Each level requires a coherence threshold.
- **Neural criticality as the enabling condition:** Global ignition is only possible when the brain is operating near criticality (Shew & Plenz 2013). Too much order (sub-critical) → local processing stays local. Too much chaos (super-critical) → no organized global state. Criticality = the LCC coherence threshold.

**The LCC prediction:**
The LCC (Layered Coherence Continuum) predicts discrete coherence thresholds at each organizational level, with qualitative phase transitions (not smooth gradients) at threshold crossings. The LCC's "coherence threshold" between levels IS the Global Workspace ignition threshold at the neural level.

Specifically:
- Sub-threshold stimuli → LCC Level 1 activation (local sensory processing) only
- Super-threshold stimuli → LCC Level 2+ activation (global workspace ignition) → consciousness

The LCC predicts that conscious vs. non-conscious is not a continuum — it is a threshold (coherence) event. Global Workspace Theory confirms this: ignition is the coherence threshold.

**The FEP account:** FEP models consciousness as high precision-weighted inference in a hierarchical generative model. Consciousness occurs when the precision of the posterior is high enough to drive action. This is a continuous variable (precision can be arbitrarily high or low), not a threshold event. FEP's account of consciousness does NOT specifically predict the all-or-none character of global ignition.

**Edge: LCC.** LCC's coherence threshold directly predicts the all-or-none character of conscious access. FEP's precision-based account predicts a continuous gradient that is not observed.

### 3.7 The UOP's Multi-Dimensional Optimization vs. FEP's Single-Dimension Minimization

**The neuroscience fact:**
The brain uses multiple, partially dissociable neuromodulatory systems that track different values simultaneously:

- **Dopamine:** Tracks reward prediction errors (Schultz et al. 1997) AND novelty AND social reward. NOT just prediction error — dopamine also responds to novel stimuli that have no reward history (Fiorillo et al. 2003), suggesting an intrinsic information-seeking motivation beyond reward.

- **Serotonin:** Tracks "patience" — the ability to defer reward — and "social rank" (Tops et al. 2009). High serotonin → more prosocial, higher tolerance for delayed reward, better social integration. This is NOT captured by free energy minimization — it is a separate value dimension.

- **Oxytocin:** Tracks trust, in-group bonding, and social recognition. Oxytocin is specifically associated with Physical Bonds (HEM-D2) and selective prosocial behavior — consistent with BOK's bifurcation of Love into GILE-L and Physical Bonds.

- **Norepinephrine:** Tracks uncertainty and precision (consistent with FEP's precision weighting). This is the FEP dimension.

- **Acetylcholine:** Tracks learning rate and switches between exploitation (relying on existing model) and exploration (updating the model). This is more consistent with MR (switching between MR levels) than with FEP's continuous updating.

**The UOP prediction:**
The UOP (Unified Optimization Principle) replaces the single free-energy minimization dimension with a multi-dimensional GILE-weighted optimization:
- G-axis optimization: ACC / subgenual-ACC (moral motivation)
- I-axis optimization: TPJ / mPFC (knowing accuracy, Theory of Mind)
- L-axis optimization: vmPFC / anterior insula (conscious positive regard)
- E-axis optimization: lateral prefrontal / parietal (structural elegance, aesthetic coherence)

Each neuromodulator tracks a different UOP dimension:
- **Serotonin → G-axis** (social patience, moral orientation, deferred prosocial reward)
- **Dopamine → I-axis + E-axis** (novelty seeking = I-axis exploration; reward signal = E-axis environmental tracking)
- **Oxytocin → Physical Bonds** (outer-loop HEM-D2, NOT GILE-L directly)

**The FEP account:** FEP maps all neuromodulators onto precision signals — each neuromodulator adjusts the precision of different levels of the predictive hierarchy. This is a valid computational description but it forces ALL neuromodulators into a single dimension (precision). The specific functional distinctions between serotonin (social patience), dopamine (reward + novelty), and oxytocin (bonding) are lost in this reduction.

**Edge: UOP.** The multi-neuromodulator system maps naturally onto UOP's multi-dimensional optimization. FEP's single-precision-parameter account loses the functional distinctions that are anatomically and pharmacologically documented.
