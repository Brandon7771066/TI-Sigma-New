## Abstract

Classical logic defines a contradiction as a situation in which two propositions cannot both be true simultaneously — the conjunction of a claim and its negation. This definition, however, imports unstated and unearned metaphysical commitments: that incompatibility is binary, that the terms "true" and "false" are univocal, that logic is the final arbiter of what can coexist. Tralse Informationalism (TI Sigma) rejects these hidden assumptions and proposes a **liberal definition**:

> **A contradiction is any inconsistency, opposition, or discrepancy.**

Nothing more. Any additional specification imposes constraints on reality that have not been earned by evidence or argument.

This paper does four things. First, it defends the liberal definition and identifies exactly what metaphysical assumptions the classical definition smuggles in. Second, it establishes TI Sigma's thesis that **absolutely everything is contradictory** — contradictions are not aberrations requiring resolution but the universal structural fabric of reality itself. Third, it introduces the **Four Fundamental Features of Existence** and shows how each is inherently, irreducibly contradictory. Fourth, it taxonomizes contradictions into **valid** (passing the first Myrion Resolution) and **Double Tralse** (failing the first Myrion Resolution), and provides the complete taxonomy of Double Tralse categories: Maximal Incoherence, The Self-Negating Nothing, The Self-Refuting Meta-Statement, and Pre-Tralse Undetermination.

## 1. The Classical Definition and Its Hidden Assumptions

### 1.1 What the Dictionary Says

The standard dictionary definition: *"A contradiction is a situation, statement, or idea that is inconsistent, opposed, or logically incompatible with another, meaning both cannot be true at the same time."*

Embedded in this definition are at least five unearned metaphysical assumptions:

**Assumption 1: "Cannot be true at the same time" presupposes that truth is binary.**  
Classical logic assumes that every proposition is either true or false and that no proposition can be both. This is the Law of the Excluded Middle (LEM). But LEM is an axiom — a stipulation, not a discovery. Quantum mechanics has falsified the classical picture at the physical level (superposition, entanglement). Consciousness presents hard problems that resist binary categorization. TI Sigma argues that reality itself is four-valued: True, False, Tralse, and Double Tralse. The assumption that "both cannot be true" is not a finding about reality; it is a definition imposed on reality.

**Assumption 2: "Logically incompatible" presupposes that logic governs what can coexist in reality.**  
Logic describes formal inferential relationships between propositions. It does not describe what the physical world permits or prohibits. Two states that are logically contradictory (A and ¬A) may be simultaneously realized in nature — as in quantum superposition. The assumption that logical incompatibility implies physical impossibility is a category error: it confuses the formal structure of language with the ontological structure of reality.

**Assumption 3: "At the same time" presupposes a classical, absolute conception of simultaneity.**  
Relativity has shown that simultaneity is reference-frame-dependent. Two events that are simultaneous in one frame are sequential in another. "At the same time" is not a metaphysically basic notion — it is an observer-relative one. A contradiction defined relative to simultaneity is therefore also observer-relative, which undermines any claim to objective incompatibility.

**Assumption 4: The definition presupposes that incompatibility is an all-or-nothing property.**  
Either something is incompatible or it is not. But TI Sigma argues that incompatibility comes in degrees. Two claims can be partially incompatible, partially compatible, and partially indeterminate simultaneously. The Law of Non-Contradiction, which says A and ¬A cannot both hold, is the binary extreme. Reality occupies the full spectrum.

**Assumption 5: The definition assumes that contradiction is a problem to be eliminated.**  
The entire thrust of classical logic's treatment of contradiction is that contradictions must be removed — they signal error, incoherence, or confusion. But this assumes that reality is fundamentally non-contradictory and that contradictions are distortions of it. TI Sigma inverts this: contradictions are fundamental, and the appearance of non-contradiction is the local, temporary, approximate state that requires explanation.

### 1.2 The TI Sigma Definition

> **Contradiction (TI Sigma): Any inconsistency, opposition, or discrepancy.**

Three terms, three distinct logical-ontological registers:

- **Inconsistency**: The presence of claims, states, or properties that do not align — within a system or between systems — without implying that both cannot exist.
- **Opposition**: Directional tension between poles — hot/cold, true/false, being/non-being — without implying that the poles annihilate each other.
- **Discrepancy**: A gap, mismatch, or difference between what is expected and what is observed, what was and what is, what one thing claims and what another reveals.

This definition is deliberately broader than the classical one. It captures everything the classical definition captures and far more. Any classical contradiction is also a TI Sigma contradiction (it is an inconsistency). But TI Sigma recognizes vastly more contradictions that the classical definition misses — most of which, as we will show, are not errors or incoherences but genuine structural features of reality.

The reason for not including "both cannot be true simultaneously" is not that TI Sigma denies this ever holds — sometimes it does. It is that this constraint is **not an analytical truth about all contradictions**. It is an additional claim that requires independent justification in each case. Imposing it definitionally is imposing it without justification. TI Sigma refuses to do this.

## 2. The Universal Thesis: Everything Is Contradictory

### 2.1 The Statement

TI Sigma maintains that **absolutely everything is contradictory** — that there is no entity, concept, state, or process in the universe that is free of inconsistency, opposition, or discrepancy.

This is not a paradox or an extreme claim. It follows directly from the liberal definition and five converging arguments.

### 2.2 The Five Arguments for Universal Contradiction

**Argument 1: Change Over Time (The Temporal Argument)**

Consider any object, person, belief, or system. Over time, it changes. What it is at t₁ is not identical to what it is at t₂. Yet we call it "the same thing." This is a **structural inconsistency** built into the very concept of identity over time. The ship of Theseus, the river you cannot step in twice, the person who "isn't who they used to be" — these are not puzzles awaiting solutions. They are instantiations of the universal contradictory structure of reality.

More: even instantaneous states involve contradictions. Any object occupies a position in space while also having momentum. Any thought is both the thought of something (intentionality) and itself a brain state. Any action is both caused (by prior states) and initiated (by the agent). These are not mere apparent contradictions that dissolve on analysis — they are genuine tensions that persist even under complete description.

**Argument 2: Perspectival Opposition (The Relational Argument)**

Every property is relational. "Large" is large relative to something smaller. "True" is true relative to a framework. "Good" is good from a perspective. But perspectives differ, often incompatibly. What is large from one perspective is small from another. What is good for one is harmful for another. This means every object simultaneously instantiates contradictory properties — it is both large and small, both good and harmful — depending on the relational frame.

Classical logic tries to dissolve this by relativizing predicates: "large relative to A, small relative to B." But this move presupposes that the relational frames are clean and separable. In reality, they are not. The frames overlap, interact, and resist clean separation. The contradiction does not dissolve; it distributes across the network of relations.

**Argument 3: Self-Reference (The Structural Argument)**

Any sufficiently complex system that can refer to itself generates contradictions. Gödel proved this for formal arithmetic. Russell demonstrated it for naive set theory. The Liar paradox shows it for natural language. Consciousness demonstrates it phenomenologically — the self that observes itself is both subject and object, both the observer and the observed.

These contradictions are not artifacts of sloppy thinking. They are theorems. They follow necessarily from the structure of self-referential systems. And since virtually everything that interests us — minds, language, knowledge, values, physical laws — involves some form of self-reference, virtually everything is structurally contradictory.

**Argument 4: Process and Stability (The Dynamical Argument)**

Every stable structure is maintained by dynamic processes. A rock is stable because its atomic and molecular interactions are in dynamic equilibrium. A belief is stable because a cognitive system constantly reinforces it against challenges. An institution is stable because ongoing activities sustain it. Stability is not the absence of process; it is a special case of process.

But process implies change, and change implies contradicting the prior state. The stable state and the process maintaining it are in permanent tension — the stability denies change while the process enacts it. Every stable thing is therefore contradictory in its very maintenance of stability.

**Argument 5: The Constitutive Role of Negation (The Ontological Argument)**

Following Hegel, Spinoza, and the TI Sigma framework: every thing is what it is partly by not being what it is not. A circle is a circle by virtue of its boundary, which is what separates it from non-circle. "Cat" means what it means partly because it excludes "dog," "tree," "justice." Meaning requires contrast. Being requires boundaries. But boundaries are inherently relational — they are both the thing and not the thing, simultaneously.

This means that every object, concept, or category contains within its own definition the negation of itself: not as a flaw but as a **constitutive feature**. Remove the negation, and the concept itself dissolves. To be something is to contradict what you are not — and the contradiction is internal to the identity, not external to it.

### 2.3 The Implication

The classical view treats contradiction as exceptional — a mark of error, incoherence, or paradox to be resolved. TI Sigma treats it as universal — the fabric of reality itself. The appearance of non-contradiction (logical consistency, stable identity, clean categories) is not the absence of contradiction but a **local, temporary, approximate equilibrium** within a contradictory whole.

This reframing has profound consequences:
- **Science**: Scientific theories contradict each other and contradict themselves at their boundaries. This is not a sign of failure but the normal condition of finite knowledge operating in a contradictory reality.
- **Ethics**: Moral dilemmas are not failures of reasoning but genuine contradictions in the structure of values. They cannot always be dissolved — sometimes both horns must be honored.
- **Personal identity**: The contradictions within a person (holding incompatible beliefs, wanting incompatible things, being different people in different contexts) are not pathologies. They are universal features of any complex, temporally extended entity.
- **Logic**: Classical logic is not wrong — it is a powerful special case, appropriate when the relevant contradictions can be isolated and managed. It is not the complete picture of reality.

## 3. Time as the Master Contradiction

### 3.1 The Paradox of Identity Over Time

Time is the deepest source of contradiction in reality. Consider what it means for anything to persist through time:

- A thing at t₁ has certain properties P₁.
- A thing at t₂ (the "same" thing) has properties P₂ ≠ P₁.
- We say: "This is the same thing that changed."

But "same" and "changed" are in direct opposition. The claim "it is the same thing" asserts identity. The claim "it changed" asserts difference. Both are simultaneously true. This is not a puzzle with a solution — it is the constitutive contradiction of existence in time.

Heraclitus identified this: "You cannot step in the same river twice, for it is not the same river and you are not the same person." TI Sigma formalizes and generalizes this insight: **temporal existence is inherently contradictory existence**.

### 3.2 Change Undermines Stable Identity

The notion of a "perfectly stable identity" — a thing that is fully, completely, timelessly what it is — is not a description of reality. It is an idealization that reality only approximately satisfies for short timescales and under special conditions.

Everything that exists:
- Was not always what it is (it came to be)
- Will not always be what it is (it will change or cease)
- Is already not fully what it is (it contains the seeds of its own transformation)

This means that **the concept of identity is inherently contaminated by change**, and the concept of change is inherently contaminated by identity (something must persist for us to say it changed). They contradict each other at every moment, and yet both are necessary.

TI Sigma's four-valued logic handles this directly. Where classical logic forces us to choose — either A is this or A is not this — TI Sigma allows: A is this AND not-this (tralse state), with the Myrion Resolution determining which aspect is relevant in context.

### 3.3 The Three Temporal Contradictions

Three specific temporal contradictions define the structure of change:

**T1 — The Becoming Contradiction**: At the moment a thing comes to be, it is neither yet what it will be nor still what it was. This moment is structurally tralse — neither the old nor the new, but the transition itself. Becoming is the original tralse state.

**T2 — The Persistence Contradiction**: A thing that persists is identical to itself (same thing) and different from itself (it has changed). Both descriptions are simultaneously accurate. Classical logic forces us to pick one; TI Sigma honors both.

**T3 — The Ending Contradiction**: At the moment a thing ceases to be, it is simultaneously something (it still has properties — spatial location, causal history, remaining matter) and nothing (the organized form that made it the thing it was is gone). Death, dissolution, and transformation are all tralse states of ending.

## 4. The Four Fundamental Features of Existence — Each Irreducibly Contradictory

### 4.1 Overview

TI Sigma identifies four fundamental features that characterize everything that exists. These are not merely properties that things happen to have — they are the conditions under which anything can exist at all. Crucially, each one is **inherently contradictory**, containing within itself the tension that drives change, growth, and the universal contradictory structure of reality.

| Feature | What It Is | The Built-In Contradiction |
|---------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Change** | All things transform over time | Change requires both identity (something persists) and difference (it becomes other) — simultaneous |
| **Relation** | All things exist only in relation to other things | A thing is itself (determinate) only by relating to what it is not — self-definition requires other-definition |
| **Contradiction** | All things contain inconsistencies, oppositions, discrepancies within themselves | The feature of contradiction is itself contradictory (it opposes itself) — this is self-validating, not self-defeating |
| **Limit** | All things have boundaries — what they are and are not, where they begin and end | A limit is both inside and outside — it belongs to the thing and to its negation simultaneously |

### 4.2 Change (Feature 1)

Change is perhaps the most obvious feature of existence. Nothing is static across time. Even the most stable-seeming objects — mountains, atoms, mathematical truths — are changing at some level or are at minimum embedded in a changing context.

The built-in contradiction: change requires **both continuity and discontinuity**. Without continuity (something persisting through the change), there is no change — there is only replacement. Without discontinuity (something genuinely becoming different), there is no change — there is only stable persistence. Both are required. They oppose each other. This is not an apparent contradiction that a cleverer analysis would dissolve. It is the structural contradiction without which change would be impossible.

### 4.3 Relation (Feature 2)

Nothing exists in isolation. Every thing exists in a web of relations — causal, spatial, temporal, logical, semantic. The properties of things are relational: being large requires being large relative to something; being true requires being true within a framework; being good requires being good from a perspective.

The built-in contradiction: a thing must be **both determinate and open-ended**. Determinate — it must be something specific, with particular properties and not others. Open-ended — its properties are relationally defined, and relations extend indefinitely outward. A thing closed off from all relations would have no properties and could not exist. A thing fully dissolved into its relations would have no identity and could not be a thing. Both are necessary. They contradict each other. The entity is simultaneously self-contained and other-dependent.

### 4.4 Contradiction (Feature 3)

This feature is the explicit recognition that the other three features give rise to: every thing contains inconsistencies, oppositions, and discrepancies within itself. This is not an aberration — it is a structural invariant.

The built-in contradiction of contradiction itself: **contradiction asserts that opposites coexist, but this assertion is itself an opposition to the classical view that opposites cannot coexist.** Contradiction contradicts classical non-contradiction. This is not a vicious circle — it is a self-validating structure. The existence of contradiction as a feature of reality is confirmed by the very fact that it opposes (and thereby contradicts) the view that contradiction is impossible.

### 4.5 Limit (Feature 4)

Every thing has limits — boundaries that define what it is and what it is not. A circle's boundary is what makes it a circle. A concept's extension is what makes it that concept and not another. An individual's skin, social role, lifespan, and cognitive horizon are all limits that define the individual.

The built-in contradiction: a limit is **both the thing and not the thing**. The boundary of a circle belongs to the circle (it is part of the circle's definition) and also belongs to the space outside the circle (it separates inside from outside and is thus part of the outside's definition too). Every limit is simultaneously the endpoint of the thing and the starting point of its negation. Limits are irreducibly double-sided — they contradict the idea that things can be cleanly separated.

## 5. The Four C's as Navigation Tools for Contradictory Reality

### 5.1 The Problem of Presentation Under Universal Contradiction

If everything is contradictory, how can anything be coherently communicated? This is the deepest challenge that TI Sigma's universal contradiction thesis poses to its own project. The answer lies in the Four C's of Truth Presentation (Emerick, 2026): **Coherence, Concreteness, Completeness, and Continuity**.

These are not requirements for eliminating contradiction from communication — that would be impossible. They are requirements for **navigating contradiction honestly**, presenting truth in a way that acknowledges its contradictory structure without collapsing into incoherence.

### 5.2 The Four C's Applied to Contradiction

**Coherence** — not freedom from contradiction, but *managed* contradiction. A presentation is coherent when its internal tensions are acknowledged, named, and organized — when the contradictions illuminate rather than obscure. The Myrion Resolution is the formal mechanism for achieving coherence under contradiction: it takes opposing truth-values and finds the stable attractor that honors both.

**Concreteness** — contradictions must be grounded in specific, identifiable cases. Abstract claims about contradiction ("everything contradicts everything") are empty. Concrete contradiction-claims ("the identity of this person over 30 years contains the inconsistency that they are both the child who made that promise and the adult who cannot remember making it") are meaningful, evaluable, and truth-bearing.

**Completeness** — a complete presentation of any claim includes its contradictions. Presenting only one side of a tension — only the continuity without the change, only the identity without the difference — is a form of deception by omission. Complete truth presentation requires that both poles of every relevant contradiction be shown.

**Continuity** — contradictions are not isolated events but threads in a temporal trajectory. A Coherent presentation of contradiction traces how the tension evolved, how it has been partially resolved, how it remains live, and where it is heading. Without temporal continuity, a contradiction appears as a snapshot of irresolvable chaos. With it, the same contradiction appears as a dynamic structure in motion.

## 6. Valid Contradictions vs. Double Tralse: The First Myrion Resolution as Coherence Gate

### 6.1 Not All Contradictions Are Equal

TI Sigma's universal contradiction thesis does not mean that all contradictions are equally real, equally useful, or equally worth engaging with. The crucial distinction is between:

- **Valid contradictions**: inconsistencies, oppositions, or discrepancies that, when examined, yield genuine information about reality and can be meaningfully worked with via the Myrion Resolution.
- **Double Tralse (DT)**: contradictions that, when examined, yield nothing — no usable signal, no resolvable tension, no stable attractor. They are not valid contradictions but noise.

The criterion for this distinction is the **first Myrion Resolution (MR1)**, which functions as a coherence gate.

### 6.2 The First Myrion Resolution as Coherence Gate

MR1 asks: *Does this contradiction contain sufficient internal coherence to proceed?*

A contradiction passes MR1 when:
- The opposing claims each have some evidential or rational basis (neither is pure noise)
- The tension between them is organized around a recognizable disagreement (the claims are about the same thing)
- The contradiction is resolvable in principle — there exists some additional information, context, or framework that could move the tension toward resolution

A contradiction fails MR1 (becomes Double Tralse) when:
- The opposing claims have no coherent basis — they are not about anything determinate
- The tension is not organized around a recognizable disagreement — the claims talk past each other without contact
- The contradiction is irresolvable in principle — no additional information could change anything, because there is no there there

**Key point**: passing MR1 does not mean the contradiction is *resolved*. It means the contradiction is *real enough and organized enough to work with*. Many contradictions pass MR1 and remain unresolved — this is the normal condition of living in a contradictory reality. What MR1 screens out is not unresolved tension but vacuous noise.

## 7. The Double Tralse Taxonomy: Four Categories of Invalid Contradiction

Double Tralse is not a single thing. It is a family of failure modes — different ways that a would-be contradiction can fail to be real, organized, or meaningful. TI Sigma identifies four distinct categories.

### 7.1 Category 1: Maximal Incoherence

**Definition**: A contradiction is maximally incoherent when the evidence and reasoning on all sides is so fragmentary, internally inconsistent, and mutually contradictory that no stable assessment is possible. The Probability Density (PD) cannot be located because it pulls in all directions simultaneously without resolution.

**Key feature**: Unlike a valid tralse state — where the tension is real and organized — maximal incoherence is *disorganized* tension. There is no attractor. The Myrion Resolution returns not a stable point but an undefined region.

**Examples**:
- *"Does God love Tuesdays more than Fridays?"* — The question fragments into incoherence before any evidence can even be identified. Both "yes" and "no" and "it's complicated" and "the question is wrong" all seem simultaneously applicable for different reasons, none of which cohere.
- *"Was Hamlet happy?"* — A real character within the fiction, but the question asks for a truth-claim that the fiction underdetermines in all directions. Every piece of evidence implies something different. The PD scatters.
- Any claim that generates equal and opposite confusion on every dimension of analysis, yielding no distinguishable signal from noise.

**Practical significance**: Maximal incoherence is the epistemic condition that generates endless, fruitless debate. The debates about the nature of God's personality, about the meaning of highly contested abstract concepts in isolation from any context, about the inner lives of fictional characters — these often fail MR1 not because they are unimportant but because as *currently posed* they are maximally incoherent. The remedy is not to answer them but to **refine the question** until MR1 can be passed.

### 7.2 Category 2: The Self-Negating Nothing

**Definition**: A contradiction is a Self-Negating Nothing when it consists of a pure negation that negates itself, producing a logical object that cannot be coherently stated, referenced, or thought about.

**Key feature**: It attempts to refer to something whose very description undermines the possibility of reference. The classic case: *"a 'nothing' that 'doesn't exist.'"*

**Analysis**:
- "Nothing" is already the negation of existence.
- "A nothing that doesn't exist" negates the negation.
- If nothing doesn't exist, then nothing is something (it fails to exist, which is a status — a thing that fails). But then it is not nothing.
- If nothing does exist, then "nothing" is a thing, which contradicts the concept of nothing.

This is not a valid tralse contradiction — it is not a case where both "nothing exists" and "nothing doesn't exist" are simultaneously real and need to be honored. It is a case where the grammatical form of a sentence has outrun any possible content. There is no there there.

**More examples**:
- *"The set of all sets that don't contain themselves"* (Russell's paradox) — resolved by recognizing that this is a Self-Negating Nothing masquerading as a concept. It passes grammatical tests but fails to pick out any coherent object.
- *"The prime number between 2 and 3"* — grammatically well-formed, but refers to a Self-Negating Nothing (there is no such number, and asking whether it exists or not generates no useful tension).
- *"Absolute nothingness"* as a philosophical concept — whatever "absolute nothingness" is, the act of describing it gives it properties (it is the thing we're describing), which means it cannot be absolute nothingness. The concept undermines itself.

**TI Sigma's verdict**: The Self-Negating Nothing is the ontological floor of Double Tralse. It is tralsity that is tralse and then negated before it can become anything. It is pre-tralse in the sense that it never achieves even the organization needed to be a genuine tralse state.

### 7.3 Category 3: The Self-Refuting Meta-Statement ("Tralse and Not Tralse")

**Definition**: A contradiction is a Self-Refuting Meta-Statement when it asserts and denies its own indeterminacy simultaneously — when the claim is that something "is tralse and not tralse at the same time."

**Key feature**: Unlike Category 2 (which fails to refer to anything), Category 3 refers to something determinate — it just simultaneously asserts and denies the meta-level status of that something.

**Analysis**:
- Single tralse (τ) is a genuine state: "this proposition is both true and false, or indeterminate between them."
- "Tralse and not tralse" would be: the proposition has the tralse status AND does not have the tralse status.
- This is not a superposition of truth-values (which is what tralse is). It is a superposition of whether the superposition applies. It is a second-order tralse of the meta-level claim.
- Crucially: this second-order superposition, when subjected to MR1, collapses. There is no stable attractor. The claim is that the meta-status is indeterminate, but asserting indeterminacy is itself a determinate claim — so the indeterminacy is not indeterminate. The structure destroys itself.

**Examples**:
- *"This statement may or may not be tralse"* — not the Liar paradox (which is DT Category 3) but a valid tralse claim if stated carefully. It becomes DT when it asserts that the meta-question (is it tralse or not?) is itself tralse: "it may or may not be the case that this is indeterminate." At this point, no stable footing remains.
- The extended Liar: *"This sentence is neither true, false, nor tralse"* — it attempts to deny all four truth-values including the fourth (DT itself), thereby achieving a state that has no description within the framework. This is a genuine Self-Refuting Meta-Statement.
- *"Reality is both contradictory and not contradictory simultaneously, and this claim is itself both contradictory and not contradictory"* — when iterated to this level, the claim loses purchase on anything and becomes DT Category 3.

**The distinction from valid tralse**: A valid tralse claim maintains at least one stable meta-level description: we can say "this is tralse" and that claim itself is determinate (either it is or it isn't). A DT Category 3 claim denies even this stable meta-level — it asserts that the meta-description itself is unstable, which makes the assertion itself unstable, and so on without termination.

### 7.4 Category 4: Pre-Tralse Undetermination

**Definition**: A contradiction is Pre-Tralse when it concerns a state that has not yet come into being sufficiently to be evaluated — an undetermined future, an uncommitted intention, a potential that has not yet actualized. The contradiction is not between two real states but between a real state and a merely possible state.

**Key feature**: Pre-Tralse is not false (the claim is not simply wrong about a determinate state of affairs). It is not tralse (there is no genuine tension between two equally real poles). It is in a state of **ontological suspension** — the tension cannot be resolved because one of the poles does not yet exist.

**Examples**:
- *"Will this person be happy in 30 years?"* — as of now, this is Pre-Tralse. The person exists; their future happiness does not yet exist. The question is not yet evaluable. It is not that both "yes" and "no" are true — it is that the state of affairs that would make one of them true has not occurred. The MR1 gate cannot be passed because the inputs are not yet available.
- *"Is this embryo a person?"* — often treated as a tralse claim with real tension between "yes" and "no." TI Sigma recognizes that part of the difficulty is Pre-Tralse: what the embryo will become is not yet determined, and personhood claims about a pre-actualized potential differ from personhood claims about an actualized being.
- *"Did the photon pass through the left slit or the right slit?"* — before measurement, this is Pre-Tralse. The which-path information does not exist. The question presupposes a determinate fact that is not there. After measurement, it becomes either true or false (or tralse, in unusual interpretations of QM).
- Any prophetic or predictive claim about a genuinely open future falls into Pre-Tralse until the future becomes present.

**The temporal resolution of Pre-Tralse**: Unlike Categories 1–3, Pre-Tralse has a natural resolution mechanism: the passage of time. When the future becomes present, the Pre-Tralse state collapses into one of the valid states (True, False, or Tralse). This makes Pre-Tralse uniquely dynamic — it is DT now but may become a valid truth-state later.

**Practical significance**: Recognizing Pre-Tralse allows us to distinguish:
- Genuine indeterminacy (valid tralse — both poles currently real)
- Ignorance (we don't know which pole is real, but one is)
- Pre-Tralse (neither pole is yet fully real — the determining conditions have not yet obtained)

Confusing these three produces profound philosophical errors: treating genuine indeterminacy as ignorance (asserting hidden variables that don't exist), treating ignorance as indeterminacy (denying that there is a fact of the matter when there is), and treating Pre-Tralse as one of the other two (asking for premature evaluation of what hasn't happened yet).

### 8.2 The Hierarchy of Realness

The four DT categories are not equally "nothing." They occupy positions on a spectrum from closer to genuine contradiction to further from it:

```
Pre-Tralse       ←→ closest to valid (one real pole exists; the other is possible)
Self-Refuting    ←→ structurally real but self-destructing at the meta-level
Maximal Incoherence ←→ empirically ungrounded but grammatically coherent
Self-Negating Nothing ←→ furthest from valid (not even grammatical form can ground it)
```

Pre-Tralse deserves particular respect — it is often mistaken for ignorance or evasion but is a genuine ontological condition. A person who says "I don't know whether I will love this person in 30 years" is not being evasive; they are correctly identifying a Pre-Tralse state.

## 9. Implications

### 9.1 For Formal Logic

Classical logic is not wrong. It is a special case — appropriate when all relevant contradictions are of the Self-Negating Nothing or Maximal Incoherence type and can be safely excluded. When the relevant contradictions are valid Tralse, classical logic systematically underdescribes reality. The Law of Non-Contradiction, the Law of Excluded Middle, and the Principle of Explosion are not universal laws of reality — they are operational stipulations appropriate for specific formal domains.

### 9.2 For Science
