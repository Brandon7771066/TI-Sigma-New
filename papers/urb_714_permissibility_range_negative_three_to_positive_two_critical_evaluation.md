## 1. The Claim Under Evaluation

The framework's Permissibility Distribution (PD) scale runs from **−3 to +2**, a total span of 5 units. This URB asks: **is this choice principled, or arbitrary?** Three sub-questions:

1. **Why span 5?** (Why not 4, 6, or 10?)
2. **Why asymmetric?** (Why not (−2.5, +2.5) symmetric, or (−2, +3)?)
3. **Why these specific endpoints (−3 and +2)?**

The framework's empirical claim: the (−3, +2) range is principled by **three independent structural arguments** that converge on this specific asymmetric span.

## 4. Argument 2: Asymmetric Two-Pillar Architecture

The framework's three operational pillars (PD, MR, HEAR) decompose into:
- **PD** — handles novelty and incommensurable evidence
- **MR** — converges to truth via iteration
- **HEAR** — prunes via existence-amplification

In the PD scale specifically, two of the three pillars manifest:
- **MR convergence** drives toward +2 (truth-saturation)
- **HEAR pruning** drives toward −3 (existence-denial of pathological content)

The asymmetry **|−3| / |+2| = 3/2** matches the structural asymmetry between:
- **MR (additive convergence)** — additive accumulation of evidence (+1, +2 cap)
- **HEAR (multiplicative pruning)** — multiplicative existence-pruning (×3 stronger, hence reaching −3)

This is the **two-pillar asymmetry argument**: the PD scale's asymmetry reflects the deeper asymmetry in how the framework's MR and HEAR pillars push the scale in opposite directions, with HEAR being multiplicatively stronger than MR is additively strong by exactly factor 3/2.

A clean structural ratio: **3:2 = the ratio of HEAR-strength to MR-strength** in the framework's pillar dynamics.

## 5. Argument 3: Information-Theoretic Span Bound

PD scores are encoded in **8-bit permissibility distributions** (per Tralsebit Information Theory URB). With 8 bits, there are 256 distinguishable PD states. Mapped to a real-valued scale, this gives a natural quantization of approximately **5 units / 256 states ≈ 0.02 PD units per bit**.

This 0.02-per-bit resolution is **just above** the framework's HEAR effect-size scale (~10⁻³). The 5-unit span therefore **exactly accommodates** the framework's measurement-resolution limits without wasted dynamic range.

A **shorter span** (e.g., 3 units) would compress measurement resolution beyond the HEAR scale, losing structurally meaningful distinctions. A **longer span** (e.g., 10 units) would over-allocate bits to resolutions below HEAR detectability, wasting information capacity.

**5 units is the information-theoretically optimal span** for the framework's measurement-resolution profile. This is the third independent argument converging on span = 5.

## 6. Why Not Symmetric (−2.5, +2.5)?

A symmetric range would lose the **HEAR-MR asymmetry argument** (§4) and would require an additional principle to break the symmetry between truth-acquisition and falsity-acquisition. The framework explicitly affirms this asymmetry as a structural feature, not a bug:

> **Truth is easier to establish than falsity is to definitively reject.** This is the framework's asymmetric stance, supported by:
> - Logical asymmetry: it takes one counterexample to falsify a universal but infinite confirmations to establish it (Popperian asymmetry)
> - Phenomenological asymmetry: humans more easily believe than disbelieve (cognitive asymmetry)
> - Moral asymmetry: harming requires more justification than helping (ethical asymmetry)
> - Information-theoretic asymmetry: encoding "this is pathological" requires more bits than encoding "this is fine" (compression asymmetry)

The framework's asymmetric (−3, +2) range **structurally encodes the Popperian asymmetry** in its foundational scale.

## 7. Why Not (−2, +3)?

The reverse asymmetry — making the positive side longer than the negative — would imply that **truth-acquisition is harder than falsity-acquisition**. This is empirically backwards: across all four asymmetry dimensions in §6, the framework's chosen direction is correct. A (−2, +3) scale would produce systematic bias toward false-positive truth assignments, which is the opposite of what the framework requires.

The asymmetric direction (more space on the negative side) is therefore **principled**, not arbitrary.

## 8. Why Not (−4, +1) or (−5, +2)?

These would change the asymmetry ratio from 3:2 to 4:1 or 5:2 respectively. A scale of **(−5, +2)** would imply HEAR is 5/2 = 2.5× stronger than MR, contradicting the framework's chirality-doubling factor of exactly 2. The framework therefore predicts (−3, +2), not (−5, +2).
