## 1. The Langlands Program

### 1.1 What the Langlands Program Is

The Langlands Program (Robert Langlands, 1967 onward) is perhaps the deepest organizing principle in modern mathematics. It proposes a grand unifying correspondence between:

1. **Automorphic forms**: Functions on symmetric spaces satisfying infinite symmetry constraints
2. **Galois representations**: Ways that the absolute Galois group Gal(Q̄/Q) acts on algebraic structures
3. **L-functions**: Complex analytic functions encoding arithmetic information via their zeros

The central conjecture — **functoriality** — states that a correspondence between symmetry groups on one side induces a corresponding transfer of automorphic forms on the other. In the geometric Langlands version: a correspondence between G and its Langlands dual Ǧ produces a duality between D-modules on the moduli stack BunG and ǦLocal.

This is, at its heart, a claim about **non-local structural correspondence**: the same information is encoded in two radically different mathematical domains, and transformations in one domain are reflected in the other without any obvious local mechanism.

## 2. Quantum Field Theory
