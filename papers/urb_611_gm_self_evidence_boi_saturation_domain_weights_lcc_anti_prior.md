## 4. The LCC Anti-Prior Theorem — Eliminating Bayesian Priors

### 4.1 The Theorem

**Theorem (LCC Anti-Prior):** Bayesian priors do not exist as abstract objects. They exist only as representations in the minds of agents who hold them. All events are new, unique events unfolding in real time — united by LCC coherence, not by abstract prior distributions. What is called "chance" is not a real entity but a post-hoc label for LCC-governed event structure.

### 4.2 The Occam's Razor Argument

Bayesian epistemology posits that prior probabilities P(H) are abstract objects that exist independently of particular minds — the "objective Bayesian" or "logical probability" position — or, in subjective Bayesianism, as degrees of belief that represent a long-run frequency structure.

**Occam's Razor challenge:** Are priors simpler than their alternative?

Consider what positing priors requires:
- Abstract probability distributions over hypothesis spaces
- A principled method of determining prior values (principle of indifference, maximum entropy, or subjective assignment)
- A metaphysics of "chance" as a real physical property that persists across events

TI Sigma offers a simpler account:
- Every event unfolds in real time governed by the current LCC configuration
- Apparent stability of "probabilities" = stability of the LCC environment, not evidence for abstract priors
- "Prior probability of heads = 0.5" translates to: "The current LCC configuration of this coin-flip system is such that, given current physical constraints, heads and tails are equally LCC-supported outcomes"

The LCC account posits: (a) real physical systems, (b) their causal structure (LCC), and (c) measurement. Bayesian accounts additionally posit: abstract prior distributions as metaphysical entities. By Occam's Razor, the simpler account (LCC only) is preferable.

### 4.3 The Omnipresence of Tralse — No Two Events Are Alike

**Foundational TI Sigma claim:** Because Tralse/indeterminacy is omnipresent in the LCC, no two events are literally identical. Every event is a new event.

This directly challenges frequentist and Bayesian accounts that depend on the concept of *repeated trials of the same experiment.* If no two coin flips are literally the same physical event — they occur in different LCC configurations, different points in spacetime, with subtly different initial conditions — then the frequentist definition of probability (long-run frequency over identical trials) has no physical referent.

What appears to be "the same experiment repeated" is actually: a sequence of new events with sufficiently similar LCC configurations that they cluster together in our measurements. The cluster is real; the identity of the trials is not.

**Formal statement:** For any two events E₁ and E₂ in the LCC:
$$\text{LCC}(E_1) \neq \text{LCC}(E_2)$$
because LCC states evolve continuously and no two spacetime regions instantiate identical LCC configurations. Therefore E₁ ≠ E₂ as events, even if they appear identical to a macroscopic observer.

### 4.4 LCC Stability — What Priors Actually Track

If priors don't exist as abstract objects, why do probabilistic predictions work so well in stable domains?

**Answer:** What is called a "prior" is actually a compressed description of the **current LCC stability level** in the relevant domain.

- "The prior for a fair coin is 0.5" = "The LCC configuration of this coin system has no systematic bias toward either outcome; both outcomes are equally LCC-supported"
- "The prior for rain given a storm system is 0.8" = "LCC configurations with this atmospheric signature consistently produce outcomes clustering at 80% rain"
- "The prior for cancer given these symptoms is 0.15" = "LCC configurations with this symptom cluster historically resolve to cancer-positive at 15%"

In all cases, the "prior" is a *post-hoc label* for LCC-stable event clusters. The prior has no predictive power beyond what the LCC configuration already encodes.

**LCC Drift:** When the LCC environment changes — new atmospheric patterns, changed biological baselines, different population genetics — the apparent "prior" changes with it. This is called **LCC Drift**: the gradual shift in event-cluster frequencies as the LCC configuration evolves. From a Bayesian view, this looks like "prior updating." From TI Sigma, it is simply the LCC evolving and the event clusters tracking the new LCC configuration.

### 4.5 Chance Is Not a Real Entity

**Conclusion:** "Chance" — the entity that supposedly governs random events — does not exist in TI Sigma ontology.

What is labeled "chance" is the underdetermination of future LCC configurations by current information. It is an epistemic label (for our ignorance of the full LCC state) not an ontological label (for a real randomness-producing mechanism).

**Every event has a LCC cause.** The event is the outcome of the current LCC configuration acting on the system. When we cannot predict the outcome, it is because we have insufficient access to the LCC configuration — not because "chance" independently generated the outcome.

**TI Sigma is deterministic at the LCC level; Tralse at the measurement level.** The LCC fully determines what will happen (in principle). Our measurements sample the LCC at a resolution that leaves some outcomes Tralse — genuinely indeterminate from our epistemic position. But this indeterminacy is epistemic (measurement-relative), not ontological (not a feature of reality independent of measurement).

**Important caveat:** This does not eliminate quantum indeterminacy. Quantum Tralse (coherent superposition) is still genuine. The claim is that "chance" as a separate ontological entity (beyond LCC + quantum Tralse) does not exist. Random events are: LCC-governed events that we cannot fully specify, plus genuine quantum Tralse at the measurement interface.

**New definition (TI Sigma):** A probability value p for outcome O given conditions C is:

> p = the relative frequency with which LCC configurations satisfying C resolve to outcome O, across all observed instances of such configurations.

This is empirical, not abstract. It does not posit prior distributions. It does not require identical trials. It treats probability as a *measurement of LCC-stable event clusters* — a descriptor of observed physical regularities, not an abstract metaphysical object.

**Advantage over Bayesianism:** This account handles the failure cases that break Bayesian priors:
- Novel events (no reference class): assign Tralse rather than forcing a prior
- Bidirectional causation (nature/nurture): LCC models the interaction causally; no need to "split" a prior

**Connection to PD:** The Permissibility Distribution IS the TI Sigma operationalization of probability — but properly conceived as a distribution over LCC-supported i-cells in real time, not a prior-updated Bayesian belief state.
