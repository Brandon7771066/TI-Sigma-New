## 1. The thesis: these five are NOT united by "anti-establishment" — they're united by *contrarian empiricism*

The naïve reading is "Brandon endorsed five anti-establishment books." That reading is **wrong**, and seeing why is the whole point.

- Caplan's *Education* and Brennan–Magness's *Ivory Tower* attack an **academic institution**.
- Cowen's *Big Business* **defends** a frequently-attacked institution (the corporation).
- Caplan's *Open Borders* and *Build, Baby, Build* attack **policy restrictions** (immigration limits, zoning), not an institution per se.

So the common thread cannot be "tear down institutions" — Cowen runs the *opposite* direction. The actual shared spine is: **follow the evidence toward the unpopular or counter-intuitive conclusion, regardless of which tribe it flatters.** (Fig 1.) This is exactly the epistemic posture TI Sigma already encodes:

- **ASYMMETRIC #69** — skepticism is *symmetric*: applied to the popular narrative and the contrarian one alike. Endorsing a contrarian conclusion is only a virtue if it followed evidence, not contrarian aesthetics.
- **Authority Axis (AA)** — popular consensus and institutional authority are *real* evidential inputs but **never the decider** (the B88 result). Each of these books treats a popular prior as defeasible.
- **GTT-1 (truth competes with palatability)** — all five conclusions are *unpalatable* to some large audience; their authors paid a popularity cost to state them. That is the signature of truth-seeking that doesn't optimize for applause.
- **B88 interior-optimum** — these are the "foxes" (Tetlock): broad, evidence-led, willing to defect from their own side's narrative.

**This is the honest unifier, and it is also the #69 trap to avoid:** contrarianism is *not* self-certifying. A contrarian conclusion can be right (Build, Baby, Build) or oversold (the strongest Open Borders numbers). The virtue is the *method*, not the direction. So §2 rates each on evidence, not vibe.

## 2. Per-work mapping + honest #69 calibration (they are NOT equally settled)

Ratings below are *my* honest read of where mainstream economics + the evidence currently sit, on a 1–5 "empirical support for the headline claim" scale. They are calibration, not endorsement-laundering. (Fig 2.)

**(1) The Case Against Education — support ≈ 3/5 (real signal, contested magnitude).**
Headline: most of education's *private* wage return is **signaling**, not skill-building (Caplan estimates ~80%). The signaling channel is genuinely well-evidenced (sheepskin effects, credential inflation). The *magnitude* (80%) is contested — human-capital and peer/socialization effects are larger than Caplan allows, and he underweights education's consumption + civic value. **TI tie:** the credentialism critique is the macro-scale version of B88's anti-hyperexpertise point — credentials ≠ knowledge, authority ≠ truth (AA).

**(2) Build, Baby, Build — support ≈ 4.5/5 (closest to consensus).**
Headline: land-use restriction (zoning) is the dominant driver of unaffordable housing; deregulate to lower prices. This is the **most mainstream** of the five — there is broad empirical agreement (Glaeser–Gyourko and a large literature) that supply constraints raise prices. **TI tie:** a clean case where the *unpopular* policy conclusion (deregulate) is also the *well-supported* one — palatability and truth diverge (GTT-1), and the evidence wins.

**(3) Open Borders — support ≈ 2.5/5 (biggest upside, biggest model risk).**
Headline: open immigration could roughly **double world GDP** (the Clemens "trillion-dollar bills on the sidewalk" estimate). The growth-gain direction is well-supported; the *doubling* figure is a model with large assumptions (it holds institutions fixed while moving the people who arguably produce them). Political-economy, absorption, and assimilation concerns are real and Caplan's keyhole-solutions answer them only partially. **TI tie:** the **most contested** endorsement — exactly where #69 forbids treating "radical + well-argued" as "established."

**(4) Cracks in the Ivory Tower — support ≈ 3.5/5 (strong on incentives, selective on scope).**
Headline: higher ed is riddled with misaligned incentives and routine ethical failures judged by its *own* stated standards (a business-ethics audit of academia). The incentive critiques (administrative bloat, adjunct exploitation, grade inflation, gen-ed cynicism) are well-documented. It is deliberately one-sided (it's an indictment, not a balanced review). **TI tie:** institutional-self-interest critique — the engine behind the hyperexpertise paradigm is partly *rent-seeking by credentialing institutions* (B88 limit 5: credential gatekeeping).

**(5) Big Business: A Love Letter — support ≈ 3/5 (good corrective, affectionate framing).**
Headline: big business is, on average, **more honest and competent than its populist reputation** (CEOs don't lie more than average; corporations deliver enormous consumer value; "crony capitalism" is mostly a *government*-interface problem). Cowen is empirically careful and explicitly writing a *defense*, so it's a deliberate counterweight, not a neutral survey. **TI tie:** the crucial demonstration that contrarian empiricism is **not** anti-establishment — here the evidence-led move is to *defend* a hated institution. This is the book that proves Fig 1's point.

## 3. Why this belongs in the TI Sigma corpus (not just a reading list)

1. **It operationalizes #69 at the level of *which authors to trust*.** Endorsing five authors who each pay a popularity cost for an evidence-led conclusion is a *behavioral* instance of symmetric skepticism — and the calibration table (§2) is itself a #69 act: refusing to rate all five 5/5 just because Brandon endorsed them.
2. **It sharpens B88.** B88 argued authority ≠ truth at the *individual* (doctor/patient) scale. These works make the same move at the *institutional/societal* scale (universities, immigration regimes, zoning boards, anti-corporate consensus). Same axis (AA), bigger lever.
3. **It supplies a clean GTT-1 dataset.** Five conclusions plotted on (popularity vs evidential support) show that truth and palatability are *orthogonal*, which is GTT-1's core claim made concrete.
4. **The #69 firewall is explicit:** endorsement ≠ proof. The strongest defensible corpus claim is *"these authors model the right epistemic method, and Build-Baby-Build + the credential-signal critique are well-supported; Open Borders' headline number is model-dependent."* Not *"all five are correct."*
