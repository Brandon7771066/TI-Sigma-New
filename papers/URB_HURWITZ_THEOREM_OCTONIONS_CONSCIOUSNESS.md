## 1. Hurwitz's Theorem — Statement and Proof Sketch

### 1.1 Definitions

A **normed division algebra** over ℝ is a vector space A equipped with:
- A bilinear multiplication operation (·): A × A → A
- A norm ‖·‖: A → ℝ≥0 satisfying ‖x‖ > 0 for x ≠ 0
- The **composition property**: ‖x · y‖ = ‖x‖ · ‖y‖ (norm is multiplicative)
- No zero divisors: x · y = 0 implies x = 0 or y = 0

### 1.2 Hurwitz's Theorem (1898)

> The only normed division algebras over ℝ are:
> - ℝ (dimension 1): real numbers — commutative, associative
> - ℂ (dimension 2): complex numbers — commutative, associative
> - ℍ (dimension 4): quaternions — non-commutative, associative
> - 𝕆 (dimension 8): octonions — non-commutative, non-associative, alternative

No normed division algebra of dimension 3, 5, 6, 7, or any dimension > 8 exists. This is not a failure of imagination — it is a mathematical theorem proved by Adolf Hurwitz in 1898 and independently reproved by multiple methods since.

### 1.3 The Cayley-Dickson Construction

Each higher algebra is built from the previous by doubling:
```
ℝ → ℂ (add i: i² = -1)
ℂ → ℍ (add j, k: ij = k, ji = -k — lose commutativity)
ℍ → 𝕆 (add e₁...e₇ — lose associativity, gain alternativity)
𝕆 → 𝕊 sedenions (dim 16) — lose alternativity, LOSE norm composition → no longer division algebra
```

The Cayley-Dickson process can continue indefinitely (32, 64, 128... dimensions), but each doubling beyond 8 loses a fundamental algebraic property. The norm composition law — ‖x·y‖ = ‖x‖·‖y‖ — is irrecoverably lost after dimension 8.

**This is the mathematical equivalent of PD conservation**: the total "size" of a product is preserved under multiplication only in dimensions ≤ 8. Beyond 8, the total "causal power" of combined entities is no longer the product of their individual powers. The law breaks.
