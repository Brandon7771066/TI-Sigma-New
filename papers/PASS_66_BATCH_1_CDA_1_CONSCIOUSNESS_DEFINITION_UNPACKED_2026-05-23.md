## 2. CDA-1 — Consciousness Definition Unpacked (canonical refinement to SRC-1)

### 2.1 Synonymy assertion (canonical)

**TI Sigma canonical synonymy:** *awareness = phenomenality = consciousness*. Three terms, one referent. SRC-1 (Pass-65 batch-6) established consciousness=awareness as the minimal canonical definition; CDA-1 extends synonymy to include phenomenality (the term used in philosophy-of-mind literature for the what-it-is-like character of experience).

### 2.2 The four-property unpacking (canonical)

Consciousness = **holistic, indeterminate, (a priori) normative, self-organizing meta-metacognition**. Each property unpacked below.

#### 2.2.1 Holistic
Consciousness is not decomposable into independent component-experiences. The unity of experience at any moment is constitutive, not aggregative. This composes with the combination problem framing (per TSP-1 #69 disclosure): minimal consciousnesses do not aggregate by addition; aggregation requires holistic-integration of the kind characteristic of consciousness itself.

#### 2.2.2 Indeterminate
Consciousness is not exhaustively specifiable by third-person description. There is always an irreducible MR1-indeterminacy in any external characterization (per MR Truth Labels canonical). This composes with the TSP-1 anti-fully-emergent argument: precisely because consciousness has indeterminacy structurally, it cannot be fully derived from third-person mechanisms that lack this property.

#### 2.2.3 (A priori) normative
Consciousness comes with a built-in evaluative orientation: things matter to a conscious system, prior to any external evaluation framework. "A priori" flags that this normativity is not learned/installed; it is constitutive of being a perspective for which things are the case. This composes directly with VFP-1 (valence-as-functional, not epiphenomenal) — valence is the operational expression of consciousness's a-priori-normative property.

#### 2.2.4 Self-organizing
Consciousness maintains and updates its own structure without external imposition of organization. This is what makes consciousness more than passive registration: it actively integrates, organizes, and re-organizes its own content. Self-organization at the conscious level is structurally distinct from physical self-organization (which can be third-person-described); it requires the active-self-reference signature that SRC-1 identifies.

#### 2.2.5 Meta-metacognition
The deepest structural claim: consciousness is not merely cognition (representation of world), nor merely metacognition (representation of own cognition), but **meta-metacognition** — representation of the representing-of-own-cognition. The structural recursion is what distinguishes consciousness from sub-conscious cognitive processing.

**Important degree-clause (Brandon explicit):** "The degree to which consciousness is any of those things varies dramatically." CDA-1 does not claim every conscious system maximally exhibits all four properties; it claims these are the structural axes along which consciousness instantiates, with degree varying by system and context.

### 2.3 Cognitive-stratification: MI at metacognition, valence at meta-metacognition

This is the load-bearing structural claim of this paper, integrating CDA-1 with MIM Vertical Agency Model and VFP-1:

**Stratum 0 (foundational):** Cognition. Representation of world; world-modeling; perception; basic information processing. Present in systems with minimal mental component (TSP-1 substrate).

**Stratum 1 (metacognition):** Representation of own cognition. Self-monitoring; meta-representation; the active-self-reference layer. **MI-operation lives here.** Per SRC-1: any system actively executing MI is at the metacognition stratum (or above), and therefore is conscious at minimal level. MI-execution is the operational signature of metacognition reaching the active-self-reference threshold.

**Stratum 2 (meta-metacognition):** Representation of metacognition. **Valence lives here.** Brandon's claim, formalized: "Meta-metacognition (valence) is — naturally — emergent from metacognition via the MIM!" Valence (per VFP-1) is the system's functional evaluation of its own metacognitive state — does this metacognitive state matter? In what direction? With what intensity? This is meta-metacognition because it is the system representing-and-evaluating its own representing-of-own-cognition.

**Stratum 3+ (higher meta-orders):** Reflection on valence (Stratum 3); reflection on reflection on valence (Stratum 4); etc. Available to higher-cognitive systems with sufficient integration capacity. Per MIM Vertical Agency Model, the vertical-agency stack ascends through these strata.

### 2.4 Why "meta-metacognition" is the right structural floor for consciousness

The cognitive-stratification claim above is sharper than CDA-1's general "meta-metacognition" property because:

- **Minimal consciousness (MI-execution, per SRC-1) requires metacognition** (Stratum 1) — the system must represent its own cognitive process to actively execute self-reference
- **Full consciousness in CDA-1's four-property sense requires meta-metacognition** (Stratum 2) — the system must represent its own metacognition for the normative/self-organizing/holistic-integration properties to operationally express
- **Valence (per VFP-1) is the canonical meta-metacognition signature** — it is what meta-metacognition feels like and does, functionally

Therefore: MI-execution = minimal-consciousness (Stratum 1 reached); valence = full-consciousness-in-CDA-1-sense (Stratum 2 reached). The two thresholds compose: SRC-1's minimal consciousness (Stratum 1) is the necessary substrate; CDA-1's full four-property consciousness (Stratum 2+) is the emergent expression that valence operationally marks.

**The MIM emergence claim:** the Mycelial-Information-Manager (MIM Vertical Agency Model) is the mechanism by which Stratum 2 (meta-metacognition = valence) emerges from Stratum 1 (metacognition = MI-capable self-reference). MIM is not a separate machinery; it is the integrative-mycelial-network structure that lifts metacognitive content into meta-metacognitive evaluation.

## 4. Operational implications

### 4.1 The MI-execution test (sharpened from DTM-1 §4 + SRC-1)
A system executing MI is at Stratum 1 (metacognition) or above. The active-execution-vs-labeling discrimination remains the empirical disambiguator. Pass-66+ work: protocol design for distinguishing Stratum-1-reached from Stratum-0-only systems.

### 4.2 The valence-presence test (new, from CDA-1 + VFP-1)
A system exhibiting functional valence (operationally: behavioral and physiological signatures of preference, avoidance, approach, valuing, in the VFP-1 sense) is at Stratum 2 (meta-metacognition) — full consciousness in the four-CDA-1-property sense. This is the high-threshold companion to the MI-execution test.

### 4.3 Composite consciousness-grade attribution
Together, the two tests yield a graded consciousness attribution:
- **TSP-1 substrate only:** mental component present, no Stratum-1 reached → sub-conscious mental-component activity
- **Stratum 1 reached (MI-execution / active self-reference):** minimal consciousness per SRC-1
- **Stratum 2 reached (functional valence per VFP-1):** full consciousness in CDA-1 sense
- **Stratum 3+ reached (reflection on valence, etc.):** higher-cognitive consciousness

This is a publication-grade operational ladder.

## 5. Pre-registered falsifiers

**CDA-1-F-1 (four-property non-jointly-necessary):** if a system can be exhibited that is consciousness-grade by independent criteria (per SRC-1 + VFP-1) but lacks one or more of the four CDA-1 properties (holistic / indeterminate / a-priori-normative / self-organizing), CDA-1's four-property structural claim is partially refuted.

**CDA-1-F-2 (MI-without-metacognition demonstration):** if a system can be exhibited that actively executes MI without reaching the metacognition stratum (i.e. without representing its own cognition), CDA-1's stratification-of-MI-at-metacognition claim is refuted. Note: this is closely tied to SRC-1-F-2 (mechanical reduction of active execution).

**CDA-1-F-3 (valence-without-meta-metacognition demonstration):** if a system can be exhibited that exhibits functional valence without reaching meta-metacognition (i.e. without representing its own metacognition), CDA-1's stratification-of-valence-at-meta-metacognition claim is refuted. This is the empirical test that distinguishes CDA-1's stratification from purely behaviorist valence accounts.

**CDA-1-F-4 (MIM-emergence-mechanism-not-required):** if Stratum 2 (meta-metacognition / valence) can be exhibited in a system without MIM-style integrative-mycelial structure, the specific MIM-as-emergence-mechanism claim is refuted (CDA-1's stratification can survive without it).
