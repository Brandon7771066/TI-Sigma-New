## 1. Haeckel's Principle — And Its Limits

Ernst Haeckel observed that embryos of different species pass through structurally similar stages corresponding to earlier evolutionary forms. A human embryo develops gill slits, a tail, and a fish-like circulatory structure before differentiating into specifically mammalian form. Haeckel's slogan: **ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny** — the individual's development (ontogeny) replays the species' evolutionary history (phylogeny).

Modern biology has refined this: it is not strict recapitulation but **homologous developmental pathways**. The underlying genetic and structural blueprints are shared across species; what differs is when development terminates and in what direction it is elaborated. The *blueprint* is conserved even when the *product* differs.

| Individual consciousness | Rationality → Intuition → Love → Environmental competence | Brandon's developmental arc |
