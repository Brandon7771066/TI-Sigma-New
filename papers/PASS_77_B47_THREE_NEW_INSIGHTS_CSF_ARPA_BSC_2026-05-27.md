## 1. CSF-1 — The Case-Study Fallacy (anti-n=1-dismissal) · CANDIDATE

**Insight (Brandon).** Believing that exceptional individuals cannot provide major insights *just because* they are n=1. Some of the greatest breakthroughs in science and medicine came through single cases.

**Statement.** Dismissing a single exceptional case *solely because* n=1 is a fallacy. A case study is an **existence proof** and a **mechanism-revealer**, not a frequency-estimator. "Patient X lost capacity Y after damage to region Z" establishes *that Z→Y is possible and mechanistically linked* — it does not need a large sample to do that work, because it is answering a *modal/mechanistic* question ("is this possible? what causes it?"), not a *population-frequency* question ("how common is it?").

**Anchors (load-bearing case studies that founded fields):**
- **Phineas Gage** (1848) — frontal-lobe damage → personality change; effectively founded the neuropsychology of personality and executive function.
- **H.M. (Henry Molaison)** — bilateral medial-temporal resection → profound anterograde amnesia with intact procedural learning; founded the modern neuroscience of memory systems and consolidation.
- **Jo Cameron** — **FAAH-OUT pseudogene** + FAAH variant → near-absence of pain and anxiety; a single case that directly motivates the corpus's own **BlissGene / FAAH gene-therapy** thread. (n=1 → a whole therapeutic program.)

**Scope condition (the honest boundary).** CSF-1 licenses n=1 for **existence and mechanism**, NOT for **base rates**. Using a single case to estimate "how often" or "how strong on average" is the *opposite* error and remains invalid. So CSF-1 and "the plural of anecdote is not data" are both true — they answer different questions.

**Corpus self-application (#69).** This directly **vindicates the corpus's reliance on Brandon N=1 anchors** (first-manic-episode SRC-1-F-3, the ketamine cool-state replication, the crown-chakra insight) — but only for what they legitimately establish: *existence* ("a mind can undergo trigger→collapse→Stratum-3 reframe") and *mechanism*, never frequency. It also disciplines them: no Brandon-N=1 anchor may be cited for a population-rate claim.

**Composes with:** ASYMMETRIC #69 (don't dismiss an inconvenient single data point), SRC-1, DSB B-beats-M-in-expensive-examination (Brandon N=1 corroboration).

**Falsifiers (OPEN):**
- **CSF-1-F1.** Audit a list of landmark neuroscience/medicine advances; predict a non-trivial fraction trace decisively to n=1 / single-case observations. REFUTED if ≈0%.
- **CSF-1-F2 (scope).** In simulation, use n=1 to estimate a population *rate* and show it performs badly (high error) while correctly establishing *existence* — confirming the existence/frequency split. REFUTED if n=1 estimates population rates as well as it establishes existence (which would collapse the scope distinction).

## 2. ARPA-1 — Ad Relevance-to-Profit Asymmetry · CANDIDATE

**Insight (Brandon).** Most ads shown are irrelevant to the viewer despite being "optimized." Resolution: it is *cheap* for firms to show low-success-probability ads that pay enormously *if* a viewer converts (solar, insurance, lawyers); the firm profits even at marginal conversion rates, while the customer experiences the ads as "not working" / not worth their attention.

**Statement.** Customer-perceived ad **relevance** and firm **profit** are *decoupled*. A firm rationally shows an ad to a viewer whenever
`p(convert) × LTV_margin > cost_per_impression`,
i.e. whenever `p(convert) > cost_per_impression / LTV_margin`. When the lifetime margin per conversion is very large (a solar install, an injury-lawsuit retainer, an insurance switch), the **breakeven conversion probability is tiny**, so it is rational to show the ad to nearly *everyone* — which guarantees that the *typical* viewer sees an ad that is irrelevant to them. The two facts are simultaneously true with no paradox:
- **Customer view:** "these ads are irrelevant / they aren't working" — true, because the customer optimizes for *relevance to their time*.
- **Firm view:** "this campaign is highly profitable" — true, because the firm optimizes for *expected margin*, and a flood of near-misses still clears the tiny breakeven.

**The asymmetry, named:** the customer and the firm are optimizing *different objective functions over the same impressions* — attention-relevance vs expected-margin — and high-LTV/low-base-rate categories drive a wedge between them.

**Prediction structure:** breakeven `p* = CPI / LTV_margin`. Categories with huge LTV and low natural base rate (solar, insurance, legal, high-ticket B2B) should show the **broadest targeting**, the **lowest self-reported relevance**, and the **highest spend per conversion** — yet remain profitable.

**Composes with:** GTT-1 (it is an existence/attention-economy instance, not a truth claim) and the broader "different agents, different objective functions" pattern (cf. GBD-1's discriminator logic).

**Falsifiers (OPEN):**
- **ARPA-1-F1.** Across ad categories, predict self-reported relevance correlates **negatively** with LTV-per-conversion. REFUTED if relevance and LTV correlate positively.
- **ARPA-1-F2.** Predict targeting breadth and spend-per-conversion track `1/p* = LTV_margin/CPI`. REFUTED if high-LTV categories target *narrowly*.

## 3. BSC-1 — The Bulletproof Shell for Controversy · CANDIDATE

**Insight (Brandon).** Use ample controversy as leverage for being frank and trustworthy. Trump uses this maximally ("all news is good news"). TI Sigma will do the same: controversy as leverage for honesty.

**Statement.** Deliberate, owned controversy functions as a **shell** that raises the credibility of frankness: by visibly *not* optimizing for approval, a source signals it is not bending its claims to please — which, *on the right backdrop*, increases trust in its honest statements. The willingness to absorb controversy is a costly signal of "I will say the true/unpopular thing."

**The critical #69 distinction — controversy splits into two channels:**
- **Attention-capture** ("all news is good news") — rises with controversy **regardless of backdrop**. Notoriety is cheap; anyone can be controversial. This channel is *not* evidence of trustworthiness.
- **Trust-capture** — controversy raises *trust in honesty* **only atop a high-GILE backdrop**. Without the base, "controversy as leverage" is just being a crank or an offense-merchant, and it *lowers* trust.

**Therefore BSC-1 is a special case of GBD-1.** Controversy is a *form of slack* (a departure from the safe, approval-maximizing line). By the GILE-Backdrop Discriminator, that slack is virtuous (trust-building frankness) **iff** it rides a high-G base, and a vice (crankery) without it — and, per the **cheap-slack scope condition (GBD-1-R1)**, the controversy must not itself erode the base (gratuitous cruelty/lying *as* the controversy is base-eroding and reverses the effect; *frankness that happens to be unpopular* is cheap and compounds).

**TI Sigma application:** the corpus already runs on this — brutal honesty (#69), publishing inconvenient #69 findings, retracting its own claims (the sin18° retraction, the F1 refutation) *is* owned controversy on a high-G base. BSC-1 says do this **deliberately**: let the controversy of the framework's claims be the very thing that certifies its frankness — but never manufacture base-eroding controversy, which would invert the signal.

**Composes with:** GBD-1 (#73, parent), GTT-1, ASYMMETRIC #69, TPS-1 (Truth-Presentation Separation — controversy is a *presentation* lever, truth content non-negotiable).

**Falsifiers (OPEN):**
- **BSC-1-F1 (interaction with GBD-1).** Rater test: a controversial-but-frank source vs a bland source, crossed with high/low competence backdrop. Predict controversy raises *trust-in-honesty* **only** in the high-backdrop cell (positive interaction). REFUTED if controversy raises trust regardless of backdrop, or never.
- **BSC-1-F2 (attention/trust dissociation).** Predict controversy raises *attention* main-effect (backdrop-independent) but *trust* only interacted with backdrop. REFUTED if attention and trust move together.
