GILE First: Why Business Is Too Focused on Results —
and What to Put There Instead

Author: Brandon Emerick
Role: CEO, BlissGene Therapeutics
Date: March 2026
Keywords: GILE • SDT • Deontology • Intrinsic Motivation • Values-Led Leadership
Abstract
The dominant operating philosophy of modern business is implicitly consequentialist: actions are evaluated by their outcomes, values are instrumentalized as tools for producing better results, and organizational health is measured by performance metrics rather than by the quality of the human experience being created. This paper argues that this inversion — results first, values as means — is not merely an ethical problem. It is a strategic one. Drawing on Self-Determination Theory (SDT), virtue ethics, deontological moral philosophy, biblical wisdom, and the GILE framework (Goodness, Intuition, Love, Environment), we argue that the correct ordering is precisely reversed: values first, results as natural consequence. This is not anti-intellectual idealism. It is the structure that empirical psychology, philosophical tradition, and practical organizational experience all independently confirm produces the most durable and meaningful success. The problem with modern business is not insufficient data or discipline. It is that an extraordinary amount of discipline is being applied toward the wrong primary criterion.

1. Introduction: The Inversion at the Heart of Modern Business

Every organization has a theory of value, whether articulated or not. The theory governs what gets optimized, what gets sacrificed when tradeoffs arise, and what kind of human experience the organization creates in the process of pursuing its goals.

The dominant theory in contemporary business is this: results — revenue, growth, customer retention, shareholder return — are the primary criterion. Everything else, including values, culture, and the wellbeing of the people involved, is evaluated instrumentally: does it contribute to the results? If so, cultivate it. If not, it is a luxury the business cannot afford.

This paper argues that this theory is inverted. Not slightly miscalibrated. Structurally reversed.

The correct theory, confirmed by psychology, philosophy, and scripture alike, is the opposite: values — specifically the four dimensions of GILE — are the primary criterion. Results are the natural downstream consequence when values are genuinely primary. The pursuit of results as the primary criterion, by contrast, tends to corrode the very values that would have generated those results organically, producing short-term gains and long-term institutional decay.

"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight." — Proverbs 3:5–6

This is not a command to be passive or naive. It is a precise description of the correct priority ordering — and the psychological research of the last 50 years has been independently confirming it ever since.

2. The Consequentialist Structure of Modern Business

Consequentialism holds that the moral rightness of an action is determined entirely by its outcomes. Modern business is not formally utilitarian, but its operating logic follows the same structure. The primary question in nearly every business meeting, strategy session, and performance review is: what produces the desired outcome?

In this framing, nothing has intrinsic value. Everything is a means. The customer is a means to revenue. The employee is a means to productivity. Culture is a means to retention. Even the product is, ultimately, a means to return on capital.

The problem is not that results matter — they clearly do, and organizations must be sustainable to pursue any values at all. The problem is the direction of the causal arrow. When results are primary and values are instrumental, the system is structurally unstable: values that are mere means can always be abandoned the moment a cheaper means to the same result appears.

This is precisely what happens. Businesses that "value" employees until automation makes them redundant. Companies that "value" sustainability until it becomes expensive. Executives who "value" honesty until honesty becomes costly. These are not individual failures of character. They are the predictable output of a system where values are means, not ends — where everything has a price, and nothing has dignity.

3. The Deontological Alternative: Values as Ends in Themselves

Immanuel Kant's great contribution to ethics was the insistence that persons — and by extension, the values that constitute genuine personhood — have dignity, not price.

"Act so that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never as a means only." — Kant, Groundwork for the Metaphysics of Morals (1785)

Things with price can be substituted — if something can be replaced by something of equal or greater value, it has price. Things with dignity cannot be substituted — they are irreplaceable, and any action that treats them as substitutable is a fundamental misunderstanding of their nature.

The values that SDT identifies as Basic Psychological Needs — competence, autonomy, and relatedness — have dignity in precisely this sense. They are not substitutable by money. An employee whose autonomy is systematically violated is not compensated by a pay raise — the violation is a different kind of harm, in a dimension money cannot reach. An organization that offers high salaries while undermining competence and genuine relatedness is offering a trade across incommensurable dimensions. Its employees know it, even when they cannot articulate why something feels absent.

The deontological case for values-first business is therefore not merely ethical in the narrow sense. It is a matter of ontological accuracy: correctly identifying what kind of things values and persons are, and building organizations structured around correct ontology rather than convenient inversions of it.

4. Self-Determination Theory: The Empirical Validation of Values-First

Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985, 2000) is among the most rigorously validated frameworks in all of psychology. Its central finding, replicated across thousands of studies across cultures, educational contexts, healthcare, sports, and organizational behavior, is this: human beings have three Basic Psychological Needs — competence, autonomy, and relatedness — and the degree to which these are satisfied in any context determines the quality of motivation, engagement, performance, and wellbeing produced there.

ConditionMotivation TypeOutcomes
Basic Needs Satisfied
(Values primary)		Intrinsic — driven by genuine interest, meaning, and internalized valuesHigher quality, greater creativity, deeper learning, stronger persistence, greater wellbeing

(Results primary)		Controlled — driven by pressure, fear, contingent reward, performance metricsShort-term compliance, reduced creativity, diminished intrinsic interest, eventual burnout and disengagement

The organizational implication is stark: results-first business, by subordinating psychological needs to performance metrics, systematically produces controlled motivation. It is disciplined, measurable, and reliably degrades the psychological substrate that produces the best performance.

4.1 The "Trying to Please Each Other" Problem

A specific pathology emerges from results-first culture: what might be called the pleasing-performance loop. When results are primary, the most visible path to survival becomes the appearance of delivering results — which quickly mutates into doing whatever others want (managers, clients, investors) rather than genuinely pursuing what actually works.

This is the organizational version of what Hannah Arendt called the "banality of evil" — not grand villains making terrible choices, but ordinary people doing whatever the system rewards, which turns out to be an endless chain of trying to appear successful to people who are themselves trying to appear successful. The whole system becomes self-referential, losing contact with the actual substance it was supposed to serve.

SDT's autonomy need is precisely what this structure violates. Genuine autonomy means action originating from one's own values and judgment, not from pressure to conform to external expectations. An organization that has substituted pleasing for genuine value-creation has systematically undermined autonomy at every level, producing a structure that looks productive but is increasingly decoupled from reality.

5. The Biblical Wisdom Connection: Sophisticated Epistemology, Not Naivete

It would be easy to dismiss the biblical passages cited here as pre-scientific religious prescription — comforting, perhaps, but not relevant to serious business thinking. This dismissal would be an error.

The Proverbs tradition is not anti-intellectual. It is, if anything, hyper-intellectual — deeply concerned with the conditions under which genuine understanding is possible and the structural failures of human cognition that prevent it. "Lean not on your own understanding" is not a command to stop thinking. It is a diagnosis of a specific cognitive failure mode: the collapse of understanding into the self-referential loop of one's own prior assumptions and goals. The person who has made results their primary criterion is, in a meaningful sense, leaning entirely on their own prior commitments — filtering all incoming information through a pre-set criterion that may be systematically miscalibrated.

"Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well. Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will take care of itself." — Matthew 6:33–34

This is an empirical claim about priority ordering. If you correctly identify what is primary and direct your full attention there, the secondary concerns tend to resolve themselves. If you direct attention primarily toward secondary concerns (revenue, margins, market share), anxiety about those concerns expands to fill all available space — while the primary concerns go unaddressed, and paradoxically the secondary concerns also fail to resolve.

Companies that make values genuinely primary — not as brand positioning but as the actual operating criterion — tend to find that results follow. The mechanism is not mysterious: genuine values attract genuine people, who do genuine work, who create genuine products, which generate genuine loyalty, which produces durable revenue. The chain is causal, not magical. And it runs from values to results — not the other way around.

6. The GILE Framework: A Formal Structure for Values-First Business

The GILE framework provides a rigorous four-dimensional specification of the values that belong in the primary position:

DimensionBusiness MeaningPrimary Question
G — GoodnessThe moral dimension; commitment to genuine benefit, real value rather than the appearance of valueDoes this actually help the people it's supposed to help?
I — IntuitionThe epistemic dimension; access to information not available through explicit analytics — pattern recognition, genuine listening, the signal that precedes the reasoningWhat is actually happening that the metrics aren't capturing?
L — LoveThe relational dimension; genuine care for the people involved — customers, employees, partners, communities — as persons, not instruments. SDT's Relatedness elevated to primary operational valueDo we actually care about this person's flourishing?
E — EnvironmentThe systemic dimension; commitment to the health of larger systems — industry, community, the natural world, the culture — within which the organization operatesAre we contributing to or extracting from the conditions that make our existence possible?

When GILE is primary, results are understood as the downstream output of getting the dimensions right. Sustainability remains a necessary floor — an insolvent organization cannot pursue any values. But there is a profound difference between treating results as a floor (necessary for survival) and treating results as the ceiling (the highest aspiration). GILE-first business maintains results as the floor while making the four dimensions the ceiling.

7. The Performance Paradox: Why Values-First Outperforms Results-First

The most counterintuitive finding of organizational psychology is that values-first management — prioritizing psychological needs, genuine meaning, and intrinsic motivation over performance metrics — tends to outperform results-first management on the very metrics that results-first management is explicitly optimizing for.

Ryan & Deci (2000) summarized the evidence: intrinsic motivation predicts higher-quality performance, greater creativity, stronger persistence, and more positive affect compared to controlled motivation produced by external rewards and performance pressure. Grant (2013) showed that prosocial motivation — helping others as a genuine value, not a strategy — predicts both performance and wellbeing more reliably than self-interested motivation.

The direct pursuit of happiness tends to undermine happiness, because happiness is the byproduct of engagement with things that have genuine value. The same structural relationship holds between values and results in organizational life. — after Aristotle, Mill, Frankl

Those who pursue happiness as a primary goal tend to find it elusive. Those who pursue genuine excellence, connection, and contribution find that happiness follows. Aristotle called this eudaimonia — flourishing as the natural result of genuine virtue, not as a goal to be pursued directly. The organizational parallel is exact. Results pursued directly, under controlled motivation, tend to erode. Results that emerge as the natural consequence of genuine values, pursued intrinsically, tend to compound.

9. Conclusion: The Right Hierarchy

This paper is not arguing that results do not matter. They matter enormously, and a company that cannot sustain itself cannot pursue any values at all. The argument is about hierarchy: which comes first, which is the primary criterion, which is the lens through which everything else is evaluated.

The modern business world has the hierarchy inverted. Results are primary; values are instrumental. The consequences are visible everywhere: 70%+ of employees not engaged with their work (Gallup, consistently); organizational cultures that produce performance anxiety rather than genuine contribution; products that optimize for engagement metrics rather than genuine human flourishing; a nearly universal background sense that "something is missing" even in the most financially successful organizations.

The correct hierarchy — confirmed by SDT, deontological ethics, virtue ethics, and the biblical wisdom tradition — is the reverse:

GILE first.
Results follow.

Goodness, Intuition, Love, and Environment as the four primary constraints within which all business decisions are made. Results as the floor — necessary for survival — not the ceiling. The purpose of an organization is not to maximize results. It is to be a site where genuine human flourishing happens, where genuine value is created, where the four dimensions of GILE are honored in every decision. The results, attended to as a floor and released as an aspiration, tend to follow from the integrity of that commitment.

"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding."
Put the priorities that matter most front and center. Tomorrow takes care of itself.
