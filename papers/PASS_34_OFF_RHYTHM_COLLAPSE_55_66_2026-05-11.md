## §4 — u33-C discharge: Mayo (1996) severe-testing comparison

Mayo 1996 *Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge* defines a "severe test" of hypothesis H by experimental result e as: e is severe iff (i) e fits H well _and_ (ii) e was unlikely to fit H well if H were false (formally: high P(e | H) and low P(e | ¬H)). Severity = posterior gain.

**Comparison to URB-830 §4.3 v1.1 demarcation:**

| Mayo 1996 severity | URB-830 §4.3 v1.1 |
|---|---|
| Severity is a property of (test, hypothesis, result) triples; demarcation indirectly via "testable = severely-testable" | Demarcation directly via TIU-reachability over evidence space |
| Implicitly bidirectional (severity defined for both confirming and disconfirming results) | Explicitly bidirectional via clause (b) sign-axis-openness |
| Frequentist-friendly | Bayesian-native via TIU = log P(H|e)/P(H) |
| Pragmatic in scope (designed for working scientists) | Meta-philosophical in scope (designed for corpus-internal demarcation) |

**Verdict (softened per Pass-34 architect discharge):** Mayo's severity and URB-830's TIU framing exhibit **conceptual convergence** — both treat positive and negative results as informationally symmetric posterior updates, and both demarcate via testability rather than falsifiability-alone. A formal frequentist→Bayesian derivation showing strict equivalence is **not** provided here; that derivation is non-trivial (Mayo herself resists Bayesian reductions, treating severity as a primitive error-statistical concept). The original draft's wording "no technical divergence" overstated; corrected to: "no _conceptual_ divergence at the demarcation-criterion level, with formal equivalence not asserted." Per "great minds AND NOT" doctrine, this is also _not_ independent confirmation — it is two compatible framings, one of which (URB-830) was developed knowing Mayo's existed in the literature. URB-830 §4.3 v1.1 wording stands; formal Mayo-vs-TIU equivalence raised as Pass-37 candidate u33-C-formal. u33-C discharged at conceptual-convergence level.
