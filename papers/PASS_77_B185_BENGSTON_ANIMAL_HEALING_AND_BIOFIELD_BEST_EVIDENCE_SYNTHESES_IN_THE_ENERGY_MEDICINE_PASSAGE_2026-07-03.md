## 3. The two paragraphs added (substance)

### 3.1 Biofield / energy-healing syntheses (CHI)
- **Jain & Mills (2010), "Biofield therapies: helpful or full of hype? A best evidence synthesis," *International Journal of Behavioral Medicine* 17(1):1–16.** Lead author **Shamini Jain** later founded the **Consciousness and Healing Initiative (CHI)**. Reported: *strong* evidence for reducing **pain intensity** in pain populations; *moderate* evidence for reducing **anxiety** in hospitalized patients and easing cancer-related fatigue; *equivocal* for most other endpoints.
- **Astin, Harkness & Ernst (2000), "The efficacy of 'distant healing': a systematic review of randomized trials," *Annals of Internal Medicine* 132(11):903–910.** Majority of trials reported a positive effect; authors judged the evidence far from decisive (Ernst's later reviews are more skeptical).
- **So, Jiang & Qin (2008), "Touch therapies for pain relief in adults," Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.** Small, low-certainty benefit on pain.

### 3.2 Bengston's animal work
- **Bengston & Krinsley (2000), "The effect of the 'laying on of hands' on transplanted breast cancer in mice," *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 14(3).** Full remission of an otherwise-uniformly-fatal transplanted mammary cancer in mice across a series of experiments; on-site/off-site control split → the "resonant bonding" anomaly (control mice near treated mice also remitted). Aggregate (from the reproduced Table 1, via Bengston 2007 commentary, *J Altern Complement Med* 13(3):329–331, PMID 17480131): treated 44/48 = 91.7%; on-site control 33/41 = 80.5%; off-site control 0/8 = 0%. (See `papers/PASS_60_BENGSTON_JSE_RETROSPECTIVE_META_ANALYSIS_2026-05-22.md` for the pre-registered protocol + framing splits.)

## 4. Honesty rails (#69 — both directions)

**Credit fairly (undercredit is as much a failure as overcredit):**
- Bengston's **animal-model design is a genuine methodological virtue**: mice have no expectations, so a real remission in a blinded animal model **removes the placebo/expectancy confound** that dogs every human energy-healing trial. This is *why* the result is interesting rather than dismissable, and it is stated plainly in-text.
- The biofield syntheses do find a **consistent signal on self-reported pain and anxiety** — that is more than "nothing."

**Discount honestly (do not overclaim):**
- Bengston is published **almost entirely in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration*** (a fringe venue), **not independently replicated by mainstream oncology labs** (replications trace to Bengston + associates), the **"resonant bonding" anomaly is equally consistent with ordinary contamination/confound**, and curing a uniformly-fatal cancer by hands is an **extraordinary claim with no accepted mechanism** ⇒ raises, not lowers, the bar. Verdict: **provisional at best, pending independent oncological replication; open, Indeterminate-leaning** — neither vindicated nor debunked. This matches the corpus's own pre-registered PASS-60 protocol (which was a *template*; the meta-analysis was never executed).
- The biofield literature is **systematic reviews / best-evidence syntheses, NOT high-powered meta-analyses** (the word "meta-analyses" in the request is honored but corrected in-text). Trials are **small, poorly blinded, low methodological quality**; publication bias is live; **expectancy/placebo cannot be excluded** for subjective endpoints; and the **CHI synthesis's lead author is a proponent** — the same independence caution already applied to EFT. No rigorous support for effects on **objective** disease outcomes in humans.
