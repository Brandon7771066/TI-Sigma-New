# The GILE Psychometric Battery: Complete Item Bank

Validated measurement instruments for the G, I, L, and E dimensions.

**Author:** Brandon Charles Emerick, BlissGene Therapeutics
**Part of:** The GILE Framework
**Date:** April 2026

---

## In Plain Language

This document is a questionnaire toolkit for measuring four human qualities at the heart of the GILE framework: Goodness (genuine moral motivation), Intuition (how accurately a person knows themselves and others), Love (conscious, informed care for a specific person), and Existence/Aesthetics (the ability to recognize genuine quality and structure). Together these four are abbreviated GILE.

Each quality is measured with a short set of agree/disagree statements rated on a 1-to-7 scale, plus a few hands-on tasks that check the self-reports against real behavior. The scores are combined into a single overall number between 0 and 1 using fixed weights, and the document explains how to read that number and how the four qualities relate to one another.

The key idea: these are draft instruments meant to be tested and refined, not a finished diagnostic. They are offered so that researchers can pilot them, run the statistics, and see whether the four dimensions hold up as distinct, measurable traits.

---

## Overview

This battery provides scored psychometric items for each of the four GILE dimensions. Items are designed to be:
- **Distinct** — each item loads cleanly on one dimension
- **Behaviorally grounded** — preference for concrete scenarios over abstract self-ratings
- **Dependency-sensitive** — the I→L dependency (Prediction 1) is embedded in item structure
- **Domain-neutral** — applicable across life domains without modification

**Response format:** All items use a 7-point Likert scale unless otherwise specified.
- 1 = Strongly Disagree / Almost Never
- 4 = Neutral / Sometimes
- 7 = Strongly Agree / Almost Always

**Scoring:** Raw items are summed within each dimension, then normalized to 0–1 using the dimension's item count. The composite GILE score uses canonical weights: G = 0.4142 (√2−1), I = 0.25, L = 0.18, E = 0.15.

## Section 1: GILE-G (Goodness Orientation) — 12 Items

**Construct:** Genuine moral motivation — doing good because it is good, not for reward, reputation, or social pressure. G is the directional anchor of the inner loops.

**Note:** G must be distinguished from moral performance (behavior) and moral reputation (what others see). Items focus on the motivational structure, not the outcome.

---

**G1.** When I do something kind for someone, I rarely think about what I'll get in return.

**G2.** I sometimes act ethically even when no one will ever know about it.

**G3.** When I notice myself rationalizing a questionable decision, I usually stop and reconsider.

**G4.** I can identify at least three times this month when I chose a harder but better path because it was the right thing to do. *(Behavioral anchor: specify incidents)*

**G5.** My ethical standards don't shift much based on who is watching or what is socially accepted in my current group.

**G6.** I find it genuinely uncomfortable to benefit from situations I know are unfair, even when I didn't create the unfairness.

**G7.** When I make a decision with moral weight, I think through how it affects everyone involved, not just myself and my close circle.

**G8.** I have passed up personal gain at least once because I thought it was the wrong way to get it.

**G9.** I am honest about my mistakes even when honesty makes me look bad.

**G10.** I feel a real sense of wrongness when I observe injustice, even in situations that don't affect me personally.

**G11.** My moral commitments stay roughly the same whether I am in a good mood or a bad one.

**G12.** I regularly ask myself whether my actions align with my deepest values, not just with my short-term preferences.

**Reverse-scored items:** None (all items are pro-G).

**Pilot validity markers:**
- G1–G3: moral motivation structure
- G4–G6: behavioral consistency
- G7–G9: scope (self vs. others) and honesty
- G10–G12: stability and reflective practice

## Section 2: GILE-I (Knowing / Intuition) — 12 Items

**Construct:** The value of genuinely knowing self and others — the pre-evidential apprehension faculty. I is the epistemic backbone; its accuracy and directedness determine whether L can be activated. Items cover both self-knowing and other-knowing, and the pre-evidential dimension.

**Note:** I is NOT intelligence (E-dominant) and NOT self-confidence (partly G-dependent). Items are designed to distinguish genuine knowing from performance of knowing.

---

**I1.** I often know what I truly feel about a situation before I can fully articulate why.

**I2.** When I meet someone new, I usually develop an accurate sense of who they are relatively quickly, even before they tell me much about themselves.

**I3.** I notice when my first impression of a person was wrong, and I update it.

**I4.** I can tell when I am rationalizing — using reasoning to justify a conclusion I reached for emotional or social reasons — and I flag it to myself.

**I5.** I know which of my personal strengths and weaknesses I am most prone to deny or underestimate.

**I6.** When a decision feels wrong to me before I have all the evidence, I take that feeling seriously as information rather than dismissing it.

**I7.** I have a clear sense of what I actually want, separate from what I think I should want or what others expect of me.

**I8.** I can usually accurately predict how a person I know well will respond to a situation, before they respond.

**I9.** I recognize patterns in my own behavior that I might prefer not to see.

**I10.** I trust my pre-analytical reading of a situation even when I cannot immediately explain it logically.

**I11.** I can identify the difference between genuine insight and a story I am telling myself to feel better.

**I12.** My self-knowledge includes awareness of my blind spots — areas where I know I systematically misjudge myself.

**Reverse-scored items:** None (all items are pro-I).

**Pilot validity markers:**
- I1, I6, I10: pre-evidential dimension (I-axis primacy)
- I2, I3, I8: other-knowing accuracy
- I4, I9, I11: self-deception detection
- I5, I7, I12: self-knowledge accuracy

**Behavioral supplement (I-accuracy task):**
Ask participants to rate 5 close others on 10 trait scales. Collect third-party ratings from those others. Compute accuracy correlation (r between participant's ratings and third-party self-ratings). This gives an I-behavioral score independent of self-report.

## Section 3: GILE-L (Love / Conscious Positive Regard) — 10 Items

**Construct:** Conscious positive regard directed toward a specific person or group. L requires I > 0 (I→L dependency, Prediction 1). Items test whether the positive regard is conscious and directed (GILE-L) vs. automatic/habitual (Physical Bonds, HEM-D2). Items must be read in the context of a specific target person (participant specifies one person before answering).

**Instruction to participant:** "Think of one person you feel close to — a friend, partner, family member, or colleague. Keep them in mind as you answer all items in this section."

---

**L1.** When I think about this person, I am aware of directing genuine warmth and goodwill toward them as a conscious act, not just a habit.

**L2.** My positive feelings toward this person are informed by actually knowing who they are — not just by comfort or familiarity.

**L3.** I consciously wish for this person's genuine flourishing, including outcomes that might be good for them but not especially convenient for me.

**L4.** I am aware, in real time, when I am being genuinely caring toward this person versus when I am simply going through the motions.

**L5.** My regard for this person has deepened as I have come to know them better (not just grown more comfortable or familiar).

**L6.** I can distinguish between caring about this person's happiness and caring about this person themselves — even when those lead to different actions.

**L7.** I consciously consider this person's inner life — what they actually value, feel, and care about — not just their surface behavior.

**L8.** My warmth toward this person is not primarily driven by how they make me feel, but by who they are.

**L9.** When I act toward this person from love, I am aware of choosing to do so.

**L10.** I can articulate at least three specific things I know about this person's character that my regard for them is based on.

**Reverse-scored items:** None.

**I→L dependency check:** After scoring GILE-I, verify: if GILE-I < 0.3 (very low knowing), GILE-L scores above 0.6 should be flagged as potentially misreporting (claiming L without I basis — may indicate Physical Bonds / habitual attachment being mistaken for conscious positive regard).

## Section 4: GILE-E (Aesthetics / Structural Recognition) — 10 Items

**Construct:** Recognition of structural excellence — the ability to perceive genuine quality, elegance, and coherence in any domain. E is the entire Aesthetics axis: the abstract substance of environment. Items distinguish aesthetic recognition (structural) from hedonic preference (pleasure-seeking).

**Note:** High GILE-E is NOT the same as having sophisticated tastes or preferring "refined" things. It is the faculty for seeing why something is genuinely excellent — whether it's a mathematical proof, a piece of music, a social system, or a blade of grass.

---

**E1.** I can usually tell when something in my domain is genuinely well-made vs. merely impressive-looking, even if I cannot immediately articulate the difference.

**E2.** I find myself drawn to work — in any field — that has an underlying structural integrity, even if it is not conventionally beautiful.

**E3.** I notice when a solution to a problem is elegant as opposed to merely effective.

**E4.** I can recognize quality in domains I am not expert in, by attending to structural features rather than surface style.

**E5.** I am bothered by unnecessary complexity or clutter in systems, objects, or ideas — not because they are ugly but because they are structurally inefficient.

**E6.** I feel a genuine sense of recognition when I encounter something excellently made — a response that is distinct from mere pleasure or surprise.

**E7.** I can distinguish between genuine structural quality and skillful imitation of quality.

**E8.** My aesthetic judgments tend to be consistent over time — things I found genuinely excellent years ago still seem so, and things I found structurally poor still do.

**E9.** I appreciate the beauty in precision and accuracy — a well-formed argument, a correct proof, a precisely calibrated measurement — not just in sensory experience.

**E10.** I can identify, in a domain I know well, the specific structural features that make the best work better than the merely good.

**Reverse-scored items:** None.

**Behavioral supplement (E-accuracy task):**
Present 10 stimuli in a domain familiar to the participant (e.g., 10 mathematical arguments ranging from flawed to elegant, or 10 architectural designs rated by domain experts). Participant rates quality on 1–7. Compute correlation with expert ratings. This is the GILE-E behavioral score used in Prediction 2.

## Section 5: Dependency and Composite Scoring

### 5.1 I→L Dependency Check (Prediction 1)
After administering all sections:
1. Compute raw I-score and raw L-score
2. Flag any participant with L-score ≥ 5.0 AND I-score ≤ 2.5 as a potential dependency violation
3. For flagged participants: administer the I-accuracy behavioral task (other-rating accuracy)
4. If behavioral I-accuracy r < 0.30 AND L-score ≥ 5.0: classify as L-without-I (theoretically violates I→L dependency)
5. Document and analyze these cases separately

### 5.2 Composite GILE Score
```
GILE_composite = 0.4142 × (G_score/7) + 0.25 × (I_score/7) + 0.18 × (L_score/7) + 0.15 × (E_score/7)
```
Where G_score = mean of 12 G items × 7, etc. The composite ranges 0–1.

Canonical thresholds:
- GILE_composite < 0.30: below average GILE
- 0.30–0.42: approaching Emerick Threshold
- 0.42 (= √2−1): Emerick Threshold — onset of stable GM/CCC coupling
- 0.42–0.60: above threshold, Radiant developing zone
- > 0.60: high-Radiant zone; PD ≥ 2.0 accessible with GM-sourced input

### 5.3 BOK Saturation Vector
```
Saturation_vector = (G_score > 4.9, I_score > 4.9, L_score > 4.9, E_score > 4.9)
BOK_saturation = count of True values in Saturation_vector
```
Full saturation = BOK_saturation = 4 (all dimensions above median = 4.9 on 7-point scale)
Partial = 2–3 dimensions above median
Low = 0–1 dimensions above median

### 5.4 L+E Spectre Balance Score (Prediction A/C from URB #616)
```
L_norm = L_score / 7
E_norm = E_score / 7
L_plus_E = L_norm + E_norm  # spectre magnitude proxy
LxE_asymmetry = |L_norm - E_norm|  # deviation from spectre balance
```
Spectre condition: LxE_asymmetry < 0.10 AND L_plus_E > 0.80 (both high AND balanced)

## Section 6: Administration Notes

**Total time:** ~20–25 minutes for all four scales + behavioral supplements.

**Order:** Administer in order G → I → L → E. Rationale: G sets the motivational context, I primes self-knowing before the L section (which explicitly depends on I), E comes last as the most abstract and domain-general.

**L-section target:** Participants specify their target person before beginning Section 3. Allow 30 seconds for this selection. Remind them to keep the same person in mind for all L items.

**Behavioral supplements:** Administer I-accuracy task and E-accuracy task after the self-report items, not before (to avoid anchoring self-report scores to behavioral task performance).

**Biometric integration (Oura Ring):**
When paired with Oura Ring data, use:
- HRV (RMSSD) → G-proxy (biometric)
- REM sleep duration (% of total) → I-proxy (biometric)
- Sleep score / restorative sleep → L-proxy (biometric)
- Temperature deviation from baseline → E-proxy (biometric, inverted: low deviation = high E-coherence)

The self-report GILE battery and biometric GILE vector should converge (predicted r ≥ 0.40 for each matched dimension; Prediction 7 tests G specifically at r ≥ 0.50).
