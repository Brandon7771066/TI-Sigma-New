## 1. The Free Pass Theorem

**Theorem (MI-HEM Free Pass):**
> For any MI statement S, HEM(S) > 0 — unconditionally.

**Proof:**

S exists as a statement. The act of uttering or thinking S exists as a cognitive event. The cognitive state induced by S exists as a neural/intentional state. The record of S exists as a communicable artifact.

All of these are HEM-real. HEM measures existential weight — the degree to which something IS. S IS, in each of the above senses, regardless of what S says. The existence of S is independent of the truth of S.

Therefore HEM(S) > 0 for any S whatsoever, including any MI statement.

The result extends: HEM(S) can be **arbitrarily large**. A MI belief held with Tralsity=1, generating massive emotional salience, driving significant behavior, structuring entire worldviews — this MI belief has enormous HEM weight. HEM places no ceiling on MI.

## 2. There Is No HEM-Invalid Nonsense

The traditional dismissal of nonsense: "that's just nonsense — it doesn't refer to anything, so it's existentially empty."

**This is wrong in TI Sigma.**

Nonsense is existentially real precisely as nonsense:
- The statement "the sky is both blue and not-blue in the same sense at the same time" — this statement EXISTS as a speech act ✅
- The cognitive confusion it induces EXISTS ✅
- The grip it exerts on a consciousness that believes it EXISTS, potentially massively ✅
- Its record in discourse EXISTS ✅

None of this existential weight requires the content to conform to external reality. **Nonsense exists without having to be about anything real.** The existence of the nonsense IS the HEM-valid fact.

Formally: the only HEM requirement is **being** — and nonsense is. There is no "HEM conformity to reality" test. HEM validates existence, not correctness. GILE-G validates correctness. These are orthogonal. Nonsense fails GILE-G; it trivially passes HEM.

## 5. The Meinong Resolution

The Western philosophical tradition has a relevant debate:
- **Meinong** claimed the round square has "being" (Sein) even without "existence" (Dasein) — objects can be "about" without being real
- **Russell** famously objected: the round square doesn't exist at all; to speak of it at all presupposes a referent, which is confused

**TI Sigma resolves the Meinong-Russell debate:**

Both were measuring different dimensions:
- Meinong was tracking **HEM validity**: the round square IS as a concept, a speech act, a cognitive event. Meinong was right.
- Russell was tracking **GILE-G consistency**: the claim "a round square exists" fails truth-correctness. Russell was right.

There is no contradiction: the round square has HEM validity (it IS a thing in cognitive and linguistic space) and lacks GILE-G truth correctness (the claim "round squares exist in geometry" is false). Both claims are simultaneously correct. The apparent paradox dissolves once HEM and GILE-G are recognized as orthogonal.

Meinong needed HEM. Russell needed GILE-G. TI Sigma provides both.

## 6. Implications for MR Strategy

Given that MI has unconditional and potentially enormous HEM weight, MR strategy must account for this:

**Never fight MI by attacking its HEM reality.** "Your belief doesn't really exist" or "that's just nothing" — these are false and counterproductive. The MI belief IS existentially real. Denying this closes off MR by invalidating the interlocutor's genuine existential experience of the MI state.

**MR fights MI by relabeling, not erasing.** The correct MR move:
1. Affirm: "Yes, this (state, belief, content) is existentially real — your HEM signal is valid."
2. Correct: "AND the truth label attached to it is Tralse, not True."
3. Distinguish: "The reality of the content and the correctness of its label are separate questions."

This is the MR-respecting move. It honors HEM, corrects GILE-G.

**The urgency of MR scales with HEM × (1 − GILE-G).** High HEM + low GILE-G = maximum MR urgency. Enormous existential weight carrying a wrong label — this is the most dangerous and most common form of MI. Civilizational MI (high HEM, zero GILE-G) is the most urgent target for MR.
