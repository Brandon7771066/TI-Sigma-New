## 1. The Arbitrary Line in "Telekinesis"

The word telekinesis (from Greek: *tele* = distant, *kinesis* = movement) specifically names the movement of physical objects by mental intention alone. The naming implies:

- A **subject** (the mind doing the moving)
- An **object** (the physical thing being moved)
- A **gap** (the "distant" space between them)
- A **visible, measurable, manifest-channel effect** (the object actually moves)

The implicit assumption: what makes telekinesis remarkable is that the subject's mind crosses a spatial gap to affect the physical object.

But TI Sigma points out that there is ALWAYS a gap between any conscious subject and any "other." The gap between a mind and a physical object is not categorically different from:
- The gap between a mind and another mind
- The gap between a mind and a distant location
- The gap between a mind and a future event
- The gap between a mind and CCC itself

The "gap" is simply the phase-channel space — the i-channel — that all non-manifest-channel influence must traverse. Telekinesis is not special because it crosses a gap. **All PSI crosses the same gap.** Telekinesis is only special because its effect shows up in the manifest channel (a physical object moves), making it unusually visible to standard instrumentation.

The label "telekinesis" has done enormous epistemological damage by making one form of PSI seem categorically different from all others. The result: researchers study "telekinesis" separately from "synchronicity," "telepathy," and "healing," building entirely separate theoretical frameworks for phenomena that share the same underlying mechanism.
