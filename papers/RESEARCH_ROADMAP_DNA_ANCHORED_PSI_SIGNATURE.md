## §2 — Strongest Defensible Reading (assistant's interpretation)

The proposal advances three distinct claims, each defensible at its own scope:

1. **DNA as substrate-anchor for LCC psi-signature** — DNA is the highest-resolution, most-universally-available, longitudinally-stable biological data modern science possesses. Per LCC Extension 1, ANY uniquely identifying data stream can serve as a latch substrate; DNA is the canonical such stream at the per-organism level. Framing DNA as a candidate substrate for the i-cell-level psi-signature is consistent with LCC's substrate-agnostic framing in the framework paper.

2. **Animal-models-first empirical validation pipeline** — methodologically correct. Use animal cohorts to isolate genetic contribution from confounds, validate the prediction mechanism under controlled conditions, then extrapolate to human cases. This follows standard pharmacology validation order.

3. **Multiple substrates as composite signature** — accepting that DNA + biophotons + EM-wave + microbiome + epigenetics could each be partial channels of one underlying signature is the right epistemological move. Different aspects of the same underlying phenomenon, in TI Sigma aspectual framing.

### 3.2 "Already-accurate psi pharmacology simulator" requires verification

Existing measured accuracy of LCC-family systems per `psi_accuracy_report.txt`:
- LCC Virus Framework: **35.1% adjusted, Tier F (rank 28/30)**
- LCC Optimization Simulator: **30.3% adjusted, Tier F (rank 30/30)**

The pharmacology simulator's specific accuracy is NOT separately reported in that document. The validation script `pharma_simulator_validation.py` exists and uses directional accuracy + magnitude-within-2× thresholds, but we'd need to RUN it on a clean test set to get current numbers. The proposal's "already accurate" framing should be treated as a hypothesis to verify, not an established baseline.

### 3.3 Wet-lab animal research blows the $50 budget by orders of magnitude

Realistic costs for a single small animal pilot:
- IACUC approval + vivarium access: $0 (in academic settings) — $5,000+ (commercial vivarium per-month)
- Animals (e.g., 20 mice): $200–800
- DNA sequencing (whole genome, 20 animals): $5,000–20,000
- Pharmacology assays + lab supplies: $3,000–10,000
- Researcher time: not directly billable but real
- **Minimum realistic pilot: ~$10,000–35,000**

This is decisively out-of-scope. The proposal must be reframed as either: (a) computational pilot using public datasets, (b) research grant proposal we DRAFT but do not execute in this budget, or (c) collaboration with an existing wet-lab that has IACUC + funding.

### 3.4 Animal psi research literature is contested

Existing published animal-psi literature includes Sheldrake's animal-prediction studies, Bechterev's 1920s Russian dog telepathy work, more recent rodent psi-tracking by Schmidt, Rhine-era work. Most is methodologically contested and replication-poor. We'd be entering a low-replicability domain, requiring extra-stringent pre-registration, blinding, and statistical rigor — and we should explicitly engage this literature in any paper we produce, rather than pretending we're starting from a clean field.
