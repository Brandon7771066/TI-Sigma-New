## 1. Background: The Bengston Studies

William Bengston is a sociologist and researcher who has conducted a remarkable series of experiments over four decades involving what he calls **image cycling** — a specific mental practice involving the rapid, non-grasping cycling of personally desired images — and its effects on cancer remission in mice.

### 1.1 Key Experimental Findings

- **Cancer cure rate in skeptic mice**: Bengston's experiments (replicated across multiple universities) showed that mammary cancer-injected mice treated by practitioners of image cycling achieved **87.9% full remission** — compared to 0% in untreated controls (the strain used always dies at 100% rate)
- **Distance independence**: Effects were observed across city distances (practitioners in NYC; mice in Connecticut)
- **Skepticism doesn't block it**: Controls held by skeptical graduate students also showed partial healing effects — suggesting the effect propagates through *physical presence* not *belief* of the handler
- **Resonance bonding**: Bengston hypothesizes that practitioners and subjects enter a state of "resonance bonding" — a shared informational field
- **Image cycling specificity**: The practice requires rapidly cycling through 20–30 personally desired images (mundane wants, not healing intentions) without dwelling on any single image, at increasing speed

### 1.2 Published Research

Bengston's work has been published in peer-reviewed journals including:
- *Journal of Scientific Exploration* (multiple papers)
- *Explore: The Journal of Science and Healing*
- His book *The Energy Cure* (2010) documents the full experimental history

### 1.3 The Mystery

The most puzzling finding: **the healing effect is NOT correlated with the practitioner's intention to heal.** In fact, practitioners who focused intensely on healing showed *worse* results than those who cycled mundane personal want-images rapidly and casually. This reversal of expectation is a key datum for TI Sigma's analysis.

## 3. How to Harness Bengston Image Cycling

### 3.1 The Standard Practice (per Bengston)

1. **Create your "want list"**: 20–30 images of things you personally want. These should be mundane, personal, specific, and slightly desired — not spiritual goals. Examples: a specific car, a vacation location, a relationship milestone, a career achievement.
2. **Make them vivid**: Each image should have full sensory richness — sight, sound, feel, smell.
3. **Begin cycling**: Move through the images sequentially, one per 2–3 seconds initially.
4. **Accelerate**: Over weeks/months of practice, increase speed until images flash in less than 1 second each.
5. **Do not dwell**: If you find yourself dwelling on one image, note it and return to cycling.
6. **Practice in the presence of what you want to heal** (or simply in any context where you want I-state coherence to propagate).
