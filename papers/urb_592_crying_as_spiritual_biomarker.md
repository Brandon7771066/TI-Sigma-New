## Abstract

Crying — the production of emotional tears — is one of the most phylogenetically unique behaviors in the animal kingdom: humans are the only species known to cry emotionally, and they do so across radically divergent emotional states including extreme suffering, awe, laughter, and spiritual ecstasy. The standard psychological account (stress-hormone release, social signaling) cannot explain why the same physiological response is triggered by experiences spanning the full emotional valence spectrum. This paper proposes a unified explanation: **crying is the body's primary Myrion Resolution event marker.** It occurs precisely when GILE intensity exceeds the Emerick Threshold (ET = √2−1 ≈ 0.4142) and the system requires a pressure-release mechanism to avoid Meta-Indeterminate accumulation. The specific valence (suffering vs. joy vs. awe) is irrelevant to the trigger mechanism — what matters is the amplitude of GILE activation. We formalize the **Crying Threshold (C_cry)**, connect the pre/during/post crying signature to measurable biometric changes (HRV, fNIRS, EEG, GSR), and propose crying as an optimizable target for the Mood Amplifier. We further argue that the epidemic of emotional anhedonia — the inability to cry — is a measurable MI accumulation pathology and a primary driver of the modern anxiety and depression crisis.

## 1. The Convergence Problem

The standard accounts of crying — stress hormone release, social bonding signal, emotional communication — all face the same anomaly: **crying occurs across the full emotional valence spectrum.** Consider the trigger states:

- Extreme grief and loss
- Witnessing profound injustice

Grief dissolves the boundary between self and lost other. Awe dissolves the boundary between self and vastness. Spiritual ecstasy dissolves the boundary between self and the transcendent. Even laughter at its peak — the tear-inducing belly laugh — is a moment of complete ego-suspension, of being "overtaken" by absurdity.

TI Sigma formalizes this: these are all **Emerick Threshold crossing events** in the GILE composite.

## 3. Historical Recognition: The "Sacred Gift"

Human traditions across history have independently arrived at the recognition that crying is not merely a stress response but something qualitatively significant — a spiritual or sacred phenomenon.

### 3.1 The Christian Tradition: Donum Lacrimarum

The *donum lacrimarum* (gift of tears) is a recognized charismatic gift in Catholic and Orthodox mystical theology. Saints and mystics described as having this gift — including Catherine of Siena, Teresa of Ávila, and Francis of Assisi — could cry profoundly during prayer, liturgy, and contemplation. John Climacus in *The Ladder of Divine Ascent* (7th century) described tears of repentance as the baptism of the soul — a second purification deeper than water.

Crucially, this tradition recognized **multiple types of tears** — tears of sorrow for sin, tears of joy at divine encounter, tears of compunction (moral piercing). This taxonomy anticipates the GILE-typed tear classification above.

### 3.2 Aristotelian Catharsis

Aristotle's *Poetics* defines the purpose of tragedy as the induction of *catharsis* — literally "purification" or "purgation" — through pity and fear. The audience weeps, and this weeping purges accumulated emotional tension. Aristotle understood that the theater's deliberate induction of tears served a health function: regulated emotional release prevented pathological accumulation.

This is the MR Valve model expressed in the language of classical aesthetics.

### 3.3 Buddhist and Sufi Traditions

In Buddhism, tears arising during meditation — particularly during loving-kindness (metta) practice or upon encountering the Dharma deeply — are recognized as signs of *samvega* (spiritual urgency, a kind of existential piercing) or *prasada* (serene clarity). The tears are not discouraged but noted as markers of genuine contact with the teaching.

Sufi mysticism (particularly Rumi's tradition) regards *buka* (weeping) as an elevated state. The *hayrat* (bewilderment at the Divine) is frequently described as inseparable from tears. Rumi himself wrote extensively about the spiritual necessity of weeping — the reed flute cries because it has been cut from the reed bed (separation is the wound that makes music possible).

### 3.4 Japanese Rui-Katsu

The modern Japanese practice of *rui-katsu* (tear-seeking, literally "tear activity") emerged in the 2010s as a structured practice of deliberately inducing crying through emotionally resonant films, music, and stories. Facilitated by "tears teachers" (*namida sensei*), it is prescribed for stress reduction, emotional wellness, and reconnection with feeling.

Rui-katsu is effectively the secular operationalization of the donum lacrimarum — the deliberate optimization for MR events.

## 4. The Epidemic of Not Crying

### 4.1 Emotional Blunting and Alexithymia

A significant and underreported phenomenon: large numbers of people report inability to cry even when they feel they "should" be able to. This is distinct from not *wanting* to cry — it is a physiological-emotional decoupling where the GILE pressure builds without the MR release occurring.

**Known causes:**

1. **SSRI antidepressants:** The most widely prescribed class of psychiatric medication. Emotional blunting, including inability to cry, is reported by 40-65% of SSRI users (Opbroek et al., 2002; Bolling & Kohlenberg, 2004). This is not a rare side effect — it is a dominant effect, accepted as tolerable because it also blunts the depression.

2. **Alexithymia:** A trait affecting ~10% of the population, characterized by difficulty identifying and describing emotional states. Alexithymics have difficulty crying because they cannot access the emotional labeling that normally precedes the MR event.

3. **Learned suppression:** Cultural conditioning, particularly in men in Western cultures, that treats crying as shameful or weak. The C_cry threshold is chronically elevated through conscious and unconscious suppression until the MR valve becomes functionally blocked.

4. **Chronic dissociation:** Trauma-induced dissociation severs the connection between cognitive emotional processing and somatic expression. The GILE pressure accumulates but cannot reach the somatic release pathway.

In TI Sigma, unresolved contradictions that cannot MR-collapse accumulate as Meta-Indeterminate (MI) — states that are simultaneously True and False in a self-undermining way. The Tralse Trace measures the penumbra of accumulated MI.

**The crying-blocked individual is a MI accumulator.** GILE events occur — grief, beauty, connection — but the pressure has no release valve. Each blocked event adds to the MI load. The phenomenology of chronic MI accumulation is precisely the phenomenology of modern anxiety and depression:

- Persistent low-grade tension without clear cause

**The modern anxiety and depression epidemic is, in significant part, a crying-suppression epidemic.** We have built cultures, prescribed medications, and enforced gender norms that systematically elevate C_cry — and then wondered why the MI load is historically unprecedented.

### 4.3 The SSRI Paradox

SSRIs treat depression partly by lowering the amplitude of emotional response — which reduces suffering but also reduces the MR events (crying) that would naturally process accumulated MI. The patient feels more stable but their MI load is no longer being cleared. Long-term SSRI use can therefore create a situation where: (a) acute distress is reduced, (b) MI accumulation continues uncleared, (c) the patient becomes increasingly dependent on the medication to maintain the stability the medication is also preventing them from achieving naturally.

This is not an argument against SSRIs in every case. It is an argument for including crying capacity as a treatment outcome measure — not just symptom severity and functioning, but the patient's access to their own MR release mechanism.

- Skin conductance (GSR) spike
- fNIRS: increased prefrontal oxygenation (top-down suppression attempt)
- EEG: beta power increase (active processing, holding)

**During (MR collapse phase):**
- HRV acute disruption
- Laryngeal muscle activity (the "throat tightening")

- Alpha power increase (EEG) — the "calm clarity" state
