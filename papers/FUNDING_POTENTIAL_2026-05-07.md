### #7: Clean corporate credit access in 6-8 months
**Status**: Forthcoming, separate from personal credit.

**Evidential strength: MODERATE-TO-STRONG (real but bounded)**
- ✅ Corporate credit lines (especially "shelf credit" via the standard Dun & Bradstreet → tradeline-building → high-limit-card sequence) are a real, well-documented capital-access path.
- ✅ Separation from personal credit is critical for protecting Brandon's personal financial position while operating at startup scale.
- ✅ 6-8 month timeline is consistent with the standard corporate-credit-build process.

**Honest caveats (#69)** — this is where the #69 framing is most important:
- ⚠️ **Corporate credit is DEBT, not investment.** It must be repaid. For a pre-revenue R&D-stage biotech, deploying corporate credit on operations creates burn-rate without the matched revenue to service it. This is a high-risk capital channel for early-stage biotech specifically.
- ⚠️ Corporate credit limits at the 6-8-month point are typically in the $25K-$150K aggregate range, not $1M+. Useful for working capital, equipment, conference travel, contractor invoices — not for funding a Phase I clinical program or wet-lab build-out.
- ⚠️ Using corporate credit to *bridge* between confirmed near-term funding events (SBIR award incoming, term sheet signed, customer payment landing) is sound. Using it to *substitute* for those events is the well-documented founder-debt-trap pattern.
- ⚠️ Personal-guarantee waivers ("no PG") on corporate credit at the early stage are uncommon. Even "separate from personal credit" cards usually require a personal guarantee at the application stage. Need to verify the actual structure being targeted.

**Net**: Real, useful, *bounded* tool. Best deployed as bridge capital against confirmed inflows, not as primary funding. **Assigned weight: 10-15% of Capital-axis update, with a hard caveat about debt-vs-equity nature.**

## §5 — Brutal-honesty meta-note (#69)

**What this audit is NOT**: an upgrade to A or A+. No closed funding, no signed term sheet, no awarded grant. A LOS is not a check.

**What this audit IS**: a meaningful and previously-undocumented update on the Capital-axis probability density. Pre-audit framing implied "Capital is the binding constraint with no clear path." Post-audit framing is "Capital is the binding constraint with multiple credible paths active and at least one near-term high-EV swing identified."

**The shift in *path-clarity* matters more than the shift in *amount*.** A binding constraint with no path forward is a fundamentally different posture than a binding constraint with three credible paths and one high-EV near-term event. The latter is operationally actionable; the former is just stuck.

**Honesty check on Brandon's framing**: Brandon's own description of these data points was unusually well-calibrated — he explicitly noted "no one actually committed" funding on the networking front, which is exactly the right honest read. That self-calibration discipline carries through to other parts of the funding posture and is itself a positive Capital-axis signal (calibrated founders are easier for investors to trust than over-pitching ones).

**What would move this to A**: One of the following — (a) SBIR Phase I awarded, (b) Startup Warrior writes a check or organizes a syndicate, (c) a tier-1 biotech VC takes a meeting and progresses to diligence, (d) a strategic pharma partner signs an option/license agreement, (e) corporate credit reaches $100K+ deployed against confirmed near-term inflows.

**What would walk this back**: (a) SBIR resubmission rejected with new fundamental critique, (b) Startup Warrior relationship cools or doesn't materialize into capital, (c) corporate credit access slips past 12 months, (d) burn rate materializes without revenue or grants.

**Cluster status**: With Capital-axis path-clarity now meaningfully improved, the case for an A− → A movement is gathering — but it remains contingent on at least one of the above closing events. Connections and Concept are already at A−/A. The Capital lever is the one that determines whether the cluster stabilizes at A− or moves to A.
