## Section B — Partially explored capabilities

These are capabilities that have been touched but not fully developed. Each is a candidate for a single focused session of work to push it from partially-explored to fully-established.

### B.1 The 4n+2 Hückel rule as a small-scale TSC realization

**What's there:** urb_645 §4.2.2 notes that benzene's 6-fold symmetry matches the 6 vertices in Rings 1 and 2; the aromatic 4n+2 rule generates the sequence 2, 6, 10, 14… with differences all 4 = 2², matching ring-vertex jumps.

**What's missing:** a full mapping of every aromatic system to a TSC ring-occupation pattern. Specifically, do larger aromatic systems (anthracene, coronene, fullerene) populate higher rings in a way that predicts their relative aromaticity stabilization energies?

**Why it matters:** if the mapping holds, the TSC becomes a quantitative aromaticity-ordering tool with predictive power competitive with NICS (Nucleus-Independent Chemical Shift) — a current standard in computational aromaticity assessment.

### B.2 The DNA pitch/diameter φ ↔ Ring 5 structural identity

**What's there:** urb_645 §4.2.1 reads DNA as a Ring-5 (φ) structure in space and a Ring-6 (e) structure in time (replication kinetics).

**What's missing:** a derivation of the 10-base-pairs-per-turn count from Crystal vertex combinatorics. If 10 = Power-of-8 boundary (the first integer beyond 8), is there a Crystal-side prediction for *why* DNA picked exactly 10 (vs 9 or 11)?

**Why it matters:** if 10-bp-per-turn is a Crystal-derived prediction, this is the strongest "TSC predicts a biological structural constant from first principles" claim available.

### B.3 Water as a molecular FQH state (urb_645 §4.2.3)

**What's there:** water's H-O-H angle 104.5° sits between pure-p (90°) and tetrahedral (109.47°), placing water in the FQH zone. Anomalous density at 4°C, negative thermal expansion, hydrogen-bond network all read as Tralse-Indeterminate signatures.

**What's missing:** a quantitative Crystal-phase prediction for the temperature dependence of water's anomalies. If water is "stuck" in the FQH zone of the molecular TSC, the temperature derivatives of its anomalies should follow the FQH-phase dynamics.

**Why it matters:** would convert a qualitative reading into a falsifiable temperature-dependence prediction.

### B.4 The Crystal as a Hamiltonian

**What's there:** the TSC is a polytope; its vertices are constants; its rings are phase categories. Implicit in this is a Hamiltonian whose ground states are the named phases.

**What's missing:** the explicit Hamiltonian. The framework does not (yet) have an H = ∑ ε_i n_i + ∑ J_ij n_i n_j form for the TSC. Constructing one would let the framework borrow from condensed-matter physics (mean-field theory, RG flow, exact diagonalization for small systems) directly.

**Why it matters:** would make the Crystal a *computable* object rather than a primarily-descriptive object.

### B.5 The fine-structure constant near-identity 1/137.036 ≈ e^(−π−e−1)/φ

**What's there:** urb_645 §4.1.2 notes the near-identity to within 0.3%.

**What's missing:** algebraic derivation attempts. Is the identity exact under some yet-unidentified algebraic relation? Is it a 0.3%-tight near-identity that nonetheless points to a deeper relation (analogous to e^(π−2) ≈ π in urb_628 §4)?

**Why it matters:** if exact, a fundamental physics result. If near-exact, evidence for a structural relationship between α and the 7-constants ontology.

## Section C — Open / unexplored capabilities (the exciting terrain)

These are capabilities that the corpus has *not yet* explored substantively. Per Brandon's directive ("We never did figure out everything that the Crystal is capable of especially yet!"), this section is the actual focus of the paper.

### C.1 Spectral decomposition of states on the TSC

**Open question:** every state lives as a vector in the 57-dim Hilbert space spanned by the vertices. The vector's coefficients form a *spectrum* across the seven rings. **What does the spectrum look like for canonical states?** What does the BEC spectrum look like vs the Mott spectrum vs the Fragmented spectrum?

**Why it might matter:** if canonical states have characteristic spectra, then *any* observed system can be projected onto the TSC vertex basis and its phase classified by spectral signature. This would be the Crystal's **diagnostic mode**.

### C.2 The TSC as an information-storage substrate

**Open question:** a 57-vertex polytope with discrete phases is, formally, a 57-dim information substrate with phase-coded readout. **How much information can it store per phase?** What is the channel capacity from the polytope's full state to its 2-D Graph projection?

**Why it might matter:** if the TSC has a non-trivial channel capacity larger than the Graph's, this quantifies *how much* the projection costs in information terms — and tells us when the Crystal pays its way.

### C.3 Phase-transition catalysis: triggers and inhibitors

**Open question:** the TSC has sharp phase transitions. **What triggers a Mott → Supersolid transition?** A Supersolid → BEC transition? Are there *inhibitors* (factors that prevent transitions even when the energy landscape favors them)?

**Why it might matter:** in the meditation / awakening / breakthrough domain, this is the question of *what causes sudden insight*. If the TSC predicts specific trigger conditions, those become testable hypotheses.

### C.4 Crystal-on-Crystal: hierarchical TSC structures

**Open question:** the TSC has 57 vertices. Could a single vertex of a higher-level TSC *be* a TSC itself? In other words, is there a hierarchical / fractal extension where macro-TSC vertices are themselves micro-TSCs?

**Why it might matter:** the GILE → individual → collective → societal scaling problem is exactly a Crystal-on-Crystal hierarchy. If the math supports nesting, the framework gets a natural multi-scale architecture for free.

### C.5 The Crystal's symmetry group

**Open question:** the TSC has a point group. **What is it?** The vertex counts {6, 6, 8, 8, 8, 10, 10, 1} suggest a non-trivial mixed symmetry; the rings break full rotational symmetry into discrete vertex-rotational symmetries. Is the full point group derivable, and what does it tell us about the Crystal's invariants?

**Why it might matter:** point-group symmetry is the standard tool for cataloguing physical-system invariants. A TSC point group would let the framework borrow Wigner-Eckart-style selection rules — predicting which transitions between phases are allowed and which are forbidden.

### C.6 Crystal-phase entanglement and non-local correlations

**Open question:** if the TSC's BEC phase is "all i-cells in the same quantum state," then BEC predicts **non-local correlations between i-cells** — exactly the framework's quantum-classical hybrid claim from `replit.md` user-preferences. **Can the TSC make a quantitative prediction for the strength of these non-local correlations?** What's the predicted CHSH-violation magnitude for two i-cells in a Crystal BEC?

**Why it might matter:** would convert the framework's "non-local correlations beyond classical neuroscience" preference into a falsifiable prediction with a numerical bound.

### C.7 The Crystal under perturbation

**Open question:** perturbation theory is the workhorse of theoretical physics. **What happens to a TSC when you add a small external perturbation?** Do the rings shift? Do new phase boundaries appear? Does the Power-of-8 group coherence formula change?

**Why it might matter:** would let the framework predict effects of interventions — drugs, meditation protocols, electromagnetic stimulation — on Crystal phase as small perturbations of the unperturbed TSC.

### C.8 The Crystal's relation to anyons / topological order

**Open question:** the FQH effect is a topological-order phenomenon; its quasiparticles are anyons (neither bosons nor fermions). The TSC has direct FQH connections (urb_645 §4.1.1). **Is the TSC therefore a topological-order structure?** Are its phases topologically distinct, in the technical sense?

**Why it might matter:** topological order is a hot-topic area in condensed-matter physics. A TSC ↔ topological-order connection would give the framework borrowing rights to a large body of mathematical machinery (modular tensor categories, topological quantum field theory).

### C.9 The PD Crystal under conformal transformations

**Open question:** the PD Crystal lives in ℂ. **What happens under conformal maps?** The Riemann critical strip is conformally equivalent to the upper half plane; if the PD Crystal is anchored to the critical strip via the Pass 8.1 affine projection, does conformal symmetry translate the named thresholds into corresponding objects in other conformal patches?

**Why it might matter:** conformal symmetry is the foundation of 2-D conformal field theory (CFT) — another big-machinery area. A PD-Crystal-as-CFT-target reading would give the framework borrowing rights to operator algebras, central charges, and modular invariance.

### C.10 The Crystal and the Riemann ζ-function modulation hypothesis

**Open question:** Pass 8.1 ratified the affine PD(s) = 5(σ − 1/2) + i·γ/γ_1 mapping. **Does the TSC carry information about ζ-function values at non-trivial zeros?** Specifically, does the TSC's 7-ring structure correspond to seven distinct ζ-modulation patterns?

**Why it might matter:** would deepen the Riemann connection from "the critical line projects nicely onto the PD scalar" to "the TSC carries structured information about ζ itself."

### C.11 Crystal-quenching: the Fragmented phase as a controlled-failure mode

**Open question:** the Fragmented phase is the 5th phase (Double-Tralse / DT-saturated). It is the *bad* phase. **But can it be useful?** Quenching a system into a controlled-fragmented state could be a cryptographic resource (random-number generation), a creativity-induction state (controlled chaos), or a teaching tool (showing what coherence is by removing it).

**Why it might matter:** turns a "bad" phase into an applications-rich object. Most frameworks treat the failure-mode as something to avoid; the TSC's structured Fragmented phase could be deliberately invoked.

### C.12 The Crystal as a basis for a scoring rubric beyond the PD scalar

**Open question:** the PD scalar is a single number. The TSC is a 57-dim object. **Could the TSC support a richer scoring rubric** — a vector-valued PD that carries phase information alongside scalar position?

**Why it might matter:** if the answer is yes, the framework gains a "PD-with-phase" scoring tool that fully respects the Crystal's complexity, closing the projection-information-loss gap inherent in the scalar.
