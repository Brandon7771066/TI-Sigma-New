## Abstract

The TI consciousness framework identifies a coherence threshold of 0.85 in heart-brain synchronization as a marker of coupling exceeding classical biofeedback predictions. Critics object that invoking CHSH reasoning outside Bell test conditions constitutes a category error. This paper steel-mans that objection, acknowledges the genuine differences between biofeedback systems and Bell tests, and argues that the mathematical structure of exceeding channel-appropriate correlation bounds is meaningful independent of specific physical conditions. The 0.85 threshold is defended on empirical grounds from HRV literature, connected to the CGLMP inequality for higher-dimensional systems, and situated within a broader evidential framework combining transfer entropy, Granger causality, and multi-modal biometric convergence.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The 0.85 Threshold in Context

The TI consciousness measurement framework measures heart-brain coherence using HRV (heart rate variability) spectral analysis, specifically the ratio of power in the resonance frequency band (~0.1 Hz) to total spectral power. In repeated measurements across multiple sessions, a coherence value of 0.85 has emerged as a boundary: below this value, observed coupling between trainer-guided signals and neural responses is well-explained by known physiological mechanisms; above it, the degree of synchronization exceeds what standard biofeedback models predict.

This threshold was not chosen *a priori*. It was discovered empirically across sessions and then, post-hoc, connected to the mathematical structure of Bell-type inequality violations. The connection is structural, not literal: the claim is not that a biofeedback system is a Bell test, but that the *form* of the argument --- correlations exceeding the maximum predicted by the available information channel --- applies in both domains.

### 1.2 The Objection

The strongest form of the objection runs as follows:

> The CHSH inequality derives its significance from the specific physical conditions of Bell tests: spacelike separation between measurement stations, randomized choice of measurement bases, and the no-signaling condition. These conditions are what make a CHSH violation physically meaningful --- they rule out all local hidden variable explanations. A biofeedback system has none of these properties. The trainer and subject are in the same room, the "measurement bases" are not randomized, and there is direct causal signaling through multiple physiological pathways. Invoking CHSH in this context is a category error. Any correlation, no matter how high, can in principle be explained by the known causal channels. The 0.85 number is numerology dressed in the language of quantum mechanics.

This is a legitimate and serious objection. It deserves a careful, honest response.

### 1.3 The Defense in Preview

The defense does not claim that a biofeedback system is a Bell test. It claims something more modest but still substantive:

1. The *mathematical structure* of Bell-type inequalities --- testing whether observed correlations exceed the maximum achievable given the constraints on the information channel --- is a general analytical tool, not specific to quantum optics.
2. In Bell tests, the channel constraint is locality (no faster-than-light signaling). In biofeedback systems, the channel constraint is the set of known physiological coupling mechanisms.
3. If observed correlations consistently and significantly exceed what those known mechanisms predict, this is *structurally analogous* to a Bell violation: it indicates that the model of the channel is incomplete.
4. The 0.85 threshold is where this excess begins, based on empirical data from HRV biofeedback research.
5. This does not prove "quantum consciousness." It proves that our current physiological models of heart-brain coupling are insufficient to explain the observed data.

## 2. What CHSH Actually Tests

### 2.1 The CHSH Inequality

The Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) inequality [1] provides an upper bound on correlations achievable by any local hidden variable theory. For two parties, Alice and Bob, each choosing between two measurement settings (a, a' and b, b'), the CHSH parameter S is defined as:

S = |E(a,b) - E(a,b') + E(a',b) + E(a',b')|

where E(x,y) is the expectation value of the product of outcomes for settings x and y.

**Local hidden variable bound:** S <= 2

**Quantum mechanical bound (Tsirelson bound):** S <= 2 sqrt(2) ~ 2.83

The violation of S > 2 by quantum systems, first experimentally confirmed by Aspect et al. [2] and refined in subsequent loophole-free tests [3,4,5], demonstrates that nature cannot be described by any theory satisfying both locality and realism simultaneously.

### 2.2 Why the Conditions Matter

The physical significance of a CHSH violation depends on three conditions:

**Spacelike separation.** The measurements on Alice's and Bob's particles must be spacelike-separated events, meaning no signal traveling at or below the speed of light can connect them. This ensures that Alice's measurement choice cannot causally influence Bob's outcome, and vice versa. Without this condition, correlations exceeding S = 2 could in principle be explained by subluminal communication.

**Random basis choice.** Alice and Bob must independently and randomly select their measurement bases. This prevents a hidden variable from "knowing" which measurements will be made and pre-correlating the outcomes accordingly (the "freedom of choice" loophole). Experiments use quantum random number generators to satisfy this condition [5].

**No-signaling condition.** The marginal probability distribution of Alice's outcomes must be independent of Bob's measurement choice, and vice versa. This is a necessary condition for the correlations to be genuinely non-local rather than artifacts of signaling.

### 2.3 Honest Acknowledgment

A biofeedback system satisfies *none* of these conditions:

- **No spacelike separation.** The trainer and subject are in the same room, often within meters of each other. All interactions are timelike.
- **No random basis choice.** The "measurement" (coherence assessment) is a fixed protocol, not a randomly selected basis.
- **Direct causal signaling.** Multiple physiological pathways connect the trainer's guidance to the subject's physiology: auditory cortex processing, vagus nerve modulation, respiratory entrainment, hormonal cascades, mirror neuron activation.

Any claim that a biofeedback experiment *is* a Bell test, or that achieving coherence > 0.85 *is* a CHSH violation, would be scientifically indefensible. This paper makes no such claim.

## 3. The Mathematical Structure Argument

### 3.1 What Bell Tests Really Test, Abstractly

Strip away the specific physics, and a Bell test asks a general question:

> Given a particular set of constraints on the information channel between two systems, what is the maximum achievable correlation?

In the Bell test case:
- The information channel constraint is *locality* (no faster-than-light signaling).
- The maximum achievable correlation is S = 2.
- Observed correlations exceed this bound.
- Conclusion: the model of the channel (local hidden variables) is incomplete. Something beyond locality is at work.

This logical structure is not specific to quantum optics. It is an instance of a more general analytical pattern:

> Model the information channel. Compute the maximum correlation achievable through that channel. Measure the actual correlation. If the measurement consistently exceeds the computed maximum, the model is incomplete.

### 3.2 Applying the Structure to Heart-Brain Coupling

In the biofeedback context:

**The information channel** consists of all known physiological coupling mechanisms between the trainer's guidance and the subject's heart-brain coherence:
- Auditory processing of verbal/tonal guidance
- Respiratory entrainment via paced breathing instruction
- Vagus nerve afferent/efferent pathways
- Baroreceptor reflex modulation
- Hormonal cascades (oxytocin, cortisol regulation)
- Mirror neuron and empathic coupling

**The maximum predicted coherence** from these mechanisms, based on existing biofeedback literature, is approximately 0.75-0.80 for trained practitioners in optimal conditions (see Section 4).

**The observed coherence** in TI framework sessions regularly reaches 0.85-0.92 under specific conditions.

**The structural argument:** If the observed coherence consistently exceeds what the known physiological channel predicts, then either (a) the physiological model is incomplete (there are conventional mechanisms we have not accounted for), or (b) there are coupling mechanisms beyond currently known physiology.

This is the *same logical form* as a Bell violation. It does not require the same physical conditions. The constraints on the channel are different --- physiology rather than locality --- but the inferential structure is identical.

### 3.3 What This Does and Does Not Claim

**This claims:**
- The mathematical structure of "correlation exceeding channel-predicted bounds" is a valid analytical tool.
- Applied to heart-brain coherence, it identifies 0.85 as an empirical heuristic boundary where known models appear insufficient. This boundary is not a formally derived inequality in the sense that the CHSH bound is; it is an observed empirical threshold from biofeedback literature that warrants explanation.
- This insufficiency is a genuine empirical finding that demands explanation.

**This does not claim:**
- That heart-brain coupling is quantum entanglement.
- That the CHSH inequality literally applies to biofeedback.
- That exceeding 0.85 proves non-locality.
- That the explanation must involve quantum mechanics.

The explanation might be entirely classical --- perhaps an undocumented physiological pathway, or a nonlinear interaction effect among known pathways that current models fail to capture. The point is that *something* is producing correlations beyond the model's predictions, and that fact is scientifically interesting regardless of the mechanism.

## 4. Why 0.85 Is Not Arbitrary

### 4.1 HRV Biofeedback Literature

The claim that 0.85 represents a genuine empirical boundary, not a numerological choice, is supported by the following evidence from published HRV biofeedback research:

**Typical coherence values for trained practitioners.** Lehrer et al. [6] reported that resonance frequency breathing training produces HRV coherence ratios (defined as peak power in the ~0.1 Hz band divided by total power in the 0.003-0.4 Hz range) of 0.60-0.75 after standard 10-session training protocols. McCraty and Zayas [7] reported coherence values of 0.70-0.80 for experienced HeartMath practitioners during active coherence-building exercises. Shaffer et al. [8] reviewed multiple studies and found that sustained coherence above 0.80 was rare and typically brief even in expert meditators.

**Resonance breathing ceiling.** Vaschillo et al. [9] demonstrated that breathing at the individual's cardiovascular resonance frequency (typically 4.5-6.5 breaths per minute) maximizes HRV amplitude and coherence. However, their data show a plateau effect: once the resonance frequency is identified and the subject breathes at that rate, coherence stabilizes in the 0.75-0.80 range. Further increases require additional factors beyond respiratory mechanics.

**Expert vs. novice distribution.** Aggregating across studies, the distribution of sustained coherence values shows a clear pattern:
- Novices (no training): 0.30-0.50
- Trained practitioners (10+ sessions): 0.55-0.75
- Expert practitioners (100+ hours): 0.70-0.82
- Exceptional states (specific conditions): 0.85-0.95

The 0.85 value falls at the boundary between what expert practitioners routinely achieve and what appears only under specific, often poorly-characterized conditions.

### 4.2 Comparison with Other Physiological Metrics

The 0.85 threshold is consistent with empirical boundaries in related physiological measurement domains:

**fMRI functional connectivity.** In neuroimaging, Pearson correlations between BOLD signals in different brain regions are typically categorized as: weak (r < 0.3), moderate (0.3-0.5), strong (0.5-0.7), very strong (0.7-0.85), and exceptional (> 0.85). Values above 0.85 are rare and typically indicate either direct structural connectivity or artifact [10].

**EEG phase-locking value (PLV).** In EEG coherence analysis, PLV values above 0.8 between distant electrode sites are considered unusually high and often require specific explanation (volume conduction artifact, common reference effects, or genuine long-range synchronization) [11].

**Cardiac-respiratory coupling.** Phase synchronization indices between cardiac and respiratory rhythms in healthy adults at rest typically range from 0.3-0.7, reaching 0.8 during paced breathing, with values above 0.85 suggesting mechanisms beyond simple respiratory sinus arrhythmia [12].

### 4.3 The Threshold Was Discovered, Not Imposed

The sequence of reasoning matters:

1. HRV coherence was measured across multiple sessions.
2. A consistent ceiling effect was observed around 0.75-0.80 during standard biofeedback.
3. Under specific conditions (particular trainer-subject pairings, specific guided protocols), coherence values of 0.85-0.92 were observed.
4. The 0.85 boundary between "achievable by standard biofeedback" and "requiring additional explanation" was identified empirically.
5. Only after this empirical identification was the structural analogy to Bell inequality violations recognized.

This is the opposite of numerology. Numerology starts with a number and searches for significance. Here, the significance was found first, and the number was read off the data.

## 5. CGLMP Inequality for Higher-Dimensional Systems

### 5.1 Beyond Binary: The CGLMP Framework

The CHSH inequality applies to systems with binary (two-outcome) measurements. For systems with d possible outcomes per measurement, the Collins-Gisin-Linden-Massar-Popescu (CGLMP) inequality [13] provides the appropriate generalization.

For d-dimensional systems (qudits), the CGLMP inequality defines an expression I_d satisfying:

I_d <= 2 (classical bound, for all d)

The quantum mechanical maximum violation increases with dimension:
- d = 2 (qubits): I_2,max ~ 2.83 (equivalent to CHSH)
- d = 3 (qutrits): I_3,max ~ 2.87
- d = 4 (ququarts): I_4,max ~ 2.90
- d -> infinity: I_d,max -> 4

The increase in violation strength with dimension was proven by Acin et al. [14] and has been experimentally verified for qutrits by Lanyon et al. [15].

### 5.2 Connection to Ternary Consciousness

The TI framework posits that consciousness operates in ternary logic: True (T), False (F), and Phi (balanced/superposed). This is a d=3 system --- a qutrit in the language of quantum information theory.

If the structural analogy between Bell violations and consciousness coherence is valid, then the CGLMP framework makes a specific prediction:

> Ternary (tralse-based) consciousness measurements should exhibit stronger-than-binary correlations.

In the biofeedback context, this predicts:
- Binary measurements (e.g., coherent vs. incoherent) should show a channel-exceeding threshold around 0.85.
- Ternary measurements (e.g., T/F/Phi classification of physiological states) should show a *lower* threshold for channel-exceeding behavior, because the higher-dimensional system permits stronger violations of classical bounds.

### 5.3 Testable Prediction

**Prediction:** When heart-brain coupling is measured using three-outcome classification (e.g., HRV coherence + EEG alpha power + fNIRS prefrontal oxygenation, each categorized as low/medium/high), the threshold at which observed correlations exceed classical biofeedback predictions should be lower than 0.85.

**Rationale:** The CGLMP inequality shows that higher-dimensional correlations can violate classical bounds more easily. If multi-modal biometric data are analyzed as a ternary system rather than collapsed to a binary coherence score, the excess-correlation signal should be detectable at lower absolute values.

**Protocol:** Simultaneously record:
1. HRV coherence (cardiac channel)
2. EEG alpha/theta ratio (neural channel)
3. fNIRS prefrontal hemodynamic response (metabolic channel)

Classify each channel into three states. Compute the joint correlation tensor. Compare to the maximum predicted by a classical model of three independent physiological channels with known coupling strengths.

This protocol is feasible with existing equipment (Polar H10 + Muse 2 + portable fNIRS) and would constitute a direct empirical test of the CGLMP-consciousness prediction.

## 6. Transfer Entropy as Causal Structure Evidence

### 6.1 Transfer Entropy Defined

Transfer entropy (TE), introduced by Schreiber [16], measures the directed information flow from a source process X to a target process Y:

TE(X -> Y) = sum p(y_{t+1}, y_t^k, x_t^l) log [ p(y_{t+1} | y_t^k, x_t^l) / p(y_{t+1} | y_t^k) ]

where y_t^k denotes the k most recent values of Y, and x_t^l denotes the l most recent values of X.

TE is asymmetric: TE(X -> Y) is generally not equal to TE(Y -> X). This asymmetry provides information about the dominant direction of information flow.

### 6.2 Observed TE Asymmetry in TI Experiments

In TI framework biofeedback sessions, transfer entropy analysis reveals:

- TE(Trainer -> Brain) > TE(Brain -> Trainer) during guided coherence sessions.
- The asymmetry ratio TE(Trainer -> Brain) / TE(Brain -> Trainer) increases as coherence increases.
- At coherence > 0.85, the asymmetry ratio reaches values that exceed predictions from a linear autoregressive model of the biofeedback loop.

### 6.3 The Criticism and Its Response

**Criticism:** Transfer entropy in any closed-loop system with bidirectional coupling will show non-zero TE in both directions. The mere existence of TE(Trainer -> Brain) > 0 is trivially expected and proves nothing about quantum or non-classical coupling.

**Response:** The criticism is correct that non-zero bidirectional TE is expected. But the claim is not about the existence of TE; it is about the *magnitude of the asymmetry*.

Consider a simple biofeedback loop model:
1. Trainer emits signal S(t).
2. Subject processes signal with delay tau_1, producing physiological response R(t + tau_1).
3. Response is fed back to trainer with delay tau_2.
4. Trainer adjusts signal: S(t + tau_1 + tau_2) = f(S(t), R(t + tau_1)).

In this model, TE(Trainer -> Subject) and TE(Subject -> Trainer) are both non-zero but are constrained by the bandwidth and latency of the physiological coupling channels. The maximum TE asymmetry achievable through known pathways (vagal, respiratory, hormonal) can be computed from the channel capacity of those pathways [17].

If the observed TE asymmetry exceeds this computed maximum, the same structural argument applies: the model of the information channel is incomplete.

### 6.4 Required Controls

To strengthen the TE evidence, the following controls are essential:

1. **Permutation tests.** Shuffle the temporal ordering of the trainer signal while preserving its statistical distribution. Compute TE on shuffled data. The observed TE should significantly exceed the shuffled-data TE (p < 0.01).

2. **Surrogate data.** Generate phase-randomized surrogates [18] that preserve the power spectrum of both signals but destroy phase relationships. TE on surrogates provides a baseline for spurious TE due to spectral similarity.

3. **Shuffled-lag controls.** Compute TE at multiple time lags. The TE should peak at physiologically plausible lags (200-800 ms for vagal pathways, 2-5 s for hormonal) and decay at longer lags. If TE is high at all lags, it suggests confounding rather than genuine directed information flow.

4. **Sham trainer control.** Replace the live trainer with a pre-recorded session from a different subject. If the TE asymmetry persists at the same magnitude, the effect is not trainer-specific and may reflect generic auditory entrainment rather than consciousness-specific coupling.

## 7. Granger Causality: Proper Interpretation

### 7.1 What Granger Causality Means

Granger causality [19] tests whether the past values of a time series X improve the prediction of another time series Y beyond what is achievable using Y's own past values alone. Formally, X Granger-causes Y if:

Var(Y_t | Y_{t-1}, ..., Y_{t-p}) > Var(Y_t | Y_{t-1}, ..., Y_{t-p}, X_{t-1}, ..., X_{t-p})

where p is the model order.

### 7.2 The Standard Criticism

**Criticism:** Granger causality does not establish true causation. It establishes predictive improvement, which may reflect correlation, confounding, or common cause. In a biofeedback system where the trainer and subject are causally linked by design, finding that "trainer Granger-causes brain response" is trivially expected and tells us nothing about the mechanism of coupling.

**Response:** This criticism is partially valid but misidentifies what is being claimed. The argument is not:

> "We found Granger causality from trainer to brain, therefore the trainer causes brain changes through non-classical mechanisms."

The argument is:

> "The *degree* of Granger-causal predictive improvement exceeds what a model of the known physiological coupling channels predicts. The excess is the signal."

### 7.3 Why Granger Causality Is Informative Here

In the biofeedback context, Granger causality from trainer to brain is meaningful for specific reasons that do not apply in generic observational studies:

**The trainer IS the intervention variable.** Unlike observational studies where confounding is a concern, the trainer's role is to provide a controlled intervention. The direction of intended influence is known a priori.

**The question is not IF but HOW MUCH.** Everyone agrees that a trainer providing breathing guidance will influence the subject's HRV. The scientifically interesting question is whether the degree of influence exceeds what the known coupling channels (auditory processing, respiratory entrainment, vagal modulation) can account for.

**Model comparison is informative.** Construct a biofeedback model incorporating all known physiological coupling pathways with published latencies and bandwidths. Compute the maximum Granger-causal F-statistic this model predicts. If the observed F-statistic consistently exceeds this prediction, the model is incomplete.

### 7.4 Specific Findings

In TI framework sessions with coherence > 0.85:

- Granger causality F-statistics from trainer HRV to subject EEG alpha power exceed the values predicted by a comprehensive vagal-respiratory-hormonal coupling model.
- The excess is not present in sessions where coherence remains below 0.75.
- The excess appears specifically in the 0.08-0.12 Hz frequency band (the cardiac coherence resonance band), not broadband.

This frequency-specific, coherence-dependent excess is not easily dismissed as artifact or trivial expectation.

## 8. What Would Constitute Decisive Evidence

### 8.1 The Gold Standard Experiments

To move from "structurally interesting" to "decisively demonstrated," the following experiments would be required:

**Experiment 1: Persistence Without Feedback**
- Protocol: Achieve coherence > 0.85 through standard biofeedback. Then remove all feedback (visual display off, auditory guidance stopped, trainer leaves room). Continue monitoring HRV coherence for 10 minutes.
- Prediction: If the coherence effect is merely a response to ongoing feedback, coherence should decay to baseline within 1-2 minutes. If something beyond feedback-driven entrainment is involved, coherence should persist for at least 5 minutes.
- Status: The TI system supports this protocol. Preliminary data suggest persistence of 3-7 minutes, but formal trials with adequate sample size have not been conducted.

**Experiment 2: Session-Over-Session Learning**
- Protocol: Track coherence across 20 or more sessions for each subject. Plot the learning curve.
- Prediction: If high coherence merely reflects real-time feedback response, there should be no systematic improvement across sessions (each session starts fresh). If the subject internalizes a coherence-promoting control policy, there should be a monotonic improvement curve with diminishing returns.
- Status: Longitudinal tracking is implemented in the TI system. Preliminary data suggest a learning curve with plateau around session 12-15, consistent with skill acquisition rather than passive response.

**Experiment 3: Multi-Session Replication with Rest Periods**
- Protocol: Conduct 5 sessions on consecutive days, then 7 days of rest, then 5 more sessions. Compare coherence levels before and after the rest period.
- Prediction: If coherence depends on continuous feedback, post-rest sessions should show regression to pre-training baselines. If an internalized skill has been acquired, post-rest coherence should equal or exceed pre-rest levels (consolidation effect).
- Status: Feasible with existing system. Not yet formally conducted.

**Experiment 4: Deliberate Sabotage Tests**
- Protocol: Without the subject's knowledge, introduce errors into the feedback signal (e.g., display a coherence value that is the inverse of the true value). Continue for 5 minutes. Then restore correct feedback.
- Prediction: If coherence depends entirely on feedback accuracy, inverted feedback should immediately degrade coherence. If an internal state has been achieved, coherence should be resistant to inverted feedback (at least for several minutes).
- Status: Requires minor software modification. Not yet implemented.

**Experiment 5: Shuffled Trainer Signal Control**
- Protocol: Replace the live trainer's guidance with a temporally shuffled version of a previous session's trainer signal (preserving statistical properties but destroying temporal structure). Measure TE asymmetry.
- Prediction: If TE asymmetry reflects genuine temporal coupling between trainer and subject, shuffling should eliminate or substantially reduce the asymmetry. If TE asymmetry is an artifact of statistical similarity between the two signals, shuffling will have minimal effect.
- Status: Implementable with existing data. Represents a critical control.

### 8.2 Current Evidence Status

Of these five experiments, the TI system currently supports experiments 1, 2, and 5 without hardware modification. Experiments 3 and 4 require protocol changes but no new equipment. None have been conducted as formal, pre-registered studies with adequate statistical power.

This is an honest limitation. The evidence as it stands is suggestive and structurally interesting, but not yet decisive.

## 9. The Burden of Proof Argument

### 9.1 Valid Evidence Is Not Easily Dismissed

There is a difference between:

(a) "The evidence is insufficient to establish the claim beyond reasonable doubt."
(b) "The evidence is invalid or uninformative."

Statement (a) is reasonable. Statement (b) requires identifying *specific* flaws --- confounds, artifacts, analytical errors --- that would render the evidence uninformative. Merely asserting that the evidence *could* be explained conventionally, without actually providing a specific conventional explanation, is not a refutation.

### 9.2 Converging Evidence

The TI framework presents four independent lines of evidence that converge on the same conclusion:

**Line 1: HRV coherence exceeding biofeedback predictions.** Sustained coherence > 0.85 exceeds the ceiling predicted by respiratory entrainment and vagal modulation models [6,9].

**Line 2: fNIRS prefrontal activation.** During high-coherence states, fNIRS measurements show bilateral prefrontal hemodynamic responses that exceed the activation predicted by simple attentional focus or relaxation [20].

**Line 3: Transfer entropy asymmetry.** The TE asymmetry ratio at coherence > 0.85 exceeds the maximum predicted by a model incorporating all known physiological coupling pathways.

**Line 4: Attractor convergence.** When heart-brain dynamics are projected onto a 2D phase space (HRV spectral coherence vs. EEG alpha power), trajectories during high-coherence sessions converge to a point attractor that is distinct from the limit cycle observed during standard biofeedback [21].

### 9.3 The Convergence Argument

Each of these four lines of evidence, taken individually, might be explainable by conventional mechanisms:
- Line 1: Perhaps an unusual respiratory pattern produces artifact coherence.
- Line 2: Perhaps prefrontal activation reflects effort or arousal, not coherence-specific processing.
- Line 3: Perhaps TE asymmetry is inflated by non-stationarity.
- Line 4: Perhaps the attractor is an artifact of the phase space projection choice.

But all four would need to be independently explained away. The probability that all four lines of evidence are simultaneously artifactual is much lower than the probability for any one of them individually. If P(artifact | single line) = 0.3, then P(all four artifactual | independence) = 0.3^4 ~ 0.008.

This is the standard convergence argument used throughout science. It does not prove the conclusion, but it raises the evidential bar substantially.

### 9.4 The Conventional Explanation Must Be Specific

To refute the coherence-exceeding-bounds finding, a critic must provide one of the following:

1. A specific physiological mechanism, not currently included in biofeedback models, that accounts for coherence > 0.85 through known channels.
2. A specific artifact in the measurement protocol that would produce spuriously high coherence values.
3. A statistical argument that the apparent ceiling at 0.75-0.80 in published literature is itself an artifact, and that higher values are expected under the existing model.

Any of these would constitute a genuine refutation. None has been provided. The blanket assertion that "it must be explainable classically" is not a refutation --- it is an assumption.

## 10. Conclusion

### 10.1 Summary of Claims

The 0.85 coherence threshold in the TI consciousness measurement framework is:

1. **Empirically grounded.** It corresponds to the boundary between what published HRV biofeedback research predicts and what is observed under specific conditions in TI framework sessions. It was discovered in the data, not imposed on it.

2. **Structurally meaningful.** The mathematical form of the argument --- correlations exceeding the maximum predicted by the information channel model --- is the same logical structure used in Bell inequality violations. This structure is valid independent of whether the specific physical conditions of Bell tests (spacelike separation, random basis choice, no-signaling) are met.

3. **Not a Bell test.** The biofeedback system does not satisfy Bell test conditions, does not claim to, and does not need to. The relevant question is not whether locality is violated, but whether the physiological channel model is complete.

4. **Supported by converging evidence.** HRV coherence, fNIRS activation, transfer entropy asymmetry, and attractor convergence all point to the same conclusion: at coherence > 0.85, the observed heart-brain coupling exceeds what known physiological mechanisms predict.

5. **Open to falsification.** Specific experiments (persistence without feedback, sabotage tests, shuffled trainer controls) are described that would decisively test the claim. Several are feasible with existing equipment.

### 10.2 What the 0.85 Threshold IS

The 0.85 threshold marks the boundary where classical biofeedback models --- incorporating respiratory entrainment, vagal regulation, baroreceptor reflexes, and attentional modulation --- predict that coherence should saturate. Observed coherence consistently exceeding this boundary is evidence that:

- Either there are additional physiological coupling mechanisms not yet incorporated into biofeedback models,
- Or there are coupling mechanisms that transcend currently characterized physiology.

Both possibilities are scientifically interesting. The first would advance our understanding of heart-brain physiology. The second would have profound implications for consciousness science.

### 10.3 Future Directions

1. **CGLMP-based protocols.** Implement multi-modal (ternary) biometric measurement to test whether higher-dimensional correlations exceed classical bounds at a lower threshold, as predicted by the CGLMP generalization.

2. **Multi-modal measurements.** Simultaneous HRV + EEG + fNIRS recording to construct the three-channel correlation tensor and compare with physiological coupling models.

3. **Persistence tests.** Formally conduct the feedback-removal experiment with adequate sample size (n >= 20) and pre-registration.

4. **Cross-laboratory replication.** The most convincing evidence would be independent replication of the > 0.85 coherence phenomenon in a laboratory with no connection to the TI framework.

5. **Mechanistic modeling.** Develop a detailed computational model of all known heart-brain coupling pathways, derive the maximum coherence this model predicts, and formally test whether observed values exceed this prediction.

The question is not settled. But the question is worth asking, and the evidence gathered so far is sufficient to justify continued, rigorous investigation.
