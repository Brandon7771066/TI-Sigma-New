1. Fixed-point block: mathematically fine, but much weaker than the RH application suggests

fixedPoint_real, fixedPoint_re, fixedPoint_im, and fixedPoint_complex are all straightforward algebra facts about the involution s ↦ 1 - s. They look fine as Lean lemmas. The issue is not correctness; it is relevance. These theorems prove only that a point equal to its reflected partner must be 1/2. That is useful only after you prove an actual zeta zero satisfies s = 1 - s, which you do not. This matches the earlier PDF’s description of the fixed-point path as one of several structural reformulations converging on σ = 1/2, not as an independent proof about zeros themselves.

One small presentation point: the comment “Its unique fixed point is the critical line” is sloppy. A fixed point of s ↦ 1-s in ℂ is the single point 1/2, not the whole critical line. The theorem statement itself is fine; the prose overstates it.

2. EAR equidistance block: valid algebra, but again only conditional scaffolding

ear_equidistance is the cleanest theorem in the file. It really is just algebra:
|s|² = |1-s|² ↔ Re(s)=1/2.
That is a good formal lemma, and it already appeared in the PDF as one of the central sorry-free results.

But this theorem is also where the core problem becomes obvious: once you assume a zero satisfies the equidistance condition, RH is essentially done. So any later axiom that gives equidistance for zeros is already carrying the hard content.

That means this section is mathematically honest as a lemma, but strategically it shows that your proof effort is concentrated entirely in the bridge from “zeta zero” to “equidistant.”

3. UOP max-min block: elementary optimization, not evidence about zeta zeros

correct as a standalone lemma,

That proof never appears. Instead, the later axiom bypasses it.
