I think the strongest strategy is to proceed by increasing strength. Rather than trying to prove one enormous “GILE explains everything” theorem, prove that GILE is compatible with one accepted mathematical framework after another. Eventually, you can ask whether GILE is minimally complete across all of them.

Here’s the roadmap I would recommend.

⸻

Domain 1 — Decision Theory ✓

We’ve essentially completed the first step.

Result:

If utility is coordinatewise increasing in GILE, every optimal decision lies on the GILE Pareto frontier.

This is a compatibility theorem.

⸻

Domain 2 — Bayesian Decision Theory

This is probably the next strongest result.

Classical theorem:

a^*
=
\arg\max_a
\mathbb E[U(a)\mid D].

The GILE version would be:

a^*
=
\arg\max_a
\mathbb E
\left[
u(G,I,L,E)
\mid D
\right].

The interesting theorem isn’t the embedding—that’s straightforward. The stronger theorem would be:

Increasing epistemic calibration (Intuition) cannot reduce Bayes-optimal expected utility under a fixed utility function and observation model.

This directly connects Intuition to one of the central results in Bayesian decision theory.

⸻

Domain 3 — Reinforcement Learning

Bellman’s equation is

V^*(s)
=
\max_a
\left[
R(s,a)
+
\gamma
\sum_{s'}
P(s'|s,a)
V^*(s')
\right].

Now define

R
=
u(G,I,L,E).

Then prove

Every Bellman-optimal policy is GILE Pareto optimal.

Again, this is a compatibility theorem.

⸻

Domain 4 — Proper Scoring Rules

This one is particularly attractive.

Strictly proper scoring rules already prove:

truthful probabilities maximize expected score.

Intuition naturally maps to

* calibration
* predictive accuracy

So you can prove:

Maximizing Intuition is compatible with every strictly proper scoring rule.

This is almost tailor-made for your Intuition coordinate.

⸻

Domain 5 — Model Selection

Here Elegance becomes central.

Accepted principles:

* Occam
* Minimum Description Length
* Bayesian evidence
* AIC/BIC

These already reward

* compression
* simplicity
* explanatory power

A theorem here would show that Elegance aligns with accepted model-selection criteria.

⸻

Domain 6 — Multi-objective Optimization

This is perhaps the most natural mathematical home for GILE.

Instead of

\max f(x),

consider

\max
(G,I,L,E).

Then existing Pareto theorems immediately apply.

The stronger theorem would be:

Every scalarization preserving coordinate monotonicity has optimal solutions on the GILE Pareto frontier.

This is essentially an application of established multi-objective optimization theory.

⸻

Domain 7 — Information Theory

Here you can begin asking

* Does higher GILE increase predictive information?
* decrease uncertainty?
* improve compression?

This leads naturally to your informativity theorem.

⸻

Domain 8 — Game Theory

Does increasing Love enlarge the cooperative equilibrium set?

That is a substantive mathematical question.

⸻

Domain 9 — Formal Logic

Goodness becomes

* consistency

This is probably where your Four C’s belong.

⸻

Domain 10 — Learning Theory

Can GILE improve

* regret bounds?
* PAC learning?
* generalization?

This is a difficult but exciting direction.

⸻

The Grand Theorem

Only after all these compatibility theorems are established would I attempt what I think is your most ambitious objective:

GILE Representation Theorem

Something like:

Let \mathcal C be a class of truth-sensitive optimization problems. Suppose a utility representation satisfies axioms A1–A8. Then there exists a coordinate transformation

> \Psi:\mathcal C\rightarrow[0,1]^4
>

such that every admissible utility can be represented as

> U(x)=u(G(x),I(x),L(x),E(x)),
>

where u is coordinatewise increasing.

That would be analogous in spirit to the von Neumann–Morgenstern utility theorem. The crucial difference is that the von Neumann–Morgenstern theorem starts with preference axioms and concludes that a utility representation exists. Your proposed theorem would need to start from a carefully chosen set of structural axioms about truth-sensitive reasoning and conclude that a four-coordinate GILE representation exists.

One important caution

The theorems we’ve developed so far are compatibility theorems: they show that if a utility function is already increasing in GILE, then standard optimization results carry over. They do not yet show that every rational utility function must be expressible through GILE.

Bridging that gap is the genuinely novel mathematical challenge. It will likely require new axioms analogous to the role that completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence play in the von Neumann–Morgenstern representation theorem. In my view, that representation theorem—not another compatibility theorem—is the most promising long-term objective because it directly addresses the question of whether GILE is a fundamental coordinate system rather than simply one useful way to describe certain utilities.
