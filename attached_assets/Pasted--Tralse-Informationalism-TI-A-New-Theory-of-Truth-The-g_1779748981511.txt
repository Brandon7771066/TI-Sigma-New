## Tralse Informationalism (TI): A New Theory of Truth

## The greatest misconception in existence that I have identified in my lifetime is the notion that there exist only two truth states: true and false. It is this idea that everything can be captured by either a 0 or a 1. However, this notion is utterly ridiculous, despite the fact that modern computing, engineering, math, and science are built upon this foundation. Bivalent logic originated primarily from Aristotle, but it was refined tremendously by philosophers like George Boole and information theorists like Claude Shannon. The truth is that rather than there being two states of truth, everything lies on a rich multidimensional spectrum of what I call Tralse. Tralse, in plain language, is a term for the general indeterminacy that is present within all statements. That is, all statements have limits and lie on a spectrum of applicability. Moreover, every claim is finite in its existential application. There is no such thing as a statement which can saturate all of reality as being fundamentally one way or the other. Not even the statement 2+2=4 counts, since you can't even see mathematical statements to begin with! Indeed, mathematical and logical statements seem to be universally applicable. But this is merely an illusion! Every statement - without exception - has limitations, including this one! Tralse Informationalism - which I, Brandon Charles Emerick, invented and published over 100 papers about - states that there are actually four truth values which are required to adequately identify truth: True, False, Indeterminate, and Meta-Indeterminate (My published intro paper: https://zenodo.org/records/18930175). In addition to these truth values, there are a myriad number of what I call Meta-Truths (MTs), reactions to any of the four truth claims! 

## To claim that something is true means to say that it exists, is coherent, applies to reality, meets pragmatic objectives, and is authoritative. Thus, truth has a multidimensional richness which mere 0s and 1s cannot capture. To say that something is false, on the other hand, is actually to say that it is true - in the opposite direction! Thus, false is a "truth stated oppositely." The next label Indeterminate describes statements which lie in between true and false. These include statements like "I love you" or "This food is delicious" or "The next president of the United States will be x." 

## On the other hand, meta-indeterminate refers to concepts which are incoherent or undecided to an extent just like indeterminate statements are. However, they are so incoherent that they are to be regarded as nonsense. That is, they are too contradictory and inapplicable to the real world. Nonetheless, they are to be disregarded. These statements include famous paradoxes like the Liar's Paradox: "I am lying." Meta-indeterminate is named as such because it represents an (indeterminate) statement or set of concepts that - taken together - represent "indeterminate indeterminacy." That phrase is the signature of nonsense - and nonsense deserves to be named as such rather than with a 0 or 1!

## Meta-truths (MT) represent an agent's reactions to the four fundamental truth values. One example is "worth doing anyway" after something may be found to be "low risk" for example. But one of the most commonly applied meta-truth values is Moot. To claim that a statement is Moot is to accept it as it is - as true, false, indeterminate, or meta-indeterminate - but ultimately the statement "doesn't matter". This is one of the most common logical operations that humans perform and yet it hardly gets any credit! 

## It is quite clear that the a great portion of everyday speech cannot be rigidly graded as a 0 or a 1. Using Fleiss Kappa, an interrater reliability metric, I have empirically demonstrated that my four-tiered system of logic exhibits a value of k = 0.9 in AI classification, versus 0.4-0.6 for binary. AI seems to be quite advanced but the truth is that with the current architecture, it cannot even grade most statements as either true or false to begin with.
