## Next step: split LCC into two claims

**Weak LCC, provable under guardrails:**

[
\text{persistent non-spurious mutual predictability}
\Rightarrow
\text{causal coupling or shared causal mechanism}
]

**Strong LCC, not yet proven:**

[
\text{high synchrony}
\Rightarrow
\text{direct bidirectional causation}
]

The strong version is too vulnerable because hidden common drivers can mimic synchrony. So the proof should target:

[
\boxed{
\text{If common causes, measurement artifacts, autocorrelation, selection effects, and imposed stimuli are ruled out, then persistent bidirectional predictive dependence implies causal coupling.}
}
]

That is defensible.

## Formal guardrails

Let (X_t,Y_t) be systems with synchronization potential.

Require:

1. **Temporal persistence:** dependence holds across long windows, not isolated bursts.
2. **Bidirectionality:** (X_t) predicts (Y_{t+k}) and (Y_t) predicts (X_{t+k}).
3. **Surrogate survival:** real coupling exceeds shuffled, phase-randomized, and matched-common-input nulls.
4. **Conditional survival:** dependence remains after conditioning on known common drivers (Z_t).
5. **Perturbability:** changing (X) changes future (Y), and changing (Y) changes future (X), when intervention is possible.
6. **Synchronization potential:** both systems have internal dynamics capable of entrainment, phase-locking, predictive adaptation, or state-coupling.

Then LCC becomes:

[
X \leftrightarrow Y
]

not merely:

[
X \sim Y.
]

## Proof-by-contradiction skeleton

Assume high persistent bidirectional synchrony exists, guardrails pass, but there is **no bidirectional causation**.

Then the synchrony must be explained by one of:

[
X\to Y,\quad Y\to X,\quad Z\to X,Y,\quad \text{artifact},\quad \text{chance},\quad \text{selection},\quad \text{shared deterministic clock}.
]

But:

* bidirectional lagged prediction rules out pure one-way causation;
* conditional tests and surrogates rule out (Z);
* surrogate controls rule out artifact/chance/autocorrelation;
* long-window stability rules out transient coincidence;
* perturbation, when available, rules out passive common timing.

Therefore no non-bidirectional explanation remains.

So:

[
\neg(X\leftrightarrow Y)
\Rightarrow
\bot
]

hence:

[
X\leftrightarrow Y.
]
