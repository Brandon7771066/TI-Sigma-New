## Why These Three Chapters

- **Chapter 1** is the diagnostic opening — the question every reader needs to feel before they will accept any new logical machinery. It establishes the register (academic but accessible) and the reach (philosophy, mathematics, decision science, lived experience) of the book without yet asking the reader to learn anything technical.

- **Chapter 3** is the technical heart of Part I — the formal introduction of Tralse, the conservativity-over-classical-logic theorem, the three lived examples (medical diagnosis, vow conflict, scientific inference under model misspecification), and the bridge into the architectural chapters that follow. An agent or editor can read this chapter and judge whether the central contribution is defensible.

- **Chapter 7** is the longest and most practically demanding chapter — the six steps of Myrion Resolution with three full worked decisions and three named failure modes (premature convergence, paralytic recursion, bad-faith MR). It shows that the framework cashes out in actionable procedure, not just terminology.

Together the three chapters give an agent the full arc: *why this book is needed* (Ch. 1), *what the central new idea is* (Ch. 3), and *what the reader will be able to do after reading it* (Ch. 7).
