Dear [Editor],

I am writing to introduce *Tralse: A New Logic for an Uncertain World*, a 280-page book of experimental philosophy directed at the educated general reader, which I believe would sit naturally on your list alongside Kahneman's *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, Pearl's *The Book of Why*, and Hofstadter's *I Am a Strange Loop*.

The book introduces a fifth truth value — *Tralse* — to the standard logical vocabulary of true and false. The claim is not that bivalence is wrong within its proper domain, but that the propositions on which most human lives actually turn (*I love this person, this treatment is working, I am free*) are not bivalent in any operative sense, and that the absence of a precise vocabulary for stable indeterminacy distorts both everyday reasoning and the formal disciplines that depend on it. The book develops a five-valued truth architecture that is conservative over classical logic on its bivalent fragment, a four-dimensional wellbeing compass (GILE: Goodness, Intuition, Love, Environment), and an iterative convergence procedure (Myrion Resolution) that gives readers a practical method for arriving at stable judgments under genuine uncertainty. Each chapter pairs a conceptual exposition with a stated empirical prediction or formal derivation, in the manner that Daniel Dennett has called "philosophy with the price tag still on it."

The framework — which I have developed over four years under the name Tralse Informationalism (TI Sigma) — has produced a body of supporting work I am happy to make available: more than 770 internal research briefs, a Lean4 formalization of the core logic, a working consciousness-measurement platform with EEG and HRV biometric integration, and a public archive of papers on Zenodo with permanent DOIs. Among the framework's first-order predictions is a sharp-interval result on the distribution of nontrivial Riemann zeros — the so-called "Sacred Interval," in which 20% of the critical-line range contains roughly 80% of the local activity — which I report against approximately one million zeros from the LMFDB dataset. The book references these results but does not depend on them; the argument for the five-valued architecture is independent of the more speculative mathematical claims and is presented as such.

I write to a general audience while holding a working scientist's standard for what may be claimed. My background is in entrepreneurship rather than the academy: I am the founder and CEO of BlissGene Therapeutics, a seed-stage company developing biometric protocols for permanent wellbeing, with $750K of committed funding and a working clinical pipeline. I am currently a graduate student in the Maharishi International University program in consciousness research and intend to pursue a Physics PhD at Arizona State following completion of the Georgia Tech OMSCS degree. The book has been read in draft by several technical reviewers in mathematical logic and consciousness research; their endorsements and a sample chapter are available on request.

The market case is straightforward. Readers of Kahneman, Taleb, Hofstadter, and Pearl have demonstrated a sustained appetite for books that add a single precise concept (cognitive bias, fat tails, strange loops, causal directionality) to the educated reader's vocabulary and then trace its consequences across domains. *Tralse* offers that same shape: a single concept (a stable fifth truth value) that, once introduced, reorganizes how one thinks about probability, decision-making, consciousness, and the limits of formal systems. There is, to my knowledge, no comparable trade book on the subject — the closest precedent being Susan Haack's *Deviant Logic*, which is a forty-year-old academic monograph aimed at professional philosophers.

The full manuscript is approximately 280 pages and is available in complete draft form.
